# Chapter 76: Henry (Part 2)


James led them down the hallway.


The smile on his face said everything.


And somehow nothing.


Because there were no words big enough for this.


Not really.


When they reached Karen's room, James pushed open the door.


Jane went in first.


Then stopped.


Karen was sitting up in bed.


A tiny bundle wrapped in a hospital blanket rested in her arms.


Baby Henry.


For a moment everything became very quiet.


Karen looked up and immediately smiled.


"There are my favorite godparents."


Jane laughed through the tears already gathering in her eyes.


"Oh my God."


Karen looked exhausted.


Happy.


Overwhelmed.


Perfect.


Jane crossed the room and hugged her carefully.


Then leaned down and kissed the top of Henry's tiny head.


And everything broke.


Because for one devastating second...


She wasn't looking at Henry.


She was standing in another hospital room.


Another life.


Another heartbreak.


The ultrasound.


The silence.


Scott's face.


Andrew holding her.


The crushing reality that she and Andrew had lost their baby.


Their son.


Jane closed her eyes.


Trying to pull herself back.


Trying to breathe.


Trying to remember where she was.


But it was already too late.


Andrew saw it.


James saw it.


Karen saw it.


Because they remembered too.


Karen's smile softened immediately.


A mother's smile now.


Gentler than Jane had ever seen.


Maybe it was the medication.


Maybe it was Henry.


Maybe becoming a mother had changed something.


Whatever it was, Karen seemed calmer than she'd ever been.


"Honey."


Jane looked at her.


Karen's voice was soft.


"It's okay."


The words nearly unraveled her all over again.


Karen reached for her hand.


"Thank you for being here."


Another.


"Thank you for loving our son."


Another.


"Thank you for being his godmother."


Jane nodded.


Unable to speak.


Karen smiled.


"Would you like to hold him?"


James looked at Andrew.


Then at Jane.


And stepped back.


Giving them space.


Jane swallowed hard.


Then nodded.


"Yeah."


Karen carefully transferred Henry into her arms.


The second Jane held him, tears slipped free.


Tiny.


Perfect.


Warm.


Real.


Henry shifted slightly.


Then settled.


Trusting her completely.


Jane stared down at him.


And her heart hurt.


Not because she wasn't happy.


She was.


Completely.


But grief and joy had always lived closer together than most people realized.


Behind her, Andrew went still.


Completely still.


Because seeing Jane hold baby Henry brought him right back too.


Back to the hospital.


Back to twelve weeks.


Back to holding Jane while she cried.


Back to feeling completely helpless.


Back to the future that disappeared before either of them could touch it.


For a moment he couldn't breathe.


Then he forced himself back into the room.


Back to Henry.


Back to now.


Back to the life they'd actually lived.


He walked over slowly.


Stopping beside Jane.


Looking down at the baby.


Neither spoke.


Neither had to.


Karen leaned back against James.


James wrapped an arm around her shoulders.


Watching.


Because they remembered too.


They remembered the grief.


The devastation.


The dreams.


The plans.


The future Jane and Andrew had once imagined.


And maybe selfishly...


There would always be a small part of both of them that wondered about that alternate life.


The one where the four of them raised their children together.


The one that never happened.


Neither said it aloud.


They didn't need to.


Across the room, Jane shifted and sat down on the small sofa.


Andrew sat beside her.


Without hesitation.


Without thinking.


Without discussion.


Just instinct.


The argument about the divorce papers disappeared.


Temporarily.


Nothing else mattered right now.


Henry stretched one tiny hand free from the blanket.


Andrew immediately reached for it.


Henry's fingers wrapped around one of his.


The simple gesture nearly broke him all over again.


Jane looked over.


Their eyes met.


For a moment there were no words.


Just understanding.


Just history.


Just love.


Not romantic.


Not anymore.


But something no less profound.


A shared loss.


A shared memory.


A shared piece of their souls that would belong to each other forever.


Andrew gently wrapped an arm around her shoulders.


Jane leaned against him.


Just slightly.


Both of them staring down at Henry.


Time seemed to stop.


Finally a nurse entered.


"Sorry."


She smiled.


"We need him for a quick checkup."


Jane looked at Karen.


Karen nodded.


So Jane carefully handed Henry over.


The moment felt enormous.


The room felt emptier immediately.


The nurse disappeared.


And reality slowly returned.


Jane looked over at Karen.


"How are you feeling?"


Karen laughed softly.


"Like I got hit by a truck."


"Normal."


James supplied.


Karen rolled her eyes.


Then smiled.


"But good."


Another.


"Really good."


Jane smiled.


"How long are they keeping you?"


"One more night."


Karen shrugged.


"Since everything went smoothly, I should be home tomorrow."


Jane immediately pointed at her.


"Perfect."


Karen narrowed her eyes.


That tone never meant anything good.


Jane grinned.


"There will be a welcome-home party."


"Oh no."


James laughed.


"Oh yes."


Jane nodded.


"I'm not a party planner."


Another.


"Definitely not like you."


Karen smiled proudly.


"As you should acknowledge."


Jane ignored her.


"But between me, Andrew, Adam, Scott, Kirby and apparently half the hospital..."


Another.


"We can figure something out."


James laughed.


"I'd pay money to watch that planning meeting."


"It'll be chaos."


Karen agreed.


Jane nodded.


"Almost certainly."


For the first time since entering the room, everyone laughed.


And for a little while, things felt normal again.


Like family.
