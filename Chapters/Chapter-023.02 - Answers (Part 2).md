# Chapter 23: Answers (Part 2)

The room felt strangely quiet after Nurse Hayle left.

Too quiet.


Jane stared at the ceiling.

Avoiding Andrew.

Avoiding her thoughts.

Avoiding the giant possibility sitting in the middle of the room.


Andrew watched her for almost thirty seconds.

Then sighed.


"There she is."


Jane immediately groaned.


"No."


"What?"


"I know that tone."


A small smile appeared.


"Good."


"I am not doing this with you right now."


"Yeah, you are."


Jane rolled her eyes.


Because of course he wasn't letting this go.


Andrew had never been the kind of person who allowed her to hide behind vague answers.


Sometimes that had been one of the best things about him.


Sometimes it had been one of the worst.


"What are you afraid of?"


Jane looked away immediately.


"There it is."


"What?"


"The cop thing."


Andrew laughed softly.


"I'm off duty."


"No, you're not."


"Fair."


The smile faded.


And suddenly it was just Andrew.


Not Dr. Call.

Not the attending physician.

Not the cop.


Just Andrew.


His expression softened.


"Jane."


She hated that tone too.


The gentle one.


The one he only used when he knew she was hurting.


"What are you afraid of?"


Jane stared stubbornly at the ceiling.


Silence.


Andrew waited.


Then:


"You know what's funny?"


"No."


"I've known you almost my entire life."


A pause.


"And you still think I can't tell when you're lying."


Jane closed her eyes.


"I am not lying."


"You're hiding."


That landed.

Because it was true.


Another silence followed.


Then Andrew squeezed her hand.


"Talk to me."


The kindness in his voice was almost worse than the interrogation.


For a moment Jane didn't speak.


Then finally:


"What if Kelly is right?"


There it was.


The thing living in her head all week.


Andrew nodded slowly.


"Okay."


Jane laughed bitterly.


"'Okay.'"


"I'm listening."


A tear escaped before she could stop it.


"What if she is right?"


A pause.


"What happens then?"


Andrew didn't answer.


Because he knew she wasn't finished.


Jane laughed weakly.


A broken sound.


"You know the worst part?"


She looked down at her hands.


"It isn't even the possibility."


A pause.


"I can barely let myself think about that."


Another tear slipped free.


"It's Adam."


Andrew's heart broke a little.


Because of course it was.


Always Adam.


Jane swallowed hard.


"I finally let myself have this."


The words came out small.

Fragile.


"I finally let myself be happy."


Andrew remained silent.


Listening.


Letting her talk.


"I love him."


A pause.


"And I know he loves me."


Another.


"But nobody can tell me what's going to happen six months from now."


There it was.


The thing they all lived with.


Integration.


The giant uncertainty hanging over all of their lives.


The thing nobody liked discussing.


Because nobody had answers.


Not Dr. Ryan.

Not the specialists.

Not Adam.

Not Andrew.


Nobody.


Jane wiped angrily at her eyes.


"What if I lose him?"


The words finally came out.


The ones she'd been carrying around for months.


"What if integration changes everything?"


A pause.


"What if the Adam I know disappears?"


Another.


"What if Original Adam comes back?"


The room grew still.


Jane laughed weakly.


"It sounds awful when I say it out loud."


"No."


Andrew shook his head.


"It sounds honest."


That almost made her cry harder.


Because honesty had always been Andrew's thing.


Even when it hurt.


Especially when it hurt.


Jane stared at the blanket covering her lap.


Then finally said the thing she hadn't said to anyone.


Not even Adam.


"What if I have to do it alone?"


Andrew frowned.


"Do what alone?"


Jane looked up.


And for a second he could see exactly how terrified she was.


Not scared.

Terrified.


"If Kelly is right."


A pause.


"And I lose Adam."


Another.


"What if I have to raise a baby by myself?"


The words shattered something inside her.


Because there it was.


The real fear.


Not the test.

Not the nausea.

Not even the possibility itself.


The future.


The terrifying future she'd been imagining every night since dinner.


"What if he doesn't remember me?"


A tear rolled down her cheek.


"What if he doesn't want me?"


Another.


"What if he doesn't love me?"


Andrew immediately shook his head.


"Jane."


"No."


Her voice cracked.


"Andrew, nobody knows."


The tears came faster now.


"Nobody knows what happens after integration."


Another.


"And if I had a baby..."


She stopped.

Unable to finish.


Andrew understood anyway.


Perfectly.


Jane looked away.


"I don't want to do that alone."


The confession came out barely above a whisper.


"I'm so tired of being strong."


A silence settled over the room.


Then she laughed.

A small, broken laugh.


"And honestly?"


Andrew looked at her.


"This is definitely not how I imagined any of this going."


That earned the smallest smile.


"Fair."


Jane shook her head.


"I really didn't think being a mom was in the cards for me."


The sentence stopped.


Halfway through.


Because suddenly both of them knew exactly what came next.


After they lost their baby.


The memory sat between them.

Unspoken.


Years old.

Still painful.


Jane looked away.


"I just didn't."


Andrew's eyes filled.


Because he remembered too.


Every second of it.


Without thinking, he stood.


Crossed the small distance between them.


And wrapped both arms around her.


A real hug.


Tight.

Protective.

Instinctive.


Jane folded into him immediately.


And there it was.


That familiar feeling.


Safe.


The feeling she'd known since she was a kid.


Andrew rested his cheek against her hair.


For a long moment neither spoke.


Then quietly:


"Hey."


Jane closed her eyes.


"What?"


"You are trying to survive ten futures that haven't happened."


She laughed through tears.


"That sounds like something Scott would say."


A smile tugged at Andrew's mouth.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"He'd probably say it better."


That actually made her laugh.


A real laugh this time.


Andrew pulled back just enough to look at her.


"Look at me."


Reluctantly, she did.


"We don't know anything yet."


A pause.


"Not about the tests."


Another.


"Not about integration."


Another.


"And definitely not about six months from now."


His forehead rested gently against hers.


"But if there is one thing I know?"


Jane swallowed hard.


"What?"


"Adam loves you."


The tears returned instantly.


"Andrew—"


"No."


His voice was firm.

Certain.


"That man would move heaven and earth for you."


A pause.


"And whatever happens?"


Another.


"You are not doing it alone."


This time she believed him.


Not because he could predict the future.


Not because he had answers.


But because Andrew had spent twenty years proving that when her world fell apart, he showed up.


Every single time.


After a while, he brushed a tear from her cheek.


Then asked softly:


"Do you want me to call Adam?"


The question nearly broke her.


Because more than anything she wanted him here.


Wanted his arms around her.

Wanted his impossible optimism.

Wanted Adam.


But she shook her head.


"No."


"Jane."


"He's working tonight."


A pause.


"He's probably already on his way in."


Another.


"And we don't know anything yet."


Andrew studied her.

Then finally nodded.


Because she was right.


There was a difference between fear and fact.


And right now all they had was fear.


So he stayed beside her.

Still holding her hand.

Still worried.

Still ready to catch her if she started falling apart again.


And together they waited for the answers neither of them were sure they wanted.


Andrew looked at the results.

Then looked at Jane.

Then back at the results.


Jane immediately knew.


"Oh no."


Andrew swallowed.


Jane started crying before he said a word.


"Oh no."


"Jane."


She covered her face.


"Andrew..."


He crossed the room immediately.

Pulled her into his arms before she could completely fall apart.


For a moment she just cried against his shoulder.


"I'm scared."


"I know."


"I'm really scared."


"I know."


Andrew held her tighter.


"You need to call Adam."


Jane immediately shook her head.


"No."


"Jane."


"No."


"He needs to be here."


"I can't."


"Why?"


Because if she called Adam, it became real.


Before either of them could continue, the door opened.


"Jane."


Adam.


Jane immediately closed her eyes.


Perfect.


Of course.


Adam was already crossing the room.


"What happened?"


His eyes moved from Jane.

To Andrew.

Back to Jane.


"Why are you crying?"


Neither answered immediately.


Adam looked increasingly alarmed.


"What happened?"


Andrew stood.


"She was leaving her appointment with Dr. Ryan."


A pause.


"She fainted."


Adam froze.


"She what?"


"I caught her before she hit the floor."


"What?"


Adam looked directly at Jane.


"You fainted?"


Jane nodded.


Adam ran a hand through his hair.


"Why didn't anybody call me?"


"Adam..."


"No, seriously."


His voice softened immediately.


"Why didn't you call me?"


Jane looked away.


Because she didn't have an answer.


Andrew watched both of them.

Then quietly:


"Adam."


Adam looked up.


Andrew held up the chart.


Adam's eyes moved to it.


Then back to Andrew.


Then back to Jane.


And suddenly he understood.


"Oh."


The room went silent.


Adam stared.


Then looked at Jane.


"Oh."


Jane started crying harder.


"Jane."


Adam crossed the room in two steps.


Immediately pulling her into his arms.


"Hey."


A kiss against her forehead.


Another.


"Hey."


Jane buried her face against his chest.


For a moment neither said anything.


Adam was smiling.

Actually smiling.


Then he looked down at her.


And finally noticed.


The fear.


Not nerves.

Not uncertainty.


Fear.


Real fear.


His smile disappeared immediately.


"Jane."


She shook her head.


"Jane."


"Adam..."


"No."


His voice cracked.


"Talk to me."


A pause.


"Please."


Jane looked up.


And for the first time he could see how terrified she really was.


"I don't want to lose you."


Adam froze.


"What?"


More tears.


"When it was just me..."


A shaky breath escaped.


"I could live with it."


Adam stared.


"The integration."


The room became very still.


"I could handle not knowing."


Another tear slipped down her cheek.


"I could handle being scared."


Another.


"But now?"


Her voice broke.


"Adam, I don't know what I would do if I lost you."


A pause.


"I don't know what I would do if I had to do this by myself."


That hit him.

Hard.


Not the news.


The fear.


Because suddenly he understood.


The DID.


The integration.


The uncertainty.


She wasn't afraid of becoming a mother.


She was afraid of becoming one alone.


And for the first time Adam realized she had been carrying that fear for months.


"Jane..."


"I want to be happy."


A sob escaped her.


"I am happy."


Another.


"I love you."


Another.


"But I'm terrified."


Adam closed his eyes.


Because now he was scared too.


Not about today.


About her.


About how long she'd been carrying this alone.


He wrapped both arms around her.


"Hey."


A pause.


"We're going to figure it out."


Jane laughed through tears.


"You don't know that."


"I do."


"No."


"Yes."


A tiny smile appeared.


"You're being stubborn."


"You're one to talk."


That finally earned the smallest laugh.


Adam rested his forehead against hers.


"I'm not going anywhere."


Jane closed her eyes.


They both knew he couldn't promise that.

Not really.


Not with integration.


Not with everything coming.


But she let him say it anyway.


Because right now he needed to say it.


And she needed to hear it.


"Can we do something?"


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"Can we be happy for one minute?"


A tearful laugh escaped.


"What?"


"Just one."


Adam smiled.


"One minute."


A pause.


"Please."


Jane nodded.

Slowly.


Adam's eyes filled.


"We're having a baby."


The words hung between them.


Beautiful.

Impossible.

Terrifying.


"Our baby."


Jane reached up.

Touched his face.


Ran her fingers through his hair.


Because she wanted this.

She really did.


This was good.


This was wonderful.


This was the man she loved more than anyone.


And somehow they had created something together.


Even if the future terrified her.


Even if she had no idea what came next.


Adam kissed her forehead.


"We'll figure it out."


A pause.


"I promise."


And this time neither of them let go.
