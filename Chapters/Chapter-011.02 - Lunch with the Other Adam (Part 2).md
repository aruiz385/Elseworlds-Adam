# Chapter 11: Lunch with the Other Adam (Part 2)

The restaurant sat right on the edge of the bay.

Large windows reflected the water, and outside, sailboats drifted lazily through the harbor. It should have been peaceful.

Instead, Jane felt awkward.

Very awkward.

The moment they sat down at a table overlooking the water, she immediately realized she had no idea what this lunch was supposed to be.

Yesterday she'd wanted answers.

Today she had them.

Or at least some of them.

Now she was sitting across from a man who looked like Adam, sounded like Adam, knew almost everything about her—and wasn't Adam.

It was exhausting.

The waitress came and went quickly, leaving them alone with drinks and an uncomfortable silence.

Jane stared out at the water.

The Other Adam stared at her.

Eventually he broke first.

"What's on your mind Jane?"

Jane let out a breath.

"Honestly, nothing."

He looked skeptical immediately.

"I don't know what to do or say to you."

That seemed to catch him off guard.

She continued.

"It's odd because when I talked with Adam, there were never enough hours. We talked and time flew by."

A sadness settled over her.

"I just don't know what to say to you."

The words landed a little harder than she'd intended.

She saw it.

A brief flash of hurt in his eyes before it disappeared.

He leaned back.

"Okay."

A pause.

"Let me take a page out of Adam's book then."

Jane immediately looked suspicious.

The Other Adam ignored her.

"You said you saw Andrew this morning. How did that make you feel?"

"Nope."

His eyebrow rose.

"Nope?"

"You don't get to ask about Andrew."

He opened his mouth.

"No."

Jane pointed at him.

"Absolutely not."

The Other Adam sighed dramatically.

"I was trying to help."

Jane ignored him.

"But I did hear him say he was dropping by to check on you."

That got his attention.

"He thought you looked weird."

A pause.

"He said you didn't look like you."

The Other Adam looked surprisingly unconcerned.

"Just a heads up."

"You almost sound concerned."

Jane rolled her eyes.

"Just looking out for you."

His expression softened.

"You care."

Then he smiled.

"Thanks."

The eye roll he received for that was immediate.

And for some reason, it seemed to make him happy.

Jane noticed.

Unfortunately.


A few minutes later they were both sipping their drinks in relative silence.

The Other Adam watched her carefully.

Eventually he leaned forward.

"You look worried today."

Jane looked away.

"You can talk to me, you know."

She laughed.

"Can I?"

"I promised I would listen."

His voice became more serious.

"I promised I would help and listen, so I am here to do that."

Then he added quietly:

"Jane, I heard your stories before."

She narrowed her eyes.

Immediately.

"Right."

A pause.

"When you were selectively peeking in on my conversations with Adam."

The Other Adam groaned.

"Okay, fine."

He lifted both hands.

"I was listening."

Jane stared.

"I was curious about you."

His expression turned innocent.

"Is that a crime?"

"It should be."

He laughed.


Jane took another sip of her Diet Coke.

Then decided she was going to vent.

Whether he liked it or not.

"I don't know if you recall peeking in the day I introduced Adam to my best friend Karen and her boyfriend James."

The Other Adam immediately smiled.

"Yeah."

Jane blinked.

"You do?"

"Of course."

The smile widened.

"I remember Adam giving Karen a look that made her blush."

Jane groaned.

Of course he remembered that.

"But your friend looked ready to fight him."

The Other Adam laughed.

"Because she loves you."

Something softened in his expression.

"I thought that was really great."

A pause.

"Everyone needs a ride or die."

Jane stared at him.

"You remember that?"

The Other Adam looked confused.

"Why wouldn't I?"

For the first time all afternoon, something inside Jane eased.

Just a little.

Maybe her Adam wasn't completely gone.

Not if someone else remembered all the same moments.

The Other Adam seemed to see that realization happen.

"Well," he said quietly, "I see your friend Karen."

Jane waited.

"You should think of me as that for Adam."

The statement surprised her.

It wasn't what she'd expected him to say.

At all.


She looked down at her drink.

Then sighed.

"Karen and James are getting married."

The Other Adam immediately smiled.

"That's good."

"It is."

Then she grimaced.

"And it's not."

That made no sense.

Apparently.

Because he looked completely lost.

"I don't follow."

A pause.

"I hope that's a good thing."

Then he frowned.

"Unless you're one of those girls who isn't happy for her friend because she wants to be the one getting married."

Jane practically choked.

"No!"

Several nearby tables glanced over.

Jane lowered her voice.

"I am not one of those girls."

The Other Adam looked relieved.

Good.

He should.

"I've already been married."

She shook her head.

"I have zero desire to do that again."

"Oh."

He looked genuinely embarrassed.

"Sorry."

"Karen asked me to be her maid of honor."

His expression brightened.

"That's great."

Jane stared at him.

Then pointed.

"Andrew."

His face immediately fell.

"Oh."

She nodded.

"Andrew is James's best friend."

The realization finally hit.

"Smart guy."

Jane gestured.

"Do the math."

The Other Adam blinked.

"Oh."

A beat.

"So how exactly does one avoid their ex while standing beside them at an altar supporting mutual best friends?"

Jane pointed her straw at him.

"You are mocking me."

"I am not."

"You are."

"I promise I'm not."

He looked entirely sincere.

Which somehow made it worse.


Jane shook her head.

"I hid behind a column this morning."

The Other Adam frowned.

"What?"

"To avoid Andrew."

The silence that followed was unbearable.

"I hid."

She pointed at herself.

"Behind a column. Who does that?!"

The Other Adam studied her for a long moment.

Then quietly said:

"Someone who still loves the person."

The words landed heavily between them.

For the first time all lunch, neither made a joke.

Neither laughed.

Neither rolled their eyes.

They just looked at each other.

And Jane realized something.

He understood.

He actually understood.


"I am always going to love Andrew."

The confession came easily.

Too easily.

"He was my first everything."

Her eyes dropped to the table.

"My only everything."

A sad smile appeared.

"But loving someone means knowing when to let them go."

The Other Adam listened carefully.

"Especially when you know you can't give them what they want."

He leaned forward slightly.

"And what did Andrew want that you couldn't give him exactly?"

Jane looked out at the water.

"A future."

The answer came immediately.

"He wants a wife."

A pause.

"A family."

She swallowed.

"I don't know if I can ever give that to anyone."

Not after Kirby.

Not after the baby.

Not after everything.

"I couldn't promise him something I wasn't sure I could give."

She smiled sadly.

"Kelly can."

The Other Adam watched her quietly.

"He deserves that."

For several seconds he said nothing.

Then he reached across the table and took her hand.

Jane froze.


"I don't know what it's like to love someone like that."

The confession surprised her.

"I've never been in love before."

His thumb brushed across the back of her hand.

"But I hope someday I can take a page from your book."

Jane stared.

The sincerity in his voice caught her off guard.

"You love fiercely."

His expression softened.

"That's admirable."

Then he sighed.

"Andrew seems like a nice guy."

Jane waited.

"But he's an idiot."

She laughed despite herself.

The Other Adam shook his head.

"Because if I were him, I wouldn't let you push me away."

A pause.

"Titles and kids be damned."

Jane looked away quickly.


"Listen."

His tone shifted.

More serious.

"I know you're grieving."

"You feel guilt."

He squeezed her hand.

"Those aren't bad feelings."

Jane looked up.

"You just have to control them."

A pause.

"Don't let those emotions control you."

The words reminded her painfully of therapy.

Of Scott.

Of Dr. Ryan.

Of Adam.

The original Adam.

Then he added:

"This morning when you saw Andrew, you let panic take over."

Jane sighed.

"Thanks."

The sarcasm made him smile.

"And I know."

Her voice cracked.

"I can't help it."

She looked down.

"It's the same way I react when I hear one of Kirby's songs."

Suddenly she was crying.

Again.

"I feel like I can't breathe."

The tears came faster.

"I feel like I am drowning."

The Other Adam immediately tightened his grip on her hands.

"Have you ever heard of music therapy?"

Jane blinked.

"What?"

"Ask Dr. Ryan."

His voice was calm.

Steady.

"It might help."

He nodded slowly.

"She can explain it better than I can."

A pause.

"But trust me on that one."


Jane looked at him.

Really looked at him.

And suddenly fear settled in her chest.

Not fear of him.

Fear for him.

Because the thing she'd been terrified of yesterday was already happening.

They'd been sitting there talking for over an hour.

And she hadn't noticed the time.

Not once.

Worse.

For the first time since Adam disappeared—

she hadn't been thinking about Adam at all.

And that scared her more than anything.
