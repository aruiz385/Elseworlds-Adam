# Chapter 48: The Weight of the Dead


Jane never hit the floor.


One moment she was standing.


The next she was falling.


And then there was nothing.


"Jane!"


Andrew caught her before she collapsed completely.


The mug shattered against the hardwood floor.


Tea.


Ceramic.


Silence.


He lifted her carefully.


Carried her to the couch.


"Jane."


Nothing.


"Jane."


Still nothing.


Fear immediately flooded his chest.


No.


Not like this.


Not again.


He gently brushed damp hair away from her face.


"Come on."


Another.


"Jane, wake up."


Nothing.


Andrew sat beside her.


Staring at the woman he'd loved for most of his life.


The girl he'd met when he was five years old.


The girl he'd spent years trying to protect.


The girl he had failed to protect more times than he cared to admit.


And suddenly he was overwhelmed by the weight of it.


The last five years.


The grief.


The funerals.


The therapy.


The nightmares.


The guilt.


God.


The guilt.


Some wounds never fully healed.


They simply became easier to carry.


Kirby.


The wedding.


Forty-eight hours before.


The secret he and Jane carried.


The baby they lost two months later.


The grief that had nearly destroyed both of them.


The relationship they tried to build from tragedy.


The relationship tragedy eventually destroyed.


He had buried those memories.


Or at least tried to.


Now they were all back.


Every single one of them.


And he knew exactly why Jane had collapsed.


Because this wasn't just about Kirby being alive.


It was everything.


Everything she had spent years fighting to survive.


Everything she had spent years learning to live with.


Everything that had finally started to hurt less.


Adam.


Lizzy.


The life she'd rebuilt.


The happiness she'd finally allowed herself to have.


Now all of it had been thrown into question.


Andrew lowered his head.


Resting his forehead gently against hers.


"Jane."


His voice broke.


"Please wake up."


Tears slipped down his face.


"God..."


Another.


"Please wake up."


His hand tightened around hers.


"Jane, I can't do this without you."


The admission escaped before he could stop it.


Raw.


Honest.


Terrified.


"I know this is unfair."


Another.


"I know."


Another.


"I don't want to put you through this again."


His voice cracked.


"Being stuck in the middle of us again."


Another tear fell.


"I know."


Another.


"But I need you."


He closed his eyes.


Desperate now.


"You're the only other person who knows what this feels like."


Another.


"The only one."


His voice dropped to a whisper.


"Please wake up."


A tear slipped from his cheek.


Fell onto hers.


And Jane's eyes fluttered open.


For a moment she looked confused.


Disoriented.


Lost.


Then she saw Andrew.


And remembered.


Everything.


The hospital.


The DNA.


Kirby.


Alive.


The nightmare returned all at once.


"Andrew..."


The word barely existed.


The second she spoke, Andrew collapsed against her.


Neither cared.


Neither pretended.


They simply held each other.


The same way they had all those years ago.


The night Kirby died.


Two people drowning in grief.


Trying desperately to keep the other afloat.


Jane cried harder than she had in years.


Not because she was unhappy.


Not because she wanted Kirby dead.


God.


She was happy he was alive.


Somewhere underneath the panic.


Underneath the fear.


Underneath everything.


There was happiness.


But the guilt...


The guilt was crushing.


Because all she could think about was Adam.


And Lizzy.


And the life she'd built.


"Andrew."


She was shaking now.


"I can't breathe."


Another.


"Please tell me this isn't happening."


"Jane."


Andrew held her shoulders.


"You have to calm down."


Another.


"Please."


She shook her head.


Tears running freely.


"It's not that I don't want him to be alive."


Her voice cracked.


"It's..."


She couldn't finish.


Couldn't say it.


Because saying it out loud made it real.


Andrew immediately understood.


Of course he did.


Everything she felt was written all over his face too.


"Jane."


Another.


"You don't have to say it."


His own voice trembled.


"I know."


Another.


"Everything you're feeling..."


Another.


"I feel it too."


Jane squeezed her eyes shut.


For one impossible moment she wished this was all a dream.


A terrible dream.


A cruel dream.


One she would wake up from at any second.


But she didn't wake up.


Because she already was.


Finally she whispered:


"I have to see him."


Andrew nodded.


Immediately.


Because there had never been another option.


Then Jane froze.


The apartment.


Adam.


The look on his face.


Suddenly everything made sense.


"Oh God."


She covered her mouth.


"Adam."


Andrew looked at her.


"Jane..."


"This is why."


The realization hurt more than she expected.


"This is why he looked like that."


Another.


"He didn't give up."


Another.


"He looked defeated."


Her voice cracked completely.


"He already knew."


Neither spoke.


Because there was nothing to say.


Jane pulled herself together.


Or at least tried to.


Finally:


"I need to go home."


Another.


"I need to see my daughter."


Another.


"I need to see Adam."


Another.


"And then you're taking me to the hospital."


She swallowed hard.


"I need to see him for myself."


Andrew nodded.


"Okay."


A long silence followed.


Then Jane looked at him.


Really looked at him.


At the exhaustion.


At the grief.


At the fear.


At the boy she'd loved almost her entire life.


And suddenly she realized something.


She'd been thinking about Adam.


About Kirby.


About Lizzy.


About herself.


About everyone.


Except Andrew.


And this was happening to him too.


Her hand reached up.


Brushing through his hair.


The way she had done countless times over the years.


Her eyes filled again.


"Andrew."


Another.


"I'm sorry."


He immediately pulled her into another hug.


"Jane..."


His voice disappeared into her hair.


And for a moment neither moved.


Finally she whispered:


"I'm here."


Another.


"I am."


Another.


"Whatever you need, I'm here."


His arms tightened.


"I'm not going anywhere."


Another.


"I'm not leaving you."


Tears filled her eyes again.


"I promise."


Another.


"I'm sorry I forgot."


For the first time since she arrived...


Andrew smiled.


A small broken smile.


After a few minutes he pulled away.


Took a deep breath.


Doctor mode.


The only way forward.


"Okay."


Another.


"Here's what we're going to do."


Jane nodded.


"You go downstairs."


Another.


"I'm calling Kelly."


Another.


"Chris."


Another.


"Maria."


Another.


"If you're okay with it, Maria can stay with Lizzy until your parents get there."


Jane nodded immediately.


"Then you."


Another.


"Adam."


Another.


"Kelly."


Another.


"And I."


Another.


"We go to the hospital."


Jane took a shaky breath.


Andrew squeezed her hand.


"Go take care of your family."


Another.


"They need you."


Another.


"They are your number one priority."


Jane stared at him.


Then shook her head.


"Andrew."


Another.


"You're my family too."


Her voice broke.


"And so is he."


Neither needed to say Kirby's name.


It hung between them anyway.


The reality of what this meant was finally starting to settle in.


The implications.


The consequences.


The impossible weight of it.


And somewhere in the middle of all of it...


A terrible realization surfaced.


She was still married.


The thought hit her like a punch.


And for one brief moment she understood exactly what Adam had been carrying.


Why he'd looked so defeated.


Why he'd looked so hopeless.


Why he hadn't been able to answer her.


But that was a problem for later.


Right now there was only one thing that mattered.


Seeing Kirby.


Because some stubborn part of her still didn't believe it.


Couldn't believe it.


Wouldn't believe it.


Not until she saw him with her own eyes.
