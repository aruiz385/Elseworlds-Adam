# Chapter 50: The Waiting Room


The drive to the hospital was silent.


Not because nobody had questions.


Because nobody knew where to start.


Adam drove.


Jane sat beside him.


Andrew and Kelly sat in the back.


Nobody looked at anyone else.


Nobody trusted themselves to speak.


The hospital parking garage appeared far too soon.


And not nearly soon enough.


By the time they reached the ICU, Scott was waiting.


Nurse Hayle stood beside him.


The older nurse looked exhausted.


Like she had personally been carrying the weight of this secret since the moment she saw Kirby's face.


The second she saw Jane, her eyes filled.


"Oh sweetheart."


Jane nodded.


Unable to answer.


Unable to breathe.


Scott stepped forward.


"We'll take this slowly."


Jane appreciated the lie.


Nothing about this was going to be slow.


Nothing about this was going to be easy.


The moment they reached the room and Jane looked through the glass, her body stopped cooperating.


There he was.


Kirby.


Not a photograph.


Not a memory.


Not a grave.


Kirby.


Adam immediately caught her.


Andrew grabbed her other arm.


Because her knees had completely given out.


Kelly wasn't much better.


She made a sound Jane had never heard before.


A sound somewhere between a sob and disbelief.


Nobody moved.


Nobody spoke.


Eventually Scott broke the silence.


"Okay."


His voice was gentle.


"But we need to discuss some things."


Not as family.


Not as friends.


As doctors.


As people responsible for another human life.


Jane never took her eyes off Kirby.


"Legally."


Scott continued.


"Jane is the decision maker."


The hallway became very quiet.


Jane hated hearing it.


Because suddenly this wasn't abstract.


This wasn't grief.


This wasn't memory.


This was responsibility.


"Jane is Kirby's wife."


The words landed heavily.


She closed her eyes.


Not now.


Not yet.


Scott continued.


"The positive news is that the coma is medically induced."


Another.


"Which means there is a possibility we can wake him."


Nobody reacted.


Because every person standing there knew there was always a second half to that sentence.


"There are risks."


Jane nodded.


"What kind of risks?"


Andrew answered before Scott could.


Because he knew exactly how she needed to hear it.


Not as a physician.


As family.


"We don't know the extent of the neurological damage."


Another.


"We don't know why the previous facility kept him sedated this long."


Another.


"We don't know what we're walking into."


Jane folded her arms around herself.


"What happens if he wakes up?"


Scott answered.


"He could wake up and eventually recover."


Another.


"He could wake up and require years of rehabilitation."


Another.


"He could wake up with memory deficits."


Another.


"Cognitive changes."


Another.


"Personality changes."


Another.


"He could wake up and not know who any of us are."


Silence.


"And worst case?"


Scott hesitated.


"He could stroke."


Another.


"He could suffer catastrophic neurological injury."


Another.


"He could die."


The answer hung in the air.


Cold.


Honest.


Terrible.


Jane immediately leaned into Andrew.


Not because she couldn't stand.


Because she needed him beside her.


This was Kirby.


Not just hers.


Theirs.


The two people standing there had loved him longer than anyone else in the world.


Finally she looked up.


"What would Kirby want?"


Andrew laughed softly.


A broken laugh.


The first genuine thing anybody had said all evening.


"He'd take the risk."


Another.


"He always took the risk."


Jane immediately smiled through tears.


Because that was true.


Painfully true.


Kirby had never once chosen caution.


Not in love.


Not in life.


Not in anything.


The smile faded.


A moment later she turned fully toward Andrew.


Everybody kept looking at her.


Waiting.


Expecting.


As if she held all the answers.


She didn't.


And they needed to understand that.


"Everybody keeps saying I'm the decision maker."


Andrew nodded.


"You are."


Jane shook her head.


"No."


Her voice was firm.


"I'm not doing this without you."


Andrew stared at her.


"Jane..."


"No."


Tears filled her eyes again.


"He's your brother."


Another.


"He's my husband."


Another.


"He's our family."


The words hit both of them hard.


"I'm not making a decision about Kirby without you."


Andrew's eyes filled immediately.


For a moment he couldn't speak.


Then he pulled her into his arms.


Tightly.


Protectively.


The way he had since they were children.


He kissed her forehead.


And whispered:


"I know."


Another.


"I know."


And for a moment they simply stood there holding each other.


Two people who had spent years grieving the same person.


And now somehow had him back.


Or part of him.


Or the possibility of him.


Nobody knew which.


Afterward Scott checked on Andrew first.


Not Dr. Call.


Andrew.


"You okay?"


Andrew laughed.


A terrible sound.


"No."


Scott nodded.


"Good."


Andrew blinked.


"What?"


"I'd be worried if you were okay."


Even Andrew managed a weak smile.


Then Scott turned to Jane.


"How are you really doing?"


Jane stared at him.


"My dead husband is alive."


Another.


"I'm in love with somebody else."


Another.


"I have a three-month-old daughter."


Another.


"And apparently my life is insane."


Scott nodded.


"Sounds about right."


That earned a laugh.


Small.


Brief.


Necessary.


Then Scott became serious.


"When do you see Ryan next?"


Jane groaned.


Immediately.


"Oh God."


"Jane."


The warning came from Scott.


And then from Adam.


"Jane."


She looked up.


Both men were staring at her.


Exactly the same way.


And somehow that was deeply irritating.


"You two discussed this."


"No."


Scott answered.


"Unfortunately we independently had the same concern."


Jane rolled her eyes.


"Wonderful."


Adam stepped closer.


His voice softened.


"This is too much."


Another.


"For anyone."


Another.


"You need Ryan."


Another.


"I can't help you through all of this by myself."


The honesty hurt.


Because she knew he was right.


Scott nodded.


"None of us can."


Another.


"Promise me you'll call her."


Jane looked at both of them.


Then finally nodded.


"Okay."


Another.


"I promise."


Only then did they relax.


Which was infuriating.


Later Jane finally asked to go into Kirby's room.


Nobody stopped her.


The room was quiet.


Only monitors.


Only machines.


Only Kirby.


Jane approached slowly.


And suddenly she wasn't seeing a patient.


She was seeing a lifetime.


The boy she'd known since she was five.


The teenager who fell in love with her.


The boy who called her from New York constantly.


The friend who made sure she was okay no matter where she was.


The boy who showed her Christmas in Manhattan.


The one who insisted she couldn't understand Christmas until she'd seen New York in December.


The boy who made her watch The Princess Bride.


The boy who claimed It Happened One Night was cinematic perfection.


The boy who danced with her whenever Dirty Dancing appeared on television.


The boy who somehow turned songs into memories.


Storybook Love.


Christmas Magic.


Valentine.


And I've Had the Time of My Life.


Their karaoke song.


Their ridiculous karaoke song.


The song everybody hated.


The song they sang anyway.


The song that always made them laugh.


Jane reached for his hand.


Warm.


Real.


Not a memory.


Not a dream.


Real.


A sob escaped her.


"Hi."


Her voice immediately broke.


"We buried you."


Another.


"We really buried you."


Tears fell freely.


"I learned how to live without you."


Another.


"It took a long time."


Another.


"But I did."


The room became impossibly quiet.


"You missed so much."


Another.


"You missed everything."


A pause.


"You missed Lizzy."


Jane laughed through tears.


"You would've loved her."


Another.


"She's stubborn."


Another.


"Impossible."


Another.


"And perfect."


Her voice broke completely.


Outside the room Adam watched.


And for the first time since walking into the hospital he let himself feel what he'd been avoiding.


Fear.


Pure fear.


Not of Kirby.


Not of the decision.


Of losing Jane.


Because for three days he'd been preparing himself.


For grief.


For anger.


For confusion.


What he hadn't prepared himself for was the possibility that everything they'd built might disappear.


Jane eventually stepped back into the hallway.


Emotionally exhausted.


Immediately she searched for Adam.


Found him.


And walked straight to him.


No hesitation.


No questioning.


Straight to him.


"How's Lizzy?"


Adam blinked.


The question caught him completely off guard.


"What?"


"How's our daughter?"


The words were so normal.


So ordinary.


In the middle of the catastrophe.


Adam immediately pulled out his phone.


"Your mom sent pictures."


Jane looked.


Lizzy was asleep.


Completely unaware that the world had ended.


Again.


Jane laughed.


Actually laughed.


And suddenly she started crying.


At the same time.


Adam wrapped his arms around her immediately.


"Hey."


Another.


"Hey."


And suddenly everything she'd been holding together cracked.


"I'm scared."


The admission was tiny.


Barely audible.


Adam held her tighter.


"So am I."


Jane looked up.


Really looked at him.


For the first time all night.


And suddenly she understood.


What he had been carrying.


What he had thought this meant.


What he had been afraid to ask.


"Adam."


His eyes met hers.


"I'm still here."


The words broke him.


Completely.


Because God.


He needed to hear that.


Jane's hand came up to his face.


"I'm still here."


Another.


"No matter what happens."


Adam closed his eyes.


For one terrible second his composure disappeared.


And Jane saw it.


All of it.


The fear.


The heartbreak.


The love.


The uncertainty.


She pulled him toward her.


And this time neither stopped.


Neither hesitated.


Neither overthought.


The kiss was soft.


Brief.


Heartbroken.


And desperately needed.


When they finally pulled apart neither spoke.


Because there was nothing left to say.


For one moment.


One brief moment.


Neither was thinking about yesterday.


Or tomorrow.


Or Kirby.


Or guilt.


Or fear.


Only each other.


Across the hall Kelly finally stepped into Kirby's room.


And came face-to-face with the first man she ever loved.


The ghost she'd spent years grieving.


The man she'd never completely forgotten.


And the future suddenly became even more complicated.
