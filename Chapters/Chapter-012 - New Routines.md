# Chapter 12: New Routines

The weeks that followed settled into something neither Jane nor Adam had planned.

A routine.

Not the routine she had built with the original Adam.

A different one.

Yet somehow familiar all the same.


Adam made it a point to check on her.

Constantly.

Far more often than anyone probably should.


There were texts.

Simple ones.

Did you eat today?

How are the headaches?

How's the medication treating you?


Then there were phone calls.

Usually during his walks home.

Occasionally during her lunch breaks.

Sometimes when neither one of them had anything particularly important to say.


Most people would have found the attention excessive.

Jane secretly found it comforting.


Every time she came to the hospital for therapy appointments, he somehow appeared.

Sometimes outside Dr. Ryan's office.

Sometimes walking through Oncology.

Sometimes seemingly materializing out of nowhere.


"You drinking enough water?"


Jane sighed.


"Hello to you too."


"I'm serious."


"I'm fine."


"That's not an answer."


She rolled her eyes.


Every time.


The eye roll always made him smile.


"Take ibuprofen for the headaches."


"I know."


"And eat something."


"Adam."


"And water."


"Adam."


"Hydration is important."


Jane laughed despite herself.


The thing was...

She liked it.


She liked the concern.

She liked the lectures.

She liked how seriously he took every complaint she mentioned.


Watching him worry over things as simple as headaches was unexpectedly adorable.


That realization bothered her.

A lot.


Because lately she'd caught herself watching him.


Not intentionally.

Never intentionally.


But often.


The way he concentrated when reading charts.

The way he interacted with patients.

The way he could be annoyingly sarcastic one moment and intensely compassionate the next.


The more time she spent with him, the more complicated things became.


For his part, Adam worried about her far more than he should have.


He knew it.


Scott knew it.


And if they were being honest, Jane probably knew it too.


Still, he couldn't help himself.


When she started medication, he worried.

When she looked exhausted, he worried.

When she didn't answer his texts right away, he worried.


The concern had stopped being rational weeks ago.


And neither one of them was talking about it.


Fortunately, Dr. Ryan gave Jane something productive to focus on.


Music therapy.

Movie therapy.


The concept sounded ridiculous when Dr. Ryan first explained it.


"You want me to intentionally listen to songs that make me cry?"


"Essentially."


Jane stared.


"That sounds terrible."


"It will be terrible."


Dr. Ryan smiled.


"At first."


Eventually she was instructed to create a list.

Songs.

Movies.

Anything associated with panic, grief, or painful memories.


The idea wasn't to avoid them.

The idea was to reclaim them.


If she worked through them enough, Dr. Ryan explained, eventually the memories wouldn't destroy her every time she encountered them.


They could become happy memories again.

Or at least manageable ones.


So Jane started making her list.


And because Adam inserted himself into everything these days, he helped.


One afternoon they sat outside the hospital cafeteria during his lunch break.

Jane had a notebook open.

Adam had stolen half her fries.


"Really?"


He looked horrified.


Jane glanced up.


"What?"


"The song from Dirty Dancing?"


He pointed accusingly at her notebook.


"Your taste in music is questionable."


Jane laughed.


"Excuse me?"


"Questionable."


"It was Kirby's favorite."


His teasing stopped immediately.


Jane noticed.


"We used to sing it at karaoke."


She smiled softly.


"It was our song."


The strange thing was she said Kirby's name without breaking.

Without panicking.

Without crying.


That alone felt like progress.


Adam studied her for a moment.

Then looked back at the list.


"And what is up with all these Beach Boys songs?"


Jane groaned immediately.


"Leave the Beach Boys alone."


"Never."


He shook his head.


"How about something with soul?"


She narrowed her eyes.


"Oh no."


"The Temptations."


"No."


"Motown."


"No."


"Literally anything that isn't the Beach Boys."


Jane laughed.


"What's wrong with the Beach Boys?"


"Everything."


"They are California royalty."


"They are overrated."


"You are objectively wrong."


Adam looked deeply offended.


"I have so many memories tied to these songs."


A pause.


"My favorite is *Wouldn't It Be Nice*."


His eyebrow rose.


"That's because Andrew's mother made it my official song."


"Uh-huh."


She stared.


"What?"


"Andrew."


The way he said the name made her laugh.


"Oh my God."


"Andrew."


"Kirby, Andrew and I grew up together."


She threw her hands up.


"What did you expect?"


"I see."


His expression suggested he was storing information away.


Not for clinical purposes.

For teasing purposes.


Jane knew the difference now.


Wanting to change the subject before he started making observations, she pointed at him.


"So what is your favorite song?"


Adam smiled immediately.


"I like The Temptations."


Of course he did.


"*My Girl.*"


Reasonable.


"*Ain't Too Proud to Beg.*"


Expected.


Then:


"Classic kitchen dancing music."


Jane blinked.


"Kitchen dancing music?"


He looked scandalized.


"Yes."


"As in..."


"Dancing in the kitchen."


Jane stared.


"Who does that?"


Adam put a hand over his heart.


"Human beings."


"No."


"Absolutely."


She shook her head.


"That is not a real thing."


"It absolutely is."


His expression was entirely serious.


"You don't know what you're missing."


"When have you ever danced in a kitchen?"


He paused.


"Well..."


Jane immediately laughed.


"Exactly."


"I haven't."


"Thank you."


"But I would."


That answer made her laugh harder.


Adam reached over and squeezed her hand.


"I absolutely would."


The gesture felt surprisingly natural.


Neither moved away.


"You know what my favorite song actually is?"


Jane looked up from her notebook.


"No."


"*Night Shift.*"


"The Commodores?"


Adam pointed at her.


"See?"


"I know things."


"Apparently."


He pulled out his phone.


"*Night Shift* got me through med school."


The song started playing softly through the phone speakers.


"Late nights."


He smiled.


"Studying."


Another smile.


"This hospital."


Then he stood.


"Oh no."


Adam ignored her.


"Come on."


"What are you doing?"


"It's time."


"For what?"


He held out a hand.


"Time to listen to some good music."


Jane laughed.


"You are impossible."


"Correct."


Still smiling, she let him pull her to her feet.


The song continued playing.

The bay shimmered in the distance.

And for a few seconds, everything felt strangely normal.


That had become the pattern over the last few months.


Lunches.

Conversations.

Arguments about music.

Texts.

Phone calls.

Hospital hallways.


Getting to know each other.


Jane had never meant for it to happen.


She certainly hadn't planned for it.


Yet somewhere along the way she had begun genuinely looking forward to seeing him.


That realization made her happy.


It also filled her with anxiety.


Because every day she liked him a little more.


And every day she became a little more afraid of what that meant.


She hadn't wanted this.


Hadn't planned for this.


But sitting there laughing with him beside the bay, Jane found herself facing a truth she wasn't ready to admit.


How could she not like him?


That was the problem.


How could she not?
