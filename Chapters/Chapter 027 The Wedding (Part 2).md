# Chapter 27: The Wedding (Part 2)

The reception somehow became even more beautiful as the evening went on.


The sun disappeared.


The lights around the reception tent glowed softly.


Music drifted across the lake.


The dance floor stayed full.


Karen refused to sit down.


James looked completely overwhelmed.


Chris had somehow convinced three strangers to participate in a dance contest nobody else knew was happening.


And everyone was happy.


Really happy.


Jane spent most of the night being pulled in every direction imaginable.


By Karen.


By family.


By friends.


By people she'd never seen before who somehow knew exactly who she was.


At one point she found herself laughing as James twirled Karen around the dance floor.


Fifteen years.


Fifteen years and they'd finally done it.


The thought made her smile.


And ache.


Both at the same time.


The bouquet toss was a disaster.


Mostly because Karen treated it like a professional sporting event.


"If any of you let that bouquet hit the ground, our friendship is over."


"You are married now."

Jane reminded her.


"You can't threaten people anymore."


"I absolutely can."


Then Karen launched the bouquet.


Chaos followed.


And somehow...


Despite her best efforts...


Jane caught it.


The room exploded.


"Oh no."

Jane groaned.


"Oh yes."

Karen screamed.


Across the room Adam doubled over laughing.


"There is no chance she misses the symbolism."

Chris announced.


Jane pointed directly at him.


"I hate all of you."


"No you don't."

Maria replied.


A few minutes later came the garter toss.


Which somehow ended far worse.


Because Andrew caught it.


The room lost its collective mind.


Chris nearly fell out of his chair.


"No."


"YES."

Karen shouted.


Andrew covered his face.


"I'm leaving."


"You can't leave."

James informed him.


"I'm the Best Man."


"Exactly."


The laughter somehow got louder.


For a brief moment Jane and Andrew looked at each other across the room.


Then immediately looked away.


Because suddenly the joke wasn't funny anymore.


Not completely.


Not to them.


The room kept laughing.


The music kept playing.


But for a second both of them found themselves somewhere else.


Back in high school.


Back before life got complicated.


Back before Kirby.


Back before grief.


Back before everything.


Back when there had only been two couples.


Karen and James.


Andrew and Jane.


And for a second both of them thought the same thing.


Once upon a time they thought this would be them.


Later, Adam found Jane standing near the edge of the patio overlooking the lake.


Away from the crowd.


Away from the noise.


He immediately walked over.


"You okay?"


Jane smiled.


"Yeah."


Adam waited.


Because he knew her.


Eventually she sighed.


"It's just..."


A pause.


"Complicated."


Adam nodded.


"Tell me."


Jane looked toward the dance floor.


Toward Karen and James.


"Back then..."


Another pause.


"It was always us."


Adam listened quietly.


"Karen."


"James."


"Andrew."


"And me."


A smile appeared.


"We did everything together."


Another.


"We were always together."


Another.


"And I think..."


She laughed softly.


"...some stupid part of me thought one day we'd be doing this too."


Adam's heart hurt.


Not because she was wrong.


Because she wasn't.


Jane shook her head.


"And in case you forgot..."


A pause.


"Andrew and I were literally Class Couple."


Adam laughed.


"I remember."


"You remember?"


"Very difficult to forget."


That earned a smile.


Then Adam became serious.


"It's okay."


Jane looked at him.


"It is."


A pause.


"It's normal."


Another.


"Your two best friends are getting married."


Another.


"You're allowed to think about what could've been."


Jane looked down at her hands.


The ring sparkled.


Adam noticed.


And his expression softened.


"I heard your speech."


Jane looked up.


"You did?"


"I really heard it."


A pause.


"The part about steady love."


Another.


"The part about always choosing someone."


Another.


"The part about certainty."


His voice grew quieter.


"It isn't fair."


Jane frowned.


"What isn't?"


"You deserve stability."


The words came out heavier than he intended.


"You deserve certainty."


Another.


"You deserve forever."


Jane's throat tightened.


"And I hate that I can't give you that."


She immediately reached for him.


"Adam."


"I do."


Another.


"It kills me."


Jane wrapped her arms around him.


"No."


He looked at her.


"No what?"


"You need to stop doing that."


"What?"


"Apologizing for existing."


The words hit harder than she intended.


Jane took a breath.


"I just need you."


A pause.


"I'm happy."


Another.


"I love you."


Adam closed his eyes briefly.


Then quietly:


"Do you think..."


Jane looked up.


"What?"


Adam hesitated.


For a moment he almost stopped himself.


Then:


"If you hadn't met me..."


A pause.


"Maybe you and Andrew would've found your way back to each other."


The words hurt to say.


Another.


"It's obvious you two love each other."


Jane immediately shook her head.


"No."


Adam looked surprised.


"No."

She repeated.


"Don't ever say that again."


The certainty in her voice stunned him.


"Jane..."


"I can't imagine a world where I don't love you."


The honesty hit him like a freight train.


A tear immediately gathered in her eye.


"I am preparing to live in that world."


Another.


"Because I have to."


Another.


"But I wouldn't trade the last two and a half years for anything."


Another.


"Not one day."


Another.


"Not one moment."


Adam nodded.


"Thank you."


He meant it.


Deeply.


Yet despite her words...


His mind still drifted.


To the way Andrew had looked at Jane that morning.


To the walk down the aisle.


To the Best Man speech.


To the things left unsaid.


For a brief moment guilt crept in.


Then he pushed it away.


Because tonight wasn't about guilt.


Tonight was about happiness.


Adam smiled.


"C'mon."


"What?"


"We're dancing."


Jane laughed.


"I thought we already were."


"No."


Adam grabbed her hand.


"We're dancing dancing."


And somehow he meant it.


For the next two hours they barely left the dance floor.


Every slow song.


Every romantic song.


Every song.


Eventually Adam kissed her shoulder.


"Ready?"


Jane smiled.


"Yeah."


Hand in hand they walked back toward the inn.


Toward their room.


Toward the fireplace glowing softly in the darkness.


And toward the rest of the night that belonged only to them.


The music slowed again.


Neither of them had left the dance floor in what felt like forever.


Adam's arms rested comfortably around her waist.


Jane had her head against his shoulder.


For a little while they simply swayed together.


Then Adam kissed her shoulder.


Jane immediately smiled.


Then Adam kissed her shoulder.


Jane immediately smiled.


"What?"


"You know you're glowing."


Jane rolled her eyes.


"There it is."


"What?"


"You've become one of those people."


Adam laughed.


"One of what people?"


"The people who use the word glowing."


"You are glowing."


Another kiss against her shoulder.


"It's honestly a little distracting."


Jane shook her head.


"You are ridiculous."


Adam smiled.


"Did I tell you that you look beautiful today?"


Jane laughed.


"Only twelve times."


"Huh."


"What?"


"I've clearly been slacking."


Another kiss.


"You look beautiful."


Jane immediately felt herself blush.


"Adam."


"You do."


Another kiss.


"You really, really do."


"You are making this weird."


The smile on her face completely betrayed her.


Because she loved every second of it.


Adam leaned closer.


"No."


A pause.


"You know what's weird?"


Jane narrowed her eyes.


"What?"


"You agreed to marry me."


Jane groaned.


"There it is."


"There it is."

Adam agreed triumphantly.


"We've arrived."


"Adam."


"My fiancée."


Jane covered her face.


"We are in public."


Adam looked around.


"Huh."


A pause.


"So we are."


Jane laughed despite herself.


"You are enjoying this far too much."


"I really am."


Another pause.


"You should hear what I've been calling you in my head all day."


"I'm afraid to ask."


"You should be."


Jane pointed a warning finger at him.


"Adam."


"Future Mrs. Carter."


Jane immediately burst out laughing.


"You don't even know your own joke."


"I got excited."


"Clearly."


The song continued.


Adam pulled her slightly closer.


"And for the record..."


Jane groaned.


"There's more?"


"Oh, so much more."


"No."


"Oh yes."


He lowered his voice.


"You know that once this wedding is over..."


Jane immediately narrowed her eyes.


"No."


"...we're going back to our room."


"Adam."


"And I am going to do ungodly things to you."


Jane's face instantly turned bright red.


"ADAM."


"What?"


"We are at a wedding."


"I know."


"There are people."


"There are lots of people."


Jane buried her face against his shoulder.


"I cannot take you anywhere."


"That's not true."


A pause.


"You take me places all the time."


"That wasn't an invitation."


Adam smiled.


"Noted."


A beat.


"Disagreed with."


Jane groaned louder.


The laughter in his chest vibrated against her cheek.


The stupid man was entirely too pleased with himself.


Eventually she looked up at him.

Still smiling.

Still blushing.

Still hopelessly in love.


"You are impossible."


"I've heard that."


"I love you so much."


The words slipped out naturally.


Effortlessly.


Adam's smile softened.


"Good."


"Good?"


"Yeah."


A pause.


"Because I am absolutely crazy about you."


Jane laughed.


"Crazy about me?"


"Completely."


"How crazy?"


Adam pretended to think.


"I proposed to a woman who told me she was taking the ring off."


Jane immediately laughed.


"Fair."


"I'd say that's medically concerning."


The two of them laughed.


Then Jane looked around the room.


At Karen dancing with James.

At their friends.

At the lights.

At the life they had somehow built together.


Then she looked back at him.


And softer now:


"You know I'm never going to forget this, right?"


Adam's expression changed.


Because he knew she wasn't talking about the wedding.


Or the dancing.


Or even the ring.


She meant all of it.


Every moment.


Every memory.


Everything they had built together.


Adam smiled.


"I know."


A pause.


"I know, Jane."


Just then the photographer appeared beside them.


"Don't move."


Both looked over.


"What?" Jane laughed.


"Stay exactly like that."


The photographer lifted the camera.


Jane turned back toward Adam.


Still smiling.


Still wrapped in his arms.


And the flash went off.


Capturing the moment forever.


Years later, that photograph would sit on Jane's nightstand in the master bedroom she rebuilt for herself.


A picture of a lakeside wedding.

A slow dance.

A smile.

A ring.

And the man who taught her not to be afraid of wanting a future.


At one point Adam disappeared.


Then returned wearing the satisfied smile that always worried her.


"What did you do?"


"Nothing."


"Adam."


"A DJ may or may not have received a special request."


Jane immediately groaned.


"Oh no."


The opening notes started.


Her eyes widened.


"No."


Adam grinned.


"Oh yes."


"Nothing's Gonna Stop Us Now."


Jane burst out laughing.


"You did not."


"I absolutely did."


Then he dragged her onto the dance floor.


And made her sing every word with him.


Which was deeply embarrassing.


And also one of her favorite moments of the night.


Later, another familiar song began.


The Beach Boys.


"God Only Knows."


Jane froze.


Then looked up.


Andrew stood beside her.


Holding out a hand.


Neither said a word.


For a moment.


Then:


"Dance?"


Jane smiled.


"Of course."


Adam nodded.


Already understanding.


The dance was easy.


Comfortable.


Familiar.


Thirty years of friendship moving to music.


Halfway through, Jane realized she was crying.


Andrew immediately noticed.


"Pregnancy hormones?"


Jane laughed weakly.


"Pregnancy hormones."


Neither of them believed it.


The song ended.


And before she could step away, Andrew pulled her into a hug.


A real hug.


One that carried decades inside it.


"Hey."


Jane nodded.


"I know."


Andrew smiled.


"I love you."


A pause.


"Always."


Jane's eyes filled.


"Me too."


Then she smiled.


"Always."


When she stepped away, Andrew let her go.


And watched her walk back toward Adam.


Back toward the life she had built.


Kelly appeared beside him.


Saying nothing.


Just slipping her hand into his.


And somehow that understanding meant everything.


Much later, long after the reception had begun winding down, Jane and Adam slipped away.


The lake waited quietly.


Stars reflected across the water.


The world felt still.


Adam draped his jacket over her shoulders.


Then wrapped his arms around her from behind.


Jane leaned back against him.


Neither spoke.


They simply stood there looking out across the water.


The wedding.


The music.


The memories.


All of it settling around them.
