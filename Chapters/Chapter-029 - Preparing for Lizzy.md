# Chapter 29: Preparing for Lizzy

The day after they decided on her name, Adam couldn't stop smiling.


It was becoming a problem.


According to Jane, anyway.


"You're doing it again."


"What?"


"That smile."


Adam looked genuinely confused.


"What smile?"


"The one that's making me suspicious."


Adam laughed.


"I can't help it."


Jane rolled her eyes.


"Why?"


He looked down at her stomach.


Then smiled again.


"Because we're having a daughter."


There it was.


That smile.


The stupidly happy one.


Jane couldn't help smiling too.


"Lizzy."

Adam said softly.


"Lizzy."


Another smile.


Jane laughed.


"You are never getting over this, are you?"


"Nope."


A pause.


"Not even a little."


Adam's hand settled over her stomach.


For a moment he was quiet.


Then:


"You know what I hope?"


Jane looked up.


"What?"


His expression softened.


"I hope she's brave."


Another.


"I hope she's stubborn."


Jane laughed.


"Oh God."


"What?"


"Of all the things."


"It is an important quality."


"It really isn't."


"It is if she's anything like her mother."


Jane rolled her eyes.


Adam continued.


"I hope she's kind."


Another.


"I hope she laughs a lot."


Another.


"I hope she knows how loved she is."


His voice softened.


"And when things get hard..."


A pause.


"I hope she remembers that she comes from incredibly strong women."


Jane felt her eyes sting.


"Adam."


"What?"


"You're making me cry."


A grin appeared.


"I thought that was my full-time job."


That earned a laugh.


The next day they headed back to Jane's apartment.


And immediately resumed arguing about the nursery.


Which was becoming one of their favorite hobbies.


"The walls stay yellow."


Jane looked horrified.


"The walls are yellow."


"They are pastel yellow."


"They are aggressively yellow."


Adam pointed.


"They are cheerful."


"They are loud."


"They're staying."


Jane sighed dramatically.


"Fine."


Adam immediately looked victorious.


Then pointed toward the wall above the crib.


"And I'm adding Winnie the Pooh."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"Winnie the Pooh."


Another.


"And Piglet."


Another.


"Walking hand in hand."


Jane smiled.


The space he pointed to sat directly beneath the quote they had chosen together.


If ever there is tomorrow when we're not together, there is something you must always remember. You are braver than you believe, stronger than you seem, and smarter than you think.


The quote that had nearly broken both of them.


The quote Adam had chosen for Lizzy.


Jane looked around the room.


Then back at him.


"You know what?"


"What?"


"This room is your project."


Adam laughed.


"My project?"


"Your project."


A pause.


"You get final say."


His smile faded slightly.


Not from sadness.


From emotion.


He looked around the room.


The nursery.


His daughter's nursery.


Then quietly:


"This is going to be my gift to Lizzy."


Jane's chest tightened.


"That's exactly what it should be."


Then she pointed toward the dresser.


"I'm printing pictures."


Adam frowned.


"Pictures?"


"Lots of pictures."


Another.


"Pictures of us."


Another.


"Pictures of you."


Adam's smile faltered.


Jane immediately caught it.


Then winked.


"Just in case."


The words were playful.


The meaning wasn't.


Neither of them acknowledged it.


Not out loud.


Jane squeezed his hand.


Then disappeared into the living room.


Leaving Adam sitting alone in the nursery.


The silence settled quickly.


For a long time he simply stared.


At the walls.


The furniture.


The future.


And for the first time in a while, he stopped pretending.


Because he could feel it.


His other self.


Coming back.


The sensation had grown stronger every day.


The pull.


The pressure.


The feeling of something waking up.


Something reclaiming its place.


Original Adam was getting ready to return to his life.


And Adam wasn't ready to let go of his.


Not Jane.


Not Lizzy.


Not any of it.


A knot formed in his chest.


Because he knew.


Soon.


Much sooner than he had hoped.


A few nights later, lying beside Jane in bed at his apartment, Adam finally said it.


"I think he's coming back."


The room went silent.


Jane didn't look surprised.


Adam noticed immediately.


"You knew."


Jane sighed.


Then reached into the nightstand.


Pulling out a notebook.


Adam frowned.


"What is that?"


She handed it to him.


Sleep patterns.


Dates.


Notes.


Observations.


Pages and pages of them.


Adam stared.


Then looked up at her.


"Jane."


She smiled sadly.


"I've been tracking it."


"A week?"


Jane nodded.


"A little over a week."


Adam laughed softly in disbelief.


"Of course you have."


Jane shrugged.


"I wanted to understand."


Another.


"And I figured you'd tell me when you were ready."


The look on Adam's face immediately softened.


Because only Jane would do that.


Only Jane would spend hours researching.


Documenting.


Preparing herself quietly.


And still give him the space to speak when he was ready.


He pulled her into his arms.


Holding her tightly.


"I don't think I'll make it to Lizzy's birth."


The words broke.


Jane immediately wrapped herself around him.


"It's okay."


A tear escaped.


"No."


"It is."


Another pause.


"We're going to be okay."


Adam buried his face in her hair.


"I wish we had more time."


"I know."


"And I'll tell you when it's happening."


Jane nodded.


"I know."


A pause.


"You've been here before."


Another.


"And I trust you."


They held each other for a long time.


Neither wanting to say anything else.


The following weeks passed in a blur.


They announced they were having a girl.


And that her name would be Elizabeth Carter.


Lizzy.


Karen cried.


Obviously.


Chris immediately declared that Lizzy would be smarter than every adult she knew.


James agreed.


Maria agreed.


Kelly cried.


Andrew cried too.


Though he blamed allergies.


The truth was simpler.


Jane looked happy.


Happier than he'd seen her in years.


And that meant everything.


The nursery gradually came together.


Adam assembled every piece of furniture.


The changing table.


The dresser.


The shelves.


The rocker.


Especially the rocker.


A huge comfortable recliner that immediately became Jane's favorite piece of furniture.


Adam declared it mandatory equipment.


Meanwhile Andrew, James and Chris helped Jane finish the room she'd spent years avoiding.


The room she once shared with Kirby.


Now completely transformed.


The walls were different.


The furniture was different.


Everything was different.


Not because she was forgetting Kirby.


But because she'd finally learned she didn't need a shrine to remember him.


She carried him with her.


The room didn't have to.


When it was finally finished, Jane stood in the doorway smiling.


Then looked at Adam.


"What?"


"Want to try out our new room?"


The word hit him immediately.


Our.


Adam smiled.


Then frowned.


"Are you sure?"


Jane blinked.


"What?"


Adam looked around.


"You don't have to call it ours."


A pause.


"I don't want you sleeping on the couch again after I'm gone."


Another.


"If you want this place to stay yours..."


Jane crossed the room.


Immediately.


"Not a chance."


The certainty surprised him.


"I want you here."


Another.


"For as long as I can have you."


Another.


"And I've come too far to start running from rooms again."


Adam smiled.


Then kissed her.


Long.


Slow.


And later that night they spent their first night together in the room.


Their room.


A room that had once been defined by grief.


Now filled with hope.


Somewhere in the middle of all that came the baby shower.


Or rather:


Karen's baby shower.


Because nobody believed for a second it belonged to anyone else.


"It is a Jack-and-Jill shower."

Karen announced proudly.


"No."

Jane replied immediately.


"Yes."


"No."


"Yes."


The men were dragged into it.


Against varying levels of protest.


Adam loved every second.


Chris treated it like a competitive sport.


James looked overwhelmed.


Andrew quietly helped assemble gifts.


Kelly took pictures.


Maria encouraged Karen.


Which was always dangerous.


And Jane hated every minute of being the center of attention.


Naturally.


"It's not too late to leave."

Jane whispered.


Adam grinned.


"It is absolutely too late to leave."


"Karen will find me."


"Correct."


"She'll hunt me."


"Also correct."


Jane sighed.


"I'm having a terrible time."


Adam laughed.


Then pulled her closer.


His hand settling over her stomach.


His daughter.


Their daughter.


Their family.


And surrounded by absurd gifts, loud friends, and more love than either of them knew what to do with, Adam found himself wishing for only one thing.


More time.


Just a little more time.
