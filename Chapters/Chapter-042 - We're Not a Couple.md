# Chapter 42: We're Not a Couple


Neither of them wanted to think about the appointment.


Which was exactly why lunch felt oddly normal.


They sat outside a small deli a few blocks from Dr. Ryan's office.


Jane picked at a sandwich.


Adam drank coffee.


Both pretending there wasn't another therapy appointment looming over them.


"I'm starting to think Ryan planned this."


Adam looked up.


Jane narrowed her eyes.


"Starting?"


Another.


"I've known she planned this since she smiled."


Adam laughed.


"That's fair."


A pause.


"Do you think she already talked to this guy?"


"Absolutely."


Another.


"They probably had coffee together while plotting against us."


Adam smiled.


"You think she plots?"


Jane stared at him.


"You've met her."


Another.


"Of course she plots."


Twenty minutes later they were sitting in a waiting room trying not to look nervous.


Neither succeeded.


The door opened.


A man in his late fifties stepped into the waiting room.


Salt-and-pepper hair.


Glasses.


A warm smile.


The kind of smile therapists developed after years of watching people complicate their own lives.


"Jane."


Another.


"Adam."


Another.


"I'm Aaron Cohen."


He shook both their hands and led them into his office.


The room felt comfortable.


Books.


Large windows.


Two chairs.


A couch.


Nothing intimidating.


Which somehow made Jane trust him even less.


Dr. Cohen sat down.


Opened a folder.


Read for a moment.


Then looked up.


"How old is your daughter?"


"Three weeks."


His smile widened.


"Wonderful."


He made a note.


Jane immediately frowned.


The notes had begun.


"Tell me why you're here."


Jane looked at Adam.


Adam looked at Jane.


Neither wanted to answer first.


Finally Adam sighed.


"We just had a daughter."


Another.


"I'm going through integration therapy."


Another.


"And Dr. Ryan apparently thinks we need to be here."


Jane nodded.


"That's pretty much the summary."


Dr. Cohen nodded thoughtfully.


"And what are you hoping to get out of this?"


Jane laughed.


"I have absolutely no idea."


Another.


"An hour ago I didn't know you existed."


Adam pointed toward the hallway.


"We thought we were getting an update."


Another.


"Instead we got assigned another therapist."


Dr. Cohen smiled.


"That sounds like Dr. Ryan."


Jane pointed immediately.


"See."


Another.


"You know she's plotting."


Dr. Cohen laughed.


Then leaned forward.


"What do you think Dr. Ryan believes needs work?"


"The integration."


Adam answered immediately.


Jane nodded.


"Definitely the integration."


Another.


"We're trying to figure out how to navigate this."


Another.


"And failing with varying degrees of success."


He made another note.


Jane sighed dramatically.


"Again."


"What?"


"The notes."


Another.


"I know that look."


Another.


"You're writing something that's going to ruin my afternoon."


Adam laughed.


Dr. Cohen smiled.


"I promise I'm not trying to ruin your afternoon."


"I'm unconvinced."


Dr. Cohen looked at Adam.


"Tell me about the integration."


And so they did.


The accident.


The DID.


The loss of Adam's mother.


The loss of his twin brother.


The alters.


The therapy.


The memories.


The integration process.


The confusion.


Dr. Cohen listened carefully.


Eventually he looked at Adam.


"What has integration been like for you?"


Adam leaned back.


Tired.


"Exhausting."


Another.


"Some memories come back and they fit immediately."


Another.


"Like they've always belonged there."


Another.


"Because they did."


Another.


"And some days I feel like I'm meeting parts of myself for the first time."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"And what scares you about it?"


Adam was quiet.


Then:


"I'm not really afraid of the memories anymore."


Another.


"I'm afraid of what they mean."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"Meaning?"


Adam glanced at Jane.


Then back at him.


"Meaning every time I remember something, I wonder if it's going to make things harder for her."


The room grew quiet.


Dr. Cohen made another note.


Then turned toward Jane.


"And what has integration been like for you?"


Jane laughed.


Without humor.


"A nightmare."


"Expand."


She rolled her eyes.


Of course.


"He'll remember something."


Another.


"Or he'll do something familiar."


Another.


"Or say something that sounds exactly like..."


She stopped herself.


Dr. Cohen waited.


"Like who?"


Jane sighed.


"My Adam."


Silence.


"And every time it happens my brain basically short-circuits."


Another.


"Part of me recognizes it."


Another.


"And another part keeps saying that's impossible."


Dr. Cohen nodded slowly.


"What are you afraid is happening?"


Jane looked down.


A long silence passed.


Finally:


"I'm afraid he's right."


Adam froze.


Jane immediately regretted speaking.


Dr. Cohen simply nodded.


"Interesting."


Jane pointed at him.


"There."


Another.


"I hate when therapists say that."


Adam laughed.


Dr. Cohen smiled.


"Usually because you're being honest."


Jane sank lower in her chair.


"This was a mistake."


Dr. Cohen ignored her.


Then set his pen down.


"Help me understand something."


That tone.


Jane already hated that tone.


"I reviewed Dr. Ryan's notes."


Another.


"But I'd rather hear it from you."


Another.


"Tell me about the other Adam."


The room became still.


Adam looked at Jane.


Jane looked at the floor.


Finally Adam answered.


"He was dominant for about three years."


Another.


"He and Jane were together."


Another.


"They lived together."


Another.


"They got engaged."


Another.


"He's Lizzy's father."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"And he was the one who chose integration therapy?"


"Yes."


Another.


"It was his decision."


Dr. Cohen looked thoughtful.


"And initially the expectation was that he would be the one going through the process?"


"Yes."


Jane answered this time.


"At first."


Another.


"That's what everyone thought."


Another.


"That's what he thought."


Another.


"That's what I thought."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"And then?"


Jane swallowed.


"Then he started realizing something."


Another.


"He started feeling Original Adam come forward."


Another.


"Before the integration therapy had even really started working."


Another.


"And eventually..."


She stopped.


"Eventually?"


Jane looked down.


"We realized he wasn't going to be the one on the other side of this."


The room was silent.


Dr. Cohen nodded slowly.


"And that's when the clean-slate plan happened."


Jane blinked.


"Ryan told you about that?"


"I read the notes."


Jane groaned.


The notes were back.


Of course they were.


Dr. Cohen looked at her.


"Tell me about it."


Jane sighed.


"It was my idea."


Adam looked at her.


He knew that.


But hearing her say it still mattered.


"I knew Adam."


She pointed beside her.


"This Adam."


Another.


"Before any of this."


Another.


"Scott introduced us."


Another.


"He was his resident."


Another.


"We used to get coffee."


Another.


"He was my friend."


She stared at her hands.


"And I wasn't going to do that to him."


"Do what?"


Jane looked up.


"Saddle him with a life he didn't choose."


Another.


"He didn't choose me."


Another.


"He didn't choose an engagement."


Another.


"He didn't choose a daughter."


Another.


"He didn't choose the life Adam and I built."


Another.


"And I wasn't going to hand all of that to him and tell him it was his responsibility now."


The room went quiet.


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"So the clean-slate plan wasn't rejection."


"No."


Immediate.


Firm.


"No."


Another.


"It was the opposite."


Another.


"I cared about him."


Another.


"That's why I did it."


For a moment nobody spoke.


Then Dr. Cohen turned to Adam.


"Did it feel that way to you?"


Adam thought about it.


"At first?"


Another.


"No."


Jane winced.


Adam quickly continued.


"But I understand it now."


Another.


"I understand exactly why she did it."


Another.


"If our positions had been reversed, I probably would've done the same thing."


Jane visibly relaxed.


Then Dr. Cohen leaned forward.


"Okay."


Another.


"Then help me understand something."


Jane immediately groaned.


There was the tone again.


"The clean-slate plan existed to protect Adam from responsibilities he didn't choose."


Another.


"Correct?"


"Yes."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


Then looked at Jane.


"Has he behaved like someone who feels trapped?"


Silence.


Jane didn't answer.


"Has he behaved like someone looking for an exit?"


More silence.


"Has he behaved like someone carrying obligations he doesn't want?"


Jane stared at the floor.


Dr. Cohen softened.


"I think the clean-slate plan made sense."


Another.


"I think it was thoughtful."


Another.


"I think it was compassionate."


Jane looked up.


Surprised.


"But I also think it was created before Adam had the chance to make his own decisions."


Another.


"And since then..."


He looked at Adam.


"He's been making them."


Complete silence.


Because that was true.


Adam had stayed.


Every single time.


Then Dr. Cohen looked at both of them.


"Now help me understand something else."


Jane narrowed her eyes.


Danger.


"You've described yourselves as living together."


Another.


"Raising a child together."


Another.


"Making decisions together."


Another.


"Sleeping in the same bedroom."


Another.


"Supporting each other emotionally."


Another.


"Correct?"


"Yes."


Both answered.


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"So help me understand why neither of you thinks you're a couple."


The response was immediate.


"We're not."


Perfect synchronization.


Again.


Dr. Cohen sighed.


"That is a conclusion."


Another.


"I'm asking for the reasoning."


Jane crossed her arms.


"We aren't dating."


"Okay."


Another note.


Jane pointed.


"There."


Another.


"That note was annoying."


Dr. Cohen ignored her.


"Do either of you want to date someone else?"


"No."


Again.


Together.


Jane groaned.


Adam rubbed his face.


Dr. Cohen looked delighted.


"What?"


Jane demanded.


"I just find agreement interesting."


"That's therapist code for something."


"Correct."


Adam laughed.


"You're enjoying this."


Jane accused him.


"A little."


He admitted.


Traitor.


Dr. Cohen leaned forward.


"If I spent a day watching your lives..."


Another.


"At what point would I realize you weren't a couple?"


Silence.


Nobody answered.


Because neither knew.


Dr. Cohen continued.


"What exactly distinguishes this relationship from a romantic partnership?"


Jane answered immediately.


"Because he's not my Adam."


The room became still.


Dr. Cohen nodded slowly.


"There it is."


Jane immediately regretted speaking.


Then Dr. Cohen looked at Adam.


"What's the worst thing that happens if integration succeeds?"


The answer came quickly.


"She decides she doesn't want me."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


Then looked at Jane.


"What's the worst thing that happens if integration succeeds?"


Jane answered just as quickly.


"I betray him."


Silence.


Dr. Cohen looked between them.


Then set down his pen.


"Do you realize how fascinating this is?"


Jane groaned.


"No."


Another.


"And judging by your tone, I'm going to hate whatever comes next."


Dr. Cohen smiled.


"Adam is terrified she'll leave."


Another.


"Jane is terrified she won't."


Complete silence.


The worst part?


He wasn't wrong.


Not even a little.


Then he turned back toward Jane.


"Has anyone actually told you that loving him would be a betrayal?"


"No."


"Has Adam?"


"No."


"Has Dr. Ryan?"


"No."


"Then whose rule are you following?"


A very long silence followed.


Because for the first time...


Jane didn't have an answer.


Finally:


"I don't know."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"That's important."


Jane frowned.


"Why?"


"Because if nobody gave you the rule..."


Another.


"You should probably figure out why you've decided to follow it."


The session ended shortly afterward.


As they stepped into the elevator, Jane finally exhaled.


"I hate him."


Adam laughed.


"No, you don't."


"I absolutely do."


Another.


"He asks entirely too many good questions."


Adam smiled.


"That's generally considered his job."


Jane glared.


The elevator doors closed.


Quiet settled over them.


Finally Adam spoke.


"What do you think he meant?"


"Which part?"


"'What exactly distinguishes this relationship from a romantic partnership?'"


Jane stared at the floor.


Thinking.


Really thinking.


Because for the first time...


She wasn't entirely sure they had been sent there because of integration therapy.


Maybe that was only part of it.


Maybe Dr. Ryan had seen something they couldn't.


Or wouldn't.


And somehow that possibility frightened her more than anything Dr. Cohen had said all afternoon.


"I don't know."


But for the first time...


She thought maybe she wanted to find out.
