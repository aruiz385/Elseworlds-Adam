# Chapter 34: Two Weeks

Two weeks passed.


Not enough time to fix everything.


But enough time for something to change.


Adam had become a quiet presence in Jane's life.


Not intrusive.


Not demanding.


Just...


There.


He helped carry things.


Installed shelves.


Moved boxes.


Picked up things she forgot from the pharmacy.


And somehow managed to make himself useful without expecting anything in return.


Exactly the way he'd been when she'd first met him.


The Original Adam.


The friend.


Jane and Andrew were sitting in the hospital cafeteria when Andrew finally decided to bring it up.


"So."


Jane groaned immediately.


"What?"


Andrew smirked.


"He's been around a lot."


Jane looked up.


"Who?"


Andrew stared.


"Seriously?"


"Oh."


A pause.


"Adam."


Andrew pointed at her.


"Adam."


Jane stabbed a french fry.


"He's helping."


Andrew smiled.


"I've noticed."


Another fry disappeared.


"He's actually really useful."


Andrew laughed.


"High praise."


Jane shook her head.


"I realized something."


Andrew immediately looked interested.


"This should be good."


"When I knew Original Adam."


Another.


"The first time."


Another.


"We never had a meal together."


Andrew frowned.


"What?"


"Coffee."


Another.


"Coffee."


Another.


"More coffee."


Another.


"Four months."


Another.


"Nothing but coffee."


Andrew laughed.


"That sounds exactly right."


Jane pointed her fry at him.


"Do you know what I learned?"


"What?"


"He likes pizza."


Andrew blinked.


"What?"


"He loves pizza."


Another.


"Apparently he survived med school on pizza, caffeine, and Diet Coke."


Andrew immediately started laughing.


Hard.


Jane threw up her hands.


"Do you understand the betrayal?"


"No."


He was already wiping tears from his eyes.


"I genuinely don't."


"For three years..."


Jane pointed dramatically toward the ceiling.


"Three years!"


Another.


"My Adam spent his time torturing me."


"What?"


"Vegetables."


Another.


"Vegetables, Andrew."


Another.


"Everywhere."


Another.


"Kale."


Another.


"Spinach."


Another.


"Salads."


Another.


"Things nobody should voluntarily consume."


Andrew doubled over laughing.


"And all this time..."


Jane pointed accusingly at the universe.


"The Original Adam was apparently a pizza enthusiast."


"Jane."


Andrew shook his head.


"This is the funniest thing you've said all year."


"It is not funny."


"It absolutely is."


She rolled her eyes.


Then unexpectedly smiled.


"You know what's weird?"


"What?"


"It feels like I'm getting to know him again."


Andrew's smile softened.


Because that...


That was probably the most honest thing she'd said all lunch.


Then a dangerous look appeared in his eyes.


Jane saw it immediately.


"No."


"I didn't say anything."


"You were about to."


Andrew leaned back.


"I'm just saying."


A pause.


"Be careful."


"Andrew."


"You already fell for one version."


The french fry hit him directly in the forehead.


"Ow!"


"Good."


He rubbed his forehead.


"I wasn't serious."


"Yes you were."


"I was a little serious."


Another french fry.


"Ow!"


Jane pointed at herself.


"I am eight months pregnant."


Another.


"I am exhausted."


Another.


"I don't remember what normal feels like."


Another.


"My daughter thinks my organs are recreational equipment."


Another.


"And who exactly in this universe is supposed to find this attractive?"


She pointed at her stomach.


Andrew stared at her.


Then shook his head.


"Half the hospital flirts with you."


"Andrew."


"I'm serious."


"I know."


Another.


"And you're wrong."


Jane rolled her eyes.


"Whatever."


Andrew's expression softened.


"Are you eating?"


Jane groaned.


"Oh no."


"Answer the question."


"Yes."


"Sleeping?"


"No."


"Appointments?"


"Yes."


"Medication?"


"Yes."


"Water?"


Jane glared.


"Andrew."


"That wasn't a yes."


"Mostly."


"Jane."


"Fine."


Another eye roll.


"Yes."


Andrew nodded.


Satisfied.


Then reached across the table and squeezed her hand.


"You okay?"


Jane considered lying.


Then shrugged.


"Trying."


Andrew nodded.


Because that answer was honest.


After a moment he sighed.


"Sorry about the crib."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"I said I'd help."


Another.


"Then work exploded."


Another.


"And the precinct exploded."


Another.


"And suddenly the crib was already built."


Jane smiled.


"Adam helped."


Andrew's grin immediately returned.


Then he winked.


Jane groaned.


"Oh my God."


"I'm just saying."


Another.


"I'm glad he was around."


Another french fry flew across the table.


Later their conversation shifted.


"Kelly gets back tonight."


Jane immediately smiled.


"Really?"


Andrew nodded.


"Seminar's finally over."


Another.


"I missed her."


The admission came naturally.


Jane smiled.


Then reached across and took his hand.


Andrew squeezed gently.


"We need to start planning."


Jane immediately groaned.


"What now?"


"Lizzy."


Another.


"The birth."


Another.


"The logistics."


Another.


"The hospital."


Another.


"Everything."


Jane sighed.


"Right."


Andrew nodded.


"You know."


Another.


"I promised Adam I'd be there for you if you needed me."


Jane immediately smiled.


"Of course you'll be there."


Another.


"Who else would willingly volunteer to deal with me during labor?"


Andrew raised an eyebrow.


They both knew the answer.


"Ask him."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"Ask Adam."


She stared at him.


"Absolutely not."


"Why?"


"Because."


Another.


"I can't ask him that."


Another.


"That's completely inappropriate."


Andrew wasn't convinced.


But he let it go.


For now.


Two days later he took matters into his own hands.


Karaoke night.


Everyone.


No excuses.


For the first time Adam properly met Kelly and Maria.


Kelly immediately looked him over.


Evaluating him.


Which appeared to be her natural state.


Maria was kinder.


Though only slightly.


Karen and James arrived shortly afterward.


Adam smiled.


"Congratulations on the wedding."


Karen immediately lit up.


Then pointed dramatically at him.


"Oh."


A pause.


"There it is."


Adam frowned.


"What?"


"The flirty look."


Another.


"Grrr."


Adam started laughing.


"What?"


Karen shook her head.


"This is how I know you're not the other guy."


The table went quiet.


Karen continued completely unfazed.


"That guy only had eyes for Jane."


Another.


"Aloof as could be."


Another.


"You, however..."


James immediately wrapped an arm around her shoulder.


"Honey."


Another.


"Down."


Karen ignored him.


"James."


A pause.


"Like I said three years ago."


Another.


"Step up your flirting game."


She winked at Adam.


The entire table erupted into laughter.


Chris nearly fell out of his chair.


Jane was laughing so hard she couldn't breathe.


Adam looked horrified.


And James looked resigned.


"Every day."


James muttered.


"Every single day."


Later Adam found himself sitting beside Jane at the bar.


Two Diet Cokes in hand.


He slid one toward her.


"Your friends are insane."


Jane accepted the drink.


Smiling.


"You have no idea."


A pause.


"But Adam loved them."


Her smile immediately faded.


"Sorry."


Adam shook his head.


"Nope."


Another.


"None of that."


She looked up.


"I told you."


Another.


"If you want to talk about him..."


Another.


"You can."


Another.


"I meant it."


The sincerity surprised her.


Adam leaned against the bar.


"So."


Another.


"I hear you're still working through the song therapy list."


Jane groaned.


"Yup."


Another.


"Adam's fault."


Adam smiled softly.


"I watched the video where he explained it."


Jane laughed.


"Of course you did."


Another.


"He suggested music therapy."


Another.


"Then Dr. Ryan got involved."


Another.


"And suddenly I had homework."


Adam laughed.


"That sounds like Dr. Ryan."


"It absolutely sounds like Dr. Ryan."


Another.


"There are two lists now."


Another.


"Songs."


Another.


"And movies."


Adam nodded.


"I know."


Jane looked surprised.


"You know?"


Adam smiled.


"I watched the video where the two of you spent fifteen minutes arguing over whether *The Princess Bride* belonged on the list."


Jane immediately laughed.


"That was a serious argument."


"There were charts."


Another.


"And evidence."


Another.


"And what looked suspiciously like a PowerPoint presentation."


Jane was laughing harder now.


"Oh my God."


"He absolutely did that."


Adam grinned.


"Anyway."


Another.


"I think I can help with one of those songs."


Jane frowned.


Then he smirked.


And for a split second...


Something about that expression felt hauntingly familiar.


Then he walked toward the stage.


The opening notes started.


*Ain't Too Proud To Beg.*


Jane immediately looked up.


The kitchen.


The dancing.


The laughter.


The life she'd lost.


Adam sang anyway.


Badly.


Enthusiastically.


Making a complete fool of himself.


The room was dying laughing.


Especially Chris.


But Adam never looked away from Jane.


Not once.


Then near the end of the song he stepped off the stage.


Walked directly toward her.


And extended his hand.


Jane stared.


Confused.


"I know this isn't exactly the kitchen..."


A pause.


"...but would you?"


Her eyes widened.


Because those words.


Those exact words.


That was impossible.


And yet somehow...


It wasn't.


Slowly she placed her hand in his.


And for the final thirty seconds of the song...


They danced.


Everyone watching.


Everyone silent.


Everyone understanding something important had just happened.


The next morning Jane sat in Dr. Ryan's office.


Trying unsuccessfully to recover emotionally.


Then she heard it.


A passing comment.


A chart.


One sentence.


Integration therapy.


Adam had started.


Jane froze.


Everything suddenly clicked.


The confidence.


The expressions.


The familiarity.


The things she'd convinced herself she imagined.


By the time her appointment ended she couldn't focus on anything else.


She found Adam later that afternoon in the physician lounge.


He was sitting alone at one of the tables.


Charts spread around him.


Reading intently.


At first glance he looked exactly like Original Adam.


Scrubs.


White coat draped over the chair behind him.


Nothing unusual.


Then she stopped.


Because something felt different.


Or maybe familiar.


That was the problem.


The way he sat.


The way he held his pen.


The slight tilt of his head as he read.


For the briefest moment...


He looked like her Adam.


Enough to make her heart start racing.


Enough to make her question her own sanity.


Adam looked up.


The moment he saw her his expression changed.


Concern.


Warmth.


Recognition.


"Jane."


He immediately set the chart down.


"What's wrong?"


Another.


"Are you okay?"


That look.


The look her Adam always had whenever he thought she was struggling.


Her head hurt.


"I accidentally overheard something at Dr. Ryan's office."


Adam immediately understood.


"The integration therapy."


Jane nodded.


"Is that true?"


Another.


"Why?"


The question came out smaller than she intended.


And much more frightened.


Adam frowned.


"I started two weeks ago."


Another.


"That was the plan."


Another.


"Right?"


He sounded genuinely confused by her reaction.


At the same time he made a mental note to mention to Dr. Ryan that office doors are there for a reason and should be closed.


"I..."


Jane looked away.


"I don't know."


Suddenly she wasn't standing in the physician lounge.


She was standing at Thrive City.


Waiting.


Watching the entrance.


Telling herself he was just late.


Until she realized he wasn't coming.


"Jane."


Adam stood.


Concern growing.


"You need to calm down."


Another.


"What's this about?"


"I don't know."


She shook her head.


"I'm confused."


Another.


"At karaoke..."


She stopped.


Adam already knew where this was heading.


"What?"


Jane swallowed.


Then looked directly at him.


"I thought I saw him."


Silence.


"Then I told myself that was impossible."


Adam closed his eyes briefly.


Because there it was.


The thing he'd been trying to avoid.


"Jane..."


His voice softened.


"This is complicated."


Another.


"It's why I hadn't said anything yet."


Another.


"You're due in a few weeks."


Another.


"I didn't want to stress you out."


Then she asked the real question.


The one hiding underneath everything else.


"Are you going to disappear again?"


Silence.


Adam froze.


And suddenly understood.


This wasn't about integration.


Not really.


This was about Thrive City.


About losing someone.


About never truly recovering from it.


"Jane."


A pause.


"I'm not going anywhere."


Another.


"I'm not."


He waited until she looked at him.


"The point of integration therapy is to break down the wall between him and me."


Another.


"Especially when it comes to memories."


Another.


"It's not about making either of us disappear."


Another.


"It's about trying to become whole."


Jane looked unconvinced.


Terrified.


And that nearly broke his heart.


"Jane."


Another.


"I need you to stop worrying about this."


Another.


"Okay?"


Another.


"Focus on Lizzy."


Another.


"You don't need to carry this too."


But he could already see it.


She already was.


And that killed him.


Without thinking he pulled her gently toward him.


Jane froze.


Then relaxed.


And for one impossible moment...


She felt her Adam.


Not completely.


Not literally.


Just...


Enough.


Enough to make her chest ache.


Enough to make tears burn in her eyes.


Enough to make her wonder if she was losing her mind.


"Am I going insane?"


The question came out painfully honest.


Adam closed his eyes.


Because she shouldn't have been going through this.


She shouldn't have been forced into any of it.


This was the thing he still struggled with most.


The thing that still made him angry.


Original Adam had spent every moment he knew Jane trying to protect her.


His other self had knowingly brought her into this impossible situation.


And now she was paying the price.


"You're not going crazy."


His voice was gentle.


"It's part of the integration."


Another.


"You're going to notice things."


Another.


"Small things."


Another.


"Subtle things."


Another.


"That's normal."


Jane looked up.


Still scared.


Still confused.


Adam smiled softly.


"If you want..."


Another.


"I'll ask Dr. Ryan to sit down with both of us."


Another.


"She can explain it better than I can."


Another.


"But after Lizzy's born."


Another.


"One thing at a time."


Jane nodded.


Then looked away.


"You know he wanted to be there."


The words barely escaped her.


The pain in them unmistakable.


Adam swallowed.


Because he knew exactly who she meant.


"I know, Jane."


A pause.


"I know."


He held her a little tighter.


Then kissed the top of her hair.


"I'm working on that."


Jane frowned.


But before she could ask what he meant...


Adam said nothing else.


And somehow...


That frightened her more than anything else.
