# Chapter 40: Coming Home (Part 2)


Melissa and Marty walked them downstairs.


Neither grandparent seemed particularly interested in leaving.


Which wasn't surprising.


They'd only just met Lizzy.


Melissa kissed Jane's forehead.


Then immediately kissed Lizzy's.


Then did both again just to be safe.


"We'll see you at the apartment."


Jane laughed.


"You just said goodbye."


"I know."


Another kiss to Lizzy's forehead.


"But she's my granddaughter."


Apparently that explained everything.


Marty hugged Jane carefully.


Then Adam.


"Drive safe."


"We will."


Melissa pointed directly at Adam.


"And you."


Adam immediately looked concerned.


"Me?"


"Feed her."


Another.


"Make sure she sleeps."


Another.


"Make sure Jane sleeps."


Another.


"And if either one of them cries, fix it."


Adam blinked.


"That seems like a lot of responsibility."


"It is."


Melissa patted his arm.


"Good luck."


Then the grandparents headed for their rental car.


Eventually they would return to Hilldale.


But not before the homecoming celebration.


Because of course there was a homecoming celebration.


Adam carefully carried Lizzy to the car.


Checking the car seat three separate times.


Then checking it a fourth.


He gently secured her.


Only after he was completely satisfied did he turn back toward Jane.


"Ready?"


"No."


Adam laughed.


"Fair."


He carefully helped her into the car.


Every movement still hurt.


The C-section made sure of that.


Jane settled into the backseat beside Lizzy.


Adam closed the door.


Walked around.


Started the engine.


And for several minutes neither spoke.


The city rolled by outside.


Quiet.


Calm.


Then:


"You're mad."


Jane refused to answer.


Adam smiled.


She caught him in the rearview mirror.


That made her even angrier.


"You did that on purpose."


There it was.


Adam tried very hard not to laugh.


Failed.


Slightly.


That only made things worse.


"You absolutely did that on purpose."


Another.


"You waited until my parents were standing there."


Another.


"You knew I had no way to argue."


Another.


"And as soon as they leave..."


Jane pointed at her finger.


"You are getting this ring back."


Adam smirked.


The smirk.


The exact one.


The one she'd spent three years alternately hating and loving.


And suddenly Jane hated her own brain.


Because she remembered every time he'd looked at her like that.


Every argument.


Every ridiculous conversation.


Every impossible moment.


Her Adam.


That was her Adam.


And immediately her thoughts spiraled.


Because the man driving the car wasn't supposed to be him.


Except according to him...


He was.


And lately she wasn't so sure anymore.


"I did do it on purpose."


Adam answered calmly.


Jane stared.


The absolute nerve of this man.


"Seriously?"


"I tried giving it back before."


Another.


"You told me to hold onto it."


Another.


"You said I'd probably find someone else to give it to someday."


He glanced at her through the mirror.


A small smile appearing.


"I did."


Another.


"It belongs to you."


Jane's jaw dropped.


Then immediately closed again.


Because there were no words.


None.


Not a single one.


"You are unbelievable."


Another.


"You have absolutely no right..."


She stopped.


Unable to finish.


Adam knew exactly why.


Because neither of them knew where the line was anymore.


"Jane."


His voice softened.


"I didn't do it to upset you."


Another.


"From what I remember..."


Another.


"We never broke our engagement."


Jane glared.


"Technically."


He smiled.


Knowing perfectly well he was pushing every button she possessed.


"Technically."


She looked moments away from throwing something at him.


If she could lift it.


Eventually Adam relented.


A little.


"You can give it back after the homecoming."


Another.


"After your parents leave."


Another.


"Okay?"


Jane looked out the window.


Refusing to answer.


The rest of the drive passed mostly in silence.


By the time they arrived at her building, the anger had burned away.


Leaving behind something else.


Something sadder.


Adam came around to help her from the car.


The moment she stood, he saw tears in her eyes.


And immediately knew.


The ring wasn't really the problem.


His expression fell.


"I'm sorry."


Jane looked away.


Adam carefully steadied her.


The parking garage suddenly felt very quiet.


"I just don't know what I'm supposed to do."


Jane looked back at him.


The frustration in his voice surprised her.


Not anger.


Pain.


Confusion.


Exhaustion.


"Yesterday you asked me how I do it."


Another.


"How I act like before."


Another.


"And honestly?"


Another.


"I'm struggling too."


Jane's eyes filled.


Adam looked down.


Searching for the words.


"Going back to the apartment today was hard."


Another.


"Walking into our bedroom was hard."


Another.


"Looking at the pictures."


Another.


"The closet."


He swallowed.


Hard.


"I don't know what I'm supposed to do sometimes."


Jane listened quietly.


Somehow that hurt worse.


Because she could see how much he was trying.


"Some days I know exactly who I am."


Another.


"And some days I'm not sure."


Another.


"I looked in that closet today and remembered buying those clothes."


Another.


"Remembered us putting them away."


Another.


"Remembered wearing them."


He laughed bitterly.


"And then I still hear this little voice saying they aren't yours."


Another.


"That they belong to someone else."


His eyes met hers.


"The problem is..."


Another.


"They are mine."


Another.


"I remember."


Another.


"So eventually that voice shuts up."


Silence.


"And when it comes to you..."


He stopped.


Immediately.


Because he knew where that sentence was headed.


And knew better than to finish it.


Jane's tears finally fell.


"Seeing you walk into my hospital room today was hard."


Another.


"It felt like seeing a ghost."


Another.


"You looked like him."


Another.


"You sounded like him."


Another.


"You dressed like him."


Her voice cracked.


"I am so confused."


Another.


"This is harder than I thought it would be."


Another.


"You need to cut me some slack."


Adam immediately nodded.


"I know."


"I'm trying."


Another.


"My brain and my heart aren't in sync."


She looked down.


"My heart feels one thing."


Another.


"And my brain tells me something else."


The words slipped out before she could stop them.


Adam froze.


"What does that mean?"


The question was quiet.


Dangerously quiet.


Jane looked away.


Almost ashamed.


The reaction told him everything he needed to know.


Immediately he stepped back.


The expression on his face softening.


"I'm sorry."


Another.


"Forget I asked."


Another.


"I promised we would wait."


Another.


"And we're going to wait."


Jane looked up.


Surprised.


"We're going home."


Another.


"We're taking care of Lizzy."


Another.


"We're getting settled."


Another.


"And when we're ready..."


Another.


"We'll deal with the rest."


The relief on her face was immediate.


And heartbreaking.


Because she wanted the conversation.


And feared it.


At exactly the same time.


Adam picked up her bag.


Then carefully lifted Lizzy.


Before returning to Jane's side.


His free arm wrapped gently around her.


Supporting her weight.


Helping her walk.


"Come on."


Another.


"Everyone's waiting."


Neither of them particularly wanted to go upstairs.


But they did anyway.


The elevator ride was silent.


Mostly.


Jane never let go of his hand.


Not once.


Her grip remained tight.


Almost painfully so.


Adam didn't complain.


Didn't mention it.


Because he understood.


Jane was scared.


Overwhelmed.


Confused.


And right now she needed something solid to hold onto.


So he let her squeeze his hand.


And silently promised himself he'd get her through the homecoming celebration.


Then through tonight.


Then tomorrow.


And every day after that.


One day at a time.


Just like he always had.
