# Chapter 5: Curiosity

Jane spent the entire week thinking about Adam's promise.

Not *Adam.*

The other guy.

The mysterious second person she had accidentally become fascinated with.

It irritated her.

She told herself repeatedly that she was simply curious.

Anyone would be curious.

A doctor with dissociative identity disorder who casually mentioned an entirely different personality existing inside his head?

Of course she wanted answers.

Of course she wanted to understand.

That was normal.

Perfectly normal.

Which was exactly why she definitely did not spend half the week wondering what he looked like, sounded like, or acted like.

At least that's what she told herself.


The following Thursday she arrived at the hospital twenty minutes early.

A fact Adam noticed immediately.

"You realize group therapy doesn't start for another twenty minutes."

Jane looked up from her coffee.

"Coincidence."

"Sure."

"It is."

"You got here before I did."

She frowned.

"That's irrelevant."

Adam smiled.

"No, Jane. It's extremely relevant."


They walked toward the therapy room together.

By now, the routine felt strangely comfortable.

Coffee.

Conversation.

Therapy.

More conversation.

The familiarity was dangerous.

At least according to the part of Jane's brain that still believed attachment inevitably led to loss.


Group went better that week.

Not easy.

Better.

Jane didn't share nearly as much.

But she stayed.

And that alone felt like progress.

Adam seemed pleased by it.

Though most things seemed to please Adam.

The man had the emotional range of an excited golden retriever.


Afterward they found their usual coffee shop.

Jane was stirring her coffee when Adam suddenly said,

"So."

She immediately narrowed her eyes.

"So?"

"You remembered."

"Remembered what?"

"The deal."

Jane rolled her eyes.

"There was never a deal."

"There absolutely was."

"There wasn't."

"There was."

"Fine. There was."

Adam looked victorious.

She hated that.

"You wanted to know about him."

Jane leaned forward immediately.

Caught.

Adam laughed.

"Subtle."

"You said you'd tell me."

"I did."

For a moment he became thoughtful.

Something shifted in his expression.

For the first time since Jane had met him, he looked uncertain.

It surprised her.

Adam usually seemed comfortable discussing anything.

Now he looked hesitant.

"What exactly do you want to know?"

Jane considered that.

Everything.

The answer was everything.

Instead she asked,

"Is he really that different from you?"

Adam was quiet for several seconds.

Then he nodded.

"Yeah."

"How?"

"He's..."

Adam searched for the right word.

"More serious."

Jane immediately laughed.

"That's impossible."

"It's really not."

That earned another laugh from her.

"He doesn't smile much."

"Okay."

"He doesn't trust people easily."

Jane nodded.

"Okay."

"He thinks I spend too much time trying to help everyone."

She smirked.

"He might have a point."

"He definitely has a point."

Adam sighed.

"He carries things differently."

"What does that mean?"

It took him a while to answer.

"When bad things happen to me, I try to find the good."

Jane nodded.

That sounded exactly like Adam.

"He doesn't."

That surprised her.

"If something hurts, he lets it hurt."

Adam stared down into his coffee.

"If someone disappoints him, he gets angry."

A sad smile crossed his face.

"He doesn't forgive the way I do."

Something about that answer made Jane unexpectedly emotional.

Because it reminded her of Andrew.

Not the Andrew everyone else knew.

The version she'd seen when nobody else was looking.

The one who carried every hurt.

Every loss.

Every injustice.

The person who felt everything deeply and privately.

"You said before he reminds you of someone."

Adam immediately looked up.

"I did."

"Who?"

His amusement disappeared.

"My ex”

Jane stared into her coffee.

Thinking.

"Do you hate him?"

The question escaped before she could stop it.

Adam blinked

"The other guy."

His expression softened.

"No."

The answer came immediately.

Without hesitation.

"Not even close."

Jane waited.

Adam smiled sadly.

"He saved my life."

The words immediately changed the conversation.

"When everything happened with my mom and my brother..."

His voice grew quiet.

"The person who came out was him."

Jane listened carefully.

"For a long time I thought of him like a problem."

He laughed softly.

"Therapy helped."

"How?"

Adam leaned back.

"I realized he wasn't trying to hurt me."

His gaze drifted toward a nearby window.

"He was trying to protect me."

Jane felt something twist in her chest.

Because she knew what it was like to have someone protect you.

Sometimes even at their own expense.

"Do you ever miss him?"

The question surprised even her.

Adam looked genuinely shocked.

"Nobody's ever asked me that."

"That's not an answer."

A smile slowly appeared.

"Sometimes."

She wasn't expecting that.

"Why?"


Adam thought for a moment.

Then answered honestly.

"Because he's part of me."

His voice was gentle.

"Because I know he's lonely."

Jane immediately looked away.

The statement hit far harder than it should have.

Lonely.

She understood lonely.

For several minutes neither of them spoke.

Then Adam suddenly pointed at her.

"Oh no."

Jane frowned.

"What?"

"You have that look."

"What look?"

"The look you get when you're thinking too much."

"I don't have a look."

"You absolutely have a look."

She rolled her eyes.

"There it is."

"What?"

"The eye roll."


Jane laughed despite herself.

"You realize you're impossible."

"So I've been told."

They continued talking for another hour.

About movies.

Music.

Books.

Everything except grief.

Everything except trauma.

Everything except the things that had defined her life for years.

And for the first time in a very long time, Jane felt normal.

Not fixed.

Not healed.

Normal.

As they finally got up to leave, Adam stopped beside her.

"Hey."

She looked up.

"You did good today."

There was something about the sincerity in his voice that made her smile.

"Yeah?"

"Yeah."

For a brief moment Jane simply looked at him.

At the easy smile.

The warmth.

The kindness.

The ridiculous optimism.

Then she surprised herself.

"I'm glad I came."

Adam's grin widened immediately.

"Me too."

As they headed toward the parking garage, neither realized how important those simple words were.

Because for the first time since Andrew had left, Jane wasn't attending therapy because she was desperate.

She wasn't attending because Scott insisted.

She wasn't attending because Dr. Ryan thought it might help.

She was attending because she wanted to.

And though she would never admit it aloud, there was another reason too.

She was beginning to look forward to seeing Adam Carter.

That realization scared her far more than group therapy ever had.
