# Chapter 4: The First Session

Jane almost didn't go.

Three separate times that morning she considered texting Adam and telling him she couldn't make it.

Work emergency.

Migraine.

Food poisoning.

Sudden and overwhelming desire to move to another country.

Anything.

Anything seemed better than walking into a room full of strangers and talking about the worst moments of her life.

Yet somehow she still found herself standing outside the psychiatric building at UCSF.

Staring at the entrance.

Trying to convince herself not to run.

"Well, you look thrilled."

Jane turned.

Adam stood there carrying two coffees and entirely too much optimism.

She narrowed her eyes.

"You're late."

Adam glanced at his watch.

"I'm early."

"You feel late."

"That's not a thing."

"It is today."

He laughed and handed her a coffee.

"Bribery?"

"Absolutely."

She took it anyway.


They sat outside for a few minutes.

Neither eager to go inside.

Jane stared at the building.

Adam watched Jane stare at the building.

"So," Adam said casually, "if you run, should I chase you?"

Jane considered it.

"Probably."

"Good to know."

"If I scream?"

"I'll evaluate the situation."

"If I cry?"

Adam's expression softened.

"I'll stay."

The answer hit harder than she expected.


Eventually they went inside.

The room wasn't what she expected.

A circle of chairs.

Warm lighting.

A therapist.

People who looked completely normal.

That somehow made it worse.

Because Jane didn't feel normal.

Not anymore.


Adam sat beside her.

Close enough to be reassuring.

Far enough away that she didn't feel trapped.

Group began.

People shared stories.

Some about anxiety.

Some about addiction.

Some about grief.

Loss.

Depression.

Fear.

Jane listened.

The longer she listened, the less she wanted to talk.


Then the facilitator looked toward her.

"Jane, would you like to share today?"

Her stomach dropped.

Adam glanced at her.

Not pushing.

Not rescuing.

Just present.

Jane swallowed hard.

Then nodded.


"I was in a relationship for six years."

Her voice sounded foreign.

Distant.

"I was engaged for two."

She stared at her hands.

"I got married."

The room felt smaller.

"My husband died twenty-four hours later."

Silence.

Nobody looked away.

Nobody judged.

Nobody interrupted.

And somehow that made it harder.


She kept talking.

About grief.

About depression.

About losing herself.

About her husband's brother.

Carefully not saying Andrew's name.

Only referring to him as:

"My dead husband's brother."

Because Andrew would be starting residency at this hospital soon.

Because hearing his name felt dangerous.

Because even now she wasn't ready.


She spoke about the miscarriage.

The guilt.

The shame.

The breakup.

How she had ended the relationship with the person she loved because she couldn't bear what she had become.

What she didn't tell them was the worst part.

The secret she carried.

The beach.

Forty-eight hours before the wedding.

Andrew.

Kirby never knowing.

The belief that she'd been punished for choosing the wrong person to love.


The room started spinning.

Her breathing became uneven.

Too fast.

Too shallow.

No.

Not here.

Please not here.


Adam noticed immediately.

The trembling hands.

The shift in her posture.

The panic.

"Jane."

She shook her head.

Tears streamed down her face.

The facilitator smiled gently.

"It's okay."

But it wasn't okay.

Nothing about this was okay.


Jane looked directly at Adam.

A silent plea.

Please.

Please get me out.


He stood immediately.

Held out his hand.

Jane grabbed it.

The facilitator thanked her for sharing and invited her to come back next week.

Jane barely heard any of it.


The hallway was cooler.

Quieter.

Safer.

Adam guided her to a nearby chair.

Sat beside her.

Handed her a bottle of water.

And waited.


Several minutes passed.

Neither of them spoke.

Jane stared at the floor.

Trying to breathe.

Trying to stop shaking.

Trying not to completely fall apart.


"You did good."

Jane immediately shook her head.

"No."

"You did."

"No."

"Jane—"

"It's not okay."

Her voice cracked.

"It's not okay."


Adam stayed quiet.

Giving her space.

Eventually speaking gently.

"Do you want to talk about it?"

Jane laughed bitterly.

"You really shouldn't ask that."

"Why?"

"Because you're not going to like the answer."

"I think I'll survive."


She looked at him.

Really looked at him.

And suddenly understood what was happening.

What he'd been doing since they met.

The same thing Andrew used to do.

The same thing Kirby always did.

Trying to save her.

Trying to carry the weight for her.

Trying to help.


Panic surged through her again.

Not because she was afraid of Adam.

Because she was afraid for him.


"Thanks so much for trying to help me."

Adam frowned.

The tone immediately worried him.

"Jane—"

"No. Listen."

She wiped her eyes.

"Know that I truly appreciate it, but don't take this personally when I say that you really should walk away from me right now before I drag you down with me."

Then she stood.

And tried to leave.


Adam immediately got up and stepped in front of her.

"Jane. Stop."

The urgency in his voice made her pause.

"What do you mean?"


She stared at him.

"You care."

"Of course I care."

"No."

She shook her head.

"You care."

The distinction mattered.

Somehow it mattered.

"You're not impartial."


Adam froze.


"I've known you a week."

Tears continued falling.

"Maybe a little more."

He didn't speak.


"I slept with my dead husband's brother."

The confession hung in the air.

"I married my husband anyway."

Her breathing shook.

"And then he died."


Adam remained silent.

Listening.


"I lost my baby two months later."

Her voice broke.

"That baby was my dead husband's brother's baby."

She laughed once.

A horrible sound.

"I hate myself for it every single day."


The hallway was silent.


"You are putting yourself on the line for me."

She looked directly at him.

"And I don't even know you."


Adam's chest tightened.

But he didn't move.

Didn't interrupt.

Didn't look away.


"I will not allow another person to do that."

She shook her head.

"I see it happening."

"What?"

"I see the look."

She pointed at him.

"You're trying to help me."

Her voice cracked.

"And eventually you're going to get hurt too."


Several seconds passed.

Then Adam finally spoke.

"Jane."

"What?"

"I don't think you have as much power as you think you have to hurt people."


She stared at him.

Completely caught off guard.


"You aren't wrong."

Adam took a breath.

"I was fine when Scott asked me to talk to you."

Jane looked away immediately.

Then he continued.

"Then I met you."


His honesty surprised her.


"Yes, I care."

The admission hung between them.

"Yes, I spent the last hour wondering if I should help you."


Jane's shoulders dropped.


"But that's not because you're some uniquely dangerous person."

He pointed toward himself.

"It's because this is part of my life."

"My therapy."

"My DID."

"My struggles."

"I ask myself that question about everything."


Jane listened.


"You keep acting like you're radioactive."

That startled a laugh out of her.


"There she is."

Adam smiled.


She rolled her eyes.


Adam pointed.

"That."

"What?"

"The eye roll."

She frowned.


"I knew there was a personality trait somewhere under all that trauma."


She laughed despite herself.


"You are impossible."

"So I've been told."


Adam leaned forward.

"So let's review."

Jane groaned.

"Oh no."


"You're stubborn."

She glared.

"Relentless."

"Not helping."

"Funny."

She looked away.

"Sarcastic."

"Fair."

"Smart."

She rolled her eyes again.

"And despite your truly questionable comic-book references, you seem like someone worth helping."


Jane blinked.


The words hit unexpectedly hard.

Not because they were dramatic.

Because they weren't.

They were simple.

Honest.


Someone worth helping.

Not someone broken.

Not someone damaged.

Not someone beyond saving.

Someone worth helping.


The silence stretched between them.

Comfortable this time.


Eventually Adam sighed.

"Okay."

Jane narrowed her eyes.

"What?"

"Last question."

"You already lied about that once."

"Twice."

"At least you're honest."


Adam smiled.

"Do you want me to stop helping you?"


The question sat heavily between them.


Jane looked down.

Thought about therapy.

Coffee.

Their conversations.

The first person she'd spoken to in months who wasn't tangled up in her history.

Then she looked back up.


"No."


Adam's expression softened.

"No?"


She shook her head.

"I don't want you to stop helping me."


Relief flashed across his face.

Quickly hidden.


"Good."

He stood.

"Then you're coming back next week."


Jane crossed her arms.

"That's not how agreements work."

"It is today."


She laughed.


A few seconds later she hesitated.

Then spoke quietly.

"Can I ask you something?"


Adam immediately smiled.

"There it is."

"What?"

"I knew you would eventually ask."


Jane frowned.

"You don't know me well enough to know that."


"I know exactly what you're going to ask."


She folded her arms tighter.

"You're insufferable."


"Ask."


Jane hesitated.

A little embarrassed.

A little curious.

A lot curious.


"What is he like?"


Adam grinned.

A real grin.

The biggest one she'd seen all day.


"The other guy?"


Jane nodded.


Adam laughed.

"That took you over a week."


She blushed.

"Sorry if that's personal."


"No."

He shook his head.

"Honestly, I expected that question on day one."


Jane rolled her eyes.

Again.


Adam pointed.

"There it is."


"Answer the question."


Instead of answering, Adam stood.


"Come back next week."


Jane blinked.

"What?"


"Come back to group therapy."


Her eyes narrowed.


"And I'll show you."


Jane stared at him.

Then pointed accusingly.


"That's a bribe."


"Yeah."


"You admit that's a bribe?"


"Absolutely."


She couldn't believe him.


"You're unethical."


"Probably."


Jane shook her head and laughed despite herself.

For the first time since entering the building, she wasn't thinking about grief.

Or guilt.

Or Andrew.

Or Kirby.

She was thinking about a ridiculous doctor who was openly bribing her with information.


"Next week."

Adam held out his hand.


Jane looked at it.

Then shook it.


"Next week."


Neither of them realized it then.

But that simple agreement would become the reason Jane kept coming back.

Not the therapy.

Not the coffee.

Not even the progress.

The promise.

The deal.

And the growing curiosity about the man sitting across from her that she couldn't quite explain.
