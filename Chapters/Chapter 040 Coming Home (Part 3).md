# Chapter 40: Coming Home (Part 3)


The apartment exploded the moment they walked through the door.


Balloons.


Food.


Flowers.


Enough snacks to survive a natural disaster.


Karen had clearly ignored every request for a quiet afternoon.


As expected.


Jane paused in the doorway.


Overwhelmed.


Not by the decorations.


By the fact that she was finally home.


Adam carefully carried Lizzy's car seat inside.


His eyes immediately found the bassinet.


Karen had moved it.


The bassinet he'd assembled earlier now sat proudly in the living room surrounded by an unreasonable number of balloons.


Of course it did.


Adam smiled.


Then carefully lifted Lizzy out of the car seat.


She slept through the entire process.


Completely unbothered.


He gently placed her into the bassinet.


Immediately eight adults materialized around her.


As if summoned.


Andrew spotted Jane first.


"Whoa."


He crossed the room immediately.


"Easy."


Jane laughed.


"I am not ninety."


"No."


Andrew slipped an arm around her waist.


"But you did get cut open three days ago."


Unfortunately that argument was valid.


He slowly helped her over to one of the recliners.


"There."


Jane sighed with relief.


"Thanks."


The next few hours became a blur.


A good blur.


Karen held Lizzy.


Then James.


Then Melissa.


Then Marty.


Then Kelly.


Then Maria.


Then Chris.


Then Andrew.


Then Scott.


Then Karen stole her back.


Repeatedly.


Jane was relieved.


Exhausted.


And grateful.


Because every time someone took Lizzy...


Another person brought Jane something.


Food.


Water.


A pillow.


A blanket.


Her medications.


And somehow Adam seemed responsible for most of it.


Without drawing attention to himself.


He appeared beside her with lunch.


Then water.


Then medicine.


Then another bottle of water.


Then a snack.


Then another pillow.


Jane accepted everything.


Thanked him.


And every single time their eyes met...


The conversation in the parking garage lingered between them.


Eventually Andrew settled into the chair beside her.


And immediately noticed the ring.


His eyebrows rose.


"Oh?"


Jane groaned.


"No."


"Yes."


Andrew pointed directly at her left hand.


"What is that about?"


Jane looked toward the kitchen.


Adam was currently attempting to make coffee for everyone.


Then she looked back at Andrew.


"That."


She lifted her hand slightly.


"Is the beginning of an argument."


Andrew laughed.


"A big one?"


"Huge."


Another.


"And it's definitely continuing the moment everyone leaves."


Andrew nodded.


That checked out.


Completely.


Because he understood exactly what Adam had been trying to do.


And exactly how spectacularly it had backfired.


At some point Andrew found Adam standing in the kitchen trying to make coffee for everyone.


"Hey."


Adam looked up.


"Hey."


Andrew studied him for a moment.


Then nodded toward the living room.


"What happened?"


Adam frowned.


"With what?"


"Jane."


Another.


"She's upset."


Another.


"And I see the ring."


Adam immediately looked away.


Toward the coffee machine.


Toward literally anything else.


Then laughed.


A tired laugh.


"Trust me."


Another.


"You don't want to know."


Andrew leaned against the counter.


"Actually."


Another.


"I do."


A pause.


"Tell me what happened."


Adam looked down into his coffee mug.


Quiet.


Then finally:


"I don't know what I'm doing anymore."


Andrew didn't interrupt.


He simply waited.


"Honestly."


Another.


"I tried for weeks to be Adam."


Another.


"The therapy friend."


Another.


"The guy who got coffee with her."


Another.


"The guy she trusted."


Another.


"The guy she felt safe with."


He laughed bitterly.


"All the while my brain is getting hit with memories."


Another.


"Memories of us."


Another.


"Our life."


Another.


"And not being able to do anything about it."


Andrew nodded.


Because he understood.


"I don't know how to be near her without wanting to be more than Adam the therapy friend."


Another.


"More than the buddy who used to get coffee with her."


Another.


"She trusted him."


Another.


"She felt safe with him."


Another.


"I tried."


Another.


"But I kept slipping."


Another.


"Without meaning to."


Another.


"And reverting back to Adam."


He sighed.


"The other guy."


Another.


"The one she spent three years building a life with."


Silence.


"I decided I wasn't going to tell her about the integration."


Another.


"At least not yet."


Another.


"I wanted her to have time."


Another.


"I wanted her to focus on Lizzy."


Another.


"Then I said something stupid in the hospital."


Another.


"She caught it."


Another.


"She thought she was losing her mind."


Another.


"So I had to tell her."


Another.


"In a hospital room."


Another.


"While she was recovering from surgery."


He shook his head.


"Now she's confused."


Another.


"I'm a mess."


Another.


"And I don't know how to fix any of this."


Andrew looked toward the living room.


Toward Jane.


Then back at Adam.


"Well."


Another.


"First."


Another.


"You were never just the therapy friend."


Adam frowned.


"What?"


Andrew laughed.


"You were just hiding."


Another.


"You were already in love with her."


Another.


"Four months after you met her, you finally stopped pretending otherwise."


That earned a reluctant smile.


Andrew continued.


"I know Jane."


Another.


"She just needs time."


Another.


"I think her heart is looking at you and telling her she knows exactly who she's looking at."


Another.


"But her brain is saying that's impossible."


Another.


"That's a hell of a fight."


Adam was quiet.


Andrew pointed toward the living room again.


"You need to cut her some slack."


Another.


"She just had a baby."


Another.


"Hormones are running wild."


Another.


"And honestly?"


Another.


"If you ask me."


Another.


"She is struggling because she loves you."


Another.


"But she doesn't want to."


Another.


"Because her brain keeps telling her that you're not him."


The words landed hard.


Because part of Adam already suspected it.


Andrew continued.


"So be patient."


Another.


"Be kind."


Another.


"Don't push too hard."


Another.


"I think you need to remember how much you can push and when you need to pull back."


Another.


"You used to know."


Another.


"And I think it'll come back."


Adam stared down at the coffee.


Then nodded slowly.


"Thanks."


Another.


"I hope you know how much I appreciate talking to you about this."


Andrew smiled.


"The funny thing is..."


Another.


"This isn't the first time."


Adam looked up.


"What?"


Andrew laughed.


"Trust me."


Another.


"We've had a lot of conversations about Jane over the last three years."


Another.


"I'm here for you."


Another.


"I want Jane happy."


Another.


"You used to know that."


Another.


"Hopefully you'll remember."


He patted Adam on the shoulder.


Then smiled.


"And for what it's worth?"


Another.


"I think you're both doing better than you think."


Then Andrew headed back toward the living room.


Leaving Adam alone with his coffee.


And a little more hope than he'd had five minutes earlier.


The party slowly wound down.


Marty and Melissa left first.


Promising to return over the weekend.


Karen and James hugged Jane.


Chris and Maria hugged her.


Kelly kissed Lizzy's forehead.


Andrew did the same.


Scott lingered longest.


Of course.


Before leaving he pulled Adam aside.


"Eight weeks."


Adam frowned.


"What?"


"Paternity leave."


Another.


"Take it."


Adam immediately shook his head.


"Call me if you need me."


Scott looked toward Jane.


Then Lizzy.


Then back at Adam.


"No."


Another.


"Focus on your family."


The words landed harder than expected.


His family.


Jane.


Lizzy.


Adam smiled.


"Thanks."


Eventually everyone left.


The apartment finally fell quiet.


Karen had apparently supervised cleanup before departing.


Everything was spotless.


Food was packed away.


Drinks were put away.


The only thing left behind was an absurd number of balloons.


Adam made one final sweep through the kitchen.


Then walked over to Jane.


"Hey."


She looked up.


"You want to change?"


Another.


"I'll take first shift."


Another.


"I'll feed her."


Another.


"I'll change her."


Another.


"I'll stay out here."


Another.


"And if she makes a sound, I'll be there."


Jane looked exhausted.


And relieved.


She nodded.


Adam helped her back to the bedroom.


Then returned to the living room.


Lizzy was awake.


Staring up at him.


Very seriously.


"Hey beautiful."


Adam smiled.


"You're finally awake."


He picked her up carefully.


"You must be hungry."


Another.


"I got you."


Another.


"Welcome home."


He made her bottle.


Fed her.


Burped her.


Changed her.


And eventually rocked her back toward sleep.


Jane watched from the hallway.


Quietly.


For several minutes.


"Hope you're kind to me tonight."


Adam whispered.


"Because your dad has absolutely no idea what he's doing."


Jane smiled despite herself.


Eventually Adam noticed her.


"Hey."


Another.


"You okay?"


She nodded.


Mostly.


He helped her back to the bedroom.


Made sure she had water.


Then changed into sweatpants and a T-shirt.


A medical textbook appeared soon after.


Classic Adam.


Two hours later Jane woke up.


The room felt empty.


Too empty.


Carefully she got out of bed.


Walked into the living room.


And stopped.


Adam was holding Lizzy again.


Rocking gently.


Humming softly.


Nightshift.


Her Adam's favorite song.


The sight hit harder than she expected.


Because this was the problem.


She loved him.


Or part of her did.


And she hated herself for it.


Because she'd once promised there was no way.


No possibility.


No chance.


Yet here he was.


Holding their daughter.


Singing to her.


And all she wanted was to believe him.


To believe this impossible thing was true.


Adam looked up.


Immediately noticing her.


"Hey."


Another.


"Are you okay?"


Jane shook her head.


No.


Not even a little.


Adam carefully placed a sleeping Lizzy into the bassinet.


Then wrapped his arms around Jane.


Holding her patiently.


Quietly.


Remembering Andrew's words.


She's struggling because she loves you.


Her brain just won't let her believe it yet.


Jane buried her face against his chest.


"Can I stay with you?"


The question surprised them both.


"Yes."


His answer came immediately.


"Of course."


He sat back down on the couch.


Making room for her.


Pulling a blanket around both of them.


Holding her close.


Jane cried quietly.


Adam simply let her.


"It's okay."


He kissed the top of her head.


"We're going to be okay."


"I'm not sure what I'm doing."


Jane whispered.


Adam laughed softly.


"Well."


Another.


"That makes two of us."


A tiny smile appeared.


Mission accomplished.


"Hey."


His voice softened.


"I'm here."


Another.


"Just tell me what you need."


Another.


"I'll do it."


Another.


"Anything."


Jane looked up.


"I..."


Then stopped.


Unsure.


Adam waited.


"If you need me to hold you, I'll hold you."


Another.


"If you need space, I'll give you space."


Another.


"If you need me to go get snacks in the middle of the night, I'll go get snacks."


A small smile appeared.


"What kind of snacks?"


Adam considered this seriously.


"Depends."


Another.


"Are we talking normal snacks?"


Another.


"Or postpartum snacks?"


Jane laughed softly.


"What if I want pizza?"


Adam groaned.


"Oh no."


Another.


"That's a dangerous question."


"Why?"


"Because I spent years pretending I was above pizza."


Another.


"I wasn't pretending very well."


"I knew it."


Adam pointed at her.


"I survived medical school on pizza and Diet Coke."


Another.


"An absolutely horrifying lifestyle."


Another.


"At some point I decided I should stop living like a raccoon."


A genuine laugh escaped her.


"So all the vegetables..."


Adam groaned dramatically.


"I was not trying to torture you."


Another.


"I was trying to take care of you."


Another.


"There's a difference."


"A very small difference."


"Disagree."


Another.


"Strongly."


He smiled.


"But yes."


Another.


"I love pizza."


Another.


"Like a true addict."


Another.


"So if you want pizza, I'll get pizza."


The smile lingered.


Then Jane looked at him carefully.


"You talked to Andrew."


It wasn't a question.


Adam laughed.


"I may have."


Another.


"And honestly?"


Another.


"I'm glad I've got somebody in my corner."


Another.


"Especially somebody who seems to know you better than you know yourself."


Jane groaned.


"I hate that you two talk about me."


The complaint lacked all conviction.


Adam laughed.


"I don't believe that for a second."


Another.


"I think you're mostly annoyed because he's usually right."


Jane pointed at him.


"See."


Another.


"That's exactly the sort of thing he would say."


Adam smiled.


"Which should probably concern you."


For a moment the tension eased.


Not gone.


Just lighter.


And for the first time all night Jane wasn't thinking about alters.


Or integration.


Or fear.


She was just sitting on a couch with Adam.


Their daughter asleep nearby.


Feeling safe.


And somehow that felt like a step in the right direction.


Adam looked at her again getting back to his original question

“Jane. What do you need? What can I do to help and make settling in easier for you? Anything”


"Can you stay with Lizzy and I in our bedroom? I wasn't sure how to say it."


Another.


"I didn't want it to be weird."


Adam smiled gently.


"It isn't."


Another.


"It makes perfect sense."


Jane looked down.


"I need both of you close."


The words escaped before she could overthink them.


Adam's expression softened immediately.


Because honestly?


He understood exactly what she meant.


"It makes sense, Jane."


His voice was warm.


Reassuring.


"I don't want you out here every couple of hours checking on us."


Another.


"That's not going to help you heal."


Another.


"You just had major surgery."


Another.


"You need to rest."


He reached for her hand.


"And I know you're going to worry."


Another.


"You're going to wonder if she's okay."


Another.


"You're going to wonder if I need help."


Another.


"And you're going to try to push yourself a lot harder than you should."


Jane opened her mouth.


Then closed it.


Because he wasn't wrong.


A small smile appeared on Adam's face.


"Exactly."


Another.


"So we'll make this easy."


Another.


"The three of us stay together."


Another.


"Nobody has to walk across the apartment."


Another.


"Nobody has to worry."


Another.


"And if Lizzy needs something, I'm right there."


He squeezed her hand gently.


"And if you need something, I'm right there too."


For the first time since she'd come into the living room, some of the tension left her shoulders.


"Thank you."


Adam smiled.


"You never have to thank me. I’m here for you. I’m here for Lizzy. Always."


Then he stood.


"Come on."


Another.


"The three of us migrate to our bedroom."


An hour later the bassinet sat beside his side of the bed.


The rocker had been moved.


A feeding station existed.


A changing station existed.


Everything was within arm's reach.


Once he finished, Adam climbed into bed.


Beside Jane.


Carefully.

Giving her space.


Jane immediately ignored that space.


Instinctively leaning against him.


The movement happened so naturally neither of them commented on it.


Adam wrapped an arm around her.


And for the first time since bringing Lizzy home...


The apartment felt quiet.


Safe.


Complete.


A few hours later Lizzy would wake again.


Then again.


And again.


Thus beginning the routine that would quietly shape the next several weeks of their lives.
