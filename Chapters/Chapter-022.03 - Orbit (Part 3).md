# Chapter 22: Orbit (Part 3)

The first few minutes of the drive home were quiet.

Not awkward.

Thoughtful.


Kelly watched the lights of the city slide past her window.

Andrew drove with one hand on the steering wheel, the radio playing softly in the background.


For several minutes neither spoke.


Then finally:


"Jane wasn't feeling well."


Andrew glanced over immediately.


The seriousness in her voice got his attention.


"No."


Kelly looked down at her hands.


"I don't think it was just stress."


A long silence followed.


Then Andrew understood exactly what she was trying to say.


"Oh."


Kelly nodded.


"Yeah."


For a moment neither spoke.


Andrew focused on the road.

Trying to organize his thoughts.


The possibility felt strange.

Unexpected.


Then eventually he shook his head.


"Adam would've noticed."


Kelly looked over.


"What?"


"Something like that."


A pause.


"He notices everything about her."


Kelly smiled slightly.


"That's kind of what I thought too."


And maybe she was right.

Maybe Adam had already noticed.

Maybe he already knew.

Maybe they were both overthinking something that would turn out to be nothing at all.


Eventually the conversation drifted elsewhere.

Neither of them sure what else there was to say.


A few blocks away, Adam and Jane walked along the waterfront toward his apartment.

The bay was calm.

The city lights reflected across the water.


Their hands were intertwined.

Neither in any hurry to get home.


For a while neither spoke.


Then Adam broke the silence.


"You scared me tonight."


Jane glanced up.


"What?"


"When you ran to the bathroom."


The concern in his voice immediately made her feel guilty.


"I was okay."


"You didn't look okay."


Jane laughed softly.


"Fair."


Adam squeezed her hand.


"What happened?"


She shrugged.


"I don't know."


Which wasn't entirely true.

But it wasn't entirely false either.


Adam waited.


She eventually sighed.


"I've been feeling kind of off lately."


His attention sharpened immediately.


"How long?"


"A couple weeks maybe."


Adam frowned.


"A couple weeks?"


"It's not that dramatic."


"Jane."


There it was.

Doctor Adam.


Focused.

Concerned.

Already running through possibilities.


"You didn't mention any of this."


"I didn't think much of it."


Another frown.


"What kind of symptoms?"


Jane thought about it.


"Tired."


Adam nodded.


"Okay."


"Nauseous."


That got his attention.


"How often?"


Jane shrugged.


"Here and there."


"A lot in the mornings."


The words were out before she thought about them.


Adam slowed slightly.

Thinking.


Stress.

Anxiety.

Exhaustion.

Burnout.

Diet.

Sleep deprivation.

A dozen different possibilities.


All perfectly reasonable.


"Anything else?"


"I don't know."


A pause.


"I just don't feel like myself."


Adam nodded.

Still thinking.

Still sorting through possibilities.


"You've been working too much."


Jane laughed immediately.


"There it is."


"What?"


"Your answer for everything."


"It's a very good answer."


"It really isn't."


A smile tugged at his mouth.


"You should probably make an appointment."


Jane groaned.


"Adam."


"I'm serious."


"I know."


And she did.


Because he genuinely looked worried.


The same way he always did when something might be wrong.


The guilt arrived immediately.


Because she still hadn't told him about the conversation with Kelly.


Not because she was hiding something.


Because she didn't have anything to tell.


At least that was what she kept telling herself.


*Maybe you should take a test.*


The words had been rattling around inside her head ever since they left the restroom.


No.


Absolutely not.


It couldn't be.


The idea felt ridiculous.


And even if it wasn't ridiculous...


Jane immediately stopped herself.


No.


She wasn't doing this.


She wasn't going to build an entire future around one comment from Kelly.


There were dozens of other explanations.


Stress.


Work.


The wedding.


Life.


Anything.


Everything.


Not that.


Because if it was that...


Jane felt her stomach tighten.


If it was that, everything suddenly became more complicated.


Not because she didn't want a future with Adam.


God knew she did.


The problem was that their future already came with so many unknowns.


Adam had DID.


Integration therapy was only a few months away.


Every article she'd read.

Every conversation they'd had.

Every specialist they'd spoken to.


Nobody could tell them exactly what would happen.


Maybe everything would be fine.


Maybe it wouldn't.


Maybe Adam would still be Adam.


Maybe he wouldn't.


Maybe Original Adam would return.


Maybe nobody really understood what that meant.


The uncertainty had been sitting quietly in the corner of her life for months now.


Waiting.


She had learned to live with it.

Mostly.


But the possibility of adding another unknown to that pile?


No.


She wasn't thinking about it.


She refused.


"Jane."


She blinked.


"What?"


Adam was watching her carefully.


His expression had softened.


"You disappeared."


A pause.


"What are you thinking about?"


The question hit harder than she expected.


For a moment she considered telling him everything.


Kelly.


The bathroom.


The comment.


The fear.


The impossible possibility she couldn't seem to stop turning over in her mind.


Then another thought arrived.


What if Kelly was wrong?


What if this was stress?


What if she told Adam and spent the next week worrying him over absolutely nothing?


No.


Not yet.


Not until she knew.


So instead she smiled.

Small.

Careful.


"Nothing."


The answer came too quickly.


Adam knew it.


She knew he knew it.


But he didn't push.


He never did.


That was one of the things Jane loved most about him.


Adam had learned a long time ago that if Jane wasn't ready to talk, forcing the conversation only made things harder.


When she was ready, she'd tell him.


Eventually.


So after a moment he simply nodded.


"Okay."


No accusation.


No pressure.


Just trust.


They resumed walking.


A few seconds later Adam slipped his arm around her shoulders.

Pulling her closer.


The gesture was automatic now.


Familiar.


Safe.


Jane leaned against him.


For a moment she let herself simply exist there.


No future.

No questions.

No possibilities.


Just Adam.


Adam pressed a kiss against the top of her head.


"You know I'm here, right?"


The words nearly broke her.


Because she knew.


God, she knew.


A lump formed unexpectedly in her throat.


"Yeah."


His arm tightened slightly.


"I love you."


The words came easily.

Naturally.


Jane closed her eyes.


"I love you too."


By the time they reached his apartment building, neither of them had said much else.


The elevator ride was quiet.


Comfortable.


The kind of silence that only existed between people who knew each other well.


But while Adam stood beside her with one hand resting lightly against her back, Jane found her thoughts wandering again.


To Kelly.


To the bathroom.


To one stupid suggestion she wished had never been made.


*Maybe you should take a test.*


Jane looked down at the floor.


No.


Tomorrow.


She could worry about tomorrow.


Tonight she just wanted to be here.


With Adam.


The future could wait one more day.

"I've been feeling kind of off lately."


Adam's attention sharpened immediately.


"How long?"


Jane shrugged.


"A couple weeks maybe."


"A couple weeks?"


"It's not that dramatic."


"Jane."


There it was.

Doctor Adam.


Not panic.

Not fear.

Just focus.


"What kind of off?"


Jane thought about it.


"Tired."


Adam nodded.


"Okay."


"Nauseous."


That got his attention.


"How often?"


Jane hesitated.


"More recently."


"A couple of times."


"Anything else?"


She shrugged.


"I don't know."


A pause.


"I just don't feel like myself."


Adam was quiet for a moment.

Thinking.


Then:


"Okay."


The word came slowly.

Methodically.


"Could be stress."


Jane smiled.


"There's the doctor."


"I'm not finished."


That made her laugh.


Adam continued.


"Stress."


A finger.


"Your medications."


Another finger.


"Sleep."


Another.


"Anxiety."


Another.


"You're still working through a lot of therapy stuff."


Another.


"You've been carrying around wedding stress for Karen for the last two months."


Jane snorted.


"Fair."


"I'm serious."


"I know."


Adam kept going.


"It could be nothing."


A pause.


"It could be a mild virus."


Another.


"It could be that your body is deciding now is a great time to complain about every decision you've made in the last three months."


That earned another laugh.


"My body would do that."


"Your body absolutely would do that."


The corner of his mouth softened.


Then more quietly:


"But I don't love that it's been going on for weeks."


Jane looked over.


The concern was there now.


Not dramatic.


Real.


"You think something is wrong?"


Adam immediately shook his head.


"No."


A pause.


"I think I'd like more information."


That was such an Adam answer that she couldn't help smiling.


"You always want more information."


"Information is useful."


"You are unbelievably boring."


"I am a doctor."


"That is not a defense."


"It absolutely is."


Adam slipped his hand back into hers.


"You should probably make an appointment if it keeps happening."


Jane sighed.


"I know."


Because what's interesting is that he's thoughtfully working through dozens of possible explanations and never even glances toward the possibility Kelly suggested.

Not because he's missing something.

Because in his head the probability tree has already been shaped by what he knows about their life.

So while Adam is busy considering medications, stress responses, sleep disruption, anxiety, dietary causes, and half a dozen other explanations, Jane is standing there thinking:

*Kelly is wrong.*

*She has to be.*

*Because if she isn't...*

And that's where her fear starts.

Not about being sick.

About what a major life change would mean when she's already staring down one of the biggest uncertainties of her life: the integration therapy and the possibility of losing the version of Adam she loves. That's the emotional conflict Adam doesn't know he's standing next to.
