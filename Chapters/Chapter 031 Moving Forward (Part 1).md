# Chapter 31: Moving Forward (Part 1)

By the middle of her seventh month of pregnancy, Jane's life had become busy enough that most days she barely had time to think.


Which was probably for the best.


The baby shower had come and gone.


Karen had somehow managed to turn it into a full-scale production.


There had been decorations.


Games.


Food.


Far too many people.


And enough gifts to fill an entire room.


Jane was currently standing in the middle of her apartment wondering how one tiny human being could possibly require this much stuff.


Boxes sat everywhere.


The living room.


The nursery.


The dining room.


There were tiny clothes folded in neat stacks.


Stuffed animals.


Blankets.


Books.


Baby monitors.


Diapers.


More diapers.


And somehow even more diapers.


Jane stared at the mountain of gifts.


Then at her stomach.


"Lizzy."


A kick answered immediately.


Jane smiled.


"Explain this."


Another kick.


"I disagree."


The baby kicked again.


"You're not helping."


The smile stayed anyway.


That happened more often lately.


Lizzy kicked.


Jane smiled.


It was a pretty effective system.


Life moved quickly after the shower.


Work remained busy.


She still met with Dr. Ryan individually every week.


Still attended group therapy.


Still had regular prenatal appointments.


Still spent her evenings trying to prepare for a baby she somehow still couldn't believe was real.


Most days she fell asleep almost immediately.


Exhausted.


Happy.


Terrified.


Usually all at once.


The nursery was nearly finished.


The pale yellow walls.


The Winnie the Pooh decals.


The pictures.


The dresser.


The changing table.


Everything was ready.


Everything except one thing.


The crib.


The crib remained inside its giant unopened box.


Waiting.


Mocking her.


Every single time she walked into the nursery.


Andrew had promised to help assemble it.


And he genuinely intended to.


Unfortunately Andrew had somehow become busier than usual.


The hospital.


The precinct.


Court dates.


Paperwork.


Life.


The result was that the crib remained exactly where it was.


Waiting.


Still, despite their schedules, Andrew and Jane maintained one standing commitment.


Tuesday lunch.


Every week.


Hospital cafeteria.


Same table.


Same routine.


It was one of the few things in life that still felt predictable.


And Jane was grateful for it.


The subject of Adam rarely came up anymore.


Not intentionally.


Mostly because Jane had somehow managed to avoid him for almost two weeks.


It wasn't planned.


At least she didn't think it was.


The hospital was large.


The city was larger.


People missed each other all the time.


At least that was what Jane told herself.


The truth was probably more complicated.


Sometimes when she walked into Dr. Ryan's office she found herself glancing toward the waiting room.


Immediately hoping she wouldn't see him there.


Not because she hated him.


Not because she was angry.


But because she didn't know what she would say.


Or what he would say.


The memory of that morning in his apartment was still too fresh.


Too painful.


So mostly she focused on Lizzy.


And the future.


Meanwhile, Original Adam had spent the last two weeks experiencing roughly three years' worth of emotional processing.


The first few days had been brutal.


He'd taken time off from work.


Sat alone in his apartment.


And started working his way through the videos.


There were so many videos.


Some practical.


Some funny.


Some heartbreaking.


Some nearly impossible to watch.


By the third day he felt physically sick.


By the fourth he felt embarrassed.


By the fifth he felt guilty.


Then angry.


Then sad.


Then guilty for being angry.


The cycle repeated endlessly.


One memory in particular refused to leave him alone.


Jane standing in his kitchen.


Her hand resting protectively on her stomach.


"My daughter is my world."


A pause.


"Lizzy is the best thing that's ever happened to me besides..."


The sentence never finished.


It didn't need to.


Adam knew exactly how it ended.


Besides Adam.


Not him.


The other Adam.


Because he'd seen the pictures.


Thousands of them.


Pictures on his phone.


Pictures people had sent.


Pictures hidden in albums.


Pictures everywhere.


And every single one showed the same thing.


Jane loved him.


The other him.


There was no denying it.


The way she smiled.


The way she looked at him.


The way he looked back.


The kind of love people spend their entire lives hoping for.


At first Adam hated that realization.


Then came jealousy.


Not of Jane.


Of himself.


Or rather...


The version of himself that had existed while he was gone.


Because that version had done something Original Adam never managed to do.


He'd been brave.


He'd told Jane how he felt.


He'd taken the risk.


And he'd gotten all of her.


For a while that realization hurt.


Then eventually came acceptance.


How could he stay angry?


The other Adam hadn't stolen anything.


He'd simply done what Original Adam never had the courage to do.


And somehow that truth made everything both easier and harder.


By the end of the second week, Adam finally called Dr. Ryan.


Filled her in.


Scheduled an appointment.


And prepared for another difficult milestone.


Returning to work.


His first morning back at the hospital felt strange.


Everything looked familiar.


Yet somehow different.


Like everyone else had continued moving while he'd been standing still.


When he walked into the physician lounge, he immediately saw Andrew.


For half a second he considered turning around.


Because he wasn't entirely sure what to expect.


Adam knew he'd hurt Jane.


Whether he'd meant to or not didn't really matter.


Part of him assumed Andrew would be angry.


Protective.


Defensive.


Instead Andrew looked up and smiled.


"Hey, Adam."


A pause.


"Welcome back."


Adam blinked.


That wasn't the reaction he'd expected.


"Hey, Andrew."


Andrew stood and walked over.


"How are you doing?"


"I'm managing."


Andrew nodded.


"Good."


A pause.


"And if you're not..."


Another.


"I'm here."


Adam smiled weakly.


"Thanks."


Andrew hesitated.


"The other you asked me to help fill in any gaps if you needed it."


A grin appeared.


"Work stuff."


Another.


"And you know..."


A pause.


"Jane stuff."


Adam immediately looked away.


"Yeah."


Another.


"I may have some work questions."


Andrew noticed the avoidance instantly.


But didn't push.


Instead he leaned back slightly.


"You know..."


Adam groaned internally.


Here it comes.


"Jane cared about you."


The words were gentle.


"She always has."


Adam remained quiet.


"The reason she even met the other guy was because she wanted to bring you back."


A pause.


"When she realized she couldn't..."


Another.


"I think she stayed because she missed you."


Adam looked down.


Andrew continued.


"She thought maybe getting to know him meant she'd still have some part of you around."


The lounge went quiet.


"She missed you when you disappeared."


Another pause.


"And speaking from experience..."


A small smile appeared.


"Jane is the kind of friend you need in your life."


Another.


"So don't shut her out."


The statement hit harder than Adam expected.


Andrew looked thoughtful.


"You know..."


"The other you made me realize something."


Adam raised an eyebrow.


"What?"


Andrew laughed softly.


"That I was an idiot."


That finally earned a smile.


"For a year I shut Jane out."


Another.


"And your other self made me see what a mistake that was."


Another.


"He helped her heal."


Another.


"He helped her open doors."


Another.


"And somehow he tore down every wall she had."


Andrew smiled.


"Don't shut her out."


Before Adam could answer, someone appeared carrying a stack of files.


Chris.


Andrew immediately waved him over.


"Perfect timing."


Chris approached.


Then stopped.


Because seeing this Adam was still strange.


Even two weeks later.


"Adam."


Andrew gestured.


"I want you to meet my partner."


A grin appeared.


"Detective Chris Morgan."


Adam smiled and extended his hand.


"Nice to meet you, Detective."


A pause.


"I've heard a lot about you."


Chris looked momentarily caught off guard.


Then shook his hand.


"Hopefully only the good stuff."


Adam smiled.


"The reviews appear mixed."


That made Chris laugh.


"There he is."


For the next few moments the three of them talked.


Not deeply.


Not emotionally.


Just enough.


Enough for Adam to feel normal.


Or at least normal adjacent.


Eventually Andrew and Chris headed out.


Adam sat down.


Then heard a familiar voice behind him.


"Well look at that."


Adam turned.


Scott stood in the doorway.


Smiling.


Fresh back from Doctors Without Borders.


"Scott."


Scott opened his arms dramatically.


"Adam."


A pause.


"You are back, kid."


For the first time all morning, Adam laughed.
