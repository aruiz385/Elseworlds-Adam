# Chapter 24: New Future (Part 2)- Boys' Night

Chris pointed across the table.

"I still don't understand how this happened."

Adam sighed.

"Which part?"

"All of it."

Chris gestured dramatically

"The entire thing."

A pause.

"You."

Another.

"Jane."

Another.

"And now somehow there's a baby."

The table laughed.

Adam shook his head.

"Honestly?"

A smile appeared.

"I'm still trying to figure it out myself."

Chris narrowed his eyes.

"That's not an answer."

"It kind of is."

Adam took a sip of his drink.

"I genuinely don't know how I got this lucky."

The sincerity caught everyone off guard for a moment.

Then Chris pointed again.

"No."

"What?"

"Start at the beginning."

Andrew immediately started laughing.

"Oh no."

"Oh yes."

Adam replied.

"Because Jane and I still disagree about how we got together."

James looked delighted.

"This should be good."

Adam leaned back in his chair.

"Our first lunch."

"A fact-finding mission."

Andrew supplied immediately.

Adam pointed at him.

"See? This."

The table laughed

"I told her it was a date."

A pause.

"Very clearly."

Another.

"I literally said, 'It's a date.'"

Chris grinned.

"And?"

"And Jane spent the next year insisting it was a fact-finding mission."

The table erupted.

Andrew nodded.

"She absolutely would."

"THANK YOU."

Adam pointed.

"I maintain that it was a date."

"Of course you do."

Chris replied.

"I will maintain that position until my grave."

"And Jane will maintain hers."

Andrew added.

"Forever."

"Forever."

Adam agreed.

The table laughed again.

Chris shook his head.

"Okay, so the date wasn't a date."

"It was a date."

Adam corrected immediately.

"Whatever."

Chris waved him off.

"How did you actually get her to be your girlfriend?"

Adam thought about it.

"We talked."

"A lot."

"A concerning amount."

Andrew added.

Chris nodded.


"About what?"

Adam started counting on his fingers.

"I questioned her taste in music."

Immediately:

"Excuse me?"

Everyone looked at Andrew.

Adam pointed directly at him.

"Who voluntarily listens to that much Beach Boys?"

Andrew looked personally attacked.

"My mother loved The Beach Boys."

"She mentioned that. Still questionable."

The table exploded.

Andrew threw a napkin at him.

Adam didn't even flinch.

"What else?" Chris asked.

"I explained that pizza and diet soda are not a major food group."

"Thank you."

Andrew said immediately.

"It was a public service."

Adam replied.

"You sounded ninety."

James added.

"I was trying to protect her cardiovascular system."

"She is thirty."

"It’s never too early to start to eat healthy. Plus she has zero concept of what good food is!”

The table laughed again.

Chris looked between them.

"So your strategy was arguing with her?"

Adam considered it.

Then nodded.

"Mostly."

"And this worked?"

"Shockingly. But honestly, the stuff we argue about, it’s the stuff I love about her."

The laughter got louder.

Then James leaned forward.

"Okay."

A pause.

"Seriously."

"When did you know?"

The table quieted.

Adam looked down at his drink.

Thinking.

Then smiled.

A real smile.

"There was a night."

Andrew glanced up.

Already knowing where this was going.

"I finally got up the nerve to tell her I loved her."

Nobody interrupted.

"And?"

Chris asked.

"I told her I thought she loved me too."

"Damn."

James muttered.

"It felt like a terrible idea at the time."

Adam admitted.

"And then?"

The smile widened.

"She admitted it."

The table immediately reacted.

"No."

Chris said.

"Yeah."

Adam laughed.

"And then we both just sat there."

The table exploded.

"What do you mean you SAT THERE?"

Chris asked.

"For like a few minutes."

"Amazing."

"It was incredibly awkward."

Even Andrew laughed at that.

Then Adam continued.

"And then Jane says maybe it's a good thing we finally got it out in the open."

The table waited.

"Even if we weren't going to do anything about it."

The entire table lost it.

Andrew covered his face.

"That is the most Jane sentence I've ever heard."

"Right?"

Adam said.

"I remember looking at her and thinking...She really would put all these feelings in a box and close the box!"

He shook his head.

Still smiling.

Adam laughed.

"So I just told her that there is no version of this story where I don't do something about it."

"And then?" James asked.

Adam's smile softened.

"Then I kissed her."

For a moment nobody said anything.

Then Chris pointed.

"So…you do in fact love her and she loves you too?”

"Oh, completely."

Adam admitted.

The table laughed again.

"And the baby?" James asked.

The laughter softened.

Adam looked down at his drink.

Then smiled.

"The baby."

A pause.

"I've got absolutely nothing."

Another.

"Complete miracle."

The smile somehow got bigger.

"The best kind."

He shook his head.

"I still don't know how I got this lucky."

The table grew quiet.

Because for the first time all evening, Adam wasn't joking.

He looked genuinely awestruck.

Happy.

Grateful.

The kind of happy that only comes around a few times in a person's life.

Chris looked at him for a long moment.

Then slowly looked over at Andrew.

A grin appeared.

"What?"

Andrew asked.

Chris pointed at Adam.

"That."

A pause.

"That's the Jane Effect."

Andrew rolled his eyes.

"Oh God."

"No seriously."

Chris pointed again.

"The stupid smile."

Another point.

"The complete inability to hide how much he loves her."

Andrew smirked into his drink.

Because unfortunately Chris wasn't wrong.

Chris shook his head.

Still smiling.

"He reminds me of someone."

Andrew immediately threw a napkin at him.

The table exploded.

And Adam laughed so hard his sides hurt.
