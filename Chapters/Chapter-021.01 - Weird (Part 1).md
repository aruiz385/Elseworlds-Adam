# Chapter 21: Weird (Part 1)

The weekly coffee had become a tradition for Jane and Andrew.

Neither of them ever said it out loud.

Neither of them acknowledged how much they both needed it.

They simply kept showing up.

Week after week.

Slowly rebuilding something they had almost lost.


This week found Jane and Andrew sitting outside a small coffee shop a few blocks from her apartment.

The weather was nice.

The coffee was decent.

And for once, neither of them felt like they were navigating a minefield.


"One month."


Andrew groaned immediately.


"Don't remind me."


Jane laughed.


"You are literally the best man."


"Exactly."


"I'm actively involved in the chaos."


A smile appeared.


"James seems suspiciously calm."


Andrew shook his head.


"That's because James was born forty years old."


Jane nearly choked on her coffee.


"That's not fair."


"It absolutely is."


A grin spread across his face.


"The man came out of the womb balancing a checkbook."


That earned another laugh.


The wedding had become everybody's main topic of conversation lately.

Karen had spreadsheets.

Several spreadsheets.

Color-coded spreadsheets.

James somehow knew where everything was despite claiming not to be involved.

Nobody believed him.

Least of all Karen.


They spent a while talking about wedding plans.

Kelly.

Work.

Adam.

Chris being Chris.


Then Andrew got quiet.


Immediately Jane became suspicious.


"What?"


"What?"


Jane pointed at him.


"That."


"What?"


"The look."


"There is no look."


"There is absolutely a look."


Andrew laughed.


Then took a sip of coffee.


For a moment Jane thought maybe she'd imagined it.


Then:


"Guys' night got weird."


Jane immediately groaned.


"Oh no."


Andrew started laughing.


"Oh yes."


"Chris?"


"Chris."


"Of course it was Chris."


Andrew nodded.


"Apparently Adam is glowing."


Jane dropped her head into her hands.


"Oh my God."


Andrew laughed harder.


"That was more or less his reaction too."


"I hate all of you."


"Fair."


The embarrassment lingered.


Then Andrew's expression softened.


"Actually..."


Jane looked up.


"You okay?"


The question caught her off guard.


He wasn't teasing anymore.


He genuinely wanted to know.


Jane looked down into her coffee.


Eventually she sighed.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"I think so."


Andrew waited.


He'd gotten very good at that.

Listening.

Waiting.

Giving her room.


Jane stared out toward the street.

Watching people walk by.


Then quietly:


"It was strange."


Andrew nodded.


That made sense.


Jane smiled weakly.


"I felt like I lost something."


The words surprised her.

Mostly because she'd never actually said them out loud before.


"But..."


She swallowed.


"I gained so much more."


A pause.


"I love him."


Another.


"I trust him."


Another.


"I needed him."


The admission settled softly between them.


Andrew looked away for a moment.


Not because he was uncomfortable.


Because he understood.

Maybe too well.


Jane recognized the look immediately.


That look.


The one that said he remembered feeling exactly the same way once.


Eventually he smiled.


"You didn't lose anything."


Jane looked at him.


"I'm serious."


His voice was firmer than she expected.


"Don't ever forget that."


The certainty in his tone made her throat tighten.


Then his expression softened.


"And for whatever it's worth..."


Jane looked up.


"I'm happy for you."


The words caught her off guard.


Andrew smiled.

A real smile.


"I really am."


A pause.


"I can see how happy you are."


Jane immediately looked away.

Embarrassed.


Andrew laughed softly.


"I'm serious."


Another pause.


"Adam's good for you."


The tenderness in his voice surprised her.


"So good, apparently he's glowing."


That earned an eye roll.


"You're impossible."


"I know."


Then more quietly:


"I want this for you, Jane."


A pause.


"I want you to be happy."


The words hit harder than she expected.

Because she knew they cost him something.

Not jealousy.

Not resentment.

Just accepting that life hadn't gone the way either of them once imagined.


"Thank you."


Andrew nodded.


A comfortable silence settled between them.


Then he cleared his throat.


"Uhm."


Jane immediately narrowed her eyes.


"What?"


"I do have a question."


"Oh no."


"It might make you more uncomfortable than this conversation."


Jane laughed.


"There is literally no way."


Andrew smiled.


"Oh, there is."


Now she was concerned.


"What?"


Andrew rubbed the back of his neck.


"I was talking to Kelly."


Jane froze.


Already terrible.


And somehow she knew exactly where this was going before he even finished.


"Since Karen and James' wedding activities are coming up..."


Andrew hesitated.


"Kelly thought maybe it would be a good idea if—"


"Oh no."


Andrew laughed immediately.


"Oh yes."


"No."


"Jane."


"No."


"We haven't even discussed it."


"A double date?"


Andrew blinked.


"...a double date."


Jane pointed triumphantly.


"I knew it."


Andrew shook his head.

Trying and failing not to laugh.


"Technically nobody used those words."


"What other words are there?"


A pause.


"Four adults voluntarily having dinner together."


Jane stared.


"That's a double date."


"Fair."


"No."


"Come on."


"No."


"Jane."


"No."


Andrew laughed.


"How weird can it really be?"


Jane stared at him.


"Did you just ask me how weird a double date with my ex-boyfriend would be?"


A pause.


"Because the answer is incredibly."


Andrew laughed.


"Adam and I are friends."


"That's not the problem."


"We're also colleagues."


"Still not the problem."


Andrew's smile faded slightly.


Then he nodded.


"Kelly."


Jane groaned.


"Kelly."


Because that was the problem.


Not really Kelly herself.


Kelly was kind.

Always had been.


Thoughtful.

Funny.

Genuine.


Which somehow made it worse.


Kelly was still attached to one of the most painful chapters of Jane's life.

Whether either of them wanted that to be true or not.


Andrew studied her.


"Please think about it."


"No."


"Jane."


"No."


"It would actually be good."


"No."


"It would."


"No."


Andrew sighed dramatically.


"You know Adam and Kelly are going to spend time together during wedding week anyway."


That got her attention.


"What?"


Andrew nodded.


"You and I are going to be busy."


A pause.


"Best man."


Another.


"Maid of honor."


Another.


"There are rehearsals."


"Dinners."


"Photos."


"Karen-induced emergencies."


That finally made Jane laugh.


Andrew pointed immediately.


"See?"


"I hate when you do that."


"What?"


"You make ridiculous things sound reasonable."


"It's a gift."


"It's infuriating."


Andrew looked entirely too pleased with himself.


Then his expression softened.


"Jane."


She looked up.


"I'm not asking because Kelly and I want to torture you."


That earned a reluctant smile.


"I know."


"She likes Adam."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"As a person."


A pause.


"She thinks he's good for you."


The statement caught her completely off guard.


Andrew smiled.


"So do I."


For a moment neither spoke.


Then more quietly:


"I meant what I said earlier."


Jane looked at him.


"What?"


"I'm happy for you."


Another pause.


"Truly."


Jane looked down at her coffee.


"I know."


Because she did.


That was what made hearing it from him hurt and heal at the same time.


Then Andrew ruined the moment.


"So you'll think about it?"


Jane immediately groaned.


"There it is."


Andrew laughed.


"That's not a yes."


"It's not a no."


"That's worse."


"It really isn't."


A grin appeared.


"I'll talk to Adam."


Andrew's smile widened.


And that was when Jane realized she had made a terrible mistake.


Because Adam liked people.

All people.

Especially the weird ones.


And as she sat there looking across at Andrew's infuriatingly satisfied face, she already knew exactly how that conversation was going to end.


Adam was absolutely going to say yes.


And somehow that was the most terrifying part of the entire thing.

Adam grinned.
