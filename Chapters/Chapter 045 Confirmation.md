# Chapter 45: Confirmation


Andrew arrived in twelve minutes.


It was probably some kind of record.


Adam was waiting outside the room when the elevator doors opened.


Andrew didn't run.


Didn't panic.


Didn't ask questions.


He simply walked.


Fast.


Purposeful.


Terrifyingly calm.


Which somehow worried Adam more.


Nurse Hayle was still there.


Still standing near the nurses' station.


Still pale.


She looked up the moment she saw Andrew.


And immediately covered her mouth.


Because suddenly she wasn't looking at one brother.


She was looking at the other.


Andrew stopped outside the room.


His eyes moved to the patient.


Then everything inside him seemed to stop.


Completely.


Nobody spoke.


Nobody moved.


The hallway disappeared.


The hospital disappeared.


There was only Andrew.


And the man lying in the bed.


Finally:


"No."


The word barely escaped him.


Adam looked away.


Because the sound hurt.


Slowly Andrew stepped closer.


One step.


Then another.


And another.


Until he was standing beside the bed.


Looking down.


At his brother.


Or the ghost of him.


Or whatever impossible thing this was.


A long minute passed.


Then another.


Finally Andrew reached out.


His hand trembling.


And touched Kirby's shoulder.


Nothing happened.


The patient remained motionless.


The machines continued their quiet rhythm.


Andrew closed his eyes.


For a second Adam thought he might cry.


Instead he took a shaky breath.


Opened his eyes.


And became a doctor again.


"We verify everything."


His voice was rough.


But steady.


"DNA."


Another.


"Dental records."


Another.


"Fingerprints."


Another.


"Everything."


Adam nodded.


"I know."


Andrew looked at him.


For the first time.


Really looked at him.


"Does Jane know?"


"No."


Relief.


Immediate.


Visible.


"Good."


Another.


"Not yet."


The next twenty-four hours were a blur.


Calls.


Paperwork.


Hospital administrators.


Medical records.


Lawyers.


State databases.


Questions nobody knew how to answer.


The anonymous funding source remained anonymous.


The transfer records raised more questions than they answered.


The only thing everyone agreed on was that none of it made sense.


Scott arrived shortly after midnight.


Looked through the glass.


Then immediately sat down.


Because his knees apparently stopped working.


Adam understood.


He felt something similar.


Scott stared at the patient for a long time.


Then finally looked at Andrew.


"You're sure?"


Andrew didn't answer immediately.


Finally:


"No."


Another.


"But I know my brother."


That was somehow worse.


The DNA sample was expedited.


Nobody wanted to wait.


Nobody wanted to wonder.


Nobody wanted to spend days living inside uncertainty.


Least of all Andrew.


Meanwhile Jane had absolutely no idea any of this was happening.


She texted Adam Wednesday morning.


A picture of Lizzy.


Covered in what appeared to be spit-up.


Your daughter has declared war.


Adam stared at the message.


And nearly broke.


Because suddenly everything felt fragile.


Every normal moment.


Every ordinary conversation.


Every joke.


Every plan.


Jane had no idea her life might be hours away from changing forever.


Tell her I surrender.


Jane immediately responded.


Coward.


The exchange made him smile.


Then immediately feel guilty for smiling.


The waiting became unbearable.


Wednesday became Thursday.


Thursday became Friday.


Three days.


Three impossibly long days.


Nobody slept.


Not really.


Andrew barely left the hospital.


Scott had to physically force him to eat twice.


Nurse Hayle started bringing coffee.


And prayers.


Lots of prayers.


Even she looked exhausted.


By Friday afternoon everyone had reached the same point.


They couldn't do anything else.


They simply had to wait.


Then the call came.


Andrew answered immediately.


The conference room fell silent.


No one breathed.


No one moved.


Adam watched Andrew's face.


Watched the color drain from it.


Watched his hand begin to shake.


Finally Andrew sat down.


Very slowly.


Like somebody had taken all the bones out of his body.


The call ended.


Nobody spoke.


Nobody dared.


Scott looked at him.


Adam looked at him.


Nurse Hayle stopped praying.


The entire room waited.


Andrew stared at the table.


For a long time.


Then finally:


"It's him."


Silence.


Complete silence.


Another shaky breath.


Then:


"The DNA is a match."


Nobody moved.


Because suddenly the impossible had become real.


Kirby Keyger was alive.


Not missing.


Not misidentified.


Not a mistake.


Alive.


Andrew laughed.


A single broken sound.


Then covered his face.


And finally cried.


Not quietly.


Not carefully.


Years of grief.


Years of loss.


Years of believing his brother was gone forever.


All collapsing at once.


Scott moved first.


Crossed the room.


Wrapped his arms around him.


Andrew held on.


Like a little boy.


Like a brother.


Like someone who had just gotten a miracle and didn't know what to do with it.


Adam looked through the glass at Kirby.


The man who had unknowingly become a ghost in all of their lives.


Then he thought about Jane.


About Lizzy.


About the apartment.


About Thrive City.


About late-night feedings.


About all the things she'd rebuilt from the ashes.


And suddenly Adam felt sick.


Because now came the hardest part.


Not the DNA.


Not the confirmation.


Not the impossible miracle.


The telling.


Andrew finally pulled away from Scott.


Wiped at his eyes.


And looked at Adam.


Both of them already knowing what came next.


Neither wanting to say it.


Finally Andrew whispered:


"We have to tell her."


Adam looked through the glass one last time.


Then nodded.


Because they did.


And neither one of them had any idea how.
