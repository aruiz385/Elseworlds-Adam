# Chapter 14: Conflicted

Adam wasn't entirely sure what had just happened.

In fact, the more he thought about it, the less certain he became.

The entire walk back to the oncology floor was spent replaying the afternoon.

The panic attack.

The confession.

The kisses.

Plural.

That last detail was particularly problematic.


What had he done?


Should he have done that?


The questions repeated themselves over and over.

The answers never came.


The truth was simple.

He couldn't help himself.


Jane had been terrified.

Terrified of losing him.

Not Adam.

Him.


The realization alone was enough to leave him reeling.


She cared.

Maybe not in the same way he did.

Maybe not yet.

But she cared.

Enough to panic when she thought he might disappear.

Enough to cry.

Enough to hold onto him like he was the only thing keeping her grounded.


And Adam loved her.


He'd known it for months.

Long before today.

Long before lunch.

Long before the kiss.


At first he'd convinced himself it was curiosity.

Then concern.

Then protectiveness.


Now there was no point pretending anymore.


He loved her.

Entirely.

Hopelessly.

Completely.


The problem was what that love meant.


Because loving Jane made him want something he wasn't supposed to want.


He wanted to stay.


He didn't want Adam to come back.


The thought alone filled him with guilt.


Because Adam deserved to come back.

This was his life.

His friends.

His career.

His future.


And eventually he would return.

The Other Adam knew that.


What he didn't know was how he was supposed to survive when that happened.


So he made a decision.


He would deal with Adam's return when it happened.

Not now.


For now, he would focus on Jane.


Love her while he could.

Make her happy while he could.

Be there for her while he could.


The future could wait.


A few hours later there was a knock at the door of the office where he was finishing charts.


Scott Howard stepped inside.


"Got a minute?"


Adam immediately knew what this was about.


"Sure."


A few moments later he was sitting across from Scott.


"Hi Scott."


"Hey Adam."

Scott studied him carefully.


"How are you today?"


Adam almost laughed.


"I'm okay."

A pause.


"Really."


Another pause.


"I've never been better."


Scott's eyebrow rose.


Neither of them entirely believed the statement.


Silence settled between them briefly.


Then Scott asked the question Adam knew was coming.


"You and Jane have been spending a lot of time together."


Straight to the point.

Typical Scott.


"Do I need to worry about her?"


Adam sighed.


"No."


His answer was immediate.

Certain.


"You don't."


"Scott—"


"You love her."


Adam stopped talking.


Scott continued.


"And you're conflicted."


The honesty of the statement made him laugh bitterly.


"Yeah."


He rubbed a hand across his face.


"That's an understatement."


Scott waited.


"What are you worried about, Adam?"


Where was he supposed to begin?


He let out a long breath.


"Let's see."


His eyes dropped to the floor.


"I've fallen in love with a girl who's still mourning her dead husband and her ex."


Scott remained silent.


"I work with her ex."


A pause.


"Whom I like."


Another.


"Sometimes I wish I disliked him."


Scott almost smiled.


Adam wasn't finished.


"I'm afraid I'll disappear when Adam comes back."


The words felt heavier spoken aloud.


"And then I'll hurt her."


His voice dropped.


"I'll become another person she has to mourn."


Scott's expression softened.


"I don't want to hurt her."


A pause.


"I love her."


For a moment the room became painfully quiet.


"I feel like a coward because I haven't told her."


His jaw tightened.


"I'm scared she doesn't feel the same way."


Another bitter laugh.


"So conflicted doesn't even begin to explain how I feel."


Scott leaned back in his chair.

Thinking.


Then he surprised him.


"Do you really think Jane doesn't feel the same way?"


Adam looked up.


Scott held his gaze.


"Think about the last few months."


And so he did.


The lunches.

The conversations.

The arguments.

The eye rolls.

The tears.

The music.

The movies.

The hand squeezes.


The kisses.


Plural.


And suddenly he remembered something very important.


She kissed him back.


Every single time.


Scott watched the realization settle across his face.

Then nodded.


"If you want to understand Jane..."


He glanced through the office window.


"...you may want to talk to the person who knows her best."


Adam followed his gaze.


Andrew.


Standing outside filling out a chart.


The sight immediately made him tense.


"I..."


Scott smiled knowingly.


"I'm not telling you to talk to him about Jane."


Adam relaxed slightly.


"Good."


"I'm saying you should talk to him."


A pause.


"I've noticed you've been avoiding him."


Adam looked away.


"I feel guilty."


The confession surprised even him.


"I've never really felt guilt before."


Not like this.


"Jane makes me feel things I've never felt before."


Scott nodded.


"I don't know how to talk to Andrew."


The truth sounded ridiculous even to his own ears.


"Adam knew how."


Scott shook his head.


"You'd be surprised how easy talking to Andrew is."


Then he smiled.


"Jane certainly would be."


A pause.


"Everyone needs friends, Adam."


His tone softened.


"That's not a bad thing."


Then Scott leaned forward.


"As for your feelings..."


Adam groaned internally.


Scott ignored him.


"Jane has a habit of causing those."


That actually made Adam laugh.


"Because she cares."


Scott's smile faded.


"She gives so much of herself to other people."


A pause.


"That's why I need to make sure she's okay."


The protective godfather was speaking now.


"I see how much you care about her."


His eyes met Adam's.


"I'm not worried about your intentions."


Adam blinked.


That acknowledgment meant more than Scott probably realized.


"But I need you to make sure something."


"What?"


"When this is over..."

Scott's voice became serious.


"She comes out better."


A pause.


"Not worse."


Adam nodded immediately.


That, more than anything, was what mattered.


"She will."


And he meant it.


A little while later Adam stepped out of Scott's office.


Andrew immediately looked up.


"Hey Adam."


He smiled.


"Haven't talked in a bit."


A pause.


"Everything okay?"


Adam hesitated.

Then remembered Scott's advice.


"Yeah."


A sigh.


"I've just been preoccupied."


Andrew laughed softly.


"I get it."


He glanced around the busy department.


"Things don't exactly get easier around here."


Adam smiled.


"No."


"They really don't."


Andrew looked thoughtful.


"Actually."


Adam immediately knew he wasn't going to like whatever came next.


"I meant to ask you something."


Of course he did.


"Okay."


"I saw you with your girlfriend a few months ago."


Adam nearly choked.


"Andrew."


"What?"


Andrew looked completely innocent.


"She okay?"


Adam sighed.


"She had a panic attack."


True.


"She's been dealing with a lot."


Also true.


"But she's doing better."


Andrew nodded immediately.


"That sounds hard."


His expression became distant.


"My ex used to deal with panic attacks."


Adam listened.


"There were all sorts of triggers."


Andrew looked down briefly.


"Songs."


A pause.


"Random comments."


Another.


"Memories."


Adam felt a strange ache looking at him.


"If you ever need to talk..."


Andrew shrugged.


"I'm around."


The sincerity caught him off guard.


"Thanks."


A moment passed.


"Andrew."


"Yeah?"


Adam took a deep breath.


"There's something I need to tell you."


The conversation that followed was one of the hardest he'd ever had.


By the end of it, Andrew looked stunned.


"So you're telling me..."


He shook his head.


"The night that kid died."


Adam nodded.


"When I came back later..."


Another nod.


"You weren't the same person."


"Correct."


Andrew sat back.

Trying to process it.


"I'm not really sure what to say."


Adam smiled.


"I get that a lot."


Then he explained.

Everything.

The diagnosis.

Scott.

The years of history.


By the end, Andrew simply nodded.


"Honestly?"


Adam braced himself.


"A lot suddenly makes sense."


That wasn't the reaction he'd expected.


"Thanks for telling me."


Andrew offered a hand.


"And seriously."


He squeezed it.


"If you ever need anything."


A pause.


"I'm around."


For the first time that day, some small piece of the weight lifted.


Meanwhile, across town, Jane sat at her desk accomplishing absolutely nothing.


She opened a project plan.

Closed it.

Opened an email.

Forgot to read it.

Started a report.

Stopped halfway through.


Every single thought eventually circled back to the same problem.


The kisses.


Plural.


"Oh my God."


She buried her face in her hands.


"Why did I let him do that?"


The internal spiral began immediately.


"And then he just kept talking like nothing happened."


She groaned.


"Then he did it again."


Another groan.


"And I let him."


Jane stared at her computer screen.


"What am I doing?"


She wasn't supposed to feel this way.


She was supposed to help Adam.

The original Adam.

Her friend.


Not fall for him.


Or his alter.


Or whatever impossible category this situation belonged in.


She considered calling Karen.

Rejected the idea immediately.


Karen would simultaneously panic, meddle, and demand details.


She considered calling Scott.

Rejected that too.


Because that would require explaining the real problem.


And the real problem was terrifyingly simple.


She had feelings for him.


Not Adam.

Not her friend.


Him.


The one she'd never intended to know.


The one she'd never intended to care about.


The one she was rapidly falling for.


And she had absolutely no idea what to do about that.
