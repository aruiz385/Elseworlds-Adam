# Chapter 44: John Doe


Two months had passed since Adam became a father.


He couldn't be happier.


He had Lizzy.


And he had Jane.


Because despite everything...


He knew she loved him.


They were still trying to figure out exactly what they were.


Dr. Cohen certainly hadn't stopped trying.


Neither had Dr. Ryan.


But for the first time in a very long time, Adam wasn't worried about labels.


He had once told Jane that labels be damned.


And he had meant it.


Because he didn't care if they never found the perfect definition.


They were together.


That was enough.


And somehow, despite all the fears that had haunted him for months...


Adam was happy.


Truly happy.


The integration therapy was working.


Every day he remembered a little more.


Some memories hurt.


Some made him laugh.


Some left him staring at the ceiling for hours.


But all of them belonged to him.


For the first time in his life...


He felt whole.


Or at least closer to whole.


Jane had changed too.


Slowly.


Painfully.


Bravely.


She had stopped referring to him as different versions of Adam.


No more:


"My Adam."


Or:


"The other Adam."


Or:


"This Adam."


Now he was simply Adam.


Just Adam.


He knew that hadn't happened easily.


He knew exactly how much work it had taken.


And he loved her for it.


More than she would ever understand.


The fear that she would leave had finally begun to disappear.


Not completely.


Maybe it never would.


But she had spent months proving something to him.


Again.


And again.


And again.


If she intended to leave...


She would have done it already.


So every day they lived life.


Just life.


Messy.


Complicated.


Wonderful life.


Andrew and Adam had become surprisingly close.


Guy's nights had returned.


Something neither of them would have predicted a year ago.


Scott found this development endlessly entertaining.


Which meant nobody was allowed to forget it.


Not ever.


Meanwhile Jane seemed lighter.


More at peace.


For the first time in years she looked less like someone surviving and more like someone living.


Sometimes Adam would wake up in the middle of the night and find her watching him.


She always looked away when she realized he'd caught her.


He never called her out on it.


Mostly because he recognized the look.


The one that appeared when she forgot to be afraid.


The one that made him feel loved.


Those moments became his favorite.


Late-night feedings.


Sleep-deprived conversations.


The tiny apartment they were rapidly outgrowing.


Normal things.


The best things.


Every night he held her.


Every night she curled into him.


As though neither wanted to risk letting go.


Their life wasn't extraordinary.


It was takeout.


Movies.


Text messages.


Hospital shifts.


Grocery lists.


Pediatrician appointments.


The sort of life Jane had once believed she'd never have again.


Yet somehow...


Here it was.


Eventually she even took Adam and Lizzy to Thrive City.


A trip she had postponed for years.


Adam loved every second of it.


Especially when live music started playing on the lawn.


"Jane."


"No."


"Jane."


"No."


"We're dancing."


"We're adults."


"Exactly."


She rolled her eyes.


Then danced with him anyway.


Because it was him.


And there wasn't much she wouldn't do to make him happy.


The feeling was mutual.


Then one Tuesday night everything changed.


Adam was covering admissions.


One of the nearby hospitals had officially closed.


Several long-term patients were being transferred.


Most were routine.


Nothing unusual.


Nothing memorable.


Until John Doe.


Male.


Thirties.


Unknown identity.


Coma patient.


Anonymous funding source.


No known family.


Minimal records.


Strange.


But not unheard of.


Adam picked up the chart.


Read through it.


Then headed toward the room.


Nurse Hayle followed.


One of the oldest nurses in the hospital.


A woman who had been around longer than some departments.


The patient lay motionless in bed.


Adam started his assessment.


Routine.


Vitals.


Medications.


Neurological status.


Nothing remarkable.


Then he finally looked up.


Really looked at him.


And paused.


Something felt familiar.


Not enough to place.


Just enough to linger.


Across the room Nurse Hayle had gone completely still.


Adam looked up.


"Nurse Hayle?"


No response.


Her eyes remained fixed on the patient.


"Oh gracious Lord."


The words barely escaped her mouth.


"Nurse Hayle?"


She slowly made the sign of the cross.


Then took a step backward.


"No."


Another step.


"No."


Adam immediately moved toward her.


"What's wrong?"


She pointed toward the bed.


Her hand visibly shaking.


"Dr. Carter..."


A pause.


"You need to call Dr. Call."


Adam frowned.


"Why?"


Nurse Hayle never looked away from the patient.


Then finally she turned toward him.


"Because if that isn't Kirby Keyger..."


She swallowed hard.


"...then that boy has a twin somewhere."


Everything inside Adam stopped.


Kirby.


Jane's husband.


The photographs.


The stories.


The memories.


The man everyone believed had died years ago.


His eyes snapped back toward the patient.


"Oh my God."


Nurse Hayle nodded.


"You see it too."


Adam couldn't speak.


"Nurse Hayle..."


"It shouldn't be possible."


She agreed immediately.


"But I knew those boys."


Another.


"I worked oncology in Hill Dale before coming here."


Another.


"Dr. Call's mother used to bring both boys in."


Another.


"After she got sick they practically lived there."


Adam stared at her.


"Andrew and Kirby?"


She nodded.


Absolute certainty.


"If that's Kirby Keyger..."


She slowly crossed herself again.


"...Andrew's world is about to fall apart."


A pause.


"And Jane's."


Adam immediately grabbed his phone.


Andrew answered on the third ring.


"Hey Adam."


A pause.


"What's going on?"


Something in Adam's silence immediately put him on alert.


"Adam?"


"You need to come to the hospital."


"What happened?"


"You need to come right now."


"Adam."


Andrew's voice sharpened.


"What happened?"


Adam looked through the glass.


At the unconscious patient.


At Nurse Hayle.


At the impossible face.


"It's Kirby."


Silence.


Complete silence.


Then:


"What?"


The single word was sharp.


Confused.


Dangerously hopeful.


"We got a transfer from the hospital that's closing."


Adam began.


"It's a John Doe."


Another.


"Male."


Another.


"Thirties."


Another.


"Long-term coma patient."


Nothing.


Only breathing.


"Adam..."


Andrew sounded distant now.


"What are you saying?"


"I've only seen Kirby in photographs."


Adam looked through the glass again.


"But Nurse Hayle recognized him immediately."


A pause.


"And honestly..."


His voice lowered.


"So did I."


Nothing.


"Andrew."


Another.


"If this isn't Kirby, I've never seen two people look more alike."


Silence stretched.


Then movement.


A chair scraping.


Footsteps.


Andrew standing.


"Tell me exactly what you're seeing."


"The same jaw."


Another.


"The same face."


Another.


"Andrew..."


His voice almost failed.


"It looks exactly like the pictures in Jane's apartment."


More silence.


Then:


"I'm coming."


Immediately Andrew's voice changed.


Doctor.


Detective.


Focused.


"Listen carefully."


"Okay."


"You do not tell Jane."


Adam nodded immediately.


"I know."


"No."


Andrew's voice cracked.


"I mean it."


A pause.


"We don't know anything."


Another.


"We could be wrong."


Adam closed his eyes.


Because he understood exactly why.


The moment Jane heard:


Kirby might be alive.


There would be no taking it back.


Ever.


"We confirm everything first."


Andrew continued.


"DNA."


Another.


"Dental records."


Another.


"Whatever we need."


Another.


"We do this right."


"I understand."


A shaky breath came from the other side.


Then:


"Call Scott."


Adam nodded.


"I was already going to."


"Good."


Silence followed.


Then for the first time Andrew stopped sounding like a doctor.


Stopped sounding like a detective.


Stopped sounding like a grown man.


He sounded like a little brother.


"Adam?"


"Yeah?"


"If that's really him..."


His voice broke.


"My God..."


The sentence never finished.


Because neither man knew how.


Adam slowly lowered the phone.


Then looked back through the glass.


At the unconscious man lying in the bed.


At Nurse Hayle standing frozen beside him.


At a face that belonged to a dead man.


And for the first time in months...


Adam understood exactly what losing Jane would feel like.


He hated himself for thinking it.


Because this wasn't about him.


Not anymore.


If that really was Kirby...


Jane deserved to know.


No matter what it cost.


Adam stared through the glass.


At the impossible patient.


At the man who should not exist.


And for the first time in a very long time...


Adam felt genuinely afraid.


Because if that really was Kirby Keyger...


Nothing in any of their lives would ever be the same again.
