# Chapter 6: Ride or Die

By the third week, group therapy had settled into something Jane could tolerate.

That was about the highest praise she was willing to give it.

She still hated walking into the room.

She still hated talking about herself.

She still hated that Adam seemed to know exactly when she was thinking about running.

But she kept showing up.

And after each session, they would grab coffee.

It wasn't some grand thing.

It was just coffee.

Two people talking.

One trying to untangle years of grief.

The other trying very hard not to push too hard while still making sure she kept coming back.

It worked.

Mostly.

This particular Thursday, Adam and Jane walked out of group therapy and into the main hospital lobby.

Jane was halfway through explaining why his continued refusal to honor their deal was unethical.

Adam was pretending to take her argument seriously.

"You're stalling."

"I'm not."

"You absolutely are."

"I said I'd show you."

"You said that three weeks ago."

Adam grinned.

"Patience is a virtue."

"I don't have that virtue."

"I know."

That earned an eye roll.

Adam immediately pointed.

"There it is."

Jane groaned.

"One day, I'm going to stop doing that."

"No, you won't."


They stepped into the lobby.

Then everything went sideways.

"JANE!"

Adam looked up.

Jane immediately closed her eyes.

"Oh no."

Before he could ask what that meant, a woman came flying across the lobby with enough energy to power a small city.

A man followed behind her looking resigned to whatever was about to happen.

Adam immediately knew which one was Karen.

Jane sighed.

"Adam, these are Karen Keaton and James St. James."

Then she pointed at Adam.

"Guys, this is my new friend, Dr. Adam Carter. He's helping me with group therapy."

Before Adam could even say hello, Karen stepped forward.

"Ride or Die from age twelve."

She pointed directly at herself.

"I am the one that will kill anyone who tries to hurt her."

Adam blinked.

Then looked at Jane.

Then at Karen.

Then back to Jane.

Something about the interaction immediately made him smile.

He could see it instantly.

The loyalty.

The protectiveness.

The complete inability to filter a single thought before speaking.

Karen reminded him of the other guy in a strange way.

Not because they were similar people.

Because they were both the type who would throw themselves in front of a train for someone they loved without stopping to think about it.

And Jane was clearly someone Karen loved.

Deeply.

James groaned.

Jane looked embarrassed.

Scott Howard, who happened to be nearby, looked entertained.

Karen narrowed her eyes suspiciously.

"So, Dr. Adam."

Adam nodded.

"You're Jane's new therapy friend?"

"Therapy Friend? That is somewhat accurate" Adam replied looking at Jane for a second.

Karen stared.

Adam laughed.

A real laugh.

Then looked directly at Karen.

Just long enough.

Karen completely forgot what she was about to say.

Adam noticed the blush immediately.

So did Jane.

So did James.

So did Scott.

"Oh no," Karen muttered.

"What?" Adam asked innocently.

"Do not do that."

"Do what?"

"That."

"I have absolutely no idea what you're talking about."

Karen pointed at him.

"Oh, he's dangerous."

James immediately sighed.

"No, Karen. You're dangerous."

Scott finally lost the battle and laughed.

Jane wanted to disappear.

The conversation only got worse from there.

"So, Dr. Adam," Karen continued. "You're helping Jane with therapy."

"I am."

"How's she doing?"

Jane's eyes widened.

"KAREN."

"What?"

"You can't ask him that."

Karen looked genuinely confused.

"I literally just did."

Adam tried very hard not to laugh.

Then he looked at Jane.


"You didn't tell me she was this much fun."

Jane looked horrified.

Karen looked delighted.

"See? I like him."

"Please don't."

"Too late."

James looked at Adam.

"I'm so sorry."

Adam smiled.

"I figured this was normal."

"It's really not."

"Yes it is," Karen and Jane said simultaneously.

They looked at each other.

Then frowned.

Adam was beginning to understand exactly how these friendships worked.

And more importantly, he was beginning to understand something about Jane.

For weeks he'd known Therapy Jane.

The grieving version.

The guarded version.

The woman trying desperately to keep herself together.

But standing here with Karen and James, he was glimpsing something different.

Someone lighter.

Someone quicker.

Someone who could give as good as she got.

Somebody whose life wasn't entirely grief.

It made him inexplicably happy.

Eventually Scott rescued everyone.

"Adam helped Jane through her first group session."

Adam nodded.

"She did the hard part."

Jane looked away immediately.

Adam noticed that too.

Then he checked his watch.

"I should probably get back upstairs."

Karen pointed.

"Smart."

"Why?"

"Because I have approximately fifty more questions."

Adam laughed.

"I don't doubt that."

Then he looked at Jane.

"Remember our deal."

Jane narrowed her eyes.

"You still haven't delivered."

"Patience."


"I hate that answer."

"I know."

Then Adam started walking backward toward the elevators.

A strategic retreat.

"See you next week."

"Unfortunately."

"That's your version of friendship, isn't it?"

Jane rolled her eyes.

"There it is again!"

Adam called before disappearing.


The second the elevator doors closed, Karen spun toward Jane.

There was exactly one second of silence.

Then—

"So, Jane."

"No."

"Doctor Adam."

"KAREN."

"That dude is straight out of Grey's Anatomy! Is he even real?"

James immediately looked exhausted.

"KAREN."

"Honey, I love you."

Karen pointed at James.

"But Dr. Adam is hot."

James closed his eyes.

"And did you see the look he shot my way?"

"There was no look."

"There was absolutely a look."

"There wasn't."

Karen ignored her completely.

"The nerve of him."

Scott was openly laughing now.

"I don't recall the last time I blushed like that."

Then Karen rounded on James.

"You need to step up your game."

"I WAS STANDING RIGHT THERE."

"And now you know the competition."

"You're impossible."

"I know."

Then Karen pointed dramatically at Jane.

"And you, missy, better watch out with that one."

Jane groaned.

"What does that even mean?"

"It means I have eyes."

"It means she's incapable of minding her own business," James translated.

Karen ignored him.


But Adam wasn't there to hear the rest.

And maybe that was for the best.

Because if he had been, he probably would have laughed just as hard as everyone else.

The truth was, he already liked Karen.

Not because she embarrassed Jane.

Not because she embarrassed James.

Not because she embarrassed herself.

But because within thirty seconds of meeting her, he'd learned something important.

Jane wasn't alone.

She had people.

People who loved her.

People who fought for her.

People who had clearly spent years trying to keep her together.

And somehow, without meaning to, Karen and James had given Adam the first real glimpse into Jane's world.

A world he was beginning to care about far more than he should.
