# Chapter 18: The Conversation

By noon, Adam had replayed the upcoming conversation in his head so many times that he was exhausted by it.

Not because he thought Andrew would be angry.

If anything, anger would've been easier.

The problem was that Andrew's concerns were probably going to be reasonable.

And reasonable concerns were much harder to argue with.

Especially when Adam shared most of them.

Especially when those concerns had a name.

Jane.

After finishing rounds, Adam found Andrew in the physician lounge.

There was a chart in front of him.

A cup of coffee beside it.

The same cup Adam suspected had been there for hours.

Andrew looked up immediately.

Almost like he'd been expecting him.


"Hey."

"Hey."

Andrew closed the chart.

"So."

A pause.

"I take it this is the conversation."

Adam laughed softly.

"Yeah."

Andrew gestured toward the empty chair across from him.

"Let's get it over with."

Adam sat.

Neither spoke for a moment.

The silence wasn't hostile.

Just heavy.

Finally Andrew folded his arms.

"Look."

Adam waited.

"I want you to understand something before we start."

"I'm listening."

Andrew took a breath.

"Last night wasn't about jealousy."

Adam nodded.

"I figured."

"Good."

Andrew leaned back.

"Because this isn't about me."

A pause.

"It's about Jane."

Adam nodded immediately.

"I know."

Andrew studied him.

"Do you?"

There wasn't hostility in the question.

Only concern.

"Because from where I'm standing, Jane has spent the last several years surviving loss after loss."

Adam stayed quiet.

"Kirby."

A pause.

"The baby."

Another.

"Us."

The last word lingered.

"And now the person she loves is somebody who openly admits he might not always be here."

There it was.

The real conversation.

Not jealousy.

Not possessiveness.

Fear.

For Jane.

Adam looked down.

"I knew that was coming."

Andrew laughed once.

Without humor.

"Yeah."

A long silence followed.

"What exactly is the plan here, Adam?"

"What do you mean?"

"What happens if the original Adam comes back?"

A pause.

"What happens to Jane then?"

Adam rubbed a hand over his face.

"I don't know."

The honesty surprised Andrew.

Adam continued before he could respond.

"You think I haven't asked myself that?"

A bitter laugh escaped him.

"I ask myself that every day."

His eyes dropped to the table.

"I worry about disappearing."

A pause.

"I worry about integration."

Another.

"I worry about becoming another person she has to mourn."

The room became quiet.

For the first time, Andrew really looked at him.

Not as a coworker.

Not as a complication.

Not as the man dating Jane.

Just as someone who was scared.

"You love her."

The statement wasn't a question.

Adam smiled sadly.

"Yeah."

The answer came immediately.

"More than I ever intended to."

Andrew nodded slowly.

"That's what I was afraid of."

Adam frowned.

"What?"

Andrew sighed.

"Because if you didn't love her, this conversation would be easy."

The honesty caught him off guard.

Andrew leaned back in his chair.

"Jane doesn't do halfway."

A faint smile appeared.

"Never has."

Adam smiled despite himself.

"No."

"She loves completely."

A pause.

"Karen."

"Kirby."

Another.

"You."

The statement hung between them.

"And she never really stops."

Adam understood exactly what he meant.

"That's why I'm worried."

Andrew finally admitted it.

"Not because I think you're a bad guy."

A pause.

"Not because I think you're taking advantage of her."

Another.

"But because she finally let somebody in."

His voice softened.

"Do you have any idea how hard that was?"

Adam closed his eyes briefly.

"No."

The answer was honest.

"I don't."

A pause.

"But I know she still thinks she's broken."

Andrew laughed sadly.

"Yeah."

"She still thinks everyone would be better off without her."

Another laugh.

"Yeah."

Adam looked directly at him.

"And she's wrong."

For the first time during the conversation, Andrew smiled.

A real smile.

"Yeah."

"She is."

A silence settled between them.

Not uncomfortable.

Just reflective.

Then Adam asked the question that had been bothering him for months.

"Can I ask you something?"

Andrew nodded.

"Sure."

"Why did you stay away?"

Andrew frowned.

"What do you mean?"

"You lived upstairs."

Adam shrugged.

"You knew she was there."

A pause.

"You obviously still care about her."

Another.

"So why spend an entire year pretending she didn't exist?"

The room became quiet.

Andrew looked away immediately.

The reaction alone answered part of the question.

Finally he sighed.

"Because she asked me to."

Adam blinked.

"What?"

Andrew shook his head.

"Not directly."

A pause.

"But for years she kept telling me the same thing."

Another.

"That I'd be better off without her."

Adam thought immediately of every conversation he'd ever had with Jane.

The guilt.

The grief.

The self-blame.

The certainty that she ruined everything she touched.

Andrew nodded.

"I fought her on it."

A pause.

"For years."

A sad smile appeared.

"Probably longer than I should have."

The room became quiet again.

"The thing is..."

Andrew paused.

Looking for the right words.

"You can't force somebody to want to be with you."

A silence fell between them.

"Especially when they've convinced themselves they can't be what you need."

Adam felt that one.

Because it sounded exactly like Jane.

"I did fight for her."

Andrew looked down.

"I wanted a future with her."

There was no hesitation.

No uncertainty.

"A real one."

Adam listened carefully.

"But even something as simple as defining what we were became difficult."

That caught his attention.

"What do you mean?"

A smile appeared on Andrew's face.

The first genuinely amused smile he'd shown all day.

"Oh God."

He rubbed a hand across his face.

"Okay."

He pointed at Adam.

"You get to call her your girlfriend."

Adam immediately smiled.

"Yeah."

Andrew laughed.

"Progress."

"What?"

"That was a whole thing."

Adam laughed.

"A whole thing?"

"A WHOLE thing."

Andrew shook his head.

"I wanted to call her my girlfriend."

"Seems reasonable."

"THANK YOU."

Andrew pointed immediately.

"Reasonable."

Adam laughed.

"So what was the problem?"

Andrew leaned back.

"The problem was Jane."

That answer somehow made perfect sense.

"According to Jane, if she became my girlfriend..."

He held up one finger.

"Eventually she'd become my fiancée."

Adam groaned

"Oh no."

"Oh yes."

Another finger.

"Then wife."

Another.

"Then mother."

The amusement softened.

"And that terrified her."

Adam's smile faded.

Because now he understood.

"It wasn't about the word."

"No."

Andrew shook his head.

"It was never about the word."

A pause.

"It was about what she thought came after."

Another.

"The future."

Adam thought about the previous night.

About Jane standing in her apartment.

About her smiling when he called her his girlfriend.

About how she hadn't argued.

Not once.

Apparently Andrew noticed the look on his face.

"There."

"What?"

"That's exactly what I'm talking about."

Adam laughed softly.

"Yeah."

"Trust me."

Andrew smiled.

"That would've terrified her a few years ago."

For the first time, Adam realized just how much healing had happened.

Not because Jane loved him more.

Not because she loved Andrew less.

But because she was finally allowing herself to imagine a future again.

Then Andrew's expression grew serious.

"That's why I'm worried."

The amusement disappeared.

Not because Adam disagreed.

Because he understood.

The DID changed everything.

Andrew knew about the original Adam.

The uncertainty.

The possibility that one day things could change.

"I'm not worried because you're a bad guy."

Andrew said quietly.

"I'm worried because Jane finally let somebody in."

A pause.

"And if she loses you too..."

He didn't finish the sentence.

He didn't need to.

Adam nodded slowly.

"I know."

Because it was the same fear that kept him awake at night.


A few moments later neither man noticed the person standing in the doorway.

Jane had come to find Adam after her therapy session with Dr. Ryan.

Notebook in hand.

Mind still spinning.

Then she saw them.

Together.

Talking.

Oh God.

Part of her wanted to turn around immediately.

The old Jane would've.

Instead she stayed.

A moment later Andrew looked up.

And froze.

Jane froze too.

The silence was immediate.

Awkward.

Painful.

More than a year.

More than a year since they had really spoken.

"Hey Andrew."

The words came out stiff.

Uncertain.

Andrew looked equally uncomfortable.

"Hey Jane."

Neither seemed entirely sure what to do next.

Adam sat quietly.

Watching.

For the first time he truly saw it.

Not relationship history.

Life history.

Twenty-five years of it.

Standing awkwardly in a hospital doorway.

Finally Andrew cleared his throat.

"How are you?"

Jane immediately hated the question.

"Good."

The answer sounded ridiculous.

Andrew almost smiled.

"Good?"

Jane laughed nervously.

"Yeah."

A pause.

"Good."

The awkwardness somehow became worse.

Adam was trying very hard not to laugh.

Neither missed it.

"Don't."

Jane pointed at him.

"I'm not."

"You are."

"I absolutely am."

That finally earned a small laugh from Andrew.

The tension eased slightly.

Then Andrew became serious again.

"Listen, Jane."

Immediately she tensed.

Not panic.

Uncertainty.

Andrew noticed.

"I'm not trying to force anything."

A pause.

"And I'm not asking for anything."

Another.

"But if and when you're ready..."

He hesitated.

"I'd like to talk."

Jane looked down.

Thinking.

A year ago she would've run.

Six months ago she would've panicked.

Today she simply took a breath.

Then nodded.

"Okay."

Andrew blinked.

"Okay?"

Jane nodded again.

"I think I'm okay with that."

The surprise on his face was impossible to miss.

Adam smiled.

Proud.

Jane saw it.

And somehow that helped.

"When?" Andrew asked carefully.

Jane thought for a moment.

"Tomorrow?"

Andrew looked genuinely surprised.

"Tomorrow works."

A pause.

"We can walk along the bay."

Somewhere neutral.

Somewhere safe.

Jane nodded.

"Okay."

Then Andrew looked at her.

Really looked at her.

"I care about you."

The words were quiet.

Honest.

Jane looked away briefly.

Then back at him.

"I know."

A pause.

"Thank you."

Andrew nodded.

"I'll see you tomorrow."

"Okay."

Then he left.


Jane watched him disappear down the hallway.

Only after he was gone did she finally breathe.

Adam walked over immediately.

"Well."

Jane groaned.

"Oh no."

"That was progress."

She covered her face.

"I hate that you're right."

Adam laughed.

"You looked at him."

"I did."

"You talked to him."

"I did."

"You didn't panic."

That one stopped her.

Because he was right.

Adam pulled her into a hug.

"I'm proud of you."

Jane rested her forehead against his chest.

Then looked up.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Always."

"Are you okay with me talking to Andrew tomorrow?"

Adam blinked.

Clearly surprised.

"What?"

"The walk."

She looked nervous suddenly.

"I love you."

His expression softened immediately.

"And I don't ever want to make you uncomfortable."

"You think you need my permission?"

Jane shook her head.

"No."


"Good."

He brushed her hair behind her ear.

"Because you absolutely don't."

Jane watched him carefully.

Adam smiled.

"Jane."

A pause.

"You can talk to whoever you want."

Another.

"It doesn't change how I feel about you."

Her shoulders visibly relaxed.

"And honestly?"

"What?"

"I think it's a good thing."

She looked surprised.

"You do?"

"I do."

A pause.

"He's important to you."

No jealousy.

Only understanding.

"And I would never ask you to give up somebody important."

Jane immediately wrapped her arms around him.

Adam held her tightly.

"I love you."

"I know."

He smiled.

"I love you too."

Then he kissed her softly.

And for the first time in a very long time, tomorrow felt terrifying—

but not impossible.
