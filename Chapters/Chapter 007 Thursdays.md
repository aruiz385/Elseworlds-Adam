# Chapter 7: Thursdays

Over the next several weeks, therapy settled into a routine.

Not an easy routine.

Not a pleasant routine.

Just a routine.

For Jane, that alone felt like progress.

For years her life had revolved around survival.

Getting through the day.

Getting through the night.

Ignoring memories.

Avoiding grief.

Pretending she was okay.

Now there was structure.

Every Thursday she met with her therapist.

Every Thursday she attended group therapy.

And afterward, every Thursday, she grabbed coffee with Adam.

The consistency helped.

More than she wanted to admit.


Those afternoons eventually became one of the few parts of her week that didn't feel difficult.

The conversations wandered everywhere.

Movies.

Music.

Books.

Medicine.

Work.

Childhood stories.

Neither one seemed in a hurry to leave after coffee.

Neither seemed particularly bothered by that.


One Thursday, Jane arrived at the hospital and immediately stopped.

Adam wasn't wearing scrubs.

Or a white coat.

Or anything remotely doctor-like.

Instead he was wearing jeans and a hoodie.

His glasses sat slightly crooked on his face as he read something on his phone.

Jane stared for a second.

Then another.

Then another.

Adam looked up.

Immediately catching her.

"What?"

Jane pointed.

"You are not dressed like a doctor today."

Adam looked down at himself.

"Oh."

"Oh?"

"Yeah."

"You own normal clothes?"

Adam laughed.

"Even the best doctors need a day off."

Jane looked suspicious.

"I do have a life outside of this hospital."


"I've never seen any evidence of that."

"Harsh."

"Accurate."

Adam shook his head.

"You're impossible."

"So I've been told."

They began walking toward group therapy.

Comfortably.

Naturally.

As though they'd been doing this much longer than they actually had.

"How are you doing?"

Adam asked.

The question was becoming a regular part of their routine.

Not because he was being polite.

Because he genuinely wanted to know.

Jane thought about it.

"Settling back into my old world, I guess."

There was something sad in her voice.

Adam noticed immediately.

"How's that going?"

Jane let out a long sigh.

"Ah."

Adam nodded.

"That well."

Jane laughed.

"That obvious?"

"A little."

She looked down at her coffee.

"I don't know.

Adam waited.

"It's weird."

"How?"

She searched for the right words.

"For years my entire life revolved around surviving."

The admission surprised even her.

"Then my life revolved around Andrew."

Adam stayed silent.

Listening.

"And now..."

She shrugged.

"Now it's just me."

There was no self-pity in the statement.

Just uncertainty.

"I don't know if that's good or bad."

"It can be both."

Jane looked at him.

"That sounds like something a therapist would say."


"Good thing I'm not a therapist."

"Questionable."

Adam laughed.

For a few moments neither spoke.

Then Adam asked:

"Why don't you talk about that in group?"

Jane groaned immediately.

"There it is."

"What?"

"The therapist answer."

"It was a legitimate suggestion."

"It was therapy homework disguised as a question."

Adam looked offended.

"It absolutely was not."

"It absolutely was."

Their argument lasted all the way into group therapy.

Neither one conceded.

Neither one intended to.

Later, after the session ended, they found themselves back at their coffee shop.

Neither mentioned therapy.

Neither wanted to.

Instead they argued over movies.

Again.

"Honestly," Jane said, stirring her coffee, "sometimes I think my life sounds like the origin story of a super villain."

Adam nearly choked on his drink.

"You really believe you're the villain in your story, don't you?"

Jane immediately shrugged.

"I am."

Adam stared at her.

"No."

Jane laughed bitterly.

"Adam, I technically cheated on my fiancé."

The words came quickly.

Practiced.

As though she'd repeated them to herself a thousand times.

"I failed him after six years of him choosing me every single day."

She stared into her cup.

"Now he's dead."

Her voice softened.

"And I miss him."

Adam didn't interrupt.

"He was my best friend."

For a long moment neither spoke.

Then Adam said quietly:

"I don't think villains spend this much time worrying about who they've hurt."

Jane looked up.

"And I don't think you've given yourself permission to be human."

The statement lingered between them.

Neither knew quite what to do with it.

Eventually the conversation moved on.

As it always did.

Movies.

Music.

Work.

Coffee.

Life.

When they finally stood to leave, Jane realized something.

It wasn't a dramatic realization.

It didn't arrive with fireworks or music.

It was simple.

Thursday had become her favorite day of the week.

Not because of therapy.

Not even because of coffee.

Because for a few hours every week she got to be something other than grieving.

And for the first time in years, that felt nice.

Neither she nor Adam examined that feeling too closely.

Not yet.
