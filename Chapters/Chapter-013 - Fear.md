# Chapter 13: Fear

For weeks, Thursday had become predictable.

Comforting.

Safe.

Group therapy.

Coffee.

Lunch.

A text message.

A sarcastic comment.

An eye roll.

Adam checking on her.

Adam reminding her to drink water.

Adam reminding her to eat.

Adam reminding her to take care of herself.

The routine had quietly worked its way into her life.

So thoroughly, in fact, that Jane hadn't realized how much she depended on it.

Until the day he wasn't there.


Jane arrived early for group therapy.

Like always.

She glanced around the room.

No Adam.


That didn't concern her at first.

Doctors were late.

Doctors were always late.


She settled into her chair.

Pulled out her phone.

Checked the time.


Still no Adam.


The session began.

The empty chair beside her remained empty.


Jane tried to focus.

She really did.

But every few minutes she caught herself looking toward the door.

Waiting.


Nothing.


Her pulse began to climb.


No.

Stop.

He's probably working.

He's probably stuck with a patient.

He's probably—


The thought stopped her cold.


Because suddenly she was back at Thrive City.


Waiting.


Watching the clock.


Checking her phone.


Wondering why he wasn't there.


Wondering if she had done something wrong.


The memory hit hard enough to make her stomach twist.


And then another realization followed.

One she hadn't expected.


The Adam from Thrive City.

The Adam who brought her to therapy.

The Adam who bought her coffee.

The Adam she'd spent four months getting to know.


She hadn't thought about him in months.


Not really.


The realization was so startling that she almost missed the group leader calling her name.


"Jane?"


She blinked.


Everyone was looking at her.


"You okay?"


The honest answer was no.


Not remotely.


Jane took a breath.


"I've been a little on edge."


That was putting it mildly.


"The medication adjustment is rough."


Several people nodded knowingly.


"And..."


She hesitated.


The group leader smiled encouragingly.


"And?"


Jane looked down.


"I met someone."


That got her attention immediately.

Not because she wanted to discuss it.

Because saying it out loud suddenly made it real.


"And I'm not entirely sure how I feel about them."


The room remained quiet.


Jane glanced toward the door again.


Still no Adam.


The panic returned.


"I think it makes me anxious."


No.

That wasn't true.


It terrified her.


Because over the last few months she'd stopped worrying about losing the Adam she originally knew.


Instead she had become afraid of losing the one she'd been spending time with.


The realization landed like a punch to the chest.


She cared.


Far more than she ever intended to.


And suddenly she understood exactly why she was panicking.


It wasn't because Adam missed therapy.


It wasn't because she was worried about him.


It was because she was afraid.


Afraid he would disappear too.


Just like the first Adam had.


By the time group therapy ended, Jane felt like she could barely breathe.


She stood.

Attempted to walk toward the door.


Made it halfway into the hallway before she had to grab onto a chair.


Her vision blurred.


Her chest tightened.


And then a familiar voice cut through everything.


"Jane?"


She looked up.


Adam was running toward her.


Immediately.


No hesitation.


No questions.


Just concern.


"Hey."


His hands found her shoulders instantly.


"Are you okay?"


She couldn't answer.


"Jane?"


His voice became more urgent.


He wrapped his arms around her.


"Look at me."


Slowly she did.


"What is the matter?"


His eyes searched hers.


"What is happening?"


Jane laughed weakly through tears.


That only made him look more worried.


"Hey."


"I guess you no longer believe in group therapy."


Confusion crossed his face.


"What?"


He wiped away her tears.


"I do."


A pause.


"I come all the time."


Then realization dawned.


"I had a shift today."


His expression softened.


"I had to switch my schedule."


Then more quietly:


"Jane."


His hand cupped her face.


"What's going on?"


She looked away.


"I don't know."


Immediately he lifted her chin again.


"You do."


His voice was gentle.

But firm.


"Now tell me."


Jane swallowed hard.


"I thought you would be here."


He frowned.


"When I didn't see you..."


Her voice cracked.


"I panicked."


Understanding hadn't reached him yet.

She could see that.


"I remembered Thrive City."


That got his attention.


"The day I waited for Adam."


Silence.


"The day he didn't show up."


The realization hit him all at once.


His eyes widened.


"Jane."


His voice dropped.


"Are you afraid that—"


He couldn't even finish the sentence.


Because suddenly he understood.


Completely.


And for the first time in months, Adam felt fear.


Not for himself.


For her.


"I don't want to lose you."


The words left her before she could stop them.


Neither spoke for several seconds.


Adam closed his eyes.


Four months earlier she'd begged him to bring Adam back.


Now she was terrified of losing him.


The irony wasn't lost on either of them.


He wrapped his arms around her tighter.


"I'm here."


A pause.


"I'm not going anywhere."


Another.


"I'm not."


His voice shook slightly.


"I'll be here until you don't need me anymore."


Jane buried her face against his chest.


Holding onto him like a lifeline.


Adam stroked her hair gently.


For a few moments they simply stayed that way.


Then he brushed a strand of hair behind her ear.


Looked at her.


And kissed her.


This time she kissed him back.


When they finally pulled apart, he rested his forehead against hers.


"What are you doing for the rest of the day?"


Jane blinked.

Trying to recover.


"I have to go back to work for a couple of hours."


A pause.


"But I'm done at six."


Adam smiled.


"I'm off in a few hours."


His expression brightened.


"Do you want to tackle one of the movies on your list?"


The suggestion immediately made her smile.


"Okay."


A pause.


"I'd like that."


Then:


"Pizza?"


Adam groaned.


"I hate pizza."


Jane stared.


"Who hates pizza?"


"I do."


"No."


"Yes."


"Only you."


Adam sighed dramatically.


"Maybe I just haven't found one I like."


A pause.


"Fine."


He pointed at her.


"Pizza it is."


"Good."


Jane smiled.


"Are you okay with coming to my place?"


Adam's face lit up immediately.


"Sure."


A grin appeared.


"I finally get to see your chaos."


"It is not chaos."


"It is absolutely chaos."


"It isn't."


Adam looked unconvinced.


Jane rolled her eyes.


"Okay."


She laughed.


"I'll text you the address."


A pause.


"Wear comfortable clothes."


His eyebrow rose.


"Oh?"


"If we're doing movie night, we're doing it properly."


Adam smirked.


"So you don't like how I dress?"


Jane immediately panicked.


"What?"


"No."


"I didn't—"


He started laughing.


"Relax."


She narrowed her eyes.


"I'll bring my gym clothes."


Adam looked entirely too pleased with himself.


Jane pointed at him.


"To be continued."


"Okay."


He nodded.


"I'll see you tonight."


Then, because he couldn't help himself:


"Are you sure you're going to be okay?"


Jane smiled.

A genuine smile.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"I'm okay now."


For a moment neither looked away.


Then Adam leaned down.

Kissed her one last time.


"See you tonight."


Jane watched him walk away.


"Uh-huh."


Her hand drifted to her lips.


Eyes wide.


Completely stunned.


And absolutely no idea what had just happened.
