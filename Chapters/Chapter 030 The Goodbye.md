# Chapter 30: The Goodbye

It happened during Jane's seventh month of pregnancy.


Not suddenly.


Not unexpectedly.


In some ways that made it worse.


For months Adam had been fighting it.


Fighting sleep.


Fighting the pull.


Fighting the feeling that grew stronger every day.


But lately there had been no denying it.


Every morning he woke up a little more tired.


Every night it became harder to stay awake.


And now he knew.


Tonight was the night.


Jane arrived at his apartment shortly after dinner carrying the same overnight bag she'd carried back and forth for almost three years.


Adam spotted it immediately.


The worn fabric.


The frayed handle.


The zipper that barely worked.


Then he laughed softly.


"What?"

Jane asked.


"I hate that bag."


Jane smiled.


"I know."


"I really hate that bag."


"You remind me constantly."


Adam shook his head.


And for the first time in years...


He was grateful for it.


Because somehow that terrible overnight bag had become part of their life.


Jane arriving.


Jane staying.


Jane being there.


Tomorrow the bag would leave.


And so would she.


Adam sat beside her.


Taking her hand.


"I think tonight is it."


Jane nodded.


"I know."


The answer surprised him.


"You know?"


"I know."


A silence settled between them.


Not awkward.


Just sad.


Eventually Adam looked over.


"You should leave before he wakes up."


Jane nodded.


"I know."


"I mean it."


A pause.


"I don't want you having to explain everything."


Another.


"I don't want him confused."


Another.


"And I don't want you hurting more than you already are."


Jane squeezed his hand.


"I'll stay until morning."


Another.


"And I'll leave before he wakes up."


Adam smiled sadly.


"Good."


That was the plan.


Later that night they climbed into bed together.


For the last time.


No television.


No movie.


No distractions.


Just the two of them.


Adam wrapped his arms around her.


Holding her tightly.


As if somehow he could stop tomorrow from arriving.


Eventually Jane looked up.


He looked exhausted.


Not sleepy.


Exhausted.


The kind of exhaustion that came from fighting for too long.


His eyes kept drifting closed.


And every time he forced them open again.


Trying to stay.


Trying to hold on.


"Adam."


His eyes met hers.


"I don't want to go."


The words shattered her.


"I know."


A tear slipped down his cheek.


"I don't want to leave you."


Another.


"I don't want to leave Lizzy."


Jane moved closer.


Taking his face in both hands.


Her thumbs brushed away the tears.


"Hey."


His eyes found hers.


"We're going to be okay."


Adam shook his head slightly.


"You promise?"


Jane nodded.


"I promise."


Another.


"Lizzy and I will be okay."


Another.


"You don't have to keep fighting anymore."


His face crumpled.


Because he had been fighting.


Every day.


Every night.


Holding on for her.


Holding on for Lizzy.


Holding on for himself.


Jane kissed his forehead.


"You can let go now."


More tears appeared.


"I love you."


Jane's own eyes filled.


"I know."


"No."


His voice broke.


"I really love you."


Jane kissed him gently.


"I know."


A few moments later he finally fell asleep.


A deep sleep.


The deepest sleep she'd seen him fall into in months.


Jane didn't sleep.


She stayed awake.


Watching him.


Memorizing him.


Every expression.


Every line of his face.


Every detail.


Just in case.


Hours later dawn began creeping through the windows.


Adam never stirred.


Carefully, Jane slid from the bed.


She stood in the bedroom doorway for several moments.


Looking back at him.


Then quietly walked into the living room.


She picked up her overnight bag.


Carried it to the front door.


And set it down exactly where she always did.


Right beside the door.


Ready to leave.


That had been the plan.


Her hand touched the doorknob.


Then stopped.


Because suddenly she couldn't do it.


Original Adam wasn't responsible for any of this.


He hadn't chosen it.


He hadn't asked for it.


And he was about to wake up with three years missing from his life.


Jane closed her eyes.


Then slowly let go of the doorknob.


A few minutes later she sat at the kitchen island.


Two mugs of coffee waiting.


One for her.


One for him.


And she waited.


Meanwhile, in the bedroom...


Original Adam began to wake.


At first nothing seemed wrong.


The room looked exactly the way he remembered.


The same furniture.


The same walls.


The same morning light.


Then he noticed something partially sticking out of the nightstand drawer.


A folded piece of paper.


His stomach tightened.


Slowly he opened the drawer.


The note sat there waiting.


Written in his own handwriting.


Or something close enough to it.


On the outside were two words.


READ ME


Adam unfolded it.


Inside were only a few words.


Open your phone and watch the first video before you do anything else.


Nothing more.


Adam stared at the note.


Then reached for his phone.


The folder was impossible to miss.


Dozens of videos.


The first one labeled:


START HERE


After several moments of hesitation, he pressed play.


The screen flickered.


Then another version of himself appeared.


Older.


More tired.


Sadder.


And somehow happier.


The man on the screen smiled.


"Hey Adam."


A pause.


"Welcome back."


Another.


"It's been three years."


A small laugh escaped him.


"It's been a whirlwind."


His smile softened.


"Like I promised, I left you videos to fill in the gaps."


Another pause.


"But right now I need you to do something for me."


He took a breath.


"When you walk into the kitchen, Jane will be sitting there with coffee."


Original Adam froze.


Jane.


The Adam on the screen smiled knowingly.


"I know her."


A pause.


"And knowing her the way I do, I knew she didn't listen to one damn thing I told her to do."


A small laugh.


"Which was to just walk away."


Another.


"So she will be sitting there waiting for you."


The smile faded slightly.


"I'm going to put this out there now."


A pause.


"She and I have been together for three years."


Original Adam felt the room spin.


The video continued.


"I love her."


Another.


"She loves me."


Another.


"When you watch the rest of the videos, you'll understand."


A pause.


"But I need you to just please be kind to her."


His expression softened.


"She cares about you as a friend."


Another.


"And I know she probably didn't leave because she wanted to make sure you're okay."


The Adam on the screen shook his head.


"Jane is fine."


A pause.


"She's happy."


Another.


"I know you're going to feel anger."


"Guilt."


"And all of those feelings."


Another.


"But direct them at me."


A pause.


"Not her."


His jaw tightened.


"Because none of this is her fault."


Another.


"She won't be able to see you for a while."


Another.


"Because this is just an impossible situation."


Another.


"And when you see her..."


A bittersweet smile appeared.


"You'll understand why."


His eyes dropped briefly.


Then returned to the camera.


"But know that I love her more than my life."


The words landed heavily.


"And if things were different..."


His voice cracked.


"I would never have left her."


Silence.


Then:


"She and I want you to resume your life."


Another.


"Whatever happened during the last three years has nothing to do with you."


Another.


"And she specifically worked really hard to make sure you got a chance to get your life back."


A faint smile returned.


"You're also going to see an overnight bag by the front door."


The Adam on the screen rolled his eyes.


"I hate that bag."


A pause.


"I really hate that bag."


Another.


"But she insisted on packing it and unpacking it every night."


Another.


"In case you ever came back."


Another.


"So you wouldn't get caught off guard by finding her stuff everywhere."


A laugh escaped him.


"She underestimated one thing though."


A grin appeared.


"The beautiful smell of her."


Original Adam instinctively inhaled.


The unfamiliar scent he'd noticed when he woke.


Perfume.


Something floral.


Something unmistakably feminine.


The Adam on the screen smiled.


"I never told her that."


Another.


"Because I wouldn't want her to eradicate and sterilize the room."


A pause.


"She would've."


Another.


"Trust me."


The smile softened.


"She cares about you."


Another.


"She always has."


Another.


"And she wants to make sure you get a chance to pick your own path."


Another.


"Continue your life."


Another.


"And make your own choices."


His eyes filled.


"Separate from the ones I made."


A pause.


"The ones she and I made."


Silence stretched.


Then finally:


"So."


A small smile.


"Go out there."


"Be kind."


"And mostly..."


"Live your life."


Another.


"Know that she's fine."


A pause.


"She probably won't go into details."


Another.


"But she's better off than she was."


He nodded firmly.


"I promise."


Another.


"Even if you don't believe that right now."


A faint smile appeared.


"If you want to know about the Jane of it all..."


A pause.


"Talk to Andrew."


Another.


"He can fill you in too."


Another.


"He'll explain what he can if you want to know."


The smile softened.


Then finally:


"Take care, Adam."


A pause.


"And thank you."


The words lingered.


"When you watch the rest of the videos..."


"...you'll know why."


The screen went black.


The apartment fell silent.


Original Adam sat perfectly still.


His phone remained in his hand.


Then he noticed it.


Coffee.


The smell drifted faintly through the apartment.


Fresh coffee.


And underneath it...


The same scent he'd noticed when he woke.


Jane.


Slowly, Adam looked toward the bedroom door.


He wasn't scared of the apartment.


The apartment was familiar.


What scared him was stepping out of the room.


Because now he knew.


If he opened that door, he would find Jane waiting.


And if there was any doubt left at all...


His eyes drifted toward the crack beneath the bedroom door.


Toward the hallway beyond.


Toward the front entrance.


Where he knew he would find an overnight bag sitting by the door.


Waiting to leave.


Proof that she was really here.


Proof that three years had actually happened.


For several long minutes Adam simply sat there.


Gathering the courage to open the bedroom door and meet the woman waiting on the other side.


It took Adam nearly ten minutes to finally leave the bedroom.


Ten minutes of staring at the door.


Ten minutes of hearing his other self's voice repeating the same words.


Be kind.


Be kind to her.


Be kind to her.


Eventually he forced himself to stand.


Then slowly walked toward the door.


His hand shook as it touched the handle.


The bedroom door opened.


The first thing he saw was the overnight bag.


Exactly where his other self said it would be.


Sitting beside the front door.


Worn.


Frayed.


Obviously used.


Proof.


Proof that someone else had been living here.


Proof that what he'd watched wasn't some elaborate joke.


For a moment he just stared at it.


Then slowly turned his head toward the kitchen.


And saw her.


Jane.


Sitting at the island.


Waiting.


A mug of coffee in front of her.


Another one waiting for him.


His stomach dropped.


Because she was real.


Not a story.


Not a video.


Not a memory.


Real.


Jane immediately looked up.


Their eyes met.


And for the first time in her life Jane felt like an intruder.


The look on Adam's face told her everything.


Fear.


Confusion.


Anger.


Violation.


Like a stranger had been allowed inside his life.


Jane felt her stomach twist.


Immediately regretting staying.


Adam was right.


I should've left.


For several moments neither spoke.


Finally Adam forced himself to move.


His feet carried him toward the island.


Slow.


Careful.


Like he wasn't entirely sure she was real.


He sat across from her.


The island hiding most of her body from view.


Hiding the pregnancy.


Jane had done that intentionally.


Not wanting to overwhelm him any more than he already was.


She gently pushed the coffee toward him.


"Hey."


Her voice sounded smaller than usual.


"Good morning."


Adam stared at the coffee.


Anything except her.


"Hey."


The word barely came out.


Awkward silence followed.


Jane swallowed.


"I know you're confused."


No response.


"I was supposed to leave."


A shaky laugh.


"But I wanted to make sure you were okay."


Still nothing.


The silence felt unbearable.


Jane looked down.


"I know it's going to take a while to catch up."


Another pause.


"But if you ever have questions..."


She wiped at the corner of her eye.


"If there are gaps you need filled in..."


Another.


"You can call me."


Her voice cracked.


"Probably not a great idea for us to see each other for a while."


Another.


"But you can call."


Adam finally cleared his throat.


His eyes remained fixed on the coffee.


"Thanks."


A pause.


"Uh..."


Another.


"Definitely best that we don't really see each other."


Jane visibly flinched.


Adam immediately hated himself.


But the words came out anyway.


"I'll try not to call."


The silence that followed was devastating.


A tear escaped before Jane could stop it.


Immediately she stood up.


Wanting out.


Wanting away from this.


Wanting to undo the last ten minutes.


The movement brought her fully into view.


And Adam froze.


His eyes widened.


For the first time he saw what the island had hidden.


The pregnancy.


Seven months.


His breath caught.


"You're pregnant."


The words barely escaped.


Jane stopped.


Suddenly unable to speak.


Quickly wiping away tears.


Grabbing her bag.


Trying desperately to get out the door.


Then she looked at him.


Really looked at him.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"I am."


Another.


"I'm happy."


Her hand found her stomach.


"My daughter is my world."


A small smile appeared despite everything.


"Lizzy is the best thing that's ever happened to me."


She stopped herself.


Because what she'd almost said was:


Besides Adam.


The wrong Adam.


The one who was gone.


Something hardened inside her.


Not hatred.


Protection.


For herself.


For her daughter.


For the life she'd built.


Adam felt it immediately.


The shift.


The wall.


"After my mom."


He said quietly.


Trying to help.


Trying to fix something.


Trying far too late.


Jane didn't answer.


She just picked up her bag.


Then looked at him one last time.


"Have a great life, Adam."


And then she left.


The apartment door closed.


Leaving him alone.


Adam sat in silence.


Two untouched cups of coffee.


One on each side of the island.


For several minutes he didn't move.


Anger.


Guilt.


Confusion.


Regret.


Every emotion crashed together.


Then finally one thought emerged.


I treated her terribly.


The realization hit immediately.


His other self had asked one thing.


One.


Be kind to her.


And he'd failed.


Completely.


She'd been trying to help.


Trying to make sure he was okay.


Trying to give him answers.


And he'd looked at her like she didn't belong.


Like she was an intruder.


Like she was a threat.


His stomach twisted.


Because Jane had never been those things.


Not to him.


Not ever.


She was his friend.


The girl who used to tell him he was the best part of her week.


And he'd just watched her walk away in tears.


Eventually Adam stood.


Walked back into the bedroom.


And began the process.


The videos.


The life.


The missing years.


As instructed, he opened the top drawer of the dresser.


Moved aside the sweaters.


And found the brown box.


Exactly where it was supposed to be.


His hands trembled as he opened it.


Inside sat a small velvet ring box.


Heart-shaped.


Beautiful.


Painful.


Adam stared at it.


Then closed the lid.


Because suddenly he understood.


He needed to catch up.


And he needed to do it fast.


Meanwhile Jane barely made it to her car before the tears came.


Adam had warned her.


He'd tried to protect her from this.


And she hadn't listened.


Now the last image she would have of Original Adam was someone looking at her like she was trespassing in his life.


Like she didn't belong.


The thought broke her.


Completely.


For several minutes she sat there crying.


Then she felt a kick.


Lizzy.


Immediately Jane took a shaky breath.


Then another.


Because whatever she felt...


Lizzy felt too.


And right now her daughter needed her.


Eventually Jane started the car.


And drove home.


When she pulled into the driveway, someone was already waiting outside.


Andrew.


He took one look at her and immediately knew.


"Hey."


Jane's face crumpled.


Andrew was already moving.


Pulling her into a hug.


"Adam called me last night."


Jane closed her eyes.


Of course he did.


"He said you were going to need support today."


That finally broke her.


And as she cried into Andrew's shoulder, Jane couldn't help smiling through the tears.


Because even now...


Even gone...


Her Adam was still taking care of her.


An hour later Karen and James called.


Then Maria.


Then Chris.


Kelly called from Cambridge.


Checking on Jane.


Checking on Lizzy.


Making sure she wasn't alone.


And Jane realized something.


She missed him.


God, she missed him.


But somehow he was still everywhere.


In the nursery.


In the plans he'd made.


In the friends he'd called.


In the people he'd prepared.


In the life he'd helped build.


And for the first time all day, Jane thought maybe he had known exactly what he was doing.


Because even though he was gone...


She wasn't alone.
