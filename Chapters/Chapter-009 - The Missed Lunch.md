# Chapter 9: The Missed Lunch

Jane McFly knew she was in trouble the moment she started looking forward to lunch.

Not because lunch was inherently exciting.

It wasn't.

It was lunch.

With a friend.

A friend she'd met through therapy.

A friend she'd spent the last four months talking to far more than she ever intended.

That was all.

So there was absolutely no reason she should be checking the crowd every few minutes.

The music at Thrive City started.

Adam still wasn't there.

Jane checked her phone.

Nothing.

No text.

No call.

No explanation.

She looked at the sandwiches she'd picked up.

Then at the empty chair across from her.

Then back at her phone.

An hour passed.


The music played on.

Adam never showed.

Jane let out a long breath.

At first she was disappointed.

Then worried.

Then something worse.

Guilty.

Because sitting there alone, she realized something she hadn't wanted to admit.

Adam wasn't a regular person.

Not because he wasn't kind.

Or funny.

Or dependable.

Because every day of his life required effort most people never had to think about.

He had told her that.

Repeatedly.

He actively managed triggers.

He went to therapy.

He constructed his life carefully.

Purposefully.

And what had she done?

Invited him to lunch.

Outside the safety of their normal routine.

Outside therapy.

Outside coffee.

Outside the structure they had built.

Maybe it was too much.

Maybe yesterday had been too much.

The three-hour conversation.

The stories.

The emotions.

The obvious fact that she'd practically admitted he had become one of the highlights of her week.

Jane closed her eyes.

"Oh God."

She felt sick.

Because suddenly she couldn't stop thinking about Andrew.

Not Andrew specifically.

The pattern.

For years she'd leaned on Andrew whenever she was drowning.

Whenever grief became too much.

Whenever life was unbearable.

Then she'd met Adam.

And somewhere along the way she'd started leaning on him too.

Not in the same way.

Not intentionally.

But maybe enough.

Maybe she'd done exactly what she'd always done.

Found another person to hold her together when she should have been learning to stand on her own

A slow instrumental melody drifted across the plaza.

Jane instantly recognized it.

*I've Had the Time of My Life.*

Her breath caught.

Kirby.

Of all songs.

Of all moments.

Jane lowered her head and cried.

Not dramatically.

Not loudly.

Just quietly.

When the song ended, she wiped her face.

Then took a long breath.

No.

She wasn't doing this again.

If she was serious about getting better, then she needed to actually get better.

Not keep finding people to carry her.

Dr. Ryan had mentioned medication before.

Jane had refused.

Immediately.

Every time.

Maybe it was time to stop refusing.

A short while later she left Thrive City and headed back toward UCSF.


By the time she reached the psychiatric floor she was emotionally exhausted.

Still embarrassed.

Still hurt.

Still frustrated with herself.

She spotted Scott almost immediately.

"Hey Uncle Scott."

Scott looked up.

The second he saw her face his expression changed.

"Sweetheart, are you okay?"

That was all it took.

Jane stepped into the hug.

"No."

The answer came instantly.

"No, I'm not."

Scott held her tighter.

"Today is definitely not a good day."

He waited.

"I'm here to make an appointment with Dr. Ryan."

Scott looked surprised.

"Really?"

Jane nodded.

"We talked about medication before."

A pause.

"I think I may be ready to consider it."

Scott's expression softened immediately.

"There is no shame in medication, Jane."

"I know."

Then Scott hesitated.

"There was actually something I wanted to ask you."

Jane frowned.

"Sure."

Scott studied her for a moment.

"Have you talked to Adam in the last few days?"

The question immediately tightened something in her chest.

"Yeah."

Scott nodded.

"When?"

"Yesterday."

Another pause.

"And today?"

Jane looked down.

"We were supposed to meet for lunch."

Something shifted in Scott's expression.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

"He didn't show up."

Scott exhaled.

Jane's stomach dropped.

"What happened?"

Scott was quiet.

Then finally said:

"Adam relapsed last night."

Jane closed her eyes.

The tears started again immediately.

"We were actually supposed to meet for lunch today."

Her voice shook.

"I hope this isn't my fault."

Scott blinked.

"Jane."

She looked at him.

"I talk too much."

The words spilled out now.

"I asked him to lunch."

She wiped her face.

"I told him I looked forward to hanging out with him."

Another tear slipped down her cheek.

"Uncle Scott, I broke him."

Scott immediately shook his head.

"Jane."

His voice was firm.

"No."

She looked unconvinced.

"No."

Scott repeated.

"I seriously doubt that inviting Adam to lunch and telling him you enjoy spending time with him broke him."

Jane laughed weakly through the tears.

Scott guided her toward a chair.

"Tell me exactly what happened yesterday."

So she did.

Coffee.

The conversation.

The stories.

The invitation to Thrive City.

Everything.

Scott listened carefully.

Asking occasional questions.

Making mental notes.

When she finished, he nodded thoughtfully.

"I was asking because I wanted to make sure we weren't missing something."

Jane frowned.

"What do you mean?"

Scott sighed.

"I know what happened last night."

A pause.

"I'm just making sure there wasn't anything else."

Jane nodded slowly.

Then quietly asked:

"Is he okay?"

Scott considered the question.

"Yes and no."

That answer hurt.

But she appreciated the honesty.

"He traded shifts with Andrew today."

Jane looked up briefly.

"Whatever happened last night triggered his other personality to take over."

Scott's voice remained calm.

Matter-of-fact.

"But his other self knows what to do."

Jane stared at the floor.

Then finally asked the question she'd been trying not to ask.

"Can I see him?"

Scott looked at her carefully.

"You want to?"

"I don't know."

She laughed weakly.

"I do."

A pause.

"But I don't know if I'll make things worse."

Scott shook his head.

"Jane."

She looked up.

"You're his friend."

The words landed heavily.

Friend.

Scott smiled softly.

"It might help."

Jane thought about every time Adam had shown up for her.

Every coffee.

Every conversation.

Every time he'd sat beside her and listened.

Then she nodded.

"I want to see him."

Scott stood.

"Okay."

Then, because he knew exactly what she was worried about, he added:

"Andrew's at the precinct today."

Jane visibly relaxed.

Scott smiled.

"Come on."

And for the first time since Adam failed to appear at Thrive City, Jane felt something besides guilt.

Hope.

Moments later Scott led her toward the oncology floor.

Toward the man she'd spent four months getting to know.

Only to discover that the person waiting for her wasn't Adam.

Not exactly.

It was the Other Guy.
