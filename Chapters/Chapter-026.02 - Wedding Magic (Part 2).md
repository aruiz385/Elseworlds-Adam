# Chapter 26: Wedding Magic (Part 2)

While the men occupied the dock, the women had claimed a large firepit overlooking the lake.


Well.

Three women and a very judgmental bottle of sparkling cider.


Jane sat curled into one of the Adirondack chairs, her hands wrapped around the glass.


From across the lawn she could just make out Adam, Andrew, James, and Chris standing together at the end of the dock.


"They're having feelings."

Karen announced.


Kelly immediately laughed.


"They are not."


"They absolutely are."


Jane smiled into her drink.


"Chris is there."


"Good point."

Kelly admitted.


"Feelings are unlikely."


The three women laughed.


Karen stretched dramatically.


Then raised her glass.


"Before the men ruin this moment."


"Oh no."

Jane muttered.


"Oh yes."

Karen replied.


"I would like to make a toast."


Kelly groaned.


"There it is."


Karen ignored her.


"To me."


The table erupted.


Jane nearly spilled her drink.


"Karen!"


"What?"


"I deserve this."


"Fair."

Kelly admitted.


Karen nodded.


"Thank you."


Then she stood.

Actually stood.


"I would like to congratulate myself for being the most beautiful bride this inn has ever seen."


"Sit down."

Jane laughed.


"I refuse."


"And."

Karen continued.


"The most organized bride."


Kelly looked horrified.


"That part is unfortunately true."


"It is."

Jane agreed immediately.


Karen beamed.


"And most importantly..."


Her smile softened.


"...for finally getting my happy ending."


That quieted the group.


Just a little.


Because they all knew.


Fifteen years.


Fifteen years of James.


Fifteen years of growing up together.


Fifteen years of waiting for this weekend.


Jane raised her glass.


"To Karen."


Another pause.


"The girl who was so obsessed with James in high school that I literally created an entire yearbook category."


Karen's eyes widened.


"Oh no."


"Oh yes."

Jane replied.


Kelly immediately leaned forward.


"What category?"


Jane looked entirely too pleased with herself.


"The Couple Most Likely To Still Be Together In Ten Years."


Karen burst out laughing.


"No."


"Yes."


"You actually did that?"


"It won."


The group immediately lost it.


Kelly wiped tears from her eyes.


"That is adorable."


"I was not subtle."

Karen admitted.


"No."

James' voice called from somewhere across the lawn.


"You weren't."


The women laughed harder.


Karen raised her glass again.


"Fine."


A pause.


"To James."


Another.


"For finally catching up to me."


The laughter resumed.


Then Kelly raised her glass.


"My turn."


The group settled.


Kelly smiled toward Jane.


"To Jane."


"Oh no."


"Oh yes."


Kelly pointed.


"To our new mom."


Jane immediately groaned.


"I hate this."


"Too bad."


Kelly continued.


"And to the newest member of the friend group."


Her eyes drifted briefly toward Jane's stomach.


The smile widened.


"We are all ridiculously excited."


Another.


"And I cannot wait to meet this baby."


Jane's eyes immediately softened.


"Thank you."


Karen reached over and squeezed her hand.


"That kid is so screwed."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"Look at us."

Karen said.


"There is no chance this child grows up normal."


Kelly laughed.


"None."


"Absolutely none."


The women dissolved into laughter.


When it settled, Jane looked toward Karen.


Then smiled.


"I love you."


Karen's expression immediately softened.


"I love you too."


A pause.


"I'm really lucky."

Jane continued.


"To have you."


Another.


"You're my best friend."


Another.


"My ride or die."


Karen's eyes immediately filled.


"Stop."


"No."


"Stop."


"No."


Karen pointed at her.


"I am the bride."


"And?"


"You are not allowed to make me cry before tomorrow."


The women laughed.


Kelly watched the exchange.


Then shook her head.


"You two are ridiculous."


"We know."

Jane and Karen answered simultaneously.


That only made Kelly laugh harder.


Then Karen suddenly pointed.


"Get over here."


"What?"


Before Kelly could react, both women grabbed her.


Immediately pulling her into a group hug.


"Karen!"


"No."


"You're part of this now."


Kelly immediately froze.


Caught off guard.


Jane smiled.


"You are."


A pause.


"And I'm really glad we're trying this."


Kelly looked at her.


"Trying what?"


"Being friends."


The answer surprised her.


Jane smiled softly.


"I mean it."


Another.


"And thank you."


Kelly frowned.


"For what?"


"The bathroom."


Immediately understanding appeared.


The night of the double date.


The panic attack.


The conversation in the restaurant bathroom.


Kelly squeezed her hand.


"I meant everything I said."


A pause.


"And I'm glad we're friends too."


That earned a genuine smile.


Before anyone could ruin the moment, another voice appeared.


"There you all are."


Maria.


Chris's wife approached carrying a tray of drinks.


"Oh good."

Karen said.


"More witnesses."


Maria rolled her eyes.


Then immediately raised her glass.


"To Karen."


The group laughed.


"There it is."

Jane muttered.


Maria pointed dramatically.


"Only fifteen years."


The women exploded.


"And only fifteen years to plan the wedding of the century."


Karen looked delighted.


"I like this toast."


"Of course you do."

Kelly replied.


Maria grinned.


"And thank you for helping me survive my own wedding to Chris two years ago."


A pause.


"Which frankly should qualify you for sainthood."


The laughter doubled.


Karen accepted this praise without hesitation.


"As it should."


Maria then looked toward Jane.


"And to Jane."


Her smile softened.


"Our newest addition."


Jane laughed.


"You make me sound like a rescue puppy."


"A little."

Maria admitted.


"And to the baby."


The women raised their glasses again.


Then Maria turned toward Kelly.


A mischievous smile appearing.


"And you."


Kelly immediately looked suspicious.


"Oh no."


"Oh yes."


Maria pointed.


"Ms. Hotshot Lawyer."


The group laughed.


Maria winked.


"Here's hoping for good things from Andrew."


Kelly immediately turned bright red.


The laughter was instant.


"MARIA."


"What?"


"I hate all of you."


"No you don't."

Karen replied.


As the laughter settled, Jane found herself looking toward the dock.


Toward the four men standing by the water.


James.

Chris.

Andrew.


And Adam.


They were laughing.


Talking.


Looking for all the world like they had known each other forever.


And for a moment Jane simply watched.


Adam fit there.


Perfectly.


Like he'd always belonged.


Like somehow the universe had carved out a place for him among all of them.


Her friends.

Her family.

Her people.


The sight warmed her chest.


This.


The lake.

The wedding.

Karen getting her happy ending.


Andrew and Kelly.

Chris and Maria.


Adam.


Their baby.


Everything.


For one perfect moment it felt complete.


Perfect.


Jane smiled into her glass.


She wished she could freeze the weekend exactly as it was.


Keep it forever.


Because sitting there beneath the lights of the inn, surrounded by the people she loved most in the world, she couldn't imagine anything better than this.


And for tonight, that was enough.
