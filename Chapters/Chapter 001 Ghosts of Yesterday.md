# Chapter 1: Ghosts of Yesterday

Jane McFly had become an expert at surviving.

Not living.

Not healing.

Just surviving.

Most days, she rose from the couch she slept on, went to work, smiled when people expected her to smile, and returned home to an apartment that felt more like a museum than a place anyone actually lived. Every room held memories. Every corner held ghosts.

The ghost of Kirby.

The ghost of a baby she never got to meet.

And the ghost of Andrew Call—the man she had loved almost her entire life.

The world saw Jane as resilient. Her friends often described her as relentless, strong, the person who somehow always found a way forward.

They were wrong.

Because strength implied choice.

Jane hadn't chosen any of this.

She was simply still standing because nobody had taught her how to stop.


Long before grief became her constant companion, there had been three children running through California sunshine.

Jane.

Andrew.

Kirby.

They had grown up together so completely that for years it felt impossible to think of one without the others.

Andrew Call had been Jane's first everything.

Her first best friend.

Her first kiss.

Her first love.

At ten years old, after Andrew lost his mother to cancer, the two of them sat together under a tree in silence, neither quite understanding how to process grief.

Jane remembered the way he'd cried.

She remembered taking his hand.

She remembered kissing his cheek because she didn't know what else to do.

Years later, Andrew would joke that she'd technically stolen his first kiss.

Jane always rolled her eyes and argued otherwise.

It didn't matter.

The memory belonged to both of them.

By high school they weren't simply friends anymore.

They were inseparable.

The kind of couple everyone assumed would eventually get married.

The kind of love people wrote songs about.

They spent four years together.

Four years of football games, late-night phone calls, shared dreams, and promises neither of them understood would someday be tested.

Then college came.

Andrew left for Massachusetts to pursue pre-med at UMASS.

Jane stayed in California.

At first they tried.

Long-distance calls.

Weekend visits.

Promises that distance wouldn't matter.

Eventually Andrew made the decision for both of them.

He insisted it would be better.

Cleaner.

Easier.

Jane had hated him for it.

More accurately, she had hated that a part of her understood.

They broke up.

The first fracture in a life that would eventually shatter.


Not long afterward, Kirby stepped into the space Andrew left behind.

Kirby Keyger.

Andrew's brother for all intents and purposes.

Jane's childhood friend.

The boy who always knew how to make people laugh.

Bright where Andrew was serious.

Warm where Andrew was intense.

Steady in all the ways Jane desperately needed.

When Jane was nineteen, she began dating Kirby.

At first it seemed like exactly what everyone wanted.

Their families approved.

Their friends approved.

Even Andrew, from thousands of miles away, approved.

At least that's what everyone assumed.

Then came the summer disaster.

The breakup.

Kirby sleeping with Kelly Healy.

The heartbreak.

The reconciliation.

And somehow, despite all of it, Jane and Kirby found their way back to each other.

Life moved forward.

Or pretended to.


Then Andrew came home.

Not really home.

But close enough.

Before junior year, Andrew transferred to UCLA.

The same school Jane attended.

The same school Kirby attended.

Suddenly Andrew wasn't a memory anymore.

He wasn't a voice on the phone.

He wasn't a chapter she'd managed to close.

He was real again.

Walking the same campus.

Breathing the same air.

Existing.

And that simple fact nearly destroyed her.

Because Jane discovered something she had spent years trying not to admit.

She wasn't over him.

Not even close.


For four years they barely spoke.

The silence became its own kind of relationship.

Painful.

Necessary.

Impossible.

Andrew dated Kelly Healy.

Jane stayed with Kirby.

Everyone tried moving forward.

No one was particularly successful.

Then Andrew came back into their orbit for good.

He balanced medical school ambitions while becoming a police officer.

Kirby went to law school.

Jane built a career.

Their friend group settled into adulthood.

The future began to take shape.

And then Kirby proposed.

Jane said yes.

Because she loved him.

Because she genuinely believed she could build a life with him.

Because loving Andrew and loving Kirby had never been as mutually exclusive as people assumed.

That was part of the problem.


When Kirby asked Andrew to be his best man, Jane felt her world tilt.

Andrew was coming back.

Not temporarily.

Not occasionally.

Permanently.

The first year of the engagement was brutal.

She and Andrew fought constantly.

Every conversation became an argument.

Every argument concealed years of unfinished feelings.

Then something changed.

By the second year they became friends again.

At first for Kirby's sake.

Then because they genuinely missed each other.

That was the dangerous part.

Neither of them realized how much damage friendship would do.

Because friendship brought comfort.

Comfort brought honesty.

Honesty brought closeness.

And closeness brought everything they had spent years trying to avoid.


One year before the wedding, Kirby mentioned New York.

A future.

A plan.

A life far away from California.

Far away from the only home Jane had ever known.

She smiled when he talked about it.

Then she panicked.

Every fear she had buried surfaced.

Was she becoming the person Kirby wanted her to be?

Or was she slowly losing herself?

Questions became anxiety.

Anxiety became dependence.

Dependence led her directly to Andrew.

For an entire year before the wedding, she leaned on him more than she leaned on anyone else.

Even Kirby.

Especially Kirby.

And both of them knew exactly how dangerous that was.


Forty-eight hours before she was scheduled to become Jane Keyger, everything finally broke.

The beach.

The ocean.

The history between them.

Years of unresolved love.

Years of grief.

Years of wanting.

It all collided.

And Jane slept with Andrew.

The man she should never have touched.

The man she still loved.

The man who wasn't the person she was about to marry.

They regretted it immediately.

Not because it meant nothing.

But because it meant everything.

And they agreed never to tell anyone.

Especially Kirby.

Especially him.

Andrew wanted to fight.

Wanted to stop her from making what he believed was a mistake.

But in the end he made the same choice he had always made.

He put Jane first.

Even if it meant losing her.

Even if it broke him.


Two days later she married Kirby.

She stood at the altar.

Spoke the vows.

Became his wife.

For twenty-four hours.

Exactly twenty-four hours.

That night, as she and Kirby prepared to leave for New York and begin their new life together, Andrew helped carry luggage.

Then a gunshot shattered everything.

The bullet wasn't meant for Kirby.

It was meant for Andrew.

Retaliation for a narcotics investigation Andrew had worked.

Wrong place.

Wrong moment.

Wrong brother.

Kirby died.

And the world stopped.


Jane never stopped wondering if fate had gotten confused.

If somehow she had stolen someone else's future.

If her betrayal had cursed them all.

The guilt arrived first.

Then grief.

Then despair.

And together they built a prison she could not escape.

For weeks she barely ate.

Barely slept.

Barely existed.

Andrew tried everything.

Nothing worked.

So he called Karen and James.

Together they created what became known as Jane Watch.

A rotating schedule.

Someone checking on her.

Someone making sure she ate.

Someone making sure she survived.

Because surviving had become the best she could manage.

And the most terrifying part?

Jane wasn't sure she wanted even that.
