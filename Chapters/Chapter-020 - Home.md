# Chapter 20: Home

It happened gradually.

Then all at once.

At some point Adam stopped asking if Jane was staying over.

At some point Jane stopped pretending she wasn't.

And at some point they both realized they spent most of their free time at Adam's apartment.

Not because they'd planned it.

Not because they'd talked about it.

It had simply happened.

The way most important things seemed to happen with them.


Jane still owned her apartment.

Still paid the mortgage every month.

Still loved the place she'd worked so hard to buy.

But lately it had become harder to be there.

Not because she didn't want to be.

Because the upstairs hallway still existed.

The two bedrooms still existed.

The memories still existed.

Some days were easier than others.

Some days she could walk by those doors without a second thought.

On other days she couldn't.

Adam never pushed.

Never asked.

Never suggested she should be handling it differently.

He simply accepted where she was.

And because of that, most evenings ended the same way.

Jane arrived carrying the same overnight bag she'd been carrying for months.

Dropped it by the couch.

Took off her shoes.

And immediately acted like she belonged there.


"What's for dinner?"


Adam didn't look up from the stove.


"Vegetables."


Jane groaned.


"You hate me."


"Incorrect."


A pause.


"I care about your cardiovascular system."


"I'm thirty-five."


"Exactly."


Jane threw a kitchen towel at him.


Adam caught it without looking.


Which somehow made her more annoyed.


Movie nights continued.

Adam still insisted science fiction contained romance.

Jane still insisted he required professional intervention.

The arguments became part of the routine.

Almost as important as the movies themselves.


The one thing they agreed on was pizza.

Well.

Mostly.


Jane agreed on pizza.

Adam tolerated pizza.


Once a week they sat down with a movie and ordered one.

And eventually Adam made the mistake of suggesting they make their own.


The first sign of trouble came when Jane accidentally got flour on his shirt.


She froze.


Adam looked down.

Then looked back up.


"Oh."


"What?"


The look on his face immediately made her laugh.


"You chose violence."


"Oh please."


Three seconds later Adam retaliated.


"Adam!"


"What?"


Now there was flour on her cheek.


Five minutes later the kitchen looked like a disaster zone.


Flour covered the floor.

The counters.

The cabinets.

The island.


At one point Adam discovered flour in his hair.


At another point Jane somehow managed to get it on the ceiling.


Neither could explain that one.


"How?"


"I don't know."


"That concerns me."


"You worry too much."


The pizza burned.


The movie never started.


Instead they ordered takeout and ended up laughing on the kitchen floor until their stomachs hurt.


Those nights happened more often than not.

Movie nights increasingly failed to involve movies.

Sometimes they made it halfway through before one conversation led to another.

Sometimes they gave up entirely and ended up curled together in Adam's bedroom talking until two in the morning.

About work.

About friends.

About nothing.

About everything.

The movie quietly forgotten somewhere in the background.

Neither caring enough to restart it.


For all intents and purposes, they were building a life together.

Which was exactly why Adam eventually started noticing something.


Every morning Jane packed her overnight bag.

Every single morning.

Without fail.


Toothbrush.

Charger.

Medication.

Makeup.

Hairbrush.

Pajamas.

Everything.


By the time she left for work there wasn't a single trace she'd been there.


No book on the coffee table.

No sweatshirt.

No shampoo in the bathroom.

No evidence whatsoever.


At first Adam ignored it.

Then he couldn't stop noticing it.


A few months later, during boys' night, Chris made the problem worse.


"The Boyfriend has arrived."


Adam immediately regretted coming.


"Oh no."


"Oh yes."


Chris looked entirely too pleased with himself.


Across the table James sighed.

The kind of sigh that usually meant Chris was about to become a problem.


Andrew laughed.


"Leave him alone."


"Absolutely not."


Chris narrowed his eyes.


"Something's different."


Adam froze.


James immediately closed his eyes.


"No."


"What?"

Chris asked.


"No."

James repeated.


"Whatever thought just entered your head."


A pause.


"Keep it there."


Chris looked offended.


"I'm being observant."


"You're being dangerous."


Andrew laughed.


Chris ignored both of them.

Then pointed dramatically at Adam.


"You look happy."


Adam wished for death.


Immediate death.


"Oh my God."


"No seriously."


Chris leaned forward.


"The man is glowing."


James nearly choked on his drink.


Andrew finally understood where this was headed.

And immediately wished he didn't.


For one brief moment all he could think was:

Jane finally got there.


The thought hurt more than he expected.


Not because he wasn't happy for her.


He was.


God, he was.


But some small part of him still grieved the life he'd imagined once.


Adam saw it.


Andrew saw him see it.


Neither said anything.


Neither had to.


After that Adam mysteriously became unavailable for boys' night for several weeks.


Nobody believed the work excuse.

Especially Andrew.


Life settled back into routine.

Dinner.

Work.

Movie nights.

Pizza.

Flour disasters.

Late-night conversations.


And that same overnight bag.


One evening after dinner Adam finally asked.


"Can I ask you something?"


Jane immediately narrowed her eyes.


"No."


Adam laughed.


"You don't even know what it is."


"Experience says no."


He smiled.

Then nodded toward the overnight bag near the door.


"The bag."


Jane followed his gaze.


"What about it?"


Adam leaned back.


"Why do you still pack it every day?"


She blinked.


"What?"


"Jane."


A pause.


"You spend five nights a week here."


"That's an exaggeration."


"It really isn't."


"You counted?"


"I absolutely counted."


Jane groaned.


"That's weird."


Adam ignored her.


"You know where everything is."


Another pause.


"You know where I keep the coffee."


"You hide the good coffee."


"Because you're irresponsible."


She pointed.


"See? That's rude."


Adam laughed.

Then became serious again.


"You don't leave anything."


Jane froze.


"What?"


"No clothes."


A pause.


"No books."


Another.


"No toothbrush."


His voice softened.


"It's like you're making sure nobody could tell you were ever here."


The statement hit harder than she expected.

Because it was true.


"It's Adam."


Immediately he understood.


Not him.


The other Adam.


The original Adam.


The one sitting quietly in the center of everything.


Jane looked down.


"He was my friend."


The words came quietly.


"And I care about him."


Adam's chest tightened.


Of course she did.


"What if he comes back one day?"


A pause.


"What if he opens a drawer and it's full of my things?"


Another.


"What if he feels trapped?"


Another.


"What if he feels like I took over his life?"


She finally met his eyes.


"Please understand."


A pause.


"I'm okay with the way things are."


Another.


"This isn't because I love you less."


Adam shook his head immediately.


"I know."


And he did.

He'd never doubted it.

Not once.


Jane sighed.


"I'm trying to be considerate."


Another.


"I don't want to bombard somebody else's life with my stuff."


Adam rubbed a hand across his face.


"Jane."


"What?"


"It doesn't work like that."


She laughed weakly.


"You keep saying that."


"Because it's true."


A silence settled between them.


Then Adam looked at her.

Really looked at her.


"He cared about you too."


Jane immediately rolled her eyes.


"Not like you do."


"You don't know that."


"Adam."


"I'm serious."


A pause.


"He was excited about Thrive City."


She blinked.


"What?"


"The day before everything happened."


A pause.


"He was looking forward to seeing you."


Another.


"He talked about it."


Jane shook her head immediately.


"No."


A laugh escaped her.


"He friend-zoned me the second he met me. He called me his therapy friend…friend!."


Adam barked out a laugh.


"You are unbelievable."


"I am right."


"You are impossible."


She pointed at him.


"Then I saw the Laney picture."


Adam groaned.


"Oh no."


"Oh yes."


"It all made sense."


"Jane."


"I'm just saying."


A grin appeared.


"I never stood a chance."


Adam laughed despite himself.


"Laney is married."


"So?"


"Very married."


A pause.


"Four children."


Jane stared.


"Four?"


"Four."


Adam shook his head.


"And for the record, she struggled with one Adam."


A grin tugged at his mouth.


"Two would've killed her."


That finally made Jane laugh.

A real laugh.


Eventually the laughter faded.


Then she grew quiet.


"I've been reading."


Adam immediately became suspicious.


"Oh no."


"DID articles."


His expression softened.


"I'm trying to understand."


A pause.


"Trying to get ready."


Another.


"For integration."


The room became quiet.


"I don't know what's going to happen."


A pause.


"And that's terrifying."


Another.


"What if you come back and you're different?"


Adam reached across the table and took her hand.

Immediately.


"You have me right now."


Her eyes filled.


Because that was what he always said.


No promises.

No guarantees.


Just truth.


"You have me right now."


Jane laughed through the tears.


"You keep saying that."


Adam smiled.


"Because it's true."


A pause.


Then he pointed toward the overnight bag.


"And for the record?"


"What?"


"I'm still winning."


Jane immediately groaned.


"Adam."


"The bag."


A grin appeared.


"One day."


"No."


"One day I'm going to open that bathroom drawer and there will be a toothbrush in it."


"No."


"A hairbrush."


"No."


"A sweatshirt."


"Absolutely not."


Adam laughed.


"Jane."


She pointed at him.


"No."


"You spend more time here than I do."


"Still no."


Adam shook his head.


"You are impossible."


"So I've been told."


The smile lingered for a moment.

Then slowly faded.


Jane looked down at her hands.


"Adam."


The seriousness in her voice immediately got his attention.


"Yeah?"


"I need you to understand something."


The smile disappeared completely.


"Okay."


Jane took a breath.


"I'm not waiting to leave."


Adam looked at her quietly.


Because that had always been the fear underneath the conversation.


"I'm not keeping the bag packed because I'm getting ready to go."


A pause.


"I'm here because I want to be here."


Another.


"Every night."


Another.


"Every movie."


The corner of her mouth lifted slightly.


"Even the terrible science fiction ones."


That earned the smallest smile from him.


Jane continued.


"I just..."


She struggled for a moment.

Trying to explain something that lived more in feelings than words.


"I hope you understand."


A pause.


"I care about him."


Neither needed to clarify who.


The other Adam.


The original Adam.


The friend she'd met first.


The friend who never got the chance to come to Thrive City.


The friend who never got the chance to see what happened next.


"I know."


Jane nodded.


"I don't want him waking up one day and feeling like he missed his own life."


The room fell quiet.


Adam looked at her for a long moment.


Then sighed.

Not frustrated.

Not angry.

Just resigned.


Because he understood.


He didn't agree.

But he understood.


And understanding Jane had always mattered more than winning.


Finally he reached across the table and squeezed her hand.


"Okay."


Jane blinked.


"Okay?"


Adam nodded.


"Okay."


A smile tugged at his mouth.


"I still think you're wrong."


That made her laugh.


"Adam."


"You asked me to understand."


A pause.


"I do."


Another.


"I understand."


His thumb brushed lightly across her hand.


"I just don't agree."


A smile finally appeared.


"That's fair."


"Good."


A pause.


"But for the record..."


Jane groaned immediately.


"No."


"The drawer offer remains open indefinitely."


"Adam."


"I am a very patient man."


"You're impossible."


"Yet strangely handsome."


That earned a rolling of eyes so dramatic it made him laugh.


The conversation moved on after that.


The overnight bag remained by the door.

Exactly where it had been every night for months.


Adam never brought up the drawer again.

Not because he'd given up.


Because he understood now.


This wasn't about commitment.

It wasn't about love.

It wasn't about whether Jane saw a future with him.


It was about hope.

And fear.

And a man they both loved in different ways.


So he let it be.

For now.


The bag stayed.

And Jane stayed too.


For the moment, that was enough.
