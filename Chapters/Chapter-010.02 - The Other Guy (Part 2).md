# Chapter 10: The Other Guy (Part 2)

Jane wasn't sure what to say.

For the first time since meeting him, the Other Adam looked almost uncomfortable.

The intensity remained, but something softer slipped through the cracks.

He reached for her hands carefully.

"Listen, I am not trying to scare you. I am not."

His grip was warm.

Steady.

"I do feel for you. I am sorry that Adam didn't show up today."

Jane looked away.

The disappointment from Thrive City came rushing back.

The empty chair.

The music.

Waiting.

Wondering if she had somehow ruined everything.

The Other Adam sighed.

"I could have shown up in his place, but I know you would have seen it right away, and it would have scared you."

A faint smile touched his mouth.

"Seeing you in person is weird. I know it probably is for you too."

Jane let out a humorless laugh.

Weird wasn't even close.

"I wish I could bring Adam back," he continued quietly. "But it's not that easy. It really isn't."

For the first time, she believed he was genuinely trying to be kind.

This wasn't mockery.

This wasn't amusement.

He was trying.

Even if he wasn't very good at it.

"This is weird," Jane admitted.

Her voice was small.

"Not the way I expected to meet you."

She swallowed.

"Actually, I never expected to meet you at all."

The Other Adam stayed quiet.

Listening.

"I didn't mean to imply that you're not a person. It wasn't my intention at all."

Her eyes dropped to their joined hands.

"I just don't know how to handle this."

The truth hurt.

"I just want my friend back."

The Other Adam's expression softened.

"He told you about the videos..."

Jane nodded.

The messages.

The recordings.

The arrangement between them.

The evidence that Adam had survived this before.

The evidence that there had always been another person there.

"I know you miss him."

His voice was gentler than she expected.

"But it's going to take a bit of time."

Time.

She hated that word.

Because people always said it when they couldn't promise anything else.

The Other Adam looked away briefly.

Then back at her.

"I'm going to regret this, truly..."

A pause.

"But if you want, we can try to be friends."

Jane blinked.

"What?"

He shrugged.

"Maybe help you understand better if Adam ever decides to come back."

The word hit her immediately.

"If?"

He immediately realized what she'd latched onto.

"It's not only up to me."

Jane stared at him.

Realizing all over again how little she actually understood.

The tears started again.

"I can't do this again."

The words tumbled out before she could stop them.

The grief she'd been carrying all day finally broke free.

"I already lost two people I love and a baby that I would've loved so much."

Her voice cracked.

"I can't lose someone else."

The Other Adam moved before she could react.

His arms wrapped around her.

Careful.

Protective.

Not forcing.

Just there.

"I'm sorry."

His voice was low.

Earnest.

"I know you've lost a lot."

Jane pressed her eyes shut.

"Nothing about this is fair."

For a moment neither moved.

"You haven't lost Adam."

He said it firmly.

Again.

"You haven't."

A pause.

"It's just going to take time."

Time.

That word again.

"I can try to help you if you'd let me."

His voice softened further.

"I'm not him."

The admission seemed to cost him something.

"But I can try."

Jane pulled away.

Not because he had done anything wrong.

Because everything about this felt wrong.

Because he looked like Adam.

Sounded like Adam.

Remembered things Adam remembered.

Yet wasn't Adam.

The entire situation made her chest hurt.

She looked at him.

Really looked at him.

And suddenly the irony hit her.

Twice now.

Twice in her life.

She lost the bright one.

And was left with the brooding one.

First Kirby.

Then Andrew.

Now Adam.

Or some version of Adam.

She couldn't survive that pattern again.

"I can't accept your help."

The words came quietly but firmly.

"I have to do this on my own."

The Other Adam nodded.

Not arguing.

Just listening.

"But I am not heartless enough not to want to get to know you."

His eyebrows rose slightly.

Jane sighed.

"I just can't continue to rely on people to avoid dealing with my own stuff."

A pause.

"But maybe we can grab a cup of coffee one of these days."

The Other Adam immediately smirked.

"Like you did with Adam?"

Jane pointed a finger at him.

"No."

"Not like I did with Adam."

The distinction mattered.

It mattered a lot.

"We knew each other. It took months."

The Other Adam considered that.

"You know I know everything that Adam knows about you."

The statement landed like an admission.

Or maybe a confession.

"When I care, I pay attention."

He tried to hide the smile.

Failed.

Jane rolled her eyes instantly.

The smile widened.

Apparently he recognized those too.

"I don't know anything about you, really."

"Fair."

He accepted that one without argument.

Jane folded her arms.

"You know it's weird, right?"

"Probably."

"No, really weird."

The Other Adam looked amused already.

"Are you just peeking into someone's conversations and their consciousness whenever you like?"

Jane shook her head.

"You just decide what you want to be in on and what you don't?"

Now she sounded genuinely annoyed.

And exposed.

The Other Adam laughed.

Actually laughed.

"That's not how it works."

"Sure."

"It isn't."

"Sure."

His smile only grew.

"Do you even have a name?"

The question surprised him.

"I'm still Adam."

A pause.

"Just not the one you've been hanging out with for the last four months, Jane."

They stared at each other.

The answer somehow made perfect sense.

And none at all.

"Fine."

Jane sighed.

"Let's say I get to know you."

The Other Adam looked curious.

"Let's say I even grow to like you."

That clearly caught him off guard.

"What happens then?"

For the first time all afternoon, he genuinely didn't have an answer.

Jane could tell.

He looked away.

Thinking.

Which somehow made him seem more human.

Finally he shrugged.

"I honestly don't know, Jane."

The honesty surprised her.

"I really don't."

A longer pause.

"I have not been around long enough to know what would happen then."

Then the sarcasm returned.

Like armor.

"Have you ever heard of taking things one day at a time?"

Jane immediately shook her head.

"Yes."

Then frowned.

"And every time that happens, it never ends well."

The Other Adam winced.

Fair point.

"So you're asking me to try to be friends with you until Adam decides to make his reappearance..."

She looked directly at him.

"...and then when I care about you, you go away?"

The question hit harder than she intended.

Because suddenly he realized something.

Jane already assumed she would care.

Not might.

Would.

He saw the heart behind the question.

And it affected him.

"Okay."

He exhaled.

"You're right."

A reluctant smile appeared.

"It's not fair."

The honesty stung.

"So maybe the right thing to do is walk away."

He stood.

Gathering his chart.

"I don't blame you if you do."

Jane's stomach tightened unexpectedly.

"I am glad I got to meet you face to face."

That surprised her too.

"I am around if you change your mind."

Then he pointed toward the nurses' station.

"I actually have patients to look after."

A small smirk.

"Yes. I am still a doctor."

Jane rolled her eyes immediately.

The Other Adam laughed.

"I got Adam through med school."

He started walking away.

"So everything he knows, I know before you ask."

Jane should have left.

Anyone with common sense would've left.

Instead she stood there watching him.

Then—

"Okay."

The Other Adam stopped.

Turned.

"What?"

For the first time all afternoon he looked hopeful.

Jane sighed.

Already regretting this.

"Lunch."

His eyebrows rose.

"Lunch?"

"I have questions."

"A shocking development."

"And you have answers."

She paused.

"...sorta."

Now he was definitely smiling.

"We can try having lunch."

Another pause.

"It's not coffee."

The distinction mattered.

The Other Adam understood immediately.

"Good."

His answer came too quickly.

"I'm more of a tea guy anyway."

A beat.

"Coffee is a bit overrated."

Jane groaned.

Of course he would say that.

"Please do not make me regret this."

A pause.

"Though I am already starting to."

Another eye roll.

The biggest yet.

"And you are going to show up, correct?"

This time his amusement faded.

He understood exactly why she asked.

Thrive City.

The empty chair.

The waiting.

"Lunch tomorrow."

His voice was steady.

"Off hospital campus."

A pause.

"To avoid your ex."

Jane pointed at him.

"Don't."

"You got it."

Then:

"I will be there."

And before she could stop him—

"It's a date."

Jane's eyes widened.

Immediately.

"Regretting this already."

She was already pulling out her phone.

"There is a lunch place overlooking the water."

The Other Adam looked entirely too pleased with himself.

"I'm texting you the details."

She started walking away.

"See you tomorrow."

A beat.

"Goodbye."

She gave him the biggest eye roll of the day.

The Other Adam watched her go.

Then looked back down at his chart.

Despite himself—

He laughed.
