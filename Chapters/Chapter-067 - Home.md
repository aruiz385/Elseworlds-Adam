# Chapter 67: Home


One month.


One month since Kirby opened his eyes.


One month since the impossible happened.


And somehow...


Life was beginning to settle into something resembling a routine.


Kirby had been moved out of the ICU nearly two weeks earlier.


Now he had his own room in the rehabilitation unit.


He was walking.


Slowly.


But independently most of the time.


Physical therapy had become part of his daily routine.


Occupational therapy too.


And at Adam's recommendation, Scott had arranged for him to begin seeing one of the hospital therapists.


The progress was real.


Visible.


Hard-earned.


The kind everyone had been hoping for.


And because Kirby was improving...


The conversations were changing too.


Andrew had started helping.


Carefully.


Thoughtfully.


Giving Kirby pieces of the last eight years.


Not all at once.


Never all at once.


Just enough.


Stories about medical school.


Stories about Karen and James.


Stories about their father.


Stories about himself.


Stories about life after the accident.


Little pieces.


Building a bridge.


Helping Kirby understand the world he'd woken up into.


All while protecting him from the parts he wasn't ready for.


Unknown to Jane.


Who was too busy trying to keep everything together.


That afternoon she sat beside Kirby's bed.


Her tablet balanced on her lap.


The same cup of coffee she'd been holding for nearly an hour sat untouched beside her.


Kirby watched her yawn.


Then yawn again.


Then try to hide the third one.


He sighed.


"Jane."


She looked up immediately.


"What?"


"Go home."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"Go home."


Another.


"For the night."


She laughed.


Obviously assuming he was joking.


Kirby wasn't.


"I'm serious."


"No."


Another.


"I'm staying."


Kirby rolled his eyes.


A familiar gesture that immediately made her smile.


"You've practically lived at this hospital for a month."


"I'm fine."


"You are the worst liar I've ever met."


That earned a reluctant laugh.


Kirby sat back.


"I'll be okay."


Another.


"I promise."


Another.


"I'll see you tomorrow."


Jane hesitated.


Kirby softened.


"Jane."


Another.


"I want you to sleep in your own bed."


The words landed harder than they should have.


Because she couldn't remember the last time she'd done that.


Really done that.


Not worrying.


Not waiting for a phone call.


Not sleeping in a chair.


Just sleeping.


Kirby looked at her carefully.


"You need to take care of yourself."


Another.


"I know you're worried about me."


Another.


"But I'm worried about you."


Jane looked away.


Because suddenly her eyes were burning.


And she hated crying.


Especially in front of him.


This.


This was why she'd loved Kirby so much.


Not because she'd been in love with him.


Not anymore.


But because this was who he was.


The guy who always noticed.


The guy who remembered to make her eat.


The guy who somehow knew when she was exhausted before she realized it herself.


The guy who took care of people.


Especially her.


She smiled.


"You sound old."


Kirby laughed.


"I earned it."


Another.


"I was asleep for eight years."


Another.


"That's practically retirement."


Jane laughed despite herself.


Then finally nodded.


"Okay."


Kirby's face brightened immediately.


"Good."


Another.


"See you tomorrow."


Jane stood.


Collected her things.


Then kissed the top of his head.


A habit from another lifetime.


"Goodnight, Kirby."


His smile softened.


"Goodnight, Jane."


Another.


"And seriously."


Another.


"Take care of yourself."


This time she didn't argue.


She simply nodded.


Because maybe...


He was right.


Home


By the time Jane found Adam, the hospital was starting to quiet down.


His office door was open.


He sat behind his desk finishing notes.


Glasses on.


Tie loosened.


Looking exhausted.


The second he saw her, he smiled.


"There she is."


Jane leaned against the doorway.


"Kirby kicked me out."


Adam laughed.


"Finally."


Another.


"It only took a month."


Jane rolled her eyes.


"He said I need sleep."


Adam stood.


Crossed the room.


And wrapped his arms around her.


Holding her tightly.


Neither moved.


Neither wanted to.


After a moment he kissed the top of her head.


"You ready to go home?"


Jane smiled.


"More than ready."


Lizzy


The apartment was quiet when they arrived.


Safe.


Comfortably quiet.


The kind of quiet that felt like home.


After dinner.


After baths.


After pajamas.


Jane carried Lizzy into her nursery.


The little girl was already fighting sleep.


Her eyelids getting heavier by the second.


Jane settled into the rocking chair.


Holding her close.


For a moment she simply sat there.


Memorizing her.


The weight of her.


The warmth of her.


The tiny sleepy sounds she made.


One month.


One month of missed bedtimes.


One month of rushed visits.


One month of trying to squeeze motherhood into hospital hallways.


Jane kissed her daughter's forehead.


Then began singing softly.


The same lullaby she'd sung since Lizzy was born.


The same one she'd sung during midnight feedings.


The same one she'd sung whenever one of them needed comfort.


By the second verse, Lizzy was asleep.


Completely.


Jane carefully placed her in the crib.


Then stood there for another minute.


Watching.


Just watching.


Feeling something she hadn't felt in weeks.


Peace.


When she finally stepped into the hallway, Adam was waiting.


Exactly where she'd expected him to be.


Jane smiled.


"I missed that."


Adam nodded.


"I know."


Another.


"So did she."


Jane walked directly into his arms.


And for the first time all day...


Nobody needed anything from her.


Us


Later they sat together on the couch.


The television was on.


Neither of them were watching it.


Adam's arm was around her shoulders.


Jane's head rested against his chest.


The familiar rhythm of his heartbeat made her feel calmer than anything else had in weeks.


For a long time neither spoke.


Just enjoying the quiet.


The normalcy.


Eventually Jane sighed.


"You okay?"


Adam laughed softly.


That earned a look.


"What?"


Adam shook his head.


"I forgot how nice this is."


Another.


"Having you home."


Jane's expression softened immediately.


"Oh."


Adam nodded.


"Yeah."


Another.


"I know why you've been there."


Another.


"And I wouldn't change it."


Another.


"But God, I've missed you."


Jane closed her eyes.


Because she'd missed him too.


More than she'd admitted.


Adam rested his cheek against the top of her head.


"The hospital wasn't the hard part."


Another.


"I got to see you during the day."


Another.


"I got lunches."


Another.


"I got coffee breaks."


Another.


"I got to steal twenty minutes here and there."


His voice softened.


"The nights were awful."


That made Jane look up.


Adam smiled.


"I don't sleep well without you."


Another.


"I never have."


Jane laughed quietly.


"You survived."


"Barely."


Another.


"Do you know how much room is in that bed when you're not there?"


That earned a genuine laugh.


Adam looked pleased with himself.


For a moment silence settled between them again.


Comfortable.


Familiar.


The kind that only existed between people who knew each other completely.


Jane traced lazy circles across his chest.


"I'm sorry."


Adam immediately shook his head.


"No."


Another.


"Don't do that."


Another.


"You have absolutely nothing to apologize for."


Jane looked away.


But Adam gently tipped her chin back toward him.


"You were helping someone you love."


Another.


"That's one of the things I love about you."


Her eyes immediately filled.


Because lately she'd felt pulled apart.


Between the past.


And the present.


Between obligation.


And love.


Adam seemed to understand.


He always did.


"We're okay."


Another.


"You and me."


Another.


"We're really okay."


Jane smiled.


Then leaned forward and kissed him.


Slowly.


Not because she needed reassurance.


But because for the first time in weeks she finally could.


When she pulled back, Adam looked entirely too happy.


Which made her laugh.


"There he is."


Adam grinned.


"Your fiancée has returned."


Jane rolled her eyes.


"My fiancée has been trapped in a hospital."


"Details."


Another.


"I have her back now."


The warmth in his voice made her chest ache.


In the best possible way.


A little while later they finally turned off the lights.


The apartment was quiet.


Peaceful.


For the first time in weeks.


Jane paused beside the bed.


Just looking at him.


Adam smiled.


"What?"


Jane shook her head.


"Nothing."


Another.


"I just missed you."


The expression on his face immediately softened.


"I missed you too."


Neither moved away.


Not yet.


Four weeks.


Four weeks of hospital rooms.


Four weeks of quick lunches.


Four weeks of stolen conversations.


Four weeks of saying goodbye every night.


Of reaching for each other and finding empty space instead.


Adam gently brushed a strand of hair behind her ear.


And suddenly neither of them wanted to talk anymore.


Not because there was nothing left to say.


But because some things didn't need words.


Jane smiled.


Then reached for him.


And Adam met her halfway.


The rest of the world could wait until morning.


For tonight...


They chose each other.


The bedroom door quietly clicked shut behind them.


And for the first time in four weeks...


Neither of them was alone.


Morning


Jane woke slowly.


Warm.


Comfortable.


Happy.


The feeling was so unfamiliar after the last month that it took her a moment to recognize it.


Beside her, Adam slept peacefully.


One arm wrapped around her waist.


Still holding her.


Even in his sleep.


Jane smiled.


Last night had felt like exhaling.


A month of fear.


A month of stress.


A month of carrying everyone else.


And then one night where she got to simply be herself again.


Not a caretaker.


Not a crisis manager.


Not someone carrying impossible secrets.


Just Jane.


Adam's fiancée.


Lizzy's mom.


A woman deeply in love.


The closeness had helped.


More than she'd realized it would.


Being held.


Being loved.


Being home.


Being with the man she'd built her life with.


The father of her daughter.


The man she planned to spend forever with.


She felt lighter.


So much lighter.


As if a month of tension had finally melted away.


Adam stirred.


Pulled her closer.


Still asleep.


Making her laugh quietly.


Apparently unconscious Adam was clingy too.


Breakfast


The apartment smelled like coffee.


Lizzy happily attacked her breakfast.


Adam was staring at her.


Again.


Jane narrowed her eyes.


"What?"


Adam grinned.


"Oh nothing."


Another.


"You just have a glow."


Jane groaned immediately.


"No."


"Oh yes."


Another.


"People are absolutely going to notice."


Jane laughed.


"They are not."


Adam stood.


Wrapped his arms around her waist.


And pulled her gently against him.


"Oh, they absolutely are."


Another.


"Scott will notice."


Another.


"Andrew will definitely notice."


Another.


"Nurse Hayle is going to notice."


Another.


"Half the rehab floor is going to notice."


Jane buried her face in his shoulder.


Laughing.


"You are impossible."


Adam kissed her forehead.


"Maybe."


Another.


"But you look happy."


This time she didn't argue.


Because she did.


Today would be busy.


A tour of Bright Horizons daycare.


A visit with Kirby.


Dr. Ryan.


Dr. Cohen.


And whatever new chaos the hospital decided to throw at them.


But for the first time in a month...


Jane felt ready.


Because last night she hadn't just come home.


She'd remembered who she was.


And that changed everything.
