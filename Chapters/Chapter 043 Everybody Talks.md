# Chapter 43: Everybody Talks


Jane was still annoyed.


Not at Adam.


Not at Lizzy.


Not even at herself.


At Dr. Cohen.


Which, unfortunately, meant she spent most of the week thinking about him.


Because apparently that was how therapists won.


The more she tried not to think about what he had said...


The more she thought about it.


Adam noticed almost immediately.


Mostly because she had been glaring at her coffee for nearly five minutes.


"You've been staring at that mug for a while."


Jane looked up.


"I'm thinking."


"That's usually dangerous."


She pointed at him.


"This is your fault."


Adam blinked.


"My fault?"


"Yes."


Another.


"If you hadn't gotten integrated, I wouldn't have met Dr. Cohen."


Adam laughed.


"I don't think that's how cause and effect works."


Jane narrowed her eyes.


He wisely retreated.


Temporarily.


A few days later she found herself sitting across from Dr. Ryan.


Alone.


No Adam.


No Lizzy.


Just therapy.


Which somehow felt more dangerous.


Dr. Ryan looked down at the notebook resting in Jane's lap.


Then back up at her.


"You actually did the homework."


Jane sighed.


"I hate that you're surprised."


"I'm not surprised."


Another.


"I'm amused."


Jane rolled her eyes.


"Same difference."


Dr. Ryan smiled.


Then opened the notebook.


Several pages had been filled out.


Some neatly.


Some less neatly.


One page appeared to have been written after midnight.


Dr. Ryan had a fairly good guess which one.


She turned a few pages.


Then stopped.


"Let's talk about this one."


Jane immediately knew which entry she meant.


Of course she did.


She had spent entirely too much time staring at that particular page.


Dr. Ryan turned the notebook around.


Adam watching me feed Lizzy in the middle of the night.


Why does this scare me?


Dr. Ryan looked up.


"Tell me about it."


Jane leaned back.


Thinking.


"It's not one thing."


Another.


"It's a lot of little things."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"Tell me."


Jane glanced down.


"If Lizzy wakes up in the middle of the night and I'm feeding her..."


Another.


"I'll look up and he's watching us."


Another.


"Sometimes he's sitting in the rocker."


Another.


"Sometimes he's standing in the doorway."


Another.


"Sometimes he's supposed to be sleeping."


A tiny smile appeared.


"He's very bad at sleeping."


Dr. Ryan smiled.


"So I've heard."


Jane's smile faded.


"It's the look."


Another.


"The way he looks at us."


Another.


"The way he smiles."


Another.


"The way his entire face softens."


She stopped.


Because she knew exactly what she had almost said.


Dr. Ryan noticed immediately.


"The way his face softens like..."


Jane looked away.


For a second she almost said it.


Like my Adam.


But she stopped herself.


Dr. Ryan had challenged her to stop talking about him as two separate people every time she talked about him.


Not because the distinction wasn't important.


But because Jane used it as a shield.


Finally she sighed.


"Like before."


Dr. Ryan nodded slowly.


Not pushing.


Just allowing her to hear herself.


Jane stared down at the notebook.


"I spent years seeing that look."


Another.


"I know exactly what it means."


Another.


"And then I see it now."


Another.


"And it completely unnerves me."


Dr. Ryan made a note.


Jane immediately pointed.


"There."


Another.


"The note."


Dr. Ryan smiled.


"The note?"


"The note."


Another.


"That means you're about to say something annoying."


Dr. Ryan laughed.


"Probably."


Jane groaned.


"Knew it."


Dr. Ryan flipped through a few pages.


Entry after entry.


The same pattern.


The way he remembered my coffee order.


The way he held Lizzy.


The way he knew I was exhausted before I admitted it.


The way he smiled when he thought I wasn't looking.


The way he remembered something from our life.


Dr. Ryan tapped the notebook.


"Do you see the pattern?"


"No."


Another.


"And I suspect that isn't going to stop you."


Dr. Ryan smiled.


"No."


Another.


"It isn't."


She turned the notebook back around.


"Jane."


Another.


"None of these things scare you because they're different."


Silence.


"They scare you because they're familiar."


The room fell quiet.


Jane looked down.


Because she knew that was true.


Even if she hated hearing it.


Dr. Ryan leaned forward slightly.


"What does that look mean to you?"


Jane swallowed.


"It means..."


Her voice caught.


"It means he loves us."


The words escaped before she could stop them.


Her eyes widened.


Dr. Ryan didn't react.


Instead she simply asked:


"And that scares you?"


Jane immediately shook her head.


"No."


Another.


"That's the problem."


The room grew still.


"I'm not scared of him loving Lizzy."


Another.


"Of course I'm not."


Another.


"And honestly..."


Another.


"I'm not even scared of him loving me."


Another.


"I think what scares me is that when I see it..."


She stopped.


Dr. Ryan waited patiently.


"...I believe him."


Silence.


Long silence.


Dr. Ryan nodded once.


"I think every time one of those moments happens..."


Another.


"You become a little less certain about the story you've been telling yourself."


Jane knew exactly what story she meant.


The story that said Adam was different.


The story that said her feelings meant something terrible.


The story that said loving him would somehow be wrong.


Dr. Ryan's voice softened.


"I don't think Adam is the thing that's scaring you."


Another.


"I think the possibility that you may need to rethink your assumptions is."


For once Jane didn't argue.


Because she didn't have one.


And somehow that was more unsettling than anything Dr. Cohen had said.


Later that afternoon she met Andrew for their usual Tuesday lunch.


The moment she sat down he looked at her.


"You look irritated."


"I am irritated."


"Ryan?"


"Worse."


Andrew looked genuinely concerned.


"Worse than Ryan?"


"Cohen."


Andrew immediately started laughing.


Jane pointed at him.


"Stop."


"I'm trying."


Another laugh.


"I'm really trying."


Jane groaned.


"This is why I shouldn't have come."


"What happened?"


And Jane told him.


Everything.


The questions.


The notes.


The observations.


The absolutely infuriating ability to summarize her entire emotional life in one sentence.


Andrew listened patiently.


Then took a bite of his sandwich.


"So."


Another.


"Why aren't you and Adam a couple?"


Jane nearly choked.


"You too?"


Andrew blinked.


"What?"


"You sound exactly like Dr. Cohen."


Andrew shrugged.


"Maybe Dr. Cohen is smart."


Jane groaned dramatically.


"You know why."


Andrew studied her.


Then shook his head.


"No."


Another.


"Actually, I don't."


Jane stared.


Andrew continued.


"I understand why you're scared."


Another.


"I understand why you're confused."


Another.


"But if you're asking me what your lives look like?"


Another.


"You and Adam look like a couple."


Jane pointed accusatorily.


"See."


Another.


"This is why I hate talking to all of you."


Andrew laughed.


Then his expression softened.


"Jane."


Another.


"You've spent so much time trying to figure out what Adam is..."


Another.


"You stopped paying attention to what Adam is doing."


The words landed harder than she wanted them to.


Because they sounded suspiciously like Dr. Cohen.


Which was deeply annoying.


The next day Adam was back at the hospital.


His shift wasn't long.


Just enough to check in.


Enough to remind himself that medicine still existed outside of diapers and bottles.


He spotted Scott finishing a chart.


Scott looked up.


Immediately smiled.


"Oh no."


Adam sighed.


"You have that look."


"What look?"


"The one that means Ryan told you something."


Scott laughed.


"She did."


"Of course she did."


Another.


"I hate all of you."


Scott's smile widened.


"So."


Another.


"How was Cohen?"


Adam groaned.


"There it is."


Another.


"Knew it."


Scott looked delighted.


"That bad?"


"That good."


Another.


"Which somehow made it worse."


Scott laughed.


"I'll take that as a positive review."


Adam shook his head.


"He spent an hour asking questions neither of us wanted to answer."


"Sounds like Aaron."


Another.


"What did you think?"


Adam leaned against the counter.


Thinking.


"He asked what I was afraid of."


Scott nodded.


"And?"


Adam was quiet.


Finally:


"I think I've been waiting for her to leave."


Scott's expression softened.


"Since the beginning?"


Adam nodded.


"Yeah."


Another.


"Every step."


Another.


"Every decision."


Another.


"I keep expecting her to wake up one morning and realize this is all too much."


Scott listened.


Then asked:


"Has she?"


Adam blinked.


"No."


"Has she moved closer or farther away?"


Adam immediately looked annoyed.


"You sound like Cohen."


Scott laughed.


"I'll take that as a compliment."


Adam rolled his eyes.


"Closer."


Another.


"She's moved closer."


Scott nodded.


"I know."


That answer bothered Adam more than it should have.


Mostly because Scott sounded so certain.


Two days later Jane met Karen for brunch.


Or more accurately, she met Karen and Karen's pregnant stomach.


At this point they felt like separate people.


Karen lowered herself carefully into a chair.


Then sighed dramatically.


"Oh thank God."


Jane laughed.


"Tough morning?"


Karen stared at her.


"I dropped my phone."


Another.


"And then I had to decide whether it was worth picking up."


Jane immediately burst out laughing.


Karen pointed.


"Don't laugh."


Another.


"You know exactly what this is like."


Another.


"You did this three weeks ago."


Another.


"At least you got your ankles back."


Jane laughed harder.


Karen looked deeply offended.


"I miss seeing my feet."


Another.


"I took them for granted."


Another.


"If I ever see them again, I'm apologizing."


The image alone nearly killed Jane.


After a few minutes, Karen pointed at her.


"How's sleeping?"


Jane laughed.


"What's sleeping?"


Karen immediately pointed.


"See?"


Another.


"That's what I'm talking about."


Another.


"You have a baby keeping you awake."


Another.


"I have a baby practicing kickboxing on my bladder."


Another.


"Neither of us is winning."


Eventually the conversation drifted to Adam.


Then therapy.


Then inevitably Dr. Cohen.


Karen listened.


Then blinked.


"Wait."


Another.


"You mean you guys aren't a couple?"


Jane dropped her head onto the table.


"Not you too."


Karen looked genuinely confused.


"I thought you were."


"We're not."


Karen started laughing.


Hard.


"Honey."


Another.


"You are more together than most people I know."


Jane buried her face in her hands.


"No."


"Yes."


Karen pointed.


"Look at Andrew and Kelly."


Another.


"They've been together for years."


Another.


"They're together together."


Another.


"But they aren't sitting around playing house."


Another.


"They don't have a baby."


Another.


"They don't sleep in the same room."


Another.


"They aren't raising a child together."


Karen grinned.


"You and Adam basically skipped six chapters."


Jane laughed despite herself.


Karen wasn't finished.


Of course she wasn't.


"Honey."


Another.


"You have a baby together."


Another.


"You're living together."


Another.


"You're sleeping in the same room."


Another.


"Are you blind?"


Jane laughed harder.


"Karen."


"No."


Another.


"Seriously."


Karen's expression softened.


For the first time all brunch.


"Jane."


Another.


"I've known you for a very long time."


Another.


"I know you feel guilty."


Another.


"I know you're scared."


Another.


"But honestly?"


Another.


"You and Adam, this Adam, make complete sense to me."


Jane looked down.


Karen continued gently.


"I think it makes sense to you too."


Another.


"You're just too stubborn to see it."


Silence.


Then:


"I wish you'd let yourself be happy for once."


No teasing.


No jokes.


Just honesty.


The kind only Karen could get away with.


That night the apartment was quiet.


Miraculously.


Lizzy had fallen asleep.


Adam was lying beside her.


Reading.


Jane stared at the ceiling.


Thinking.


Again.


Adam noticed.


Of course he did.


"You're thinking."


Jane sighed.


"Apparently everyone thinks we're a couple."


Adam lowered his book.


Then laughed.


"Everyone?"


"Everyone."


Another.


"Andrew."


Another.


"Karen."


Another.


"Dr. Cohen."


Adam groaned.


"That's a terrifying group."


Jane smiled.


The smile faded slowly.


The room grew quieter.


More honest.


She looked at him.


"What are we doing?"


The question escaped before she could stop it.


Adam stared up at the ceiling.


Thinking.


For a long time.


Finally:


"I don't know."


Another.


"I think I'm trying to build a life with my daughter."


Another.


"And not lose my best friend."


The answer hit harder than he intended.


Jane turned toward him.


"What are you afraid of?"


Adam was quiet.


Then:


"That one day you'll decide this is too hard."


Another.


"That you'll look at me and decide I'm not him."


Another.


"And that'll be it."


Jane looked down.


Because she had asked.


And he had answered honestly.


Finally Adam asked:


"What are you afraid of?"


The room became very still.


Jane stared at Lizzy's bassinet.


At their daughter.


Then at Adam.


Finally:


"I'm afraid you're right."


Silence.


Adam didn't move.


Didn't interrupt.


Didn't try to fix it.


Jane continued.


"I'm afraid you really are him."


Another.


"Or enough of him."


Another.


"And if I stop fighting that..."


Her voice cracked slightly.


"I don't know what happens next."


For a moment neither spoke.


Then Jane frowned.


"Can I ask you something?"


Adam nodded.


"I don't understand why you're so afraid I'm going to leave."


Another.


"I haven't."


Another.


"And I don't plan to."


She looked at him.


"Why can't you see that?"


Adam was quiet.


For a moment he seemed to consider the question.


Then he looked at her.


"Then can I ask you something?"


Jane nodded.


"Why are you so scared that I really am him?"


Silence.


Adam held her gaze.


"Why are you so scared of loving me?"


The words settled between them.


Neither looking away.


Neither hiding.


For a long moment Jane simply looked at him.


And for the first time...


She realized she didn't know.


Not really.


Not anymore.


The guilt.


The fear.


The rules she'd built for herself.


None of them felt as solid as they once had.


Finally she whispered:


"I'm not sure anymore."


Something softened in Adam's expression.


Not relief.


Not victory.


Just understanding.


Because they were standing in the same place now.


He was terrified she would leave.


She was terrified she wouldn't.


He was afraid of losing her.


She was afraid of what it meant if she stopped fighting her feelings.


Different fears.


The same impasse.


Without thinking, Adam slipped an arm around her and pulled her closer.


Jane didn't resist.


Neither spoke.


Because there was nothing left to say.


Not tonight.


For now they simply lay there together.


Listening to Lizzy breathe.


Holding on to each other.


And for the first time...


Neither of them was entirely sure what they were afraid of anymore.
