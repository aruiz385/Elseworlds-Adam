# Chapter 10: The Other Guy (Part 1)

Scott led Jane through the Oncology floor.

The walk wasn't long, but it felt endless.

She wasn't entirely sure what she expected. A part of her still hoped she would find Adam sitting in an office somewhere, looking exhausted and embarrassed and apologizing for missing lunch.

Instead, she saw him standing near a patient room.

And immediately knew.

The man across the hall looked like Adam.

Same face.

Same eyes.

Same glasses.

Same body.

But the resemblance ended there.

Adam moved through the world like sunlight. There was an openness to him that put people at ease. Patients trusted him. Strangers spoke to him. He smiled easily.

This man didn't.

He looked heavier somehow.

Like he was carrying the weight of the world by himself.

When he turned toward them, Jane understood exactly what Adam had meant when he said the Other Guy reminded him of Andrew.

Not physically.

Emotionally.

The intensity was familiar.

The feeling was familiar.

The weight was familiar.

Scott walked forward.

"Hey Adam. How are you doing?"

"I'm good."

The answer was directed at Scott.

His attention wasn't.

His eyes stayed fixed on Jane.

Scott noticed.

Jane knew he noticed.

"This is Jane. She is friends with—"

"Yeah."

The Other Adam interrupted.

Then looked directly at her.

"Hi Jane. Nice to meet you."

Jane's stomach dropped.

Scott glanced between them.

"Honey, are you okay if I step away?"

She wasn't.

Not even remotely.

But she nodded.

A moment later Scott disappeared down the hallway.

Leaving her alone.


The Other Adam sat next to her.

Then just...

stared.

Not casually.

Not awkwardly.

Intently.

Like he was studying her.

Jane lasted maybe ten seconds.

"So, you're the other guy..."

"Yeah, I am."

Another long stare.

"You're Jane. The therapy friend."

The therapy friend.

Jane blinked.

"The therapy friend?"

"That's what Adam calls you."

The strange possessiveness of that statement bothered her instantly.

Mostly because it implied something.

Something she wasn't sure she liked.

"Do you always stare at people down so intently? It's off-putting."

"You talk a lot."

Jane exhaled sharply.

"You're avoiding answering my question."

"If I recall correctly, you hate when Adam asks you questions."

That made her pause.

"How do you even know what Adam and I talk about?"

The Other Adam didn't break eye contact.

Not even for a second.

"Just because Adam doesn't remember when I take over, doesn't mean I don't when he does."

Jane froze.

For a moment she simply stared at him.

That wasn't something she had ever really considered.

The Other Adam knew about her.

Not just her name.

Not just basic facts.

Her.

The real her.

The panic attacks.

The coffee conversations.

The therapy sessions.

The stories.

The jokes.

The eye rolls.

Everything.

"Ok. Why are you here?"

"You ask an awful lot of questions just like he does."

"I am trying to understand."

"So does he when he is asking you."

Jane let out a long frustrated sigh.

This was impossible.

"How do I get him back?"

The amusement disappeared from his face.

"You know that is awfully rude of you."

"What?"

"Because asking that is implying that you don't want me here."

Jane folded her arms.

"I don't know you. I just know that I need him to come back."

"It doesn't work like that."

"So how does it work?"

The Other Adam smiled.

Not kindly.

Amused.

As though she had just asked something absurd.

"Adam needs a minute, Jane."

His expression finally softened slightly.

"He was overwhelmed."

The words hit harder than she expected.

"Did I do something to cause this?"

The Other Adam laughed.

Actually laughed.

"You are cocky. I like you."

Jane stared.

"What?"

"You really think that you alone have the power to cause Adam to relapse."

He shook his head.

"I think you either need to learn more about this or maybe learn how to be a little humbler."

"I'm trying but you are impossible to talk to. Why can't you just talk like—"

She stopped herself.

The Other Adam smirked.

"A normal person?"

Jane looked away.

"Adam didn't relapse just because of you. He is a doctor, Jane. He sees things. He talks to people outside of you."

The statement embarrassed her more than she liked.

"But I do find it awfully amusing that you think that this is about you."

A pause.

"It is a little, I'm not going to lie."

Jane looked up.

His expression had become serious.

"But you were not his trigger, if that's what you're asking me."

Relief hit first.

Then confusion.

Then guilt.

The Other Adam continued.

"I am not going to lie to you. He was excited to meet with you today for lunch at that music thing."

Jane's breath caught.

The Other Adam noticed.

Of course he noticed.

"But last night he came back to get his stuff and walked into your ex trying to save the life of one of his cancer patients who was terminal."

Jane went completely still.

"He knew the kid was going to die. But reality hit. And when that little boy died, it triggered him. That and your ex being the one trying to save him didn't help."

Her eyes immediately filled with tears.

"He was already anxious about you. It's true."

His voice remained matter-of-fact.

"He feels guilty because he has to work with your ex."

The next words landed hardest of all.

"Adam cares more than he will ever tell you."

The Other Adam studied her carefully.

"I, however, have no problem telling you."

A pause.

"I can see why he cares about you."

Jane broke.

The tears came immediately.

The Other Adam sighed.

"I am not without feeling, you know."

Jane wiped at her eyes.

"I feel what Adam feels. I do."

His voice softened.

"I can see you are upset."

A pause.

"I'm sorry about that."

Jane laughed bitterly.

"Are you really? Because you don't seem sorry at all. Maybe you're just saying that because you think that's the right thing to say."

The Other Adam shook his head.

"That's not true."

For the first time since meeting him, he seemed genuinely irritated.

"You know I am a person too."

The words were immediate.

Personal.

"I am not just Adam's coping mechanism."

Jane went silent.

"I have had a life outside of Adam."

He leaned forward.

"You're talking to me like I'm a problem Adam has to deal with."

His gaze locked onto hers.

"I'm not."

A beat passed.

"I've taken a back seat and watched him fall back into old patterns. Guilt. Anxiety."

Then quieter:

"And last night, reliving losing his brother."

Jane's heart broke.

The Other Adam looked tired suddenly.

Not angry.

Just tired.

"And while you are not his trigger, caring about you doesn't help."

He held up a hand.

"But him trying not to care about people doesn't help either. He told you about avoiding emotional attachments?"

For the first time, she saw genuine affection there.

So unusual it almost startled her.

"So I couldn't stand by and let him continue to do that."

Then the confession that left her utterly speechless.

"I know you want Adam back, Jane."

His voice softened.

"But getting him back means that I go away."

Jane stared.

She had never thought about it like that.

Not once.

"I want to live just like he does."

A sad smile appeared.

"Or like he tries not to."

Then his expression became almost vulnerable.

"And just so you know..."

The pause stretched.

"I feel everything he does too."

Jane took a step back.

The implication hit immediately.

The Other Adam saw it.

"Unfortunately for you," he said quietly, "whatever he feels for you..."

Another pause.

"I feel too."

And suddenly Jane had absolutely no idea what to say.
