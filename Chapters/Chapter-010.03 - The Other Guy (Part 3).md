# Chapter 10: The Other Guy (Part 3)

Jane left Oncology feeling emotionally exhausted.

The conversation with the Other Adam replayed in her mind over and over again as she walked through the hospital corridors.

She wasn't even sure what to call him.

Adam?

The Other Adam?

The Other Guy?

He insisted he was still Adam.

Just not the version she'd spent the last four months getting to know.

The distinction somehow made everything harder.

For months she'd known exactly where she stood.

Now she felt completely untethered.


The truth was, she wanted to be strong.

She really did.

She wanted to be the version of herself that didn't need to cling to people.

The version that could stand on her own.

The version she was supposed to become through therapy.

Instead she found herself grieving again.

Not a death.

Not exactly.

But a loss nonetheless.


Part of her almost wished Adam had simply stood her up.

That he had decided hanging out with her was too much.

That he had gone home and chosen to walk away.

At least that would have been simple.

Painful.

But simple.


Instead she'd learned the truth.

A little boy had died.

One of Adam's patients.

A child he had been treating.

A child he knew was terminal.

And somehow that reality felt infinitely worse.


Jane had heard Andrew talk about this before.

Not a specific patient.

Just the fear.

The unavoidable reality of medicine.

The fact that sooner or later someone wouldn't make it.

That sooner or later there would be a patient whose death followed you home.


The Other Adam had told her Andrew had been there.

Trying to save the boy.

Trying to keep him alive.

And it wasn't lost on Jane that if losing the child affected Adam enough to disappear, Andrew couldn't have walked away untouched either.


She hoped Kelly had been there.

The thought came automatically.

She hoped Kelly listened.

She hoped Kelly understood.

She hoped Andrew wasn't carrying that weight alone.


Then immediately Jane hated herself for thinking about him.

Again.

Always him.


The guilt hit hard enough to make her stop walking.

The familiar pressure settled in her chest.

Breathing became difficult.

The hospital hallway suddenly felt too narrow.

Too crowded.

Too loud.


By the time she reached Dr. Ryan's office, she was struggling just to hold herself together.

She lowered herself into one of the chairs outside the office and focused on breathing.

In.

Out.

In.

Out.


A few minutes later the office door opened.


Dr. Ryan stepped out carrying a folder.

She was in her late fifties, with kind eyes and the calm demeanor of someone who had spent decades helping people navigate their worst moments.

The second she saw Jane, her expression softened.

"Hi Jane."


Jane looked up.

"Hi."

Dr. Ryan immediately frowned.

"How are you?"

Jane laughed weakly.

Neither of them believed the answer was going to be good.

"We didn't have an appointment today," Dr. Ryan said gently, "but come on in."

Jane ollowed her into the office.


Once seated, she didn't waste time.


"I think I'm ready."


Dr. Ryan studied her.


"For medication?"


Jane nodded.


The words felt heavy.

Final somehow.


"I don't know what else to do."


For weeks she'd resisted.

Argued.

Rationalized.

Said she didn't need it.

Said therapy was enough.


Today that certainty was gone.


Dr. Ryan nodded thoughtfully.


"We can absolutely try medication."


Jane felt both relief and fear.


Dr. Ryan talked her through the basics.

Starting dose.

Side effects.

Expectations.

Patience.

Adjustment.


"Escitalopram is a reasonable place to start."


Jane listened carefully.

For once she wasn't looking for a way out.


Then Dr. Ryan leaned forward slightly.


"Is everything okay?"


The question opened a floodgate.


Jane talked about group therapy.

The anxiety.

The exhaustion.

The constant feeling that she was trying so hard and still somehow falling behind.


She talked briefly about Adam.

Or at least tried to.

The explanation sounded ridiculous even inside her own head.


She talked about Andrew too.

Not much.

Just enough.


She admitted she still missed him.


That confession always hurt.


She admitted she'd succeeded in avoiding him.

Something that should have felt like progress.

And somehow didn't.


Dr. Ryan listened.

The way she always did.

Without judgment.

Without interruption.


A year ago she'd suggested Jane speak with Andrew.

Not reconcile.

Not get back together.

Just speak.


Jane had refused every single time.


Today was no different.


There were some wounds she still couldn't touch.


Eventually she told Dr. Ryan what happened to Adam.

Or as much as she understood.


Dr. Ryan listened quietly.

Then offered the same advice she always did.


"Put your own mental health first."


Jane nodded.


"I will."


She wasn't entirely sure she believed herself.

But she meant it.


A little while later she left the office with a prescription in hand.


Before she could think too much about it, she pulled out her phone.

Found the new number sitting in her recent contacts.


The Other Adam.


Jane stared at it for several seconds.

Still unconvinced this was a good idea.

Still unconvinced any of this was real.


Then she typed the address of the restaurant overlooking the water.

The place she'd mentioned.

The place she'd somehow agreed to meet him.


She hit send.


Tomorrow.


She would worry about tomorrow when it arrived.


By the time she got home she was exhausted.

Emotionally.

Physically.

Mentally.


She filled the prescription.

Took the first dose.

Then collapsed onto her couch.


The phone rang almost immediately.


Karen.


Jane smiled despite herself and answered.


"Where have you been? I've been trying to reach you!"


Karen sounded like she was about to explode.


"I have news!"


Jane immediately sat up.


"What happened?"


"James and I are getting married!"


The scream that followed nearly blew out Jane's eardrum.


She laughed for the first time all day.

A real laugh.


"Oh my God, Kare!"


James was laughing somewhere in the background.


"That is amazing!"


Karen squealed.


"I know!"


Jane couldn't help smiling.


"I am so happy for you."


There was more screaming.


"We should go celebrate."


"Of course we are celebrating."

Karen paused dramatically.


"And you know you're my maid of honor, right?"


Jane smiled.

Then immediately noticed the hesitation that followed.


Uh oh.


"What?"


Karen sighed.


"Well..."


Never a good sign.


"We're planning an engagement party in a few months."


Jane waited.


"And James asked Andrew to be his best man."


Silence.


Karen winced.

Even over the phone Jane knew she had.


"Kare."


"I know."


"No, seriously."


"I know."


Jane rubbed her forehead.


"You know I had to bow out of Chris and Maria's wedding because I wasn't ready to be around Andrew."


"I know."


Another pause.


"But it's a few months away."


Karen's voice softened.


"We'll figure it out."


Jane wasn't nearly as confident.


Then Karen changed subjects.


"How are you?"


It was such a simple question.

Jane wasn't sure how to answer it.


"It's been a bit tough."


She stared out the window.


"But I am going to be okay."


The words sounded convincing enough.

At least to Karen.

Not to herself.


"Jane."


Uh oh.


"What?"


"Are you okay?"


She considered lying.

Failed.


"I'm trying."


Karen let it go.

Mostly.


Then:


"How is your therapy friend Dr. McHotty?"


Jane laughed despite everything.


Then immediately felt sad.


"Adam is gone."


The words slipped out before she could stop them.


"What?"


Karen sounded genuinely alarmed.


"Where did he go?"


Jane closed her eyes.

Trying to figure out how to explain something she barely understood herself.


"Something happened at the hospital."


Not a lie.


"And he had to take a break."


Also not a lie.


Karen sighed.


"Oh."


A pause.


"That's too bad."


Another pause.


"He was nice."


Jane looked toward the window.

Thinking about coffee.

Group therapy.

Questions.

Eye rolls.


"Yeah."


Her voice softened.


"He was nice."


Not the one she'd met today.

The one she'd spent four months getting to know.

The one she missed.


"Kare, I'll call you tomorrow."


"Promise?"


"Promise."


"Love you."


"Love you too."


The call ended.


Across town, Karen immediately turned toward James.


"Adam is gone."


James looked confused.


"What are you talking about?"


Karen frowned.


"Jane said something happened and that Adam is gone."


James blinked.


"That can't be right."


Now Karen was confused too.


"What do you mean?"


James shrugged.


"Andrew literally saw him today."


That got her attention.


"What?"


James nodded.


"He mentioned it earlier."


Karen folded her arms.

Listening.


"The reason Andrew even brought it up was because he asked Adam to trade shifts."


A pause.


"And he was checking on him after losing his patient."


Karen listened carefully.


"Andrew said Adam was acting strange."


A longer pause.


"He looked weird."


Now Karen was extremely interested.


"But Adam was there."


James frowned.


"So I don't understand why Jane thinks he's gone."


Neither did Karen.


And that bothered her.

A lot.


Because when Jane sounded sad, Karen paid attention.


And Jane had definitely sounded sad.


Karen stood.

Already planning.


"You are meddling."


James didn't even look surprised.


Karen pointed at him.


"I prefer the term investigating."


"You are absolutely meddling."


"Correct."


James laughed.


"Karen."


"I'm going to the hospital tomorrow."


"Karen."


"I need to know what's going on."


James shook his head.


"There it is."


Karen smiled unapologetically.


"If Jane is hurting, I'm getting answers."


And with that declaration made, Karen began planning her impromptu hospital visit for the following morning.
