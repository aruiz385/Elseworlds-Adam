# Chapter 2: Letting Him Go

For the first two months after Kirby died, Jane McFly barely existed.

She moved through the world like a ghost.

She didn't sleep.

She didn't eat unless someone forced her to.

She spent entire days sitting in the same spot on her couch staring at absolutely nothing.

The grief was unbearable.

The guilt was worse.

Because every time she closed her eyes, she saw the beach.

Forty-eight hours before the wedding.

Andrew.

The secret.

Then Kirby.

Dead.

Twenty-four hours after becoming her husband.

No matter how many times people told her it wasn't her fault, Jane couldn't escape the feeling that somehow it was.

That somehow loving Andrew had destroyed everything.

Andrew tried to help.

God, he tried.

He showed up every day.

Sometimes after twelve-hour shifts as a police officer.

Sometimes after classes.

Sometimes after doing both.

Karen and James helped too.

Together they created what became known as Jane Watch.

Someone was always with her.

Someone always checking in.

Someone making sure she ate.

Someone making sure she showered.

Someone making sure she survived.

It became a routine.

A terrible routine.

But a routine all the same.


Then two months after Kirby's death, everything got worse.

Jane lost the baby.

At first she didn't understand what was happening.

Then she was in a hospital bed.

Then she was listening to doctors explain things she didn't want to hear.

Then it was over.

Just like that.

Gone.

The baby had been Andrew's.

Neither of them had known what to do with that information.

Neither of them had even had time to process it.

And now they never would.

Jane remembered staring blankly at the wall while doctors spoke.

Remembered Andrew sitting beside her.

Remembered him holding her hand.

Remembered him crying.

That might have been the worst part.

Seeing Andrew cry.

Because for all the pain she was carrying, she had somehow forgotten he was grieving too.

Kirby wasn't just her husband.

He was Andrew's brother.

The baby wasn't just her baby.

It was Andrew's child.

And somehow he was trying to carry both of their grief at the same time.


The miscarriage broke something inside Jane.

Whatever tiny progress she had made after Kirby's death disappeared.

She shut down completely.

For weeks she barely spoke.

The world became gray.

Muted.

Distant.

Nothing felt real.

Nothing mattered.

Nothing hurt and everything hurt simultaneously.

Andrew stayed anyway.

Sometimes she wondered why.

Sometimes she wished he wouldn't.

Because every time she looked at him, she saw everything they had lost.


It started slowly.

The first time they slept together after the miscarriage, neither of them talked about it.

Neither acknowledged what it meant.

Neither wanted it to mean anything.

For a long time that was how it stayed.

No conversations.

No promises.

No labels.

Just two broken people reaching for something familiar in the dark.

Something that made them feel less alone.

Something that helped them survive another day.

Months passed.

Then more months.

And somewhere along the way, Andrew fell in love with her again.

Not that he had ever truly stopped.

But this was different.

This wasn't the memory of the girl he'd loved growing up.

This was the woman she had become.

Broken.

Grieving.

Complicated.

Stubborn.

He loved all of her.

Even the pieces she hated.

Especially those pieces.


Jane didn't notice at first.

Or maybe she noticed and pretended not to.

Andrew became more attentive.

More protective.

More determined.

When she couldn't sleep, he stayed.

When she had nightmares, he stayed.

When she wanted to sit in silence, he sat with her.

And eventually he broke through.

Slowly.

Patiently.

The way only Andrew could.

For the first time in years, Jane started feeling something other than grief.

Hope.

Small.

Fragile.

Dangerous.

But hope nonetheless.


Their relationship became real.

Not official.

Not defined.

But real.

They spent two years building a life together.

Movie nights.

Coffee runs.

Inside jokes.

Lazy Sundays.

Arguments over nothing.

Arguments over everything.

For brief moments, Jane felt something she thought she'd lost forever.

Normal.


Then Andrew made a mistake.

Or maybe it wasn't a mistake.

Maybe it was simply the inevitable conversation.

"What are we?" he'd asked one night.

Jane immediately froze.

Andrew noticed.

"Jane..."

"Don't."

"Why?"

"You know why."

"No, I don't."

And that was the problem.

Because Andrew genuinely didn't understand.


To him, the answer was obvious.

They loved each other.

They were together.

Why couldn't they just say it?

Girlfriend.

Boyfriend.

Partners.

Anything.

He wasn't even asking for marriage.

Not yet.

He simply wanted them to exist in a way that made sense.

A way he could explain to other people.

A way he could explain to himself.

But Jane heard something entirely different.

Because to Jane, labels came with expectations.

Girlfriend became fiancée.

Fiancée became wife.

And wife...

Wife was a word she couldn't survive again.


She had already been someone's wife.

For twenty-four hours.

Twenty-four hours before the universe ripped it away.

She couldn't do it again.

Wouldn't do it again.

Every conversation eventually led back to the same place.

Andrew wanting a future.

Jane terrified of one.


Then there was the subject neither of them wanted to discuss.

Children.

Not because Andrew pressured her.

He never did.

The pressure came entirely from inside Jane.

Every time she imagined becoming a mother, she saw the baby she lost.

Every possibility ended there.

A hospital room.

A doctor.

Silence.

Loss.

She couldn't separate motherhood from grief.

Couldn't imagine carrying a child without imagining losing one.

And how could she promise Andrew a family when she wasn't sure she could survive trying again?


The fights became more frequent.

Never cruel.

Never explosive.

Just painful.

Two people wanting different things.

Two people loving each other enough to keep trying.

And hurting each other anyway.


Then Kelly Healy came back.

And suddenly everything became crystal clear.

Kelly wanted the things Jane couldn't give.

A future.

Commitment.

Marriage.

Children.

Hope.

Kelly wasn't broken.

She wasn't drowning in grief.

She wasn't haunted by a dead husband and a dead child.

She wasn't carrying enough guilt to sink a ship.

She was simply...there.

Ready.

Open.

Willing.


For weeks Jane thought about it.

Thought about Andrew.

Thought about what he'd sacrificed.

Thought about everything he'd already given up for her.

Then one night she made the decision.

The hardest decision of her life.

Harder than marrying Kirby.

Harder than saying goodbye to her baby.

Harder than surviving.

Because this time she had to let go of someone she still loved.


Andrew sat across from her in the apartment.

Confused.

Afraid.

Watching her carefully.

Jane already knew what he was going to say.

That they could work through it.

That they always worked through things.

That he'd wait.

That he loved her.

She knew all of it.

Which made what came next even harder.

"You should be with Kelly."

The words landed like a gunshot.

Andrew stared.

"What?"

"You heard me."

"No."

Jane closed her eyes.

"Andrew—"

"No."

His voice cracked.

"Don't do this."

"I have to."

"You don't."

"I do."

"Jane—"

"You deserve more than this."


He looked devastated.

Actually devastated.

Not angry.

Not frustrated.

Heartbroken.

And somehow that almost made her change her mind.

Almost.

But she forced herself to continue.

"You deserve a wife."

His expression hardened.

"No."

"You do."

"I want you."

"You deserve children."

"I want you."

"You deserve a future."

"I WANT YOU."

The force of it stole her breath.

Tears immediately filled her eyes.

Because that had always been the problem.

Andrew chose her.

Always.

No matter what it cost him.

And Jane couldn't bear being the reason he gave up everything he wanted.


When he finally left that night, neither of them felt relieved.

Neither felt convinced.

Both felt destroyed.


The weeks after the breakup were somehow worse than the grief that came before.

Because this pain had been a choice.

Her choice.

Every morning she woke up knowing she had done this.

Knowing she had pushed him away.

Knowing she still loved him.

Knowing she would probably always love him.


Then one afternoon she came home from work.

And found the key.

Lying on the table.

Beside a note.

The realization hit her immediately.

Andrew had finally come for the last of his things.

The apartment suddenly felt colder.

Emptier.

Final.

She stood staring at the key for what felt like hours.

Then she broke.

Not because she regretted her decision.

Because she finally understood its cost.


That night she sat on her couch until sunrise.

Thinking about Kirby.

Thinking about the baby.

Thinking about Andrew.

And for the first time she admitted something she had spent four years avoiding.

She had never dealt with any of it.

Not really.

She had simply survived.

First by clinging to grief.

Then by clinging to Andrew.

Now Andrew was gone.

And there was nobody left to hide behind.

The next morning, Jane called Dr. Scott Howard.

For the first time in a very long time, she was ready to ask for help.

And neither she nor Scott had any idea that one conversation would eventually lead her to meeting a doctor named Adam Carter.
