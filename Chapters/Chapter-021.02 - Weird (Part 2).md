# Chapter 21: Weird (Part 2)

By the time Jane reached Adam's apartment, she had convinced herself of exactly two things.

The first was that Andrew had completely lost his mind.

The second was that Adam was somehow going to make the situation worse.


She found him in the kitchen.

Naturally.


"You're cooking vegetables."


Adam glanced over his shoulder.


"Hello to you too."


Jane dropped her overnight bag beside the couch.


"You know normal people make food that tastes good."


"Vegetables taste good."


"No."


"Your palate is broken."


Jane pointed dramatically.


"This is exactly the kind of thing I'm talking about."


"What thing?"


"The judgment."


Adam laughed.


"Have you considered eating a vegetable voluntarily?"


"You've changed."


"I've always been like this."


"That's somehow worse."


He shook his head, smiling.

Then immediately stopped.


"What happened?"


Jane froze.


"What?"


"You made the face."


"What face?"


"The face."


She groaned.


"I hate that you know my faces."


"I know all your faces."


A pause.


"This is the Andrew face."


Jane blinked.


"There are categories now?"


"Oh, absolutely."


He pointed with a wooden spoon.


"Karen face."


Another point.


"Work face."


Another.


"This one is definitely Andrew."


Jane stared at him.


"That's disturbing."


"I'm usually right."


The annoying part was that he was.


Adam lowered the heat on the stove.


"What happened?"


Jane sighed.


"We had coffee."


"So far this sounds normal."


"It was."


"Okay."


"We talked about the wedding."


"Still normal."


"Chris."


Adam immediately groaned.


"Never normal."


"Apparently you're glowing."


Adam closed his eyes.


"Oh dear God."


Jane laughed despite herself.


"That was more or less your exact reaction according to Andrew."


"I hate him."


"You love him."


"Unfortunately."


A smile tugged at his mouth.


"Then what happened?"


And there it was.

The question she had been dreading.


Jane sat down at the island.


"Andrew had an idea."


Adam immediately looked concerned.


"Oh no."


"See?"


"What?"


"That was my reaction."


Adam pointed.


"Your reactions are usually correct."


"Thank you."


"What was the idea?"


Jane stared at him.

Trying to figure out the least painful way to say it.

Eventually she gave up.


"Kelly wants to go on a double date."


Silence.


Adam blinked.


Once.


Twice.


"Huh."


Jane waited.


"Huh?"


"Huh."


She narrowed her eyes.


"That's your reaction?"


"I'm processing."


"Process faster."


Adam leaned against the counter.

Thinking.


"That's...surprisingly weird."


Jane pointed triumphantly.


"THANK YOU."


"I'm just saying."


He nodded.


"I didn't see that coming."


"Nobody saw that coming."


"I assume you said no."


"I absolutely said no."


Adam seemed pleased.


"Good."


Jane blinked.


Now it was her turn to be surprised.


"Good?"


"Good."


A pause.


"You don't have to do anything you don't want to do."


The sincerity in his voice caught her off guard.


For a moment she just looked at him.


"That's it?"


Adam shrugged.


"That's it."


Another pause.


Then:


"Why did you say no?"


Jane immediately groaned.


"There it is."


"What?"


"The follow-up question."


Adam laughed.


"It was a perfectly reasonable question."


"No."


"Yes."


"No."


He folded his arms.


"Jane."


She sighed.


Because of course he deserved the honest answer.


"Kelly."


Adam nodded.

Waiting.


"She's still..."


Jane searched for words.


Complicated.

Painful.

A reminder.


"I know she didn't do anything wrong."


Adam nodded again.


"I know."


"That's the problem."


A weak laugh escaped her.


"She's nice."


Another.


"She's genuine."


Another.


"And every time I look at her, I remember one of the worst periods of my life."


The admission hung between them.


Adam walked around the island.


Then sat beside her.


Close enough that their shoulders touched.


Without a word, he took her hand.


"That sounds exhausting."


Jane smiled.


"It kind of is."


A long silence followed.


Neither seemed in a rush to break it.


Finally Adam spoke.


"Can I be honest?"


The question immediately worried her.


"No."


Adam laughed.


"Too bad."


Jane groaned.


"Go ahead."


He thought carefully before speaking.


"I think Andrew is trying to make the wedding easier."


Jane closed her eyes.


"I know."


"And I think Kelly probably wants everyone to be comfortable."


"I know."


"And I think they both care about us."


Jane let her forehead hit the counter.


"I know."


Adam smiled.


"The reason you're hitting your head against my island is because you know they're not wrong."


Muffled against the countertop:


"I hate you."


"Fair."


Jane lifted her head again.


"You know what the worst part is?"


"What?"


"Andrew made it sound reasonable."


Adam laughed immediately.


"He's very good at that."


"It's infuriating."


"Agreed."


Another silence settled between them.

Comfortable.

Warm.


Then Adam squeezed her hand gently.


"What's the actual question?"


Jane frowned.


"What?"


"The real one."


A pause.


"Not the double date."


Another.


"What are you afraid happens if we do it?"


The question landed directly in the center of the problem.


Jane stared at their joined hands.


Then sighed.


"I don't know."


A pause.


"Maybe nothing."


Another.


"Maybe I spend the entire night realizing she's a really good person."


Another.


"And then I feel guilty for still being hurt."


Adam was quiet.

Listening.


Always listening.


Jane leaned her head against his shoulder.


For the first time all evening she sounded tired.


"I don't want to be angry anymore."


A pause.


"I just don't know how to separate her from everything that happened."


Adam rested his head lightly against hers.


"You don't have to figure it out tonight."


The words wrapped around her like a blanket.


Simple.

Steady.

Adam.


A smile appeared despite everything.


"You know Andrew thinks you're going to say yes."


Adam laughed.


"Of course he does."


"You probably are."


That earned another laugh.


"Probably."


Jane groaned.


"There it is."


"What?"


"The reason I didn't want to ask."


Adam smiled.


Then kissed the top of her head.


For a moment neither spoke.


Finally:


"So..."


Jane immediately pointed.


"No."


"I haven't even asked the question."


"No."


Adam laughed harder.


"Okay."


A pause.


"But if we hypothetically went—"


"Adam."


"If."


"No."


He grinned.

"You know you're only proving Andrew right."

Jane dropped her head back onto the counter.

Immediately regretting ever agreeing to discuss the idea.


"I hate all of you."


Adam laughed.


"No, you don't."


"At this particular moment?"


She lifted her head.


"I'm considering it."


That only made him laugh harder.


Outside, the last light of the evening reflected across the bay beyond the apartment windows.

The city was beginning to settle into night.

Inside, dinner sat forgotten on the stove.

The conversation had drifted far away from vegetables and somehow landed on double dates, exes, wedding logistics, and Kelly.

Which honestly felt very on brand for her life lately.


Adam squeezed her hand again.


"You know we don't have to do it."


Jane looked at him.


She knew he meant it.


That was the worst part.


Because if he had pushed, saying no would be easy.


If he'd dismissed her concerns, she'd have an argument.


Instead Adam just sat there.

Patient.

Supportive.

Prepared to follow her lead.

The way he always did.


"I know."


A pause.


"But you're going to want to."


Adam smiled.


"Maybe."


Jane groaned.


"There it is."


"What?"


"The reason I didn't want to ask you."


His smile widened.


For a moment neither of them spoke.


Then Adam leaned over and kissed her forehead.


And despite everything—

the weirdness,

the anxiety,

the looming possibility of a dinner with Kelly—

Jane found herself smiling.


Because unfortunately Andrew was probably right.


Adam was probably going to say yes.


And somehow she already knew she would end up saying yes too.


Which was a terrifying realization.


But wrapped up in Adam's kitchen, with the city lights reflecting across the bay outside and his hand still holding hers, it felt a little less terrifying than it had that afternoon.
