# Chapter 41: The New Normal


The first week settled into a routine.


A sleep-deprived.


Chaotic.


Wonderfully exhausting routine.


Most nights Adam handled almost everything.


At least that had been his plan.


Jane wasn't entirely convinced it had ever been discussed.


Because every night he promised he would wake her when he needed help.


And every night he somehow forgot.


Conveniently.


By the second night Jane noticed.


By the third night she started setting alarms.


Not because Lizzy needed her.


Because Adam refused to.


She'd wake up at two in the morning.


Three.


Four.


And every single time she'd find him exactly where she expected.


Watching Lizzy.


Holding Lizzy.


Feeding Lizzy.


Changing Lizzy.


Or simply sitting beside the bassinet making sure she was breathing.


The sight melted her heart every time.


Because nobody had ever told Adam to love their daughter this much.


He simply did.


Naturally.


Completely.


As if loving Lizzy was the easiest thing he'd ever done.


The moment Jane stirred he would immediately be at her side.


Instantly.


Every time.


Eventually it became too much.


Sweet.


But too much.


One morning Jane finally glared at him.


"You need to stop fussing over me."


Adam blinked.


"What?"


"I'm okay."


Another.


"I want to take care of Lizzy too."


He smiled.


"You are still recovering."


"I'm fine."


Another.


"I've got it."


Another.


"Sleep."


Adam looked unconvinced.


Very unconvinced.


Jane pointed toward the bed.


Adam sighed dramatically.


Then reluctantly climbed back into bed.


Only to continue staring at both her and Lizzy.


Which defeated the entire purpose.


Some nights he would walk her to the rocker.


Hand her Lizzy.


Then sit nearby.


Attempting to stay out of the way.


Jane would give him a look.


Then focus entirely on Lizzy.


And talk.


Constantly.


About everything.


"Okay."


Another.


"Today your dad was being awfully annoying."


Lizzy blinked.


Clearly fascinated.


"He can't help himself."


Another.


"He just loves you too much."


Adam smiled from across the room.


Jane ignored him.


Mostly.


"You know he's going to make sure you love that soul music he's always singing."


Another.


"We're going to have to do something about that."


Adam looked offended.


Jane continued anyway.


"Regardless of what he thinks, you are absolutely learning the Beach Boys."


Another.


"That part is non-negotiable."


Adam laughed.


Jane ignored that too.


Mostly.


"Your godmother bought you the cutest outfit."


Another.


"It comes with a hat."


Jane looked horrified.


"I know."


Another.


"Mommy hates hats too."


Another.


"But we are going to wear it once."


Another.


"Otherwise Auntie Karen will yell at me."


Adam nodded.


"Accurate."


Jane pointed at him.


"No interruptions."


Adam immediately surrendered.


Then the conversations became more personal.


More real.


"Today I talked to Uncle Andrew."


Another.


"You know he loves you very much."


Another.


"Your dad is going to ask him to be your godfather."


Another.


"He's going to be ridiculously happy."


Adam smiled.


Because she was right.


Andrew would be.


Very.


"You are so lucky."


Another.


"You have so many people who love you."


Another.


"Even Uncle Chris."


Another.


"Though don't listen to everything he says."


Another.


"He tends to say ridiculous things."


The list always continued.


Weekend plans.


Family updates.


Stories.


Dreams.


Sometimes worries.


Sometimes fears.


Sometimes things Jane couldn't bring herself to say to anyone else.


Then she'd sing.


One quiet evening Adam listened as she rocked Lizzy.


"Today I'm singing my favorite song."


Another.


"And it's a Beach Boys song."


Another.


"So don't tell your father."


Then she began humming Wouldn't It Be Nice.


Adam sat quietly.


Listening.


Watching.


Feeling something warm settle inside him.


Because hearing Jane talk to Lizzy about him...


Made him feel loved.


He knew they were in a strange place.


A complicated place.


A place with very few labels.


But somehow...


Over the past few weeks...


Something had happened.


When Scott told him to focus on his family...


Adam hadn't understood what he meant.


Now he did.


He looked toward the rocker.


His daughter.


His little girl.


And Jane.


The woman he desperately wanted to call his girlfriend.


Which she wasn't.


His fiancée.


Which she had made very clear she wasn't.


Especially after returning the ring the same night he had placed it back on her finger.


Despite that...


Adam never doubted she cared about him.


He wasn't entirely sure how.


Friend.


Partner.


Co-parent.


Something else.


But she cared.


And for now that was enough.


By the second week Jane felt human again.


Mostly.


She informed Adam of this immediately.


"You're sleeping tonight."


Adam looked skeptical.


"That's adorable."


Jane crossed her arms.


"No."


Another.


"I'm serious."


Another.


"You look terrible."


Adam looked offended.


Jane pointed at him.


"You have bags under your eyes."


Another.


"You're sleeping."


And somehow she won that argument.


Eventually.


During the day they settled into another routine.


Adam continued integration therapy.


A different specialist handled the treatment.


Though Dr. Ryan remained updated on his progress.


The memories continued returning.


Slowly.


Steadily.


With increasing clarity.


Jane noticed the changes.


The subtle shifts.


The familiar mannerisms.


The memories surfacing naturally.


She noticed all of it.


And she still didn't know what to do with any of it.


The agreement remained in place.


No major conversations until Dr. Ryan could help.


So they waited.


Mostly.


Jane continued seeing Dr. Ryan.


Though she temporarily reduced her group therapy schedule.


One afternoon she finally admitted what had been bothering her.


Their living arrangement.


The bedroom.


Asking Adam to stay.


The way it all felt.


Dr. Ryan listened carefully.


Then asked:


"Why does it feel weird?"


Jane hesitated.


"Because it feels inappropriate."


Another.


"It feels like I'm giving him hope."


Dr. Ryan made a note.


"Are you?"


Jane looked down.


"I don't know."


Another appointment.


Another difficult question.


"What are you afraid of when it comes to Adam?"


Jane sat quietly.


Then finally whispered:


"I'm afraid of loving him."


Another.


"This version of him."


Dr. Ryan looked up.


"Why?"


Jane swallowed.


"Because I promised my Adam that I couldn't."


Silence.


"And you feel like that would be a betrayal."


Jane nodded.


Dr. Ryan wrote something down.


Then changed directions.


Which Jane immediately recognized as trouble.


Homework.


Jane hated homework.


Dr. Ryan smiled.


Unapologetically.


"This week I want you to write things down."


Jane groaned.


Dr. Ryan ignored it.


"For every time you notice something that scares you about Adam..."


Another.


"Write it down."


Another.


"And write down why it scares you."


Jane looked skeptical.


Dr. Ryan continued.


"What I'm hearing is that you love Adam."


Another.


"But you're scared."


Another.


"Not of him."


Another.


"Of what it means."


Jane looked away.


Because that was exactly the problem.


Dr. Ryan's voice softened.


"I also need you to ask yourself something."


Another.


"What is the worst thing that happens if you let yourself feel what Jane wants to feel?"


Jane didn't answer.


She couldn't.


Dr. Ryan continued carefully.


"I think you're trying very hard not to betray someone."


Another.


"And I think you've done this before."


Another.


"With Andrew."


Another.


"After Kirby died."


Jane's stomach dropped.


Because she was right.


Dr. Ryan closed her notebook.


"I want you to think about whether you're carrying guilt again."


Another.


"Especially when the man you're trying not to betray..."


Another.


"And the man you're trying not to betray him with..."


Another.


"May be the same person."


The weekends became easier.


Melissa and Marty visited regularly.


Giving Adam and Jane chances to leave the apartment.


Sometimes they grocery shopped.


Sometimes they planned schedules.


Sometimes they just walked.


Neither wanting to admit how nice it felt.


Sometimes they talked.


Sometimes they didn't.


And somewhere along the way they developed a habit.


Holding hands.


It happened naturally.


Without discussion.


Without meaning.


At least officially.


Neither mentioned it.


Neither questioned it.


It simply became part of the routine.


At night Jane still fell asleep beside him.


And Adam still held her.


And for the first time in a very long time...


Jane felt safe.


Eventually the day arrived.


Dr. Ryan.


The conversation.


The plan.


Adam sat beside Jane.


Without thinking he reached for her hand.


Without thinking she held on.


Tightly.


Like a lifeline.


Dr. Ryan smiled.


Then said four words neither expected.


"Couple's counseling."


Both immediately looked horrified.


Then looked at each other.


Then back to Dr. Ryan.


"We are not a couple."


They spoke in perfect unison.


Dr. Ryan looked down.


At their tightly intertwined hands.


Then back up.


"Right."


Neither appreciated that response.


At all.


"You may not have a title."


Another.


"But you live together."


Another.


"You're raising a child together."


Another.


"You're trying to navigate integration together."


Another.


"That sounds like couple's counseling territory."


Jane groaned.


Adam dropped his head back.


Dr. Ryan was entirely too pleased with herself.


"I've already arranged an appointment."


Adam blinked.


"What?"


Jane looked equally alarmed.


"What?"


Dr. Ryan smiled.


"Two hours from now."


Another.


"Dr. Aaron Cohen."


Another.


"He has an opening."


Another.


"I took it."


Neither seemed thrilled.


Dr. Ryan ignored that.


"Go get lunch."


Another.


"And be back in two hours."


Jane looked nervous.


"We won't be doing this with you?"


Dr. Ryan shook her head.


"I can't."


Another.


"I counsel you individually."


Another.


"Conflict of interest."


She smiled reassuringly.


"You're going to like Dr. Cohen."


Eventually the session ended.


Outside the office Adam shook his head.


"So."


Another.


"We came here for a referral."


Jane sighed.


"At least it's same day."


Another.


"I am so tired of therapy."


Adam laughed.


"Fair."


Another.


"How do you feel about couple's counseling?"


Jane groaned.


"I hate adding another appointment to my life."


Adam nodded.


Then surprised her.


"I'm sorry."


Jane frowned.


"Why are you apologizing?"


Adam looked away.


Because the answer seemed obvious.


"You wouldn't be dealing with any of this if it wasn't for me."


Jane stared.


Then laughed.


Not because it was funny.


Because it was ridiculous.


"My husband died."


Another.


"I cheated on him with his brother."


Another.


"I lost a baby."


Another.


"I isolated myself for years."


Another.


"And all of that happened before you."


Adam smiled despite himself.


She continued.


"So trust me."


Another.


"I was already destined to be financially supporting Dr. Ryan."


That earned a real laugh.


"But you wouldn't have to deal with my part of it."


Another.


"Having your daughter's father be..."


He pointed at himself.


"This."


Jane looked at him.


Thought carefully.


Then smiled sadly.


"It could be worse."


Adam looked genuinely curious.


"Really?"


Another.


"I'd love to hear how."


Jane held his gaze.


"She could have no father for me to worry about."


Another.


"And that would be so much worse."


The teasing immediately disappeared from Adam's face.


Jane continued quietly.


"Because I'm not sure how I would be able to do any of this without you."


The words slipped out before she could stop them.


Before she could overthink them.


Before she could pull them back.


For a moment neither spoke.


Adam simply stared at her.


Because that wasn't pity.


That wasn't gratitude.


That wasn't obligation.


It was honest.


Painfully honest.


Jane looked away first.


Embarrassed by how much truth had accidentally made its way into the sentence.


And Adam felt something tighten in his chest.


Because for all her confusion.


For all her fear.


For all the walls she kept trying to rebuild...


She had just admitted something important.


He mattered.


Maybe not in the way he wanted yet.


Maybe not in the way either of them fully understood.


But he mattered.


And for now...


That was enough.


They continued walking toward their lunch.


Then toward a therapist neither of them had asked for.


And for the first time since all of this began...


Neither of them was walking toward it alone.
