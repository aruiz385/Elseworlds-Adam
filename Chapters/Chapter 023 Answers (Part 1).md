# Chapter 23: Answers (Part 1)

The following week started badly.

Which, unfortunately, wasn't unusual anymore.


Jane sat across from Dr. Ryan and tried to focus on the session.

Tried being the important word.


Because thirty minutes in, her stomach rolled again.


The nausea wasn't sudden anymore.

That was almost worse.

It had become familiar.

Predictable.

Part of her day.


Dr. Ryan stopped mid-sentence.


"Jane."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"You've checked out three times in five minutes."


A pause.


"Are you okay?"


Jane leaned back in the chair and rubbed at her eyes.


"Honestly?"


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"I've been feeling awful."


The psychiatrist's expression immediately shifted.


"How long?"


"A few weeks."


That got her attention.


Jane sighed.


"I thought maybe it was the medication."


Dr. Ryan shook her head immediately.


"You haven't adjusted your medication in almost a year."


Jane grimaced.


"I know."


"But I needed to ask."


Because she had been hoping.

Hoping there was an easy answer.

A simple answer.

A fixable answer.


Unfortunately Dr. Ryan looked unconvinced.


"I don't think it's your medication."


Jane nodded slowly.


She'd known that too.


"Okay."


A pause.


"I think we're done for today."


That surprised her.


"What?"


"Jane."


A pause.


"You're pale."


Another.


"You look exhausted."


Another.


"And you are clearly not feeling well."


The psychiatrist offered a gentle smile.


"Go get checked out."


Jane laughed weakly.


"The doctor is kicking me out."


"The psychiatrist is kicking you out."


"That's somehow worse."


It earned the smallest smile.


A few minutes later Jane left the office.


She didn't make it very far.


The hallway stretched strangely in front of her.


The lights felt too bright.


Too sharp.


Her stomach twisted again.


"Oh no."


Jane stopped walking.


The floor shifted beneath her.


Suddenly standing felt impossible.


Across the floor, Andrew looked up from a chart.


Immediately.


Years in emergency medicine had taught him to spot trouble quickly.


Years of knowing Jane had taught him even faster.


He was already moving before he consciously registered why.


"Jane."


She turned toward his voice.


Everything felt distant.


Muted.


Andrew reached her just as she swayed.


"Hey."


His hands found her shoulders.

Steadying her.


Kelly's words from a week earlier flashed through his mind.


*I don't think it's just stress.*


His stomach dropped.


"Hey, are you okay?"


One hand moved instinctively to her cheek.


She was frighteningly pale.


"Kelly mentioned you weren't feeling great after dinner."


His voice remained calm.


Professional.


Inside?


Inside he was absolutely panicking.


"I've been worried about you."


Jane tried to focus on him.


Tried to answer.


"Andrew..."


Everything went black.


The next thing she felt was cold.


Very cold.


Something pressed against her forehead.


Jane winced.


Then cracked one eye open.


Instant regret.


"Oh my God."


She squeezed her eyes shut again.


"It's so bright."


The relief that hit Andrew nearly took his legs out from under him.


"Jane."


She blinked repeatedly until his face finally came into focus.


"Andrew?"


"Yeah."


A pause.


"You fainted."


Jane stared.


"I what?"


"You fainted."


Another pause.


"Right outside Dr. Ryan's office."


She groaned.


"Great."


"Jane."


"That's embarrassing."


"I don't care."


The words came out sharper than he intended.


Andrew immediately exhaled.

Trying to regain control.


"I had Nurse Hayle draw blood."


A pause.


"Full panel."


Another.


"I hope that's okay."


Jane nodded.


"Of course."


Andrew's shoulders relaxed slightly.


"I was really worried."


There it was.


That look.


The one she hated.


The one Scott got.

The one Adam got.

The one Andrew got.


The look that said:

*Please don't scare me like this.*


Jane hated that look.

Because people she loved always seemed to wear it eventually.


Nurse Hayle squeezed her hand.


"Oh honey."


Jane looked over.


The older nurse smiled warmly.


"I know who you are."


Jane blinked.


"You do?"


"Of course."


A grin appeared.


"You're Dr. Howard's goddaughter."


Jane laughed weakly.


"That's one way to be identified."


Across the room Andrew rolled his eyes.


"That should concern you."


"It does."


The nurse chuckled.

Then looked back at Jane.


"We'll run the tests."


A pause.


"But honestly?"


She tilted her head thoughtfully.


"I already have a pretty good idea."


Andrew looked up immediately.


"You do?"


Nurse Hayle smiled.


"Honey, I've been doing this for forty years."


A grin appeared.


"I've forgotten more medicine than you've learned."


That finally earned a smile from Andrew.


"Fair."


The nurse turned back to Jane.


"Honey."


A pause.


"Have you considered that you may be pregnant?"


The room went completely silent.


Jane closed her eyes.


Of course she had.


Ever since Kelly.


Ever since that stupid bathroom conversation.


Ever since the suggestion planted itself in her head and refused to leave.


Slowly, she nodded.


Once.


Andrew's eyes widened immediately.


The expression on his face changed so dramatically that Jane almost felt bad for him.

Almost.


Without looking away from her, he spoke.


"Nurse Hayle."


The older nurse already knew.


"You want the labs rushed."


"Please."


She nodded.


"I got you, Dr. Call."


Then she left.


The room became quiet again.


Too quiet.

Nurse Hayle disappeared down the hallway with the samples.

The door clicked shut behind her.

Leaving the room unexpectedly quiet.


Neither of them spoke.


Jane stared up at the ceiling.

Trying very hard not to think.

Failing miserably.


Andrew sat beside her bed.

One hand still wrapped tightly around hers.

As if letting go might somehow allow something bad to happen.


Jane noticed.


Of course she did.


"Andrew."


His eyes immediately snapped back to her.


"Yeah?"


A weak smile appeared.


"I'm okay."


"No, you're not."


The answer came immediately.

Too quickly.


Jane blinked.


For a moment Andrew looked away.

Running a hand through his hair.

Trying to regain control of himself.


Then he looked back.


"You passed out."


A pause.


"You scared the hell out of me."


The honesty surprised both of them.


Jane's expression softened.


"Andrew..."


"No."


He shook his head.


"Jane, you looked at me."


A pause.


"And then you were gone."


Another.


"I don't ever want to do that again."


There it was.


The fear.


The same fear she'd seen before.


The same fear he'd worn after Kirby.


After losing patients.


After every person he'd ever loved who had ended up in a hospital bed.


His hand tightened around hers.


Almost unconsciously.


"I'm sorry."


Andrew laughed softly.


"I know."


A silence settled between them.


Then suddenly he stood up.

Walked around the bed.

And before Jane could protest, pulled her into a hug.


A real hug.


The kind neither of them gave very often anymore.


Instinctive.

Protective.


Andrew held her tightly.

Too tightly.


Which told her exactly how scared he'd been.


Jane immediately relaxed into him.


And there it was.


That familiar feeling.


Safe.


A feeling she'd known for years.


Not romantic.

Not anymore.


Just home.


A piece of her life she'd never entirely lose.


Andrew rested his cheek against the top of her head.


For a moment neither of them spoke.


Then quietly:


"Do you want me to call Adam?"


The question made her eyes sting.


Because yes.


God yes.


She wanted Adam.


Wanted his arms around her.

Wanted him talking through every possible explanation.

Wanted him telling her it would be okay.


But Jane shook her head.


"No."


Andrew pulled back slightly.


"Jane."


"He's working tonight."


A pause.


"He'll be coming in for night shift soon anyway."


Another.


"And we don't even know anything yet."


Andrew studied her carefully.


She wasn't wrong.


There was nothing to tell.

Not yet.


No answers.

No results.

No certainty.


Just fear.


And possibilities.


So eventually he nodded.


"Okay."


Then more softly:


"If that changes—"


"I know."


The interruption earned the smallest smile.


"You'll call him?"


Jane nodded.


"Yeah."


Andrew sat back down beside the bed.

Still close.

Still worried.

Still looking like he was only barely holding himself together.


Jane looked away.

Toward the ceiling.


Toward the future she suddenly wasn't sure how to think about anymore.


Because if Kelly was right...


Everything got more complicated.


Not because of a baby.


Because of Adam.


Because she loved Adam.


Because Adam had DID.


Because in only a few months he would begin integration therapy.


Because nobody knew what happened after that.

Not Dr. Ryan.

Not the specialists.

Not even Adam.


Nobody could promise her anything.


And suddenly she found herself thinking something she hated.


*Of course this would happen now.*


Because life never seemed content to hand her one impossible problem at a time.


It always came in groups.


Jane closed her eyes.

Trying unsuccessfully to stop her thoughts from spiraling.


Beside her, Andrew squeezed her hand again.


Neither of them spoke.


They simply waited.


For answers.


And for the future to decide what came next.
