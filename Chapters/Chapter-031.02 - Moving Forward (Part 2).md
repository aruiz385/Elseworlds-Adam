# Chapter 31: Moving Forward (Part 2)

"Scott."


Scott Harper grinned.


"Adam."


A pause.


"You are back, kid."


For the first time all morning, Adam laughed.


"Looks like you are back too."


Scott nodded.


"Apparently Doctors Without Borders survived without me."


That earned a small smile.


One of the few genuine smiles Adam had managed lately.


Scott studied him.


He looked tired.


Not physically.


Emotionally.


"How are you doing?"


Adam looked away.


"Catching up has been rough."


Scott nodded.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"Come on."


He stood.


"My office."


A few minutes later they sat across from each other.


The familiar office.


The familiar mentor.


The familiar safe place.


Scott opened a folder.


"Before we get into the emotional disaster."


Adam groaned.


"There's a disaster?"


"Oh, definitely."


Scott slid the paperwork over.


"But first."


A grin.


"Congratulations."


Adam frowned.


"For what?"


"The pediatric oncology position at UCSF Oakland."


Adam froze.


"What?"


Scott nodded.


"It looks like it's yours if you want it."


The room grew quiet.


"I applied?"


"The other you did."


Adam stared at the paperwork.


Oakland.


Home.


His father.


A completely different life.


Scott watched him carefully.


"The other you thought it would give you a fresh start."


Adam didn't answer immediately.


Instead he asked:


"Does Jane know?"


Scott immediately understood.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"She knows."


Adam nodded.


Then looked down.


And for some reason that answer bothered him.


A lot.


Scott saw it immediately.


"Kid."


Adam rubbed his face.


"I had a terrible thought."


Scott leaned back.


"Let's hear it."


Adam laughed without humor.


"I don't know if I'm angry."


Another.


"Or frustrated."


Another.


"Or just confused."


A pause.


"But I feel like my life got planned."


Scott didn't interrupt.


Adam pointed at the folder.


"Oakland."


Another.


"The job."


Another.


"The apartment."


Another.


"The videos."


Another.


"The plans."


Another.


"Everything."


He sighed.


"I understand why they did it."


Another.


"I really do."


Another.


"They wanted to protect me."


Another.


"They wanted me to have choices."


Another.


"They wanted me to be free."


His jaw tightened.


"But nobody ever asked me what I wanted."


The office went quiet.


Scott listened.


Simply listened.


Then Adam said the thing that had been eating him alive for two weeks.


"Lizzy."


Scott's expression shifted immediately.


"What about her?"


Adam swallowed hard.


"She's my daughter."


Silence.


Heavy.


Complicated.


Painful.


Adam ran a hand through his hair.


"I know who her father is."


Scott continued listening.


"I know who loved Jane."


Another.


"I know who built that nursery."


Another.


"I know who picked her name."


Another.


"I know who sat up at night talking to her before she was even born."


His voice softened.


"The other me."


A pause.


"I know all of that."


Another.


"And I know Jane loves him."


Another.


"Not me."


Another.


"Him."


The room remained quiet.


"But biologically..."


A pause.


"That's still my daughter."


Scott slowly nodded.


Because that part was true too.


Adam stared at the floor.


"And I don't know what to do with that."


Another.


"Because they worked so hard to take responsibility away from me."


Another.


"They made plans for me."


Another.


"They wanted me to have options."


Another.


"They wanted me to have a clean start."


His eyes drifted toward the Oakland folder.


"But what if I don't want a clean start?"


Scott's eyes narrowed slightly.


Adam laughed bitterly.


"They took away the biggest choice."


Another.


"If I wanted to know her."


Another.


"If I wanted to be there."


Another.


"If I wanted to be part of her life."


Another.


"If I wanted to be there when she's born."


The words hung in the air.


"No one even asked."


For several moments neither man spoke.


Then Adam sighed.


"And that's the messed up part."


Scott waited.


"I don't want to make Jane's life harder."


Another.


"I really don't."


Another.


"After everything she's been through..."


Another.


"After everything she built..."


Another.


"I don't want to show up and make things complicated."


His voice cracked.


"But I also don't know how I'm supposed to walk away."


A pause.


"Because I want to know her."


Another.


"I want to know Lizzy."


Another.


"And there is a part of me that keeps wondering if that's selfish."


Scott was quiet for a long time.


Because this was bigger than jealousy.


Bigger than regret.


It was grief.


Identity.


Fatherhood.


Love.


All tangled together.


Finally Scott spoke.


"Do you still love Jane?"


Adam laughed immediately.


The exhausted kind.


"That's not even a fair question."


"Answer it."


Adam looked away.


Then nodded.


"Yeah."


Another.


"I do."


Another.


"I know she doesn't love me."


Another.


"I know she loves him."


Another.


"I'm not delusional."


Scott stayed quiet.


Adam continued.


"But here's the thing."


A pause.


"He and I are still the same person."


Another.


"I know that sounds insane."


Another.


"But when I watch the videos..."


Another.


"When I listen to him talk..."


Another.


"When I see the way he loved her..."


Another.


"I understand every single decision he made."


His eyes filled slightly.


"And I would've loved her too."


Scott let that sit.


Because there was truth in it.


Not all of it.


But enough.


Adam laughed softly.


"I spent four months falling for her."


Another.


"He got three years."


Another.


"And now she's carrying our daughter."


Another.


"His daughter."


Another.


"My daughter."


Another.


"I don't even know what language to use anymore."


That finally surprised a laugh out of Scott.


"Welcome to the strangest situation I've ever encountered."


Adam laughed too.


Then the laughter faded.


"What do I do?"


For once Scott didn't answer immediately.


Finally:


"First."


A pause.


"You talk to Ryan."


Another.


"Because this is way above my pay grade."


That earned a smile.


Scott continued.


"Second."


A pause.


"Eventually, you talk to Jane."


Adam tensed.


Scott raised a hand.


"Not today."


Another.


"Not tomorrow."


Another.


"But eventually."


Another.


"Because this is her life too."


Another.


"And she deserves the chance to decide what she wants."


Adam nodded slowly.


Scott leaned forward.


"And kid?"


"What?"


"I wouldn't assume you know what Jane would say."


The words landed immediately.


Because Scott was right.


Nobody had asked Jane either.


Not really.


Everyone had been trying to protect everyone else.


And maybe somewhere along the way...


Nobody had actually talked about it.


Scott stood.


Then clapped him on the shoulder.


"For now?"


Adam looked up.


"Go see Ryan."


A grin appeared.


"And try not to have any additional existential crises before lunch."


A reluctant smile spread across Adam's face.


"I'll do my best."


An hour later Adam stepped off the elevator outside Dr. Ryan's office.


His first appointment since coming back.


His stomach churned.


That seemed to happen a lot these days.


He started down the hallway.


Then stopped.


Because someone was hurrying toward the waiting room carrying a clipboard.


And a notebook.


And what appeared to be three separate lists.


Jane.


She hadn't seen him yet.


She was too busy reading something.


Walking quickly.


Very pregnant.


Very focused.


Very Jane.


For a moment Adam simply stood there.


Watching her.


And for the first time since that terrible morning in his apartment...


He didn't just feel guilt.


He wanted a chance to make things right.


Then Jane looked up.


And saw him.
