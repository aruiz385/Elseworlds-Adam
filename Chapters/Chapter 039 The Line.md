# Chapter 39: The Line


The first few days after delivery were harder than Jane expected.


Everyone talked about the baby.


The nursery.


The bottles.


The diapers.


Very few people talked about the fact that someone had essentially cut her in half.


Walking hurt.


Standing hurt.


Breathing sometimes hurt.


Existing hurt.


Adam quickly developed a system.


Every few hours he got her up.


Every few hours they walked the hallway.


Slowly.


Painfully.


One lap.


Then two.


Then three.


He timed medications.


Helped her sit up.


Helped her stand.


Handed her Lizzy when she needed her.


Took Lizzy back when Jane got tired.


And somehow managed to do all of it without making Jane feel helpless.


Mostly.


By day three she was finally strong enough to attempt a shower.


Which terrified her almost as much as labor.


Adam was asleep on the reclining couch beside her bed.


Or at least pretending to be.


Jane wasn't sure anymore.


She carefully swung her legs off the bed.


Immediately regretted it.


Pain shot through her abdomen.


She bit back a cry.


And slowly pushed herself upright.


One step.


Then another.


She made it approximately three feet before Adam was suddenly awake.


"Jane!"


His voice was filled with horror.


He was beside her instantly.


"What are you doing?"


Another.


"I told you to call me or one of the nurses."


Jane's eyes immediately filled.


Mostly from pain.


Partly from frustration.


"A shower."


The answer came out more emotional than intended.


Adam looked unconvinced.


"By yourself?"


Jane laughed.


Then winced.


"Bad choice."


"No kidding."


She shook her head.


Then suddenly tears appeared.


To her embarrassment.


"I swear this whole process dehumanizes people."


Adam's expression softened.


Jane looked away.


"Any body shame I've ever had should really be gone by now."


Another.


"I feel exposed."


Another.


"Gross."


Another.


"And honestly?"


Another.


"I don't even feel like a person anymore."


Adam stared at her.


Then smiled.


"Jane."


Another.


"You've never looked more beautiful."


Jane looked at him like he had suffered a brain injury.


"I'm serious."


"You are completely insane."


"Also possible."


Despite herself a small laugh escaped.


Adam carefully offered his arm.


"Come on."


Another.


"Let me help you."


Jane immediately shook her head.


"No."


Another.


"This is embarrassing."


Another.


"You shouldn't even have to see me like this."


Adam frowned.


"Jane."


Another.


"It's not like this is the first time I've ever..."


He stopped.


Instantly.


Too late.


Jane's entire expression changed.


Confusion.


Shock.


Then horror.


"What do you mean?"


Silence.


Adam closed his eyes.


Briefly.


Damn it.


"Adam."


Another.


"What do you mean?"


The room suddenly felt very small.


"I shouldn't have said that."


"No."


Jane stared at him.


"You absolutely should explain it."


Another.


"Now."


Adam looked away.


Toward the sleeping baby.


Anywhere except Jane.


"Jane."


Another.


"We agreed we'd talk to Dr. Ryan first."


"No."


Her voice was firm.


"That comment seems like something we need to discuss immediately."


Silence.


Long silence.


Finally Adam sighed.


"Okay."


He sat down carefully.


Trying to choose every word.


"The integration therapy."


Another.


"It's been working."


Jane's stomach dropped.


"What does that mean exactly?"


Adam hesitated.


Then answered honestly.


"It means memories are coming back."


Another.


"Not the videos."


Another.


"Not the recordings."


Another.


"Not what I saw in the videos-those are messages and highlights of things that happened. Actual things that happened in the last 3 years."


Another.


"Things I actually remember."


Jane froze.


Adam continued.


"Not like seeing a movie."


Another.


"Like remembering I lived it."


Another.


"Like I was there."


The room fell silent.


Jane already knew the answer.


But asked anyway.


"What kind of memories?"


Adam visibly winced.


That told her everything.


Her eyes widened.


"Oh."


Another.


"Oh."


She immediately looked away.


Embarrassment flooding through her.


Shock.


Confusion.


Something else.


Something she couldn't name.


"What have you remembered?"


Adam rubbed a hand over his face.


"Jane..."


The look on his face answered.


And she suddenly understood why he hadn't wanted this conversation.


The intimacy.


The relationship.


Their life.


Not the highlights.


Not the stories.


The real memories.


The actual experiences.


The room became painfully quiet.


After several minutes Jane finally whispered:


"I feel like a line got crossed somehow."


Adam nodded.


"Yeah."


Another.


"So do I."


Jane looked up.


Surprised.


Adam laughed bitterly.


"You think you're confused?"


Another.


"I don't even know where the line is anymore."


He finally met her eyes.


"This is why I didn't want to tell you."


Another.


"I don't get to choose what comes back."


Another.


"I don't decide."


Another.


"It just happens."


Another.


"They flood in."


Another.


"Whether I want them to or not."


His voice softened.


"You once accused me of selectively peeking into conversations when we first met. Our coffee conversations. Just like I've been trying to explain. This isn’t that."


Another.


"This isn't me peeking at things."


Another.


"It's not me choosing."


Another.


"It's remembering."


Jane looked completely overwhelmed.


Then something else occurred to her.


The way he talked.


The way he said it.


The conversation about peeking was not one she had with him. She had that conversation with her Adam but here he was talking about it like it was his memory. His life.


As naturally as breathing.


Like he belonged in those memories.


Like they belonged to him.


"I'm confused."


A tear slid down her cheek.


"You keep talking like you're him."


Another.


"And I don't understand."


Adam's frustration finally surfaced.


Not anger.


Pain.


Exhaustion.


"Jane."


Another.


"I am him."


She immediately shook her head.


"No."


"I am."


Another.


"That's the whole point."


Another.


"That's literally the entire purpose of the therapy."


He took her hands carefully.


Not letting go.


"We talked about this."


Another.


"For years."


Another.


"I think you always imagined he would be the one doing this."


Another.


"Not me."


Jane couldn't answer.


Because he was right.


She had.


Adam squeezed her hands.


"Jane."


Another.


"We are the same person."


Another.


"Different experiences."


Another.


"Different years."


Another.


"But the same person."


His voice softened.


"I know it's going to take time."


Another.


"I know you don't believe me yet."


Another.


"And that's exactly why we need Dr. Ryan."


Jane sat quietly.


Trying desperately to process.


Trying and failing.


Eventually she nodded.


"Okay."


Another.


"We wait."


Adam nodded.


"We wait."


Another.


"A few weeks. Just till we go home and settle in with Lizzy."


Another.


"Then we talk to Dr. Ryan."


Jane looked at him.


Still uncertain.


Still scared.


But there was one more question.


He could see it.


"Go ahead."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"You have another question."


Another.


"Ask."


Silence.


Then:


"How do you do this?"


Adam frowned.


"What?"


"Knowing what you know now."


Another.


"Remembering what you remember."


Another.


"How do you sit here and act…like the guy I met 4 years ago before everything happened?"


A sad smile appeared.


Because that question...


That one hurt.


Because she had no idea.


"Honestly?"


Jane nodded.


"I'm grateful."


She stared.


"For what?"


Adam looked toward the bassinet.


Toward Lizzy.


Then back at Jane.


"For this."


Another.


"For being here."


Another.


"For you letting me back into your world."


His smile became softer.


"I've spent months trying to be the guy you met originally."


Another.


"The one that took you to group therapy."


Another.


"The one who helped you because it made you comfortable."


Another.


"But the truth is..."


Another.


"I forget."


Jane frowned.


"What do you mean?"


Adam held her gaze.


"During the C-section."


Another.


"You thought you saw him."


Jane froze.


Adam's expression never changed.


"You did."


The words stole the air from her lungs.


"You saw the man you loved. You did. At Karaoke, you thought you saw him. You did"


Another.


"I've been trying to dial it back."


Another.


"Trying not to put this burden on you."


Another.


"But sometimes I stop paying attention."


Jane stared at him.


Completely speechless.


Then quietly:


"I wish you didn't feel like you had to do that with me."


That surprised him.


More than anything else she'd said.


Adam smiled.


"I was trying to be considerate."


Another.


"You already have enough to carry."


He stood.


Then held out his hands.


"Now."


Another.


"Can we please focus on today's crisis?"


Jane looked at him.


"The shower?"


"The shower."


She laughed weakly.


Then took his hands.


Allowing him to help her stand.


Carefully.


Slowly.


When they reached the bathroom door Adam stopped.


"I'll be right outside."


Another.


"If you need anything."


Another.


"Just yell."


Jane nodded.


Then hesitated.


"Adam?"


He looked at her.


"When we go home."


Another.


"What happens?"


The answer came immediately.


"I help."


Another.


"With Lizzy."


Another.


"With you."


Another.


"I'm not going anywhere."


Jane's eyes filled.


Adam continued.


"I want to be there like we planned originally. I know it’s not the way you expected it but I told you then that I wanted to be here to see our daughter being born. I am and I will continue to be."


Another.


"If you'll have me."


Jane nodded.


Immediately.


The answer came without thought.


Without hesitation.


"Okay."


Adam smiled softly.


Then stepped back.


Giving her privacy.


Giving her space.


The bathroom door closed.


Adam leaned his head against it.


And closed his eyes.


For a moment the exhaustion finally caught him.


His daughter slept peacefully in her bassinet.


Just beyond his shoulder.


The reason he was doing all of this.


The reason he'd started the integration therapy.


The reason he stayed.


At least that was what he kept telling himself.


Then his eyes drifted toward the bathroom.


Toward the woman on the other side of the door.


And Adam admitted something to himself he hadn't wanted to say out loud.


Lizzy wasn't his only reason anymore.


And that realization terrified him almost as much as it would terrify Jane.
