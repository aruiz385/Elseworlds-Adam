# Chapter 32: Questions Without Answers


Dr. Ryan waited until Adam sat down before saying anything.


She'd known him long enough to recognize when someone needed space.


And Adam clearly needed space.


A lot of it.


For several moments neither spoke.


Then Dr. Ryan smiled gently.


"Welcome back."


Adam laughed softly.


"Everybody keeps saying that."


"That's because it's true."


A pause.


"How are you doing?"


Adam looked at the floor.


Then laughed again.


The exhausted kind.


"That's a dangerous question."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"Usually the important ones are."


The room fell quiet.


Finally Adam exhaled.


"I watched a lot of the videos."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"I figured."


Another pause.


"I don't even know how to explain it."


Another.


"Waking up after three years."


Another.


"Watching somebody else's memories."


Another.


"Seeing decisions I didn't make."


Another.


"Relationships I didn't build."


His voice softened.


"A life I didn't live."


Dr. Ryan listened.


Not interrupting.


Not redirecting.


Just listening.


Adam continued.


"Some days I feel angry."


Another.


"Some days I feel guilty."


Another.


"Sometimes I watch a video and honestly feel proud of him."


A small laugh escaped.


"Then ten minutes later I hate him."


Dr. Ryan smiled.


"That's probably normal."


"Really?"


"You've compressed three years of emotional experiences into two weeks."


Another.


"I'd be more concerned if your feelings weren't all over the place."


Adam nodded.


That made sense.


Eventually Dr. Ryan leaned forward.


"There are a few things you should know."


Adam looked up.


"What?"


"Your alter attended every appointment."


Adam blinked.


"Every appointment?"


She nodded.


"Every single one."


Another.


"And before he left..."


She smiled softly.


"He gave me complete authority to speak with you."


Adam wasn't surprised.


Not really.


That sounded exactly like him.


Or rather...


Exactly like the other him.


There was a strange comfort in that thought.


"What else?"


Dr. Ryan opened a folder.


Then slid a piece of paper toward him.


Adam frowned.


"What is that?"


"Integration therapy."


The room grew quiet.


Adam stared at it.


He already knew.


One of the videos had explained it.


His alter had spent months preparing for it.


Months preparing for the possibility that Original Adam would return.


The appointment was scheduled to begin in two weeks.


Dr. Ryan watched him carefully.


"You don't have to do it."


Adam looked up.


"You don't?"


She shook her head.


"No."


Another.


"You have autonomy."


Another.


"You have choices."


Another.


"If you don't feel ready, we cancel it."


A pause.


"If you never feel ready, we don't do it."


Adam stared at the paper.


Then thought about Jane.


About the videos.


About the three years.


About the other Adam.


The man who had left dozens of recordings because he didn't want to disappear completely.


Dr. Ryan watched him.


Already knowing where his mind had gone.


"You're thinking about him."


Adam smiled faintly.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"I'll let you know."


Another.


"But keep the appointment."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"Okay."


Then she crossed her arms.


And gave him a look.


Adam immediately became suspicious.


"What?"


"What's really going through your head?"


Adam sighed.


Because apparently everyone in his life had become impossible.


Scott.


Andrew.


Now Dr. Ryan.


"I feel like my life got planned for me."


Dr. Ryan didn't look surprised.


"Tell me more."


So he did.


The apartment.


The videos.


The Oakland job.


His father.


Every carefully prepared piece of a future he never asked for.


Dr. Ryan listened quietly.


Then Adam finally reached the part that mattered.


"Jane."


Dr. Ryan remained silent.


"I'm still in love with her."


The room felt smaller after he said it.


Real.


Honest.


Painful.


"I know she loves him."


Another.


"I know she doesn't love me."


Another.


"I've seen the pictures."


Another.


"I watched three years of videos."


A pause.


"I'm not confused about that."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


Understanding.


Not agreeing.


Just understanding.


Adam looked away.


"But I understand him too."


A pause.


"The more videos I watch..."


Another.


"The more I understand every decision he made."


Another.


"I would've fallen in love with her too."


The room fell quiet.


Then came the thing he'd been avoiding.


The thing that had kept him awake for days.


"Lizzy."


Dr. Ryan immediately focused.


Adam swallowed hard.


"That's where everything gets complicated."


A humorless laugh escaped.


"More complicated."


Dr. Ryan motioned for him to continue.


Adam stared at his hands.


"She's my daughter."


Silence.


Not disagreement.


Not agreement.


Just silence.


"Biologically."


Another.


"I know who her father is."


Another.


"I know who chose her name."


Another.


"I know who loved Jane."


Another.


"I know who built the nursery."


Another.


"I understand all of that."


His voice cracked slightly.


"But she's still my daughter."


A pause.


"At least part of me feels that way."


Another.


"Strongly."


Dr. Ryan didn't interrupt.


Adam continued.


"The thing I can't stop thinking about is..."


He hesitated.


"They were so focused on making sure I had choices."


Another.


"They tried so hard not to saddle me with responsibility."


Another.


"But what if I would've chosen it?"


The room fell silent.


"What if I would've wanted to be there?"


Another.


"What if I want to know her?"


Another.


"What if I want to be part of her life?"


Another.


"What if I want to be there when she's born?"


His eyes closed briefly.


"I don't know what to do with that."


Dr. Ryan sat quietly for several seconds.


Carefully considering her response.


Finally she spoke.


"First."


A pause.


"Everything you're feeling makes sense."


Adam looked up.


"You don't think that's crazy?"


"I think it's human."


Another.


"This isn't a typical situation."


Another.


"In fact, it's probably one of the most complex situations I've ever encountered."


A small smile appeared.


That sounded about right.


Dr. Ryan continued.


"You are grieving."


Another.


"You're questioning your identity."


Another.


"You're processing lost time."


Another.


"And now you're confronting the possibility of fatherhood."


Another.


"All at once."


Adam sat quietly.


Listening.


"This is why I want you to move carefully."


Another.


"Very carefully."


Another.


"Especially where Jane and Lizzy are concerned."


Adam nodded.


Because he already knew that.


Dr. Ryan leaned forward.


"My professional advice is this."


A pause.


"Do not make major decisions based on guilt."


Another.


"Do not make major decisions based on grief."


Another.


"And do not assume you know what Jane wants."


That one hit particularly hard.


Dr. Ryan noticed.


"You need information before you make decisions."


Another.


"And the only person who can provide some of that information..."


A pause.


"...is Jane."


Adam nodded slowly.


Because Scott had said the same thing.


Dr. Ryan softened.


"You don't need to solve this today."


Another.


"You don't even need to solve it this month."


Another.


"But eventually, you and Jane are going to need a conversation."


A pause.


"An honest one."


The room fell quiet.


For the first time in weeks Adam felt something other than confusion.


Not certainty.


Not hope.


Just direction.


And right now...


That was enough.
