# Chapter 46: Enough


It had taken Jane two months.


Two months of therapy.


Two months of Dr. Ryan.


Two months of Dr. Cohen.


Two months of journal entries.


Two months of overthinking every feeling she had.


And somehow she had arrived at the most infuriating conclusion imaginable.


She was in love with Adam.


Still.


Hopelessly.


Ridiculously.


In love with him.


Dr. Ryan had looked entirely too pleased when Jane finally admitted it.


Not out loud.


Not at first.


In the journal.


One stupid sentence.


One sentence that had taken her twenty minutes to write.


I am in love with Adam.


Dr. Ryan had read it.


Looked up.


Smiled.


Jane had immediately regretted everything.


The problem wasn't realizing she loved him.


The problem was figuring out what came next.


Three years ago she hadn't needed to figure it out.


Back then Adam had dragged her forward.


Sometimes against her will.


Sometimes while she complained the entire time.


But somehow he always seemed to know exactly when to push.


Exactly when to challenge her.


Exactly when to stop her from disappearing into her own head.


She missed that.


Not because she loved this Adam less.


She didn't.


That wasn't the problem.


The problem was that this Adam was careful.


Careful in a way the other one hadn't been.


Careful in a way he used to be when she first met him in Dr. Howard's office.


Patient.


Gentle.


Deliberate.


And lately he had been giving her space.


Far too much space.


Sometimes she wanted to shake him.


Other times she wanted to kiss him.


Both feelings occurred with alarming frequency.


The truth was that somewhere over the last couple of months they had reached an impasse.


Not an unhappy one.


But an impasse all the same.


Dr. Cohen kept asking what they were.


Neither of them had a good answer.


Co-parents.


Friends.


Best friends.


Two people deeply in love with each other.


Apparently.


None of those definitions helped.


When Dr. Cohen asked if they saw themselves in a romantic relationship...


Both of them froze.


Not because they didn't want one.


Because they were exhausted.


New parents.


Sleep deprived.


Emotionally drained.


They barely had enough energy to feed themselves.


Never mind redefine an entire relationship.


At night Adam still held her.


Every night.


Like breathing.


Like habit.


Like home.


And she still curled into him.


Every night.


Neither questioning it.


Neither wanting to stop.


But nobody moved forward.


Nobody moved backward.


And the longer they stayed there...


The more impossible it felt to change anything.


Jane had also discovered a new fear.


One she hadn't admitted to anyone.


Not even Dr. Ryan.


What if they tried?


What if it didn't work?


What if she lost him again?


The thought terrified her.


She had already lost him twice.


First when her friend disappeared.


Then when the man she'd spent three years loving slowly disappeared as Original Adam came back.


Just when she thought she had worked through one fear...


Another appeared.


Jane was beginning to suspect she would be seeing Dr. Ryan forever.


Friday evening arrived.


Adam's first week back from paternity leave had been brutal.


Long hours.


New admissions.


Administrative disasters.


The usual.


Jane was sitting at the kitchen table working on her therapy journal when the apartment door opened.


Adam stepped inside.


Then stopped.


For a moment he simply looked at her.


Swallowed.


And tried to figure out how to say the words.


He had rehearsed them all day.


None of them sounded right.


None of them sounded survivable.


"Hey."


Jane looked up.


Smiled.


"Hey."


The smile nearly shattered him.


She immediately noticed something was off.


Unfortunately for him...


Jane noticed everything.


"I had an idea."


Adam closed his eyes.


Of course she did.


"I don't think I like it when you get ideas."


Jane narrowed her eyes.


"Excuse me?"


"They're usually chaotic."


She rolled her eyes.


"Rude."


"It's true."


For the first time all day he almost smiled.


Almost.


"What is this horrible idea?"


Jane sat back in her chair.


Clearly pleased with herself.


Which should have worried him.


"How would you feel about going on a date?"


Adam froze.


"What?"


"A date."


Another.


"A real one."


His eyes widened.


"You are asking me on a date?"


Jane smiled.


"Maybe."


Another.


"I'm also aware it would annoy Dr. Cohen."


Despite everything, Adam laughed.


A tired laugh.


"You want to date me out of spite?"


"No."


Another.


"I want to date you because I want to date you."


A pause.


"If it annoys Cohen, that's just a bonus."


The smile faded from Adam's face.


And suddenly Jane's stomach tightened.


Something was wrong.


Really wrong.


"My parents are taking Lizzy tomorrow."


She kept talking anyway.


Because stopping felt scary.


"We get dressed up."


Another.


"We leave the apartment."


Another.


"We remember we're actual people."


Adam stared at her.


Helpless.


Because it was everything he wanted.


Everything.


And suddenly none of it seemed possible.


Jane stood.


Walked over to him.


"What?"


Without warning he wrapped his arms around her.


Holding her tightly.


Much tighter than usual.


"Jane."


Something in his voice made her pause.


"We need to talk."


The words settled heavily between them.


Her heart immediately started pounding.


"I need to tell you..."


And then he stopped.


Because he looked at her.


Really looked at her.


And saw a woman who had already survived too much.


A woman whose life was about to explode.


A woman who deserved another night before everything changed.


Jane saw it instantly.


The hesitation.


The sadness.


The distance.


And suddenly she was tired.


Just so tired.


"Adam."


He looked at her.


"Jokes aside."


Another.


"We need this."


His expression didn't change.


That terrified her.


"You know I love you."


The words came out softly.


Not as a confession.


As a fact.


"I know."


The answer broke her heart.


Because he sounded sad.


Not happy.


Not relieved.


Sad.


And suddenly she understood.


Or thought she did.


No.


No.


Not this.


Not after everything.


"I don't think you understand."


Adam's face fell.


And for the first time in months she stopped hiding.


Completely.


"Adam."


Her voice shook.


"I'm in love with you."


Silence.


Complete silence.


"Desperately."


Another.


"World-ending."


Another.


"Terrified I'll lose you."


Another.


"I am in love with you."


Tears filled her eyes.


Because somehow she was finally saying it.


After months of fear.


Months of therapy.


Months of fighting herself.


She was finally saying it.


And Adam...


Adam looked devastated.


"Jane..."


And suddenly she couldn't breathe.


The room felt too small.


Too crowded.


Too painful.


She stepped back.


Then another.


And another.


"Right."


She forced a smile.


A terrible one.


"I think I need some air."


"Jane."


Adam stepped forward.


She stepped away.


Immediately.


"No."


Another.


"I'm okay."


Neither of them believed that.


"Can you watch Lizzy?"


Adam looked like someone had punched him.


But eventually he nodded.


And Jane left.


The tears started halfway down the stairs.


By the time she reached the street she could barely see.


She kept walking.


No destination.


No plan.


Just away.


Away from the apartment.


Away from Adam.


Away from the look on his face.


Eventually she found herself standing at the edge of a familiar park.


The park.


The one she hadn't visited in years.


The one where she used to come after Kirby died.


The one where she sat alone and cried when the grief became too much.


The realization stopped her cold.


Because she hadn't needed this place in a very long time.


Until tonight.


Jane found a bench.


Sat down.


And for the first time in years she let herself think about Kirby.


Really think about him.


Her Ray of Sunshine.


The easy smile.


The ridiculous optimism.


The terrible jokes.


The effortless way he could make her laugh.


Being with Kirby had always been easy.


Until it wasn't.


For six years he had given her an incredible life.


And somehow...


Even now...


Thinking about him still made her smile.


The realization surprised her.


Maybe that was why she hadn't thought about him very much lately.


Because she hadn't needed him.


Not the memory of him.


Not to survive.


Not to be happy.


But tonight she needed something familiar.


Something warm.


Something safe.


Rain began to fall.


Light at first.


Then steady.


Jane stayed exactly where she was.


Letting herself get soaked.


Not caring.


For the first time in months she admitted something she hadn't wanted to see.


She and Adam had stopped talking.


Not really talking.


They had spent so much time discussing what they were...


That they had forgotten how to be whatever it was they were.


Therapy.


The baby.


Work.


Fear.


Exhaustion.


Everything had slowly gotten in the way.


And what she had seen in Adam's face tonight...


Looked an awful lot like someone who had finally given up.


The thought hurt more than she expected.


Eventually she stood.


Walked back toward home.


Completely soaked.


Halfway through the lobby someone stopped abruptly.


"Oh my God."


Jane looked up.


Andrew.


Concerned.


Confused.


Alarmed.


"Jane, are you okay?"


She let out a broken laugh.


"Believe it or not, this was intentional."


Andrew stared.


Then looked toward the street.


Then back at her.


Something immediately felt wrong.


"Do you want to come upstairs?"


Jane blinked.


"I can't remember the last time you invited me to your apartment."


Andrew hesitated.


Then forced a smile.


"Something tells me you didn't go stand in the rain because things were going great."


He held the door open.


"Come on."


Another.


"I'll make tea."


And for the first time all evening...


Jane didn't know that her entire life was about to change.
