# Chapter 35: The Question Neither of Them Wanted

Adam arrived at Jane's apartment just after seven.


This time she opened the door before he knocked.


She looked exhausted.


And scared.


Neither of which surprised him.


"Hey."


"Hey."


For a moment neither moved.


Neither quite sure how to start.


Finally Jane stepped aside.


"Come in."


Adam nodded.


The apartment felt different tonight.


Quieter.


The nursery light was on.


The crib stood exactly where they'd left it.


Ready.


Too ready.


Lizzy's arrival suddenly felt very close.


Adam sat in the armchair.


Jane settled carefully onto the couch.


One hand immediately resting on her stomach.


For several moments neither spoke.


Then Jane finally broke the silence.


"The integration therapy."


Adam looked up.


Straight to it.


Fair enough.


"You found out."


Jane nodded.


"I overheard it at Dr. Ryan's office."


Another.


"Then at karaoke..."


She stopped.


Adam already knew.


"The song."


Jane nodded.


"The way you looked at me."


Another.


"The things you said."


Another.


"You felt..."


She couldn't finish.


Adam looked down.


Then back at her.


"Jane."


A pause.


"We need to talk about Lizzy."


Everything changed.


Immediately.


Jane's entire posture shifted.


Protective.


Guarded.


One hand moving instinctively over her stomach.


Adam noticed.


And it hurt.


Because he understood exactly what she thought was happening.


"I'm not trying to take anything away from him."


Jane didn't answer.


"I'm not."


Another.


"I know who painted that nursery."


Another.


"I know who picked her name."


Another.


"I know who talked to her every night."


Another.


"I know who loved you."


His voice softened.


"I know who her father is."


Jane nodded immediately.


"Then you understand."


Adam closed his eyes.


For a brief moment.


Then opened them again.


"No."


The answer surprised her.


"No."


Another.


"I don't think I do."


Silence.


"Adam..."


He shook his head.


"I need you to hear me."


Jane said nothing.


Adam swallowed.


Then finally said it.


"She's my daughter too."


The room froze.


"No."


Jane shook her head immediately.


"No."


Another.


"She's not."


The words hurt.


Adam visibly flinched.


Then shook his head.


"No."


Another.


"That's exactly the problem."


Jane frowned.


"What does that mean?"


Adam looked exhausted suddenly.


Like he was struggling to find language for something that didn't have language yet.


"When I first woke up..."


Another.


"It was easy."


Another.


"There was me."


Another.


"And there was him."


Another.


"Two separate people."


Another.


"Two separate lives."


Another.


"Two separate claims to everything."


He laughed humorlessly.


"The more therapy I've done..."


Another.


"The less true that feels."


Jane's stomach tightened.


Because she already knew where this was going.


Adam continued anyway.


"I've seen everything."


Another.


"The videos."


Another.


"The memories."


Another.


"The recordings."


Another.


"The feelings."


Another.


"The reasons."


His eyes found hers.


"And the terrifying part?"


A pause.


"I understand all of it."


Another.


"Every decision."


Another.


"Every choice."


Another.


"Every reason he loved you."


Jane looked away.


Because hearing that hurt.


Adam didn't stop.


"If I had stayed."


Another.


"If I had never disappeared."


Another.


"I would have become him."


The room fell silent.


"And he would've become me."


Another.


"We were never actually different people, Jane."


Another.


"We were the same person living different years."


The words landed heavily.


Because part of her feared he was right.


Adam stepped closer.


"I know who painted the nursery."


Another.


"I know who chose her name."


Another.


"I know who sang to her."


Another.


"I know who held you every night."


His voice cracked.


"Because I'm starting to remember what it felt like."


Jane's eyes widened.


And for the first time she understood what integration actually meant.


Not merging.


Remembering.


Not replacing.


Becoming whole.


Adam shook his head.


"So when you tell me Lizzy isn't mine..."


Another.


"I honestly don't know how to answer that anymore."


Another.


"Because biologically she's mine."


Another.


"But that's not even the reason I'm standing here."


Another.


"The reason I'm standing here is because she's ours."


The room went still.


Jane stared at him.


"Ours?"


"Mine."


Another.


"And his."


Another.


"And eventually that distinction isn't supposed to exist anymore."


Another.


"That's literally the point."


His eyes filled.


"I started the integration because of Lizzy."


Another.


"Because I couldn't stand the idea of spending the rest of my life pretending she belonged to somebody else."


Another.


"I want to know my daughter."


Another.


"I want to watch her grow up."


Another.


"I want to teach her things."


Another.


"I want to embarrass her."


Another.


"I want to be there when she needs me."


Another.


"I want to be her dad."


Jane's eyes filled immediately.


But she still shook her head.


Still protective.


Still scared.


"Adam..."


Another.


"You don't understand."


"No."


His voice broke.


"I do."


Another.


"That's the problem."


Another.


"I do."


Then, softer:


"And if this therapy works..."


Another.


"I won't just be the man who shares her DNA."


Another.


"I'll be the man who loved her before she was born."


Another.


"The man who built her nursery."


Another.


"The man who picked her name."


Another.


"The man who dreamed about her for three years."


A pause.


"Because eventually those memories are going to be mine too."


Silence.


Jane looked utterly devastated.


Because for the first time she could see exactly what he meant.


And for the first time she understood why he'd chosen the therapy.


Not because of her.


Not because of himself.


Because of Lizzy.


"What if you're wrong?"


She whispered.


"What if this doesn't work?"


Adam smiled sadly.


"Then I still want to be her dad."


Another.


"Then I still want to be there."


Another.


"Then I still want to know her."


His eyes filled.


"Jane, I already love her."


The room went quiet.


Every sonogram.


Every video.


Every picture in the nursery.


Every time she'd seen him looking at Lizzy's things.


Suddenly it all made sense.


Adam took a step closer.


Then another.


And finally there was no frustration left.


No confusion.


Just a plea.


"Please."


A pause.


"Let me be there."


Another.


"I'm not asking you to replace him."


Another.


"I'm not asking you to choose me."


Another.


"I'm asking you to let me be part of my daughter's life."


Jane opened her mouth.


Then froze.


"Oh."


Adam frowned.


"Jane?"


Her hand flew to her stomach.


"Oh no."


Another.


"Oh no."


Then water splashed onto the floor.


Both of them stared.


At the puddle.


Then at each other.


Silence.


Adam blinked.


"Your water broke."


Jane pointed at him.


"That is incredibly unhelpful information."


Another contraction hit.


Hard.


Jane grabbed the edge of the counter.


Eyes squeezing shut.


"Okay."


She winced.


"That one sucked."


Doctor mode immediately took over.


"Hospital bag?"


"Bedroom closet."


"Phone?"


"Kitchen."


"Keys?"


"Purse."


Adam was already moving.


Already gathering things.


Already taking charge.


Then both of them stopped.


At exactly the same moment.


Andrew.


Andrew was supposed to be here.


Andrew was supposed to take her.


Andrew was supposed to be the one.


The plan.


The carefully constructed plan.


The one they had all made assuming Adam would already be in Oakland.


Except Adam wasn't in Oakland.


He was here.


Standing in her kitchen.


Holding her hospital bag.


Looking terrified.


And completely determined.


Another contraction hit.


Jane grabbed his arm.


Hard.


Adam looked up.


Their eyes met.


And suddenly the argument about paternity.


The argument about integration.


The argument about the future.


None of it mattered.


Because Lizzy had made the decision for all of them.


She was coming.


Now.
