# Chapter 19: Trying (Part 1)

The next afternoon, Jane almost left three separate times before she reached the hospital lounge.

The first time was in the parking garage.

The second was in the elevator.

The third was standing outside the door.

Old habits.

Old fears.

Old wounds.

A year ago she wouldn't have made it this far.

Six months ago she would've had a panic attack.

Today she took a breath and walked inside.

Andrew was already there.

Sitting at one of the tables with two bottles of water in front of him.

He immediately stood when he saw her.

Which somehow made everything more awkward.

"Hey."

"Hey."

Both smiled nervously.

Then immediately looked away.

Jane laughed first.

Andrew laughed second.

And somehow that helped.

A little.

"Well," Andrew said as he sat down. "We're incredibly good at this."

Jane rolled her eyes.

"Clearly."

They sat.

Neither seemed entirely sure where to begin.

Finally Andrew spoke.

"How was therapy?"

The normalness of the question surprised her.

"Good."

A pause.

"Actually good."

Andrew smiled.

"I'm glad."

Silence settled between them again.

Then Andrew sighed.

"Okay."

Jane immediately laughed.

"What?"

"We're both doing it."

"Doing what?"

"Avoiding the actual conversation."

That earned another laugh.

"Fair."

Andrew nodded.

Then became serious.

"Jane."

His voice softened.

"I needed to know you were okay."

Jane looked at him.

The sincerity caught her off guard.

"I know."

"No."

He shook his head.

"I mean really okay."

A pause.

"Because I worry about you."

Jane looked down.

Andrew gave a sad laugh.

"I worry about you a lot."

Neither spoke.

Then quietly:

"There were so many nights I almost came downstairs."

Jane looked up immediately.

Andrew was staring at the table.

"Andrew..."

"I'm serious."

A pause.

"Do you know how many times I stood outside your apartment?"

Jane froze.

"I'd get home from work."

A pause.

"See your lights on."

Another.

"And just stand there."

The confession hurt.

For both of them.

"I heard your music every Sunday."

Jane swallowed.

Music therapy.

Every single week.

"Some songs I'd recognize."

A pause.

"Some I wouldn't."

Another.

"But sometimes the music would stop."

His voice softened.

"And I'd know you were having a rough day."

Jane felt her eyes sting.

"You knew?"

Andrew let out a sad laugh.

"Jane."

A pause.

"I've known you since we were five."

The statement landed heavily.

"I wanted to knock on your door."

He admitted.

"More times than I can count."

A pause.

"I wanted to make sure you were eating."

Another.

"Sleeping."

Another.

"Okay."

Jane looked away.

"But you didn't."

Andrew shook his head.

"No."

"Why?"

The answer came immediately.

"Because you didn't want me there."

Jane closed her eyes.

Andrew noticed instantly.

"I'm not saying that to hurt you."

"I know."

"You were trying to survive."

A pause.

"And I was trying to respect that."

For several moments neither spoke.

Then Jane looked at him.

"I'm sorry."

Andrew looked surprised.

"For what?"

She met his eyes.

"For pushing you away."

The words finally escaped.

"You didn't deserve that."

Andrew was quiet.

A long moment passed.

Finally he sighed.

"Jane..."

She shook her head.

"No."

A pause.

"I am."

Another.

"I know why I did it."

Her voice softened.

"I don't regret why."

Andrew listened.

"At the time..."

She looked down.

"I wasn't in a place where I could be what you needed."

The sadness that crossed Andrew's face nearly broke her heart.

"Jane."

His voice was gentle.

"All I ever needed was you."

The words landed like a punch.

"I just wish you could've seen that."

Neither spoke.

There wasn't really anything left to argue about.

They'd had that argument countless times years ago.

Finally Andrew smiled sadly.

"But that's then."

A pause.

"And this is now."

Jane nodded.

"This is now."

The room grew quiet again.

Then Andrew slowly reached across the table.

Offering his hand.

Not as an ex-boyfriend.

Not as a former love.

Just Andrew.

The person who had spent most of his life at her side.

Jane looked at his hand.

Then took it.

The simple gesture almost undid both of them.

"I miss you."

The words escaped before she could stop them.

Andrew froze.

Jane felt tears threatening.

"I miss you terribly."

A pause.

"I miss my friend."

Another.

"I miss talking to you."

Another.

"I miss knowing I could call you."

Andrew swallowed hard.

"Yeah."

His voice was rough.

"I miss you too."

A long silence followed.

Then Andrew took a breath.

"I still love you."

The words weren't dramatic.

They weren't hopeful.

They were simply true.

Jane looked down.

Not because she didn't believe him.

Because she did.

"I always will."

He smiled sadly.

"But that's not me asking for anything."

A pause.

"I'm happy with Kelly."

And for the first time since they'd sat down, a genuine smile appeared.

"I really am."

Jane nodded.

She knew.

She'd seen it.

Kelly made him happy.

"She makes me laugh."

A pause.

"She's wonderful."

Then more quietly:

"But I still miss you terribly."

Jane's eyes filled.

Because she understood.

Not romantically.

But the loss was still real.

The friendship.

The family.

The life they'd shared.

"It was hard seeing you with Kelly."

The confession surprised them both.

Andrew looked down.

Not offended.

Just sad.

"I figured."

Jane swallowed.

"I knew it was for the best."

A pause.

"I still think it was but it was really hard watching you move on without me."

That hurt him.

Not because she was wrong.

Because he understood exactly why she believed it.

The baby.

The future.

Everything they lost.

Everything she was afraid she couldn't be.

"I know."

He said softly.

Then the conversation sat quietly for a moment.

Both of them thinking about the baby.

About Kirby.

About all the things they'd survived together.

And all the things they hadn't.

Finally Andrew squeezed her hand.

Then smiled.

"You know what's funny?"

Jane narrowed her eyes immediately.

"That's always a dangerous way to start a sentence."

Andrew laughed.

"You remember when you first met Kelly?"

Jane groaned instantly.

"Oh no."

"There it is."

Andrew pointed.

"I knew that reaction."

"Andrew."

"No."

He laughed.

"I'm letting you walk yourself into this one."

Jane rolled her eyes.

"I have always maintained that life was deeply unfair when it came to Kelly."

Andrew started laughing immediately.

"You still say that."

"Because it's true."

Jane pointed dramatically.

"She's gorgeous."

A finger.

"Brilliant."

Another finger.

"Kind."

Another.

"And somehow annoyingly competent at everything."

Andrew buried his face in his hands.

"You're unbelievable."

"You know it's true."

"I do."

"Exactly."

A grin spread across her face.

"And then Adam happened."

Andrew immediately frowned.

"What does Adam have to do with Kelly?"

Jane laughed.

"The reaction."

"What reaction?"

"The exact same reaction."

Andrew blinked.

Then started laughing.

"Oh no."

"Oh yes."

Jane crossed her arms.

"I spent months hanging out with sweet nerdy therapy buddy Adam."

Andrew nodded.

"Accurate."

"And then one day he disappears and..."

She gestured vaguely.

"...that guy shows up."

Andrew nearly choked laughing.

"That guy?"

"You know what I mean."

"I really do."

Jane groaned.

"I remember looking at him and thinking that life was deeply unfair again."

Andrew laughed harder.

"Jane."

"I'm serious."

"I know."

"Smart."

A finger.

"Capable."

Another.

"Good at absolutely everything."

Another.

"And somehow attractive on top of it."

Andrew shook his head.

"This is incredible."

"You know I'm right."

"No."

A grin appeared.

"The Kelly comparison is new."

Jane laughed.

"Fair."

Then her smile softened.

"It wasn't really Kelly."

Andrew noticed immediately.

"What was it?"

Jane hesitated.

Then shrugged.

"His personality."

Andrew looked at her carefully.

A small smile appeared.

"It reminded me of you."

That surprised him.

Not because he couldn't see it.

Because he'd never thought about it.

"The way he shows up."

Jane continued quietly.

"The way he takes care of people."

A pause.

"The way he worries."

Another.

"The way he makes everybody feel safe."

Andrew listened.

"I think that's probably why I liked him in the first place."

The statement hung between them.

Then Andrew smirked.

"There it is."

"What?"

"You have a type."

Jane groaned immediately.

"Oh my God."

"You absolutely do."

"I do not."

"You absolutely do."

Jane laughed despite herself.

And for a few precious minutes, they sounded less like two people rebuilding after years of grief and more like what they had once been:

Friends.

Eventually Andrew's smile softened.

"So."

Jane immediately knew where this was going.

"So."

"How are you really doing with Adam?"

The smile faded from her face.

"I love him."

The answer came immediately.

"Yeah."

A pause.

"I really do."

Another.

"But I'm scared all the time."

Andrew wasn't surprised.

"The integration therapy?"

Jane nodded.

"The original Adam."

Another.

"All of it."

She looked down.

"I'm terrified that one day I'll wake up and my Adam will be gone."

Andrew's expression softened immediately.

Because he knew that fear.

He'd lived with it for years.

Fear of losing someone.

Fear of the next tragedy.

Fear of loving someone and watching life take them away.

"I know that feeling."

Jane looked up.

"What?"

Andrew smiled sadly.

"Living in fear of losing somebody."

A pause.

"I know that feeling very well."

The shared understanding settled between them.

Then Andrew continued.

"I think you're doing something I used to do."

"What?"

"Living in the future."

That caught her off guard.

"You're trying to survive something that hasn't happened yet."

A pause.

"And may never happen."

Jane laughed weakly.

"I'm trying."

"I know."

His smile softened.

"But he's here."

A pause.

"Right now."

Another.

"He's choosing you."

Jane laughed.

"You sound like him."

Andrew laughed too.

"That's probably not a good sign."

"Probably not."

The tension eased again.

Then Jane asked quietly:

"What if it happens?"

"What if I lose him?"

The question lingered.

Andrew squeezed her hand.

Then answered honestly.

"If that day ever comes..."

A pause.

"I'll be there."

Jane immediately looked up.

Andrew's expression never changed.

"I mean it."

Another.

"You won't do that alone."

Her eyes filled.

"Andrew..."

"I'm serious."

His voice was firm.

"I'll always be around if you need me."

A pause.

"I hope you know that."

For the first time in a very long time, she believed him.

The silence that followed felt healing.

Then Andrew smiled.

"And for the record?"

"What?"

"I'm not worried about Karen and James's wedding."

Jane laughed.

"You're not?"

"No."

A grin appeared.

"I think we'll be okay."

A pause.

"We can work on it."

Another.

"If you want, maybe we do this more often."

Jane tilted her head.

"This?"

"Talk."

A pause.

"Maybe once a week."

Then he hesitated.

Jane noticed immediately.

Kelly.

Andrew laughed.

"Kelly would actually be furious if I didn't ask."

Jane blinked.

"What?"

"She's been telling me to talk to you for months."

A pause.

"Honestly, probably a year."

Jane laughed.

"Really?"

"Oh yeah."

Then she smiled.

"Adam too."

Andrew immediately laughed.

"That sounds exactly right."

A pause.

"Those two have basically been trying to fix us."

"They really have."

For the first time all afternoon neither felt sad.

Just amused.

Thinking about Kelly and Adam working from opposite sides toward the exact same goal.

Finally Andrew held out his hand again.

"So?"

Jane smiled.

"So?"

"We try?"

A year ago the answer would've been impossible.

Today it wasn't.

Jane squeezed his hand.

"We try."

And for the first time in a very long time, both of them believed they actually might succeed.
