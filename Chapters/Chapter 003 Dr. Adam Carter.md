# Chapter 3: Dr. Adam Carter

The worst part about finally admitting she needed help was that Jane McFly actually had to follow through with it.

For four years she had become remarkably skilled at avoiding anything that might force her to confront her grief.

She could attend work.

She could answer emails.

She could help manage projects.

She could even smile when necessary.

What she couldn't do was sit quietly with her own thoughts.

Because eventually those thoughts always found their way back to the same places.

Kirby.

The baby.

Andrew.

The life she should have had.

The life she lost.

The life she willingly gave away.

So when Scott Howard recommended therapy, Jane reluctantly agreed.

It wasn't courage.

It was desperation.


The psychiatrist's office was exactly what Jane imagined.

Comfortable chairs.

Soft lighting.

Bookshelves.

Tissues strategically placed in multiple locations.

The universal symbol of *you're probably going to cry in here.*

She hated it immediately.

Even worse, she hated the fact that therapy actually seemed to help.

Not dramatically.

Not overnight.

But enough that she kept going.

Week after week.

Session after session.

Dr. Ryan listened.

Asked questions.

Made observations Jane didn't want to hear.

Mostly because they were true.

"You spend a lot of time taking care of other people's grief."

Jane frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"It means you're very comfortable focusing on everyone else's pain."

"And that's a problem?"

"It becomes a problem when you use it to avoid your own."

That conversation stayed with Jane for days.

Because she realized Dr. Ryan wasn't wrong.

For years she'd focused on Andrew's grief.

Making sure he was okay.

Making sure he survived losing Kirby.

Making sure he survived losing their baby.

Making sure he got the future she believed he deserved.

At no point had she stopped to ask herself whether she was okay.

The answer, unfortunately, was obvious.


One afternoon, after a session with Dr. Ryan, Jane found herself sitting in Scott's office at UCSF.

Scott was reviewing charts.

Jane sat across from him with her arms crossed.

A posture that generally meant she was preparing to disagree with something.

Scott noticed immediately.

"Group therapy."

"No."

"We talked about this."

"We absolutely did, and my answer remains no."

Scott sighed.

Jane looked entirely too pleased with herself.

"You haven't even tried it."

"I don't need to try it."

"That's not how that works."

"Actually, that's exactly how it works."

Scott pinched the bridge of his nose.

The gesture was familiar.

He'd been doing it since Jane was a teenager.

"I'm not sitting in a room full of strangers and discussing my life."

"They wouldn't be strangers for long."

"That is not helping your argument."


The debate continued for another fifteen minutes.

At some point Scott stopped arguing.

That should have worried Jane.

Because Scott Howard almost never surrendered.

Especially when he thought he was right.


A knock sounded at the door.

"Come in," Scott called.

The door opened.

And Jane's entire train of thought disappeared.

Not because the man walking in was particularly handsome.

Though he was.

Tall.

Broad shoulders.

Brown hair.

Blue eyes.

The kind of face that suggested he spent equal amounts of time smiling and overthinking things.

No.

What caught Jane off guard was the familiarity.

The feeling she got when she looked at him.

It was impossible to explain.

He wasn't Andrew.

He wasn't Kirby.

But there were pieces of both of them hidden in him.

The easy warmth she associated with Kirby.

The thoughtful intensity she associated with Andrew.

It hit her so suddenly she forgot Scott was still speaking.


"Jane?"

She blinked.

"Huh?"

Scott smiled.

"Jane McFly, meet Dr. Adam Carter."

The newcomer extended a hand.

"Nice to meet you."

Jane shook it.

"You too."

Then immediately regretted how awkward she sounded.


Adam sat down.

Scott looked suspiciously pleased with himself.

Which immediately made Jane suspicious.

"What are you doing?" she asked.

Scott looked innocent.

A terrible sign.

"Having a conversation."

"With witnesses?"

"With help."

Jane narrowed her eyes.

Scott ignored her.


"Adam participates in one of the hospital's therapy groups," Scott explained.

Jane immediately looked at Adam.

"You're in therapy?"

Adam laughed.

"That surprised you?"

"A little."

"Doctors need therapy too."

"Fair."

He smiled.

And to Jane's annoyance she immediately found herself smiling back.


For the next hour they talked.

Not about her.

Not entirely.

Adam explained group therapy.

What happened during the sessions.

What people discussed.

What she could expect.

The entire time Jane kept waiting for him to sound rehearsed.

Or clinical.

Or detached.

He never did.

Instead he spoke like someone who genuinely believed in what he was saying.


Eventually the conversation shifted.

Jane wasn't entirely sure how.

One moment they were discussing therapy.

The next, Adam was explaining something that completely blindsided her.

"I have dissociative identity disorder."

Jane blinked.

"You have what?"

"DID."

She stared.

Then looked at Scott.

Then back at Adam.

"You are a doctor."

Adam laughed.

"That seems to be the part everyone gets stuck on."

"No, seriously."

"Seriously, I am."

"How?"


To his credit, Adam didn't seem offended.

Quite the opposite.

He looked amused.

Patient.

Almost happy that she'd asked.

So he explained.

The diagnosis.

The symptoms.

The years of therapy.

The work he'd done.

The coping mechanisms.

The accident that changed everything.

The loss of his mother.

His twin brother.

How old he'd been.

How long he'd lived with it.

Jane listened carefully.

Then asked another question.

And another.

And another.


By the time she realized it, she'd been talking to him for nearly an hour.

Actually talking.

Not giving polite answers.

Not hiding.

Talking.

The realization was unsettling.

She couldn't remember the last time she'd genuinely wanted to know more about somebody.


Adam seemed equally interested.

He asked questions too.

Not invasive ones.

Curious ones.

The kind that made it difficult to hide behind rehearsed answers.

"What are you afraid of?" he asked at one point.

Jane immediately looked away.

"Everything."

"That's not specific."

"Do I seem specific to you?"

He laughed.

"No."

"Good."


The conversation should have been exhausting.

Instead Jane found herself wanting more time.

More questions.

“So you are like the Hulk?”

“What?”

“The Hulk and Bruce Banner”

He laughs

More stories.

More of whatever this was.

That realization alone made her nervous.


Eventually Scott stood.

"Well," he said, looking entirely too pleased with himself, "I should probably get back to work."

Jane immediately narrowed her eyes.

"You planned this."

"I have no idea what you're talking about."

"You absolutely do."

Adam laughed loudly.

Scott looked even more satisfied.

It was honestly infuriating.


As they walked toward the door, Adam stopped.

"Can I make a suggestion?"

Jane hesitated.

"That depends."

"Try one session."

"Scott put you up to this."

"He absolutely did."

At least he was honest.

Jane smiled despite herself.


Adam shrugged.

"One session."

"I don't know."

"I'll go with you."

"You'll sit through it?"

"Yep."

"Voluntarily?"

"That's generally how therapy works."

That made her laugh.

A real laugh.

The first one she'd had all day.

Possibly all week.


Adam smiled.

"I'll even buy the coffee afterward."

"Bribery."

"A little."

"That's unethical."

"Probably."

"You're a terrible doctor."

"I've been accused of worse."


For a long moment Jane looked at him.

Then at Scott.

Then back to Adam.

The truth was simple.

She trusted Scott.

And for reasons she couldn't quite explain yet, she trusted Adam.

Not completely.

Not enough to tell him the whole story.

But enough.

Enough to take one step.

One terrifying step forward.

"One session," she said.

Adam immediately grinned.

"Excellent."

"Don't make it weird."

"I wasn't planning to."

"You look like you were planning to."

He laughed again.

And for the first time since Andrew left, Jane felt something she hadn't expected.

Not happiness.

Not yet.

But possibility.

And sometimes, possibility was enough.
