# Chapter 51: What Happens If He Wakes Up?


Dr. Ryan was quiet for a long time.


Jane sat curled into the corner of the sofa in her office.


Exhausted.


Emotionally.


Physically.


Spiritually.


Just exhausted.


Eventually she laughed.


A tired laugh.


The kind that usually came right before tears.


"Do you know how ridiculous my life is?"


Dr. Ryan raised an eyebrow.


Jane pointed at her.


"No."


Another.


"Seriously."


Another.


"The three men that make up my entire romantic history in the same hospital hallway."


Dr. Ryan blinked.


Jane laughed harder.


"It's absurd."


Another.


"My husband."


Another.


"The father of my child."


Another.


"And my ex-boyfriend."


Another.


"All in one place."


Another.


"While everybody waited to find out if a dead man was going to wake up."


Dr. Ryan shook her head.


"When you put it that way..."


Jane groaned.


"See?"


Another.


"Even you don't know what to do with that."


The laughter disappeared.


Because reality quickly followed.


The reality was far less amusing.


Jane looked down at her hands.


"I loved Kirby."


The statement came easily.


Without hesitation.


Without confusion.


Because it was true.


"I built a life with him."


Another.


"I chose him."


Another.


"I gave up Andrew for him."


Another.


"I married him."


A pause.


"And then he died."


The room grew quiet.


"I spent years mourning him."


Another.


"Years feeling guilty."


Another.


"Years trying to figure out how to live after him."


Her eyes filled.


"And now he's back."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"And how does that feel?"


Jane laughed.


A completely humorless laugh.


"Complicated."


Another.


"But not for the reasons everybody thinks."


Dr. Ryan waited.


Because there was obviously more.


A lot more.


Jane stared out the window.


"The thing is..."


Another.


"I'm not confused about how I feel."


Dr. Ryan nodded slowly.


"Tell me what you mean."


Jane took a breath.


"I loved Andrew."


Another.


"I loved Kirby."


Another.


"I'm in love with Adam."


Another.


"Those things are all true."


Another.


"They've been true for a long time."


Dr. Ryan noticed something immediately.


Clarity.


For the first time in weeks, Jane wasn't questioning her feelings.


She knew exactly how she felt.


The problem wasn't love.


The problem was everything else.


"What are you afraid of?"


Jane laughed immediately.


"Kirby waking up."


The answer came so fast it surprised even her.


Dr. Ryan remained silent.


"So many people keep asking me whether we should wake him up."


Another.


"Whether he'd want the chance."


Another.


"Whether he deserves the chance."


Another.


"And all I can think about is what happens after."


Her breathing became uneven.


"What happens when he opens his eyes?"


Another.


"What happens if he remembers everything?"


Another.


"What happens if he thinks it's still seven years ago?"


The tears started.


Slowly.


Then faster.


"As far as Kirby knows..."


Another.


"I'm his wife."


Another.


"We just got married."


Another.


"We were planning our future."


Her voice cracked.


"What am I supposed to do with that?"


Dr. Ryan let the question hang.


Because it was a fair one.


A terrible one.


An impossible one.


"What happens if the first thing he asks for is me?"


Another.


"What happens if the first person he looks for is me?"


Another.


"What happens if he still loves me?"


The room became very still.


There it was.


The real fear.


Not whether Jane loved Kirby.


Whether Kirby still loved Jane.


And what came with that.


Jane wiped at her eyes.


"I feel like everybody is looking at me."


Another.


"The hospital."


Another.


"The doctors."


Another.


"Scott."


Another.


"Andrew."


Another.


"Everybody keeps saying the same thing."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"What?"


"You're his wife."


Another.


"You're the decision maker."


Jane laughed bitterly.


"Do you know how unfair that feels?"


Dr. Ryan answered immediately.


"Very."


Jane froze.


Because nobody had said that yet.


Nobody had acknowledged it.


They just kept reminding her of her responsibility.


Not what it felt like.


"When I married him..."


Another.


"I wasn't agreeing to this."


Another.


"I wasn't agreeing to make life-and-death decisions seven years after he disappeared."


Another.


"I wasn't agreeing to explain seven years of history if he woke up."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"No."


Another.


"You weren't."


Jane looked down.


"And if I make the wrong decision?"


Dr. Ryan waited.


Because she knew this mattered.


"What if I tell them to wake him up and he dies?"


A tear rolled down Jane's face.


"What if I lose him twice?"


Another.


"What if I make the wrong choice?"


Another.


"I don't know if I can survive that."


Dr. Ryan leaned forward.


"Jane."


Another.


"You're confusing responsibility with control."


Jane looked annoyed immediately.


Which meant Dr. Ryan probably had a point.


"You have responsibility."


Another.


"You do not have control."


Jane frowned.


Dr. Ryan continued.


"You can make the best decision possible with the information available."


Another.


"You cannot control the outcome."


Another.


"You cannot control what his brain does."


Another.


"You cannot control whether he recovers."


Another.


"You cannot control whether he survives."


Another.


"You cannot control whether he remembers."


The words slowly settled.


Not comfortably.


But truthfully.


After a while Dr. Ryan asked:


"What about Andrew?"


Jane closed her eyes.


Immediately.


Of course.


Andrew.


Another problem she wasn't prepared for.


Or maybe an old problem returning.


"I thought I had moved past everything."


Another.


"I thought we had figured out how to be friends."


Another.


"And we did."


Another.


"We built something good."


Another.


"Real."


A soft smile appeared.


"He became one of my best friends again."


Another.


"There were boundaries."


Another.


"Healthy boundaries."


Another.


"And suddenly they're gone."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"Because of Kirby."


Jane immediately nodded.


"Exactly."


Another.


"Everything is coming back."


Another.


"The beach."


Another.


"The baby."


Another.


"The lie."


Her voice softened.


"The version of us we buried."


Dr. Ryan remained quiet.


"And now we're making impossible decisions together."


Another.


"We're spending all this time together."


Another.


"He's leaning on me."


Another.


"And part of me remembers exactly why that used to be dangerous."


A pause.


"If he had chosen me..."


Dr. Ryan waited.


Jane looked down.


"I would've gone."


Silence.


"I would've chosen Andrew."


Another.


"Back then."


Another.


"I know I would've."


Another.


"And I haven't thought about that in years."


Her eyes filled.


"Years."


Another.


"Now I have a life outside of Andrew and Kirby."


Another.


"A real life."


A genuine smile appeared.


"Adam."


Another.


"Lizzy."


Another.


"Our apartment."


Another.


"Our little family."


Another.


"My future."


The smile disappeared.


"And now I feel like I've been pulled back into something I worked really hard to leave."


Dr. Ryan nodded slowly.


Because now she understood.


This wasn't about wanting Andrew.


Or wanting Kirby.


It was about Jane fearing the loss of the life she'd built.


"What about Adam?"


The answer came instantly.


Without hesitation.


Without thought.


"I love him."


Dr. Ryan smiled softly.


Jane didn't even notice.


"I love him."


Another.


"He's my home."


Another.


"He's Lizzy's father."


Another.


"He's my family."


Another.


"He's my future."


Her voice broke.


"And I'm terrified."


Dr. Ryan nodded.


"Of losing him."


Jane nodded immediately.


"Yes."


Another.


"And of him losing me."


Another.


"Because he sees all of this too."


Another.


"He sees a husband."


Another.


"A marriage."


Another.


"A past that suddenly isn't past anymore."


She wiped at her eyes.


"And I hate that he's carrying it."


Another.


"He's already carrying enough."


Another.


"The integration therapy."


Another.


"The memories."


Another.


"Being a new father."


Another.


"And now this."


Dr. Ryan sat quietly for a moment.


Then spoke softly.


"Jane."


Another.


"The thing I hear over and over is not confusion."


Jane frowned.


"What?"


Dr. Ryan leaned forward.


"You know who you love."


Another.


"You know who your family is."


Another.


"You know the life you've chosen."


Another.


"You're not confused."


Jane stared at her.


Dr. Ryan continued.


"You're scared."


Another.


"Scared of losing it."


The words hit hard.


Because they were true.


Painfully true.


Dr. Ryan softened her voice.


"You cannot answer the question of what happens after Kirby wakes up."


Another.


"Because Kirby hasn't woken up."


Another.


"You don't know what he'll remember."


Another.


"You don't know what he'll want."


Another.


"You don't know what role he'll expect you to play."


Another.


"And you don't know what role you will choose to play."


A long silence followed.


Then Dr. Ryan smiled gently.


"You don't owe anybody those answers today."


Another.


"Not Andrew."


Another.


"Not Adam."


Another.


"Not Kirby."


Another.


"Not yourself."


The tears came again.


But this time they felt different.


Less desperate.


Less trapped.


For the first time since Kirby came back into her life...


Jane felt like she was allowed to take one day at a time.


And right now?


That was enough.
