# Chapter 26: Wedding Magic (Part 3)

The inn had finally gone quiet.

Most of the wedding guests had drifted off to their rooms hours ago.

The laughter had faded.

The music was gone.

Even the lake seemed calmer now.


Jane leaned against the railing at the end of the dock.

Moonlight reflected across the water.

Silver stretching endlessly across the surface.


Beside her, Adam stood quietly.

For once.


The silence immediately made her suspicious.


"What?"


Adam glanced over.


"What?"


Jane narrowed her eyes.


"You have a look."


"I don't have a look."


"You absolutely have a look."


Adam sighed.


"Caught."


Jane immediately groaned.


"Oh no."


"Oh yes."

Adam replied.


"Adam."


"Jane."


She stared at him.


"What are you doing?"


Instead of answering, Adam reached into his pocket.


Immediately Jane froze.


"Oh no."


Adam laughed.


"Oh yes."


"Adam."


"Just let me finish."


She was already laughing.

Already tearing up.


"Adam."


"I know."


A pause.


"I know."


The smile on his face softened.


He pulled out a small ring box.


Jane immediately looked away.


Because suddenly this wasn't funny anymore.


"Adam..."


"Let me finish."


His voice was gentle.


So she nodded.


Adam took a breath.


"I love you."


Her eyes immediately filled.


"I know."


"No."


A sad smile appeared.


"I really need you to understand how much."


A silence settled between them.


"You are my favorite part of every day."


Another.


"My best friend."


Another.


"The love of my life."


Jane felt tears rolling down her cheeks.


"And if I had things my way..."


His voice cracked slightly.


"...I would've married you already."


The tears came harder.


Because she knew.


God, she knew.


"I know."


Adam laughed softly.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"I think you do."


For a moment neither of them spoke.


Then finally:


"Jane McFly."


She immediately started shaking her head.


"No."


"Jane McFly."


"Adam."


His smile widened.


"Will you marry me?"


The question hung between them.


Beautiful.


Impossible.


Painful.


Because they both knew the answer wasn't simple.


Jane reached for him immediately.

Taking his free hand.


"Oh God."


She laughed through tears.


"Oh God."


Adam waited.


Jane looked at the ring.

Then at him.

Then back at the lake.


Finally she whispered:


"I would love nothing more."


His expression softened.


"You know I would."


"I know."


A tear slipped down her cheek.


"But you know why we can't."


Adam looked down briefly.


The smile stayed.

But sadness appeared inside it.


"My friend deserves a choice."


The words hurt.


"He deserves his life."


Another.


"He deserves the chance to choose what he wants."


Another.


"And I can't take that away from him."


Adam nodded.


Because he understood.


He always had.


For a moment neither spoke.


Then Adam took the ring out of the box.


Jane blinked.


"What are you doing?"


"What does it look like?"


"Adam."


He gently took her hand.


"Adam."


And slipped the ring onto her finger.


Jane stared.


The stone caught the moonlight.


"You are impossible."


"I know."


He lifted her hand.

Studying it.


A smile appeared.


"Looks good."


"Adam."


Then he looked at her.

Really looked at her.


"As long as I'm here..."


A pause.


"...can you just say yes?"


Jane felt her heart break.


Because somehow that was worse.


Not forever.


Not eternity.


Just now.


Just this moment.


Just them.


Jane smiled through tears.


"Fine."


Adam grinned instantly.


"Fine?"


"Yes."


A pause.


"As long as you understand something."


His eyes narrowed.


"What?"


"The ring comes off the minute Adam comes back."


Adam laughed.


"There she is."


"I'm serious."


"I know."


Jane pointed at him.


"And for the record."


"What?"


"I know exactly where you hid it."


Adam froze.


"What?"


She looked entirely too pleased with herself.


"Top drawer."


His eyes widened.


"No."


"Inside the brown box."


"What?"


"Under the sweaters."


Adam stared at her.

Horrified.


"You found it?"


"I found it months ago."


The look on his face was priceless.


"Months?"


"Months."


"You didn't say anything?"


"I was waiting."


Adam shook his head.


"You are unbelievable."


"I learned from the best."


That earned a laugh.


Then Adam grew serious again.


"No."


Jane frowned.


"What?"


"I have one condition."


"Oh no."


"Oh yes."


He pointed at the ring.


"You are not allowed to take it off."


Jane immediately opened her mouth.


"No."

Adam said.


"Adam."


"No."


She laughed.


"You literally just agreed that I would."


"Under one circumstance."


A pause.


"One."


Jane shook her head.


"I don't want to call attention to myself."


Adam rolled his eyes.


"I don't care."


That made her laugh.


"I know you don't."


"I really don't."


A pause.


"I want everyone to know."


Another.


"I want to tell the world that my girlfriend is my fiancée."


Jane felt her throat tighten.


Adam smiled softly.


"The mother of my baby is my fiancée."


Another pause.


"And if I had things my way..."


His voice softened.


"...she'd already be my wife."


The tears immediately returned.


Because she knew.


She had always known.


Then he reached up and brushed a tear from her cheek.


"You know why I'm saying this."


And instantly she did.


Years ago she'd tried to explain something to Andrew.


Why labels scared her.


Not because they were labels.


Because of what came after.


Girlfriend became fiancée.


Fiancée became wife.


Wife became mother.


And back then she couldn't imagine giving anyone those things.


Couldn't imagine promising someone a future.


Couldn't imagine being somebody's wife.


Couldn't imagine being anybody's mother.


So she avoided the labels.


Because if she never became somebody's girlfriend...


She never had to risk becoming anything else.


Then Adam had arrived.


The lunch that was definitely a date.


The boyfriend title she never officially agreed to.


The future she didn't want to discuss.


The baby she never imagined she'd have.


One by one he had walked straight through every wall she built.


Patiently.


Relentlessly.


Lovingly.


And now she stood beside him wearing an engagement ring and carrying their child.


Every future she'd spent years running from had become something she desperately wanted.


Because of him.


Because he had believed in her long before she knew how to believe in herself.


Jane laughed softly through her tears.


"You really did it."


Adam frowned.


"What?"


"You tore down every wall."


A grin appeared.


"Well."


A pause.


"They were very badly constructed walls."


Jane laughed.


And God help her...


She loved him for it.


She loved him so much for it.


Adam gently squeezed her hand.


"Promise me."


Jane looked down at the ring.


Then back at him.


The man who had changed everything.


The man who had given her back a future.


The man who had taught her not to be afraid of wanting one.


Finally she nodded.


"I promise."


The relief on his face was immediate.


"Good."


Then she reached for him.


Pulled him toward her.


And kissed him.


A real kiss.


The kind that carried every bit of love and fear and hope she'd been carrying for months.


When they finally pulled apart, neither spoke.


Adam wrapped both arms around her.

Holding her tightly.


Jane buried her face against his shoulder.


"I love you."


The words came out small.

Broken.

Honest.


Adam closed his eyes.


"I know."


A pause.


"And I love you more than anything."


He kissed the top of her head.


And standing there beneath the moonlight, with the ring shining softly on her finger and the future waiting for them somewhere beyond the lake, they simply held each other.


Tomorrow belonged to Karen and James.


The future belonged to uncertainty.


But tonight belonged to them.


And for now, that was enough.
