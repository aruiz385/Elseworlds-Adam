# Chapter 15: Complicated (Part 3)

Adam watched her quietly from the kitchen island.

He saw it immediately.

The grief.

The guilt.

The conviction that Andrew was somehow better off without her.

And why wouldn't she believe that?

Andrew lived one floor above her.

He walked past her apartment every day.

A year had passed, and he had never once knocked on her door to see if she was okay.

From Jane's perspective, the conclusion probably felt obvious.

Adam hated it.

Because he could see just how deeply she believed it.


Jane threw away her pizza and stood staring into the sink.

For a few moments neither of them spoke.

Then she sighed.

"You know..."

Adam looked up.

"You don't really have to do this."

His stomach dropped immediately.

There it was.

The retreat.

The defensive wall.

The one he was starting to recognize.


"Hanging out with me."

She gestured vaguely around the apartment.

"Watching movies you don't want to watch."

A weak smile appeared.

"Eating pizza you don't even like."


Adam said nothing.

Waiting.


"Maybe we should just keep hanging out at the hospital."

She looked down.

"You know. When I come in for my appointments."


The words hit him harder than he expected.

Because he immediately understood what she was doing.


She wasn't pushing him away because she didn't want him there.

She was pushing him away because she thought it was unfair to keep him there.


And somehow that hurt even more.


Adam stood.

"Nope."


Jane blinked.


"Nope?"


"Nope."


She almost smiled.

Almost.


"I'm not letting you do that."


"Do what?"


"Push me away."


Jane crossed her arms.

"I'm not—"


"You are."


She looked away.


Adam sighed.


"Andrew may think it's okay to live upstairs and forget the insane amount of history you two have."


The irritation in his voice surprised even him.


"I genuinely do not understand how he knows you're here and doesn't even check on you."


Jane immediately shook her head.


"I think Andrew knows it's for the best."


Adam looked unconvinced.


"Andrew and I are always a disaster."


"Still."


He ran a hand through his hair.


"Either he is secretly a robot..."


Jane laughed despite herself.


"...or were things really that bad?"


The question lingered between them.


"How do you stop talking to someone you've known your whole life?"


A pause.


"Someone you've loved your whole life."


His voice softened.


"It's insane."


Jane looked at him sadly.


"It isn't."


Adam frowned.


"It really isn't."


She leaned against the counter.


"People can love each other and still know they shouldn't be together."


Her eyes dropped to the floor.


"Sometimes loving someone means understanding that being in their life makes things harder."


A pause.


"Him and I hurt each other."


Another pause.


"And other people."


Adam stared at her.

Unable to understand.

Maybe unwilling to.


"I don't get it."


The answer came immediately.


"For the record..."


He pointed at her.


"I'm not going anywhere."


Jane shook her head.


"I'm not actively trying to push you away."


Adam raised an eyebrow.


"That sounds suspiciously like something someone pushing me away would say."


That earned another eye roll.


But it disappeared quickly.


Her expression became serious again.


"You have to see how impossible this is though."


There it was.

The real issue.

The thing she'd been thinking about all day.


Adam nodded.


"Okay."


She looked wary.


"Okay?"


"Let's talk about it."


Jane immediately looked down.


"Talk about what exactly?"


He stared at her.


She stared very intently at the floor.


Adam laughed softly.


"You know exactly what."


Jane sighed.


"No."


"Jane."


"Nope."


"Jane."


She groaned.


Adam crossed the room.

Then gently wrapped his arms around her waist.


Her breath caught immediately.


"We kissed."


Jane stared at the floor.


A pause.


"Twice."


The pause became longer.


"We did?"


Adam burst out laughing.


Jane couldn't even keep a straight face through her own denial.


"Technically," she replied carefully, "you kissed me."


"But you kissed me back."


He gently lifted her chin.

Making her look at him.


Her heart immediately betrayed her.


"You know this is really complicated, right?"


Adam smiled.


"You keep saying that."


"Because it is."


"I don't think it is."


Jane stared.


The certainty in his voice startled her.


Because he looked terrified.

And somehow completely certain at the same time.


"I think I've fallen in love with you."


The room went completely quiet.


Adam swallowed hard.


"And I think you love me too."


There.

He'd finally said it.


The thing he had been trying not to say for months.


Jane immediately looked away.


Exposed.

Seen.


Vulnerable.


"When did you figure it out?"


The question came so softly that he almost missed it.


Adam laughed.


"I didn't."


That got her attention.


"What?"


"Scott did."


Jane stared.


"No."


"Yes."


"No."


"Unfortunately yes."


She shook her head.


"How?"


Adam smiled.


"Yesterday."


A pause.


"I told him I was afraid you didn't feel the same way."


Jane's eyes widened.


"And he told me to think."


God help him.

Scott had been completely right.


"So I did."


His gaze met hers.


"I thought about every conversation."


A pause.


"Every lunch."


Another.


"The laughing."


"The arguments."


"The crying."


"The eye rolling."


"Especially the eye rolling."


Jane laughed despite herself.


Then Adam's expression softened.


"I thought about the day we met for lunch."


The laughter disappeared.


"The day we ran into Andrew."


Now she knew exactly where this was going.


"You were terrified."


Jane looked away.


"You looked at me like you were afraid I would disappear."


His voice cracked slightly.


"I think I knew then."


A pause.


"I just didn't believe it."


Then quietly:


"Am I wrong?"


Jane looked at him.

Really looked at him.


And then slowly shook her head.


"No."


One word.


But it changed everything.


Adam let out a breath he hadn't realized he'd been holding.


For a moment they simply stood there staring at one another.


Trying to process the fact that the impossible thing had happened.


And somehow that made everything harder.

Not easier.

Harder.

Because now it was real.


A few minutes later they found themselves sitting on the couch.

Side by side.

Neither speaking.


Jane looked down.

Adam did too.


Finally she sighed.


"You know..."


Adam glanced over.


"It's okay."


"What is?"


"We don't have to do anything about this."


The statement immediately horrified him.


Jane continued.


"It might just be good that we said it."


A small smile appeared.


"Out loud."


Adam stared.


The crazy thing was she actually meant it.


She would take these feelings.

Put them in a box.

Lock the box.

Then quietly suffer forever.


Which reminded him an awful lot of someone else.


The original Adam.


Apparently they had more in common than either realized.


Unfortunately for her, this Adam wasn't that selfless.


So he did the only thing that made sense.


He kissed her.


Again.


Jane made a startled noise.


When they pulled apart, Adam rested his forehead against hers.


"There is no version of this story where I do nothing."


His voice was quiet.

Steady.

Terrified.


"I hope you understand that."


Jane stared at him.


"Jane..."


His hands trembled slightly.


"I love you."


For the first time, he wasn't hiding it.

Not joking.

Not teasing.

Not deflecting.


Just honest.


"I love you so much."


His eyes shone.


"And knowing you love me back..."


He laughed softly.


"It's the best thing that's ever happened to me."


Jane's eyes immediately filled with tears.


"I know it's complicated."


"Adam—"


"I know."


He nodded.


"I know."


A pause.


"But I'm asking you to take a leap with me."


His voice almost broke.


"We'll figure it out."


Another pause.


"Whatever happens."


A final breath.


"Be with me."


Jane reached up and touched his face.


She never imagined this.

Not after Kirby.

Not after losing her baby.

Not after Andrew.

Not after all the grief that followed.


She had convinced herself she would never feel this way again.


Then somehow this impossible man had walked into her life.


Or rather...

Adam had.


And then this version had stayed.


And she couldn't walk away.


Not then.

Not now.


"Adam."


Her voice trembled.


"I love you too."


His eyes closed immediately.


Relief.


Disbelief.


Joy.


All at once.


"I didn't walk away from you before."


Jane smiled through tears.


"I'm not walking away now."


A pause.


"So okay."


Then she kissed him.


Adam laughed against her lips.

Then pulled her into a hug so tight she could barely breathe.


"So..."


Jane immediately knew that tone.


"Oh no."


His grin widened.


"So I get to call you my girlfriend?"


She laughed.


"Well..."


A pause.


"You already told Andrew I was."


Adam pointed.


"Excellent point."


Then suddenly stood.


And pulled her up with him.


"What are we doing?"


His grin became dangerous.


"We are fulfilling one of my lifelong fantasies."


Jane was immediately concerned.


"What fantasy?"


Adam pulled out his phone.


Pressed play.


The familiar opening notes started.


Jane's eyes widened.


"No."


Adam looked delighted.


"Oh yes."


"No."


"I told you."


He pulled her against him.


"There would be dancing in the kitchen."


"Adam."


"By God..."


He pointed dramatically.


"There will be dancing in the kitchen."


The music swelled around them.

*Ain't Too Proud to Beg.*


Jane laughed.

Actually laughed.


The kind she hadn't heard from herself in a very long time.


And as Adam spun her around her kitchen while singing terribly off-key, Jane realized something.


For the first time in a very long time...

She was genuinely happy.
