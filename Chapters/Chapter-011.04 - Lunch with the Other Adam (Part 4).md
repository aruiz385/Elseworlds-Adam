# Chapter 11: Lunch with the Other Adam (Part 4)

"Dr. Adam."

Karen's timing could not have been worse.

The Other Adam immediately looked up.

For a split second, Karen looked confused.

Then she noticed Jane.

Wrapped tightly against him.

Completely overwhelmed.

The confusion disappeared instantly.

He gave her a sharp look.

"Don't say a word and walk with us."

The urgency in his voice caught her attention immediately.

"I have to get her away from this place."

Karen's eyes widened.

"Andrew is in there having lunch with his lawyer girlfriend."

A pause.

"Jane is having a panic attack."

Karen didn't ask questions.

She didn't hesitate.

She simply nodded and stepped alongside them.


The three of them made their way toward the small park near the hospital.

Far enough from the restaurant.

Far enough from Andrew.

Far enough that Jane could finally breathe.

Or at least try to.


By the time they reached one of the benches, Jane's legs felt weak.

The Other Adam helped her sit down.

Karen immediately crouched beside her.

"Honey, are you okay?"

Jane couldn't answer.

"It's okay. Hey. You are okay."

Karen's voice cracked slightly.

The sight of her best friend falling apart was never something she handled well.

"God, Jane."

She shook her head.

"I knew it was bad."

A pause.

"But this is really bad."

Tears filled Karen's eyes.

"Look at us and try to breathe."

Jane nodded weakly.

Trying.

Failing.

Trying again.


Karen glanced at the Other Adam.

"I'm going to grab some water."

He nodded.

"Okay."


The moment Karen disappeared toward a nearby vendor, he dropped to one knee directly in front of Jane.


"Hey."

His voice was calm.

Steady.

Grounding.


"Jane."


She forced herself to look at him.


"You're okay."


She wasn't.

Not remotely.

But she looked anyway.


"I won't let anything happen to you."


The conviction in his voice made her want to cry again.


"Is this how you felt this morning?"


She stared at him.


"When you saw them?"


Jane slowly nodded.


The Other Adam looked heartbroken.


Not for himself.

For her.


"You need to tell Dr. Ryan this."


Jane wiped her eyes.


He wasn't letting it go.


"I mean it."


She nodded again.


"I'll help you make a list."


A faint smile touched his face.


"All the things you're supposed to tell her."


Despite herself, Jane almost laughed.


Almost.


The tears won instead.


Without thinking, she leaned forward and threw her arms around him.


The Other Adam froze briefly.

Then wrapped his arms around her.


As though she weighed nothing.


As though holding her together was the most natural thing in the world.


"You're okay."

He whispered the words into her hair.


"You're okay."


For the moment, Jane held on to him like he was the only solid thing left.


A few minutes later Karen returned carrying several bottles of water.


She slowed when she saw them.


Neither noticed her.


And for a moment Karen simply stood there.

Watching.


Her heart broke for Jane.


Because she had seen that scene before.


Not with Adam.


With Andrew.


Ever since they were children, Andrew had always been Jane's safe place.

When she was scared, she went to Andrew.

When she was overwhelmed, she went to Andrew.

When life got hard, she went to Andrew.


And now Andrew was gone.

Not dead.

Which somehow made it worse.


He was still here.

Still alive.

Still moving forward.

Still building a future.


And Jane had to watch it happen from a distance.

Every single day.


Karen had watched her grieve Kirby.

She had watched her grieve the baby.


But standing there now, she realized something.


Jane wasn't just grieving Kirby.

Or the baby.


She was grieving Andrew too.


And there was no funeral for that kind of loss.


No closure.


Just watching someone you loved move on without you.


Karen wasn't sure which pain was worse.


Eventually she approached.


"Hey honey."


Jane reluctantly pulled away from Adam.


"How are you feeling?"


Karen handed her a bottle of water.


"Here."


"Thanks, Kare."


Jane took a long drink.


"I've been better."


That was probably the understatement of the year.


"I wasn't ready."


Her voice sounded small.


"I'm not sure I ever will be."


Karen immediately wrapped her arms around her.


"Honey."


"No one is asking you to be ready."


Jane closed her eyes.


"If James and my wedding is putting that kind of pressure on you, please tell me."


A pause.


"I don't want you forcing yourself to be around Andrew if you aren't ready."


Karen squeezed her tighter.


"Jane."


Her voice cracked.


"You are important."


Another pause.


"You are my sister."


Now both of them were crying.


"My ride or die."


Jane laughed weakly through tears.


"Always."


Karen nodded.


"And while I love Andrew too, even he knows you come first."


Another tear.


"Always."


Jane smiled despite herself.


"You know I would fight him."


Karen pointed dramatically.


"And Kelly."


That earned an actual laugh.


For the first time all afternoon.


"I know, Kare."


"You don't have to fight Andrew and Kelly for me."


Karen looked unconvinced.


"I love you."

Jane's eyes softened.


"I know."


A long silence settled between them.


Then Jane took a deep breath.


"I promise you'll be my ultimate motivation to handle this."


Karen blinked.


"What?"


Jane sat up straighter.


"I'll be wedding ready."


Karen immediately laughed.


"You will?"


"I'll be the best maid of honor ever."


A pause.


"First, I need a date."


Karen frowned.


"What?"


Jane slipped into work mode without warning.


"So I can build realistic timelines."


Karen laughed so hard she nearly dropped her water bottle.


"Spoken like a true project manager."


The Other Adam looked between them.


"Is that what you do for a living?"


Jane stared.


Then rolled her eyes.


Immediately.


The Other Adam smiled.


"There it is."


Jane groaned.


"What?"


"The famous eye roll."


His smile widened.


"The one I am slowly but surely learning to love."


Karen's eyes darted between them.


Interesting.

Very interesting.


Then something occurred to her.

Something that had been bothering her since yesterday.


She turned toward Jane.

Then the Other Adam.


"Okay."


Both looked at her.


"Why did you tell me Adam was gone?"


A heavy silence followed.


Jane immediately looked toward him.

Not because she wanted him to answer.

Because she knew this wasn't her story to tell.


The Other Adam understood instantly.


And for the first time that afternoon, Karen watched the humor disappear completely from his face.


So he told her.

Everything.


About Dissociative Identity Disorder.

About Adam.

About himself.

About the videos.

About the system they had in place.

About the six-year-old patient.

About the code.

About Adam relapsing.

About stepping in when Adam couldn't.


Karen sat silently through the explanation.


By the end of it, her expression had gone from confusion to stunned disbelief.


Finally she looked him up and down.


"You do look awfully different than the guy I met."


The Other Adam laughed.


"I thought maybe you'd gained muscle or something in the last few months."


That earned a genuine smile.


"I'll take that as a compliment."


"It was mostly a compliment."


Karen tilted her head.


"I do have to admit something."


Here it came.


"The other you made me blush."


Immediately Jane groaned.


Karen ignored her.


"This you."

She pointed at him.


"You are so aloof."


The Other Adam laughed.


"What's up with that?"


A shrug.


"Charm?"


Another shrug.


"Nerves?"


A third.


"Anxiety at accidentally walking into the center of Jane's friend group?"


Karen burst out laughing.


"I don't know why Adam does some of the things he does honestly."


The conversation continued.

But the Other Adam found his attention drifting.


Back to Jane.


To the woman now laughing quietly with her best friend.


To the woman he'd watched from a distance for months.


And for the first time since taking over, he truly understood something Adam had never been able to explain.


Watching Jane struggle had been difficult.


Watching her panic had been worse.


Because now it wasn't theoretical.


Now it was real.


And seeing it in person changed everything.


He understood why Adam cared.


More importantly, he understood why Adam couldn't let her go.


Because despite every defense she built.

Every wall.

Every excuse.


Jane was still standing.

Still fighting.

Still trying.


And that mattered.


He knew one thing with absolute certainty.


Jane wanted Adam back.


Of course she did.


But he saw something else too.


There was a chance she needed him.


And as long as she did—

he wasn't going anywhere.
