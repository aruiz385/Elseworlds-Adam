# Chapter 40: Coming Home


The next morning, Jane and Lizzy were finally cleared to go home.


Adam had mixed feelings about it.


On one hand, he couldn't wait.


On the other hand...


The hospital suddenly felt safe.


Predictable.


Controlled.


Home meant reality.


Home meant sleepless nights.


Home meant figuring everything out.


And somehow that was more terrifying than the emergency C-section.


Melissa and Marty arrived early.


So did Scott.


And Andrew.


They immediately settled into the room around Jane and Lizzy.


Making themselves useful.


Or trying to.


Adam wasn't entirely sure which.


"Go."


Melissa pointed at him.


"We've got this."


Adam looked around.


Scott.


Andrew.


Marty.


Melissa.


And Jane.


Who looked surprisingly entertained.


"Go do whatever it is new fathers do before they bring home babies."


Adam laughed.


"I have no idea what that is."


"Neither does anybody else."


Melissa smiled.


"Now go."


So Adam left.


His first call was to Karen.


She answered immediately.


"How's my goddaughter?"


"Alive."


Another.


"Healthy."


Another.


"And coming home today."


A loud squeal followed.


Adam immediately held the phone farther from his ear.


"Perfect."


Another.


"We'll have everything ready."


Adam closed his eyes.


"Karen."


"No."


Another.


"I know that tone."


Another.


"There will absolutely be a welcome-home celebration."


Adam sighed.


Of course there would.


"Karen."


Another.


"I was hoping for something quiet."


She laughed.


"Oh honey."


Another.


"You clearly haven't met us."


Adam laughed despite himself.


"No."


Another.


"I definitely have."


Karen informed him that James and Chris were getting food.


Kelly was helping decorate.


Maria was bringing dessert.


Apparently all of this had already been decided.


Without consulting him.


Naturally.


After hanging up, Adam headed to Jane's apartment.


The moment he stepped inside, the silence felt strange.


The apartment was waiting.


Waiting for Jane.


Waiting for Lizzy.


Waiting for life to resume.


He checked the nursery first.


The crib.


The changing station.


The supplies.


Everything looked ready.


Then he found himself standing in the doorway to the master bedroom.


And stopped.


He hadn't really allowed himself to look before.


Not properly.


The king-size bed sat in the center of the room.


Comfortable.


Lived in.


Familiar.


His eyes drifted toward the closet.


And froze.


His clothes.


Still hanging exactly where they'd always been.


Shirts.


Jackets.


Jeans.


All neatly arranged on one side.


For a second his mind automatically corrected itself.


Not yours.


His.


Then another memory surfaced.


A real one.


Not from a recording.


Not a video.


Jane standing beside him while they organized this closet.


Laughing because he owned too many white button-downs.


Adam smiled to himself.


Then another memory followed.


The first night she'd asked him to stay.


Standing awkwardly in this room.


Asking if she was sure.


Reminding her that he could disappear.


That nothing about them was guaranteed.


Jane hadn't hesitated.


Not even for a second.


She'd simply told him she wanted him there.


She wanted the room to feel like theirs.


And she promised she wouldn't do what she'd done after Kirby died.


She wouldn't lock the bedroom away.


Looking around now...


She'd kept that promise.


The room looked lived in.


Loved.


His eyes landed on the nightstand.


A photograph.


Karen and James's wedding.


Jane smiling.


Him smiling back.


The photographer had caught them dancing.


Adam remembered the moment instantly.


A genuine memory.


The feeling of her hand.


The music.


The laughter.


He sat briefly on the edge of the bed.


Holding the frame.


Thinking.


Four years ago he had been a resident trying to survive.


Then he'd walked into Scott's office.


And there was Jane.


A patient.


A challenge.


A woman determined to hide behind walls.


His job had been simple.


Convince her that group therapy helped.


Get her to attend one.


That was it.


And now...


There was a baby.


Their baby.


Life was strange.


A box near the wall caught his attention.


A bassinet.


Still unassembled.


Jane had probably intended to get to it.


Then life had happened.


Adam smiled.


"I got it."


An hour later the bassinet stood assembled beside Jane's side of the bed.


Sheets fitted.


Blanket folded.


Ready.


He found the baby monitor system next.


One transmitter.


Two receivers.


One stayed in the bedroom.


The other went in the living room.


Everything ready.


Everything waiting.


Then he showered.


The hot water helped.


A little.


Afterward he opened the closet.


White button-down shirt.


Jeans.


Simple.


Comfortable.


His glasses.


The jacket he'd left hanging in the living room.


As he dressed, another thought surfaced.


This isn't your stuff.


Immediately followed by another.


Yes it is.


Because he remembered buying it.


Remembered putting it away.


Remembered wearing it.


The distinction was becoming harder every day.


Before leaving, he stopped briefly at his own apartment.


Opened a drawer.


And pulled out a small heart-shaped ring.


The ring Jane had handed back.


The one he'd promised to keep safe.


The one he'd promised would someday find its way back to her hand.


He slipped it into his pocket.


Grabbed the car seat.


Installed the base.


Double-checked it.


Then headed back to the hospital.


When he walked into the room, conversation immediately stopped.


Jane looked up first.


And gasped.


Adam blinked.


Confused.


White shirt.


Jeans.


Glasses.


Nothing unusual.


At least not to him.


But to Jane...


For one brief second...


He looked exactly like the man she'd spent three years loving.


Her stomach tightened.


Yesterday's conversation immediately came rushing back.


The memories.


The integration.


The things he'd remembered.


The things she didn't know how to process.


"Everything okay?"


Adam asked.


Jane immediately recovered.


Her parents were in the room.


She forced a smile.


"Yeah."


Another.


"Just surprised."


Adam accepted the answer.


But he wasn't the only one who noticed.


Across the room Scott looked up.


Then Andrew.


Both of them following Jane's line of sight.


Both of them seeing exactly what she saw.


The white shirt.


The glasses.


The posture.


The smile.


For one brief second neither man said anything.


Then they exchanged a look.


One of those annoying silent conversations they seemed capable of having.


Andrew stood first.


"Well."


Scott immediately stood too.


"We should probably get going."


Adam frowned.


"What?"


Andrew pointed toward the door.


"Karen called."


A dramatic sigh escaped him.


"Apparently we're required for homecoming preparation duty."


Scott nodded solemnly.


"We've been summoned."


Another.


"Resistance is futile."


Adam laughed.


"Traitors."


"Correct."


Andrew crossed to Jane first.


Leaning down carefully to hug her.


As he pulled away, he lowered his voice.


"Good luck."


Jane immediately narrowed her eyes.


Because she knew exactly what he meant.


And judging by the look on Andrew's face...


So did he.


Which meant Scott probably did too.


Wonderful.


Just wonderful.


Andrew's grin widened.


The kind that promised this conversation would absolutely become a Tuesday lunch topic.


At length.


"Not a word."


Jane muttered.


"No promises."


He kissed the top of her head.


Then headed for the door.


Scott followed.


Leaving Adam completely unaware that an entire conversation had just happened around him.


A few minutes later it was only Adam, Jane, Lizzy, and her parents.


Adam walked over.


Sat carefully beside Jane.


Then kissed the top of her head.


"Hey."


Jane smiled.


"Hey."


"How are you feeling?"


She considered it.


"More like a person today."


Another.


"Thank you."


Adam smiled.


Then leaned toward Lizzy.


"Hi beautiful."


Another kiss on her forehead.


"How are you doing?"


Lizzy remained asleep.


Completely unimpressed.


Jane shook her head.


"She gets that from you."


Adam laughed.


Then reached into his pocket.


Jane immediately narrowed her eyes.


That expression.


That exact expression.


Suspicious.


Annoyed.


Already preparing herself for whatever nonsense he was about to unleash.


And suddenly he found himself smiling.


Because he remembered that look.


Not from recordings.


Not from videos.


From memory.


Real memory.


Jane rolling her eyes because she already knew she wasn't going to like whatever he was about to do.


The moment she saw the ring, her eyes widened.


Absolutely not.


The message was immediate.


Melissa gasped.


Marty smiled.


Completely oblivious to the silent war breaking out between the two people sitting on the hospital bed.


Adam took Jane's hand.


Carefully.


Then slid the heart-shaped ring back onto her finger.


Exactly where it had once been.


For a second Jane simply stared.


Too stunned to react.


Too aware of her parents watching.


Too aware of the way Adam was looking at her.


"Look what I found at home."


His voice was warm.


Soft.


As if this was the most natural thing in the world.


Jane opened her mouth.


Nothing came out.


Adam smiled.


The real smile.


The one she'd been seeing more often lately.


Then he looked directly into her eyes.


"I think this belongs to you."


The room faded.


For a moment it felt like nobody else existed.


Just the two of them.


His thumb brushed gently across her hand.


"It always has."


A pause.


"It always will."


The teasing disappeared from his expression.


Replaced by something much more serious.


Something that immediately unsettled her.


Because she knew that look.


And she wasn't ready for it.


Not after the conversations they'd been having.


Not after labor.


Not after everything he'd told her.


Melissa looked seconds away from crying.


"Oh, that is so sweet."


Marty nodded approvingly.


"About time."


Jane closed her eyes.


Of course.


Of course they would encourage him.


When she opened them again, he was still smiling.


But the teasing had faded.


Just for a second.


Long enough for her to recognize something underneath it.


The promise.


The one he'd made before everything fell apart.


I'll keep it safe.


And one day, when you're ready, I'll put it back on.


The look in his eyes finished the thought.


I meant it.


The irritation softened.


Not much.


But enough.


Because she knew that look too.


And suddenly she realized something.


He wasn't doing this to show off for her parents.


He wasn't doing it to force a conversation.


He was doing it because somehow, impossibly, he remembered making that promise.


And keeping promises had always mattered to him.


Jane responded with a spectacular eye roll.


The kind that should have earned at least a little guilt.


Instead he laughed.


Which only made her roll her eyes harder.


And that made him smile even more.


Because if there was one thing he remembered loving about Jane...


It was that look.


Deep down, Adam knew they would argue about this later.


She would absolutely try to give the ring back.


Probably more than once.


That wasn't a question.


It was a certainty.


But that was a conversation for another day.


Today she was coming home.


Today Lizzy was coming home.


And today, for the first time in a very long time, Adam stopped feeling like a visitor in his own life.


Andrew's words echoed in his head.


The other you never let fear make the decision.


Adam looked at Jane.


At the woman sitting in the hospital bed.


The woman who looked exhausted.


Confused.


Overwhelmed.


And beautiful.


The woman he had loved for four years.


The woman he remembered loving for four years.


The distinction mattered less and less every day.


Jane was scared.


He understood that.


Honestly?


So was he.


But fear had already stolen enough from both of them.


It had stolen years.


It had stolen possibilities.


It had built walls.


And he remembered those walls.


Not from stories.


Not from videos.


From memory.


He remembered earning her trust.


Slowly.


Patiently.


One conversation at a time.


One argument.


One laugh.


One impossible moment after another.


He remembered watching her lower those walls.


Remembered her choosing him.


Remembered the ring.


Remembered the promise.


And sitting there beside her hospital bed, watching the irritation and confusion battle across her face, Adam made a quiet decision.


He was done stepping aside.


Not because he wanted to pressure her.


Not because he expected anything from her.


But because he loved her.


And because he loved Lizzy.


His daughter.


Their daughter.


Whatever happened next...


Whatever difficult conversations waited for them...


Whatever Dr. Ryan had to explain...


Whatever fears Jane still carried...


He would be there.


He would fight for Lizzy.


And if Jane would let him...


He would fight for her too.


One day.


One conversation.


One wall at a time.


Just like he had before.


Because some promises were worth keeping.


And some people were worth fighting for.


Jane rolled her eyes at him again.


And Adam smiled.


For now...


That was enough.
