# Chapter 15: Complicated (Part 1)

Adam spent the rest of his shift wondering if he had completely lost his mind.

The charts in front of him blurred together.

Patient notes.

Orders.

Discharge summaries.

He completed them automatically, years of training allowing his hands to work while his mind remained somewhere else entirely.

With Jane.

Always Jane.

The panic in her eyes.

The relief when she'd seen him.

The way she'd said she didn't want to lose him.

The kisses.

Plural.

That part remained difficult to ignore.


What had he done?


Should he have done it?


The question repeated itself over and over.


The truth was that he couldn't help himself.


Jane cared about him.

Deeply.

He'd known it before today, but hearing it out loud had changed something.

She'd been terrified.

Not of him.

Of losing him.


And he loved her.


He'd known that for months.

Maybe longer than he wanted to admit.

The realization hadn't arrived all at once.

It had crept up on him.

Coffee conversations.

Eye rolls.

Stories.

Lunches.

Music.

The way she worried about everyone except herself.

The way she somehow managed to keep moving despite carrying so much grief.


He loved her.

There wasn't really any point pretending otherwise.


The problem was that loving Jane made him selfish.

For the first time in his life, he wanted something for himself.

He wanted to stay.

He wanted this life.

He wanted these moments.

He wanted her.


And that thought filled him with guilt.

Because eventually Adam would come back.

Wouldn't he?

He had to.

This wasn't his life.

Not really.


But that was a problem for another day.

Today he still had Jane.

Today she still smiled when she saw him.

Today she still rolled her eyes at him.

Today she still wanted him around.


For now, that was enough.


Across town, Jane wasn't doing much better.


She spent most of the workday staring at her computer.

The same spreadsheet had been open for nearly twenty minutes.

Nothing had been accomplished.


Every road led back to the same problem.


The kisses.


Plural.


The memory made her immediately bury her face in her hands.


"Oh my God."


Several coworkers glanced over.

Jane ignored them.


Why did I let him do that?


She groaned softly.


And then he just kept talking like nothing happened.


Another groan.


Then he did it again.


Worse.

She kissed him back.

Twice.


The realization made her want to hide under her desk permanently.


What am I doing?


The entire situation was absurd.

Impossible.

Completely irrational.


She was supposed to be helping Adam.

The original Adam.

Her friend.

The man who'd brought her to therapy.


Not his alter.

Not the one she never intended to get close to.

Not the one she'd somehow fallen for.


The very thought made her chest tighten.


Because that was the truth.

Wasn't it?


At some point she stopped trying to deny it.


She had feelings for him.


And she didn't have the faintest idea what to do about them.


By six o'clock she practically sprinted out of work.


She was determined to keep the evening casual.

Completely casual.


This was not a date.


It was a movie.

A therapy exercise.

Nothing more.


Two people sitting on a couch crossing something off her trigger list.


That was all.


That explanation lasted exactly six minutes before she changed outfits three times.


Eventually she settled on what she privately called comfort wear.


An oversized sweater.

Black tights.

Hair in a ponytail.

Glasses.


The uniform she wore whenever she was hiding from the world.


Pizza was ordered.

The apartment was tidied.

Then retidied.

Then tidied again.


Her movie list sat neatly on the kitchen island.

Ready.


Jane checked the clock.


Then checked it again.


Then again.


And once more.


Eventually an unwelcome thought appeared.


What if he doesn't show up?


Her stomach dropped instantly.


What if this is too much?


The memory of Thrive City hit harder than she expected.


Waiting.

Checking her phone.

The empty chair.


No.

Stop.


The doorbell rang.


Jane froze.


Then let out a breath she hadn't realized she'd been holding for nearly an hour.


Her heart skipped three beats.


The second she opened the door, she wanted to be annoyed.

Instead she found herself staring.


It wasn't fair.


It genuinely wasn't fair.


The man was wearing sweatpants.

And a t-shirt.


That was it.


Yet somehow he still looked like he belonged on the cover of a magazine.


Glasses included.


The original Adam had never affected her like this.

Not once.


Because they were different people.

Entirely different people.


And the fact that she knew that instinctively probably explained most of the problem.


"Hey."

He smiled.


"You made it."


Then she looked him up and down.


"And you actually dressed down."


Adam looked at his clothes.


Then back at her.


"So this is what you look like in your natural habitat."


Jane immediately frowned.


"I don't know what that means."


"It sounded good in my head."


She laughed.


"I call it home wear."


Then stepped aside.


"Come on in."


Adam walked into the apartment.


"So this is where you live."


His eyes scanned the room.


"I thought it would look different."


Jane's apartment was neat.

Almost too neat.


The living room opened directly into the kitchen.

A couch covered in blankets.

A loveseat.

A recliner.

A television mounted on the wall.

The kitchen island separating the two spaces.


It looked lived in.

Sort of.


And somehow empty at the same time.


Adam noticed immediately.


The couch looked used.

The blankets looked used.


The rest didn't.


Like someone occupied the space without truly living in it.


His attention drifted toward the photographs.


A wedding picture.

Jane and Kirby.


A photograph of three children sitting beneath a tree.


One of a much younger Jane and Andrew standing with Karen and James.


Family photographs.

School photographs.

Memories.


Then his eyes settled on a silver frame.


Two photographs.

One showing a young boy and girl.

The other a teenage Jane and Andrew.


Beneath them sat a quote.


*"I have learned that love does not disappear; it changes shape."*

—Isabel Allende


Adam picked it up.

Reading it again.


For the first time, seeing all of these photographs together, the reality of Jane and Andrew's shared history fully settled over him.


Not years.


A lifetime.


"Christmas gift."


Jane's voice broke his concentration.


He looked up.


She'd been watching him.


"I like the quote."


A sad smile crossed her face.


"I gave Andrew the frame one Christmas."


Adam remained silent.


"A year later he gave it back."


Her expression softened.


"With the pictures."


A pause.


"When we broke up and tried being friends again, I made him another one."


Adam looked surprised.


"You did?"


She nodded.


"Same pictures."


A pause.


"I added another quote."


"What quote?"


Jane smiled sadly.


*"You are my home, now and forever."*


Adam's chest tightened.


Another Isabel Allende quote.


"It's actually really beautiful."


Jane took the frame gently and returned it to the table.


"I'm sure he appreciated it at the time."


The wording didn't escape him.


At the time.


The sadness in her voice was impossible to miss.


So he changed the subject.

Before either of them got lost in it.


"Are you hungry?"

Jane asked.


"The pizza is waiting."


Then she pointed toward the movie list.


"And we've got work to do."


Adam smiled.


Maybe.

Just maybe.

Tonight could still be simple.


They were both wrong.
