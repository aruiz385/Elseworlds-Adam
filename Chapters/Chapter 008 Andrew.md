# Chapter 8: Andrew

By the time spring settled over San Francisco, Thursday afternoons had become routine.

Group therapy.

Coffee.

Conversation.

Then back to real life.

Neither Jane nor Adam questioned it anymore.

The routine simply existed.

Comfortable.

Reliable.

Safe.

That afternoon, group therapy finished later than usual.

A few people had shared particularly difficult stories.

By the time Jane and Adam found their usual coffee shop, both were quieter than normal.

For a while neither spoke.

Jane stirred her coffee.

Adam watched her.

Eventually he broke the silence.

"You look like you're thinking."

Jane snorted.

"That's never a good sign."

"Usually not."

She laughed.

But only briefly.

Because there really was something on her mind.

Something she'd been avoiding for weeks.

Months, technically.

Maybe longer.

"Honestly," she said finally, "sometimes I think my story sounds like the making of a villain's origin story."

Adam looked up immediately.

There was no amusement on his face this time.

Only curiosity.

"You really do believe you're the villain in your story, don't you?"

Jane looked down at her coffee.

"I am."

"No."

"Adam."

"No."

Jane sighed.

"I technically cheated on my fiancé."

Adam remained silent.

"I failed him after six years where he chose me every day."

Her voice softened.

"And now he's dead."


Silence.

"He was my best friend."

Adam watched her carefully.

The pain in her eyes looked old.

Familiar.

Like something she'd repeated to herself a thousand times.

Then Jane took a deep breath.

"I have to tell you something."

Adam blinked.

"Okay."

She looked unusually nervous.

Which immediately made him nervous.

"I need to tell you about Andrew."

Adam immediately frowned.

"Jane, you really don't have to tell me about your ex."

"I do."

His confusion grew.

"Why?"

Because this was the part she'd been avoiding.

The part that somehow made everything more complicated.

The part that sounded ridiculous every time she explained it aloud.

"Because Andrew works with you."

Adam froze.

"What?"

Jane nodded.

"Andrew works with you."

For a full five seconds he simply stared at her.

Then realization hit.

"Oh no."

Jane closed her eyes.

"Yeah."

"Oh no."

"Yep."

Adam sat back in his chair.

Completely stunned.

"Wait."

Jane nodded.

"No."

"Unfortunately yes."

"Super Cop turned med student Andrew?"

Jane groaned.

"That Andrew?"

"That Andrew."

Adam looked genuinely horrified.

"This sounds like a subplot from a terrible soap opera."

Jane laughed despite herself.

"I know."

"No."

Adam shook his head.

"I refuse."

"I'm serious."

Adam pointed at her.

"No. Explain everything."

Unfortunately, explaining everything took a while.

She told him about growing up with Andrew.

About Kirby.

About college.

About losing touch.

About finding their way back to each other.

About losing Kirby.

About losing the baby.

About trying to make things work.

About letting him go.

By the time she finished, Adam looked emotionally exhausted.

"I need a diagram."

Jane laughed.

"I told you."

"No."

He pointed again.

"This is worse than I imagined."

Jane smiled for the first time all afternoon.

Then Adam asked the question he couldn't stop thinking about.

"If he's your ex..."

Jane already knew where this was going.

"Yes."

"You've been avoiding him."

"Yes."

"How?"

Jane looked confused.

"What do you mean how?"

"Jane."

Adam leaned forward.

"That man works at the hospital."

"I know."

"You come here constantly."

"I know."

"He also lives in my building."

Adam froze.

"He what?"

Jane sighed.

"He lives directly above me."

Adam nearly choked on his coffee.

"No."

"Yes."

"No."

"Adam."

"Directly above you?"

"Penthouse."

"Above your apartment."

"Correct."

Adam stared at her.

Then slowly shook his head.

"You've managed to avoid your ex for over a year while he lives directly above you and works at the same hospital?"

Jane nodded.

Adam looked genuinely impressed.

"You're either a ninja or the luckiest person alive."

For the first time, the conversation didn't feel painful.

It felt absurd.

Because hearing it from Adam's perspective made the entire situation sound ridiculous.

Eventually Adam settled back into his chair.

"Andrew seems nice."

Jane froze.

"What?"

"The little I've seen."

Adam shrugged.

"Broody."

Jane immediately laughed.

"That's accurate."

"Intense."

She laughed harder.

"Also accurate."

"Looks like he carries the entire world on his shoulders."

The laughter disappeared.

Because that was also true.

Adam noticed.

"I'm sorry."

"No."

Jane shook her head.

"You're right."

Silence settled between them.

Then Adam asked softly,

"If he's a good guy..."

Jane looked down.

"He is."

"Then why are you so afraid to see him?"

The question lingered.

Eventually Jane answered.

"Because Andrew and I have a terrible habit of blowing up each other's worlds and taking everyone around with us."

Adam studied her carefully.

"So this is safer."

Jane nodded.

"This is safer."

Neither of them noticed it then.

But that afternoon marked a shift.

For the first time since they'd met, Adam wasn't just hearing about Jane's grief.

He was hearing about Jane's life.

And for the first time, he was beginning to understand how much of that life still revolved around a man she was desperately trying not to think about.

For a few moments neither spoke.

Adam sat back in his chair and tried to process everything she'd just told him.

The more he learned about Jane's life, the more convinced he became that someone had taken several different dramatic stories and somehow combined them into one.

Finally he pointed at her.

"You do realize none of this sounds real."

Jane laughed.

"I know."

"No, seriously."

Adam shook his head.

"You've somehow managed to create the most complicated relationship map I've ever heard."

"I wasn't trying."

"I'm aware."


His coffee had gone completely cold.

Neither cared.

That happened a lot now.

They'd sit down for coffee and three hours would disappear.

"So let e make sure I understand."

Adam raised a finger.

"Andrew is your ex."

"Correct."

"Your best friend James is Andrew's best friend."

"Correct."

"Karen is your best friend."

"Correct."

"Your ex-boyfriend lives directly above you."

"Unfortunately."

"His current girlfriend is Kelly."

Jane groaned.

"Yes."

Adam leaned forward.

"Tell me about Kelly."

Jane immediately made a face.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because then I sound crazy."

"I promise you that after everything you've told me today, whatever comes next cannot possibly make this worse."

Jane looked at him.

Then laughed.

Then sighed.

"Fine."

Adam settled in.

"Kelly is Andrew's old college girlfriend."

"Okay."

"She's also the girl Kirby slept with when we briefly broke up in college."

Adam stared.

"Absolutely not."

Jane laughed.

"Yes."

"No."

"Adam."

"I reject this information."

"You can't."

"I can."

"You literally asked."

Adam rubbed both hands over his face.

"This is impossible."

"It gets worse."

"Oh, good."

His tone was completely deadpan.

"Please continue."

Jane laughed harder.

And Adam realized he liked that sound.

Way more than he probably should.

"After Kirby and I got back together, Kelly eventually started dating Andrew."

Adam just stared.

For several seconds.

Then pointed at her.

"Your life is ridiculous."

"I know."

"No."

He shook his head.

"I need you to understand something."

"What?"

"If somebody pitched this movie, nobody would believe it."

Jane put her head on the table.

"That's fair."

Adam laughed.

Then his expression softened.

"So you're angry at her?"

The question lingered.

Jane thought about it.

Really thought about it.

"No."

The answer surprised her.

"I'm not."

"Really?"

Jane nodded.

"I think for a long time I was."

Then she sighed.

"But honestly I don't think I'm mad at Kelly anymore."

Adam waited.

"She's just a trigger."

He immediately understood.

Not because he had some incredible medical insight.

Because he knew exactly what triggers felt like.

"Those are two very different things."

Jane nodded.

"I know."

"That sounds difficult."

"It is."

Neither spoke for a moment.

Then Adam asked the question that had been sitting in the back of his mind since she started talking.

"Do you still love him?"


Jane froze.

Not because she was offended.

Because the answer came immediately.

"Yes."

Adam stayed very still.

Jane looked down at her coffee.

"I am always going to love Andrew."

The confession felt strangely easy.

"He was my first everything."

She smiled sadly.

"My first best friend."

A pause.

"My first kiss."

Another pause.

"My first love."

Adam listened quietly.

"It doesn't mean I should be with him."

"No."

"It doesn't mean letting him go was wrong."

Adam nodded.

"But I am always going to love him."

Adam looked at her for a long moment.

Then simply said:

"That seems normal."

Jane blinked.

"What?"

"I said it seems normal."

She stared.

"You do't think that's unhealthy?"

Adam looked genuinely confused.

"Why would I?"

Jane had no answer.

"Someone can love somebody forever and still know they shouldn't be together."

The statement hit harder than she expected.

Because nobody had ever said that before.

Eventually she looked at him.

"How are you so good at this?"

Adam laughed.

"I'm not."

"That's a lie."

"It is."

They both smiled.

A few minutes later, Jane pointed at him.

"You know what? We've spent the last hour talking about my life."

Adam immediately looked concerned.

"Oh no."

"Oh yes."

"Jane."

"No."

"I'm not nearly as interesting."

"That is not my problem."

She pointed again.

"Who is Adam Carter?"

Adam sat back in his chair.

"Honestly?"

"That's generally what I'm looking for."

He smiled.

"I'm just a guy trying to do the best he can.

The conversation drifted.

To his childhood.

His father.

Books.

Movies.

Travel.

Medicine.

Doctors Without Borders.

All the things that made him who he was.

Jane found herself listening intently.

Not because she was being polite.

Because she genuinely wanted to know.


At some point Adam said:

"I actively try to avoid emotional attachments."

That made her laugh.

"What?"

"You literally spend your free time helping strangers."

"That's different."

"It is not."

"It absolutely is."

Jane rolled her eyes.

"There it is."

Adam pointed.

She laughed.

Then Adam became thoughtful.

"You know..."

"What?"

"The truth is I don't really remember a lot of my pre-med years."

Jane frowned.

"What do you mean?"

And for the next twenty minutes Adam told her more about the other guy than he ever had before.

The memory gaps.

The videos.

The messages.

The agreement they'd made.

Then he said something that immediately caught her attention.

"The other guy reminds me of Andrew."

Jane froze.

"What?"

Adam nodded.

"Not exactly."

He searched for the words.

"But they're similar."

Jane couldn't imagine that.

And yet somehow she wanted to understand.

"You still haven't shown me."

Adam grinned immediately.

"There it is."

"What?"

"The real reason you keep coming to therapy.

Jane laughed.

"Maybe."

"Maybe."

They sat there smiling at each other.

Neither realizing how much time had passed.

Eventually Jane glanced at her watch and blinked.

"Oh my God."

"What?"

"We've been here for three hours."

Adam looked at his own watch.

Three hours.

Neither of them knew how that happened anymore.

Before she stood, Jane hesitated.

Then said:

"We only see each other on Thursdays."

Adam looked up.

There was something hopeful in her voice.

Something uncertain.

"Yeah."

Jane took a breath.

"Do you want to do something before then?"

For the first time all afternoon, Adam looked genuinely surprised.

Then he smiled.

"Yeah."

"I'd like that."

And neither of them realized it then.

But that single question changed everything that came next.

For a moment neither spoke.

The conversation had shifted somewhere along the way.

It wasn't therapy anymore.

It wasn't grief.

It wasn't Andrew.

It wasn't Kirby.

It was simply two people sitting across from each other spending entirely too much time talking.

Jane couldn't remember the last time that had happened.

Maybe before Kirby died.

Maybe before everything fell apart.

"You know," she said suddenly.

Adam looked up.

"What?"

"You really don't need to keep bribing me."

His eyebrows lifted.

"I don't?"

Jane laughed.

"No."

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

Adam leaned back dramatically.

"That's disappointing."

"Why?"

"It's one of my best skills."

"You have terrible skills."

Adam put a hand dramatically over his heart.

"You wound me."

Jane rolled her eyes.

Immediately.

"There it is."

She laughed.

"No, seriously."

Adam smiled.

"You know you don't come to therapy because you're hoping to meet my broody alter ego anymore, right?"

Jane shook her head.

"Yeah."

The answer surprised her.

Mostly because it was true.

At first she had been curious.

Then interested.

Then invested.

But somewhere along the way, the mystery of the Other Guy had stopped being the main reason she kept showing up.

Now she showed up because therapy was helping.

Because she wanted to.

Because she wasn't completely drowning anymore.

And because...

Well.

Because she enjoyed these conversations.

"I figured that out a while ago."

Adam smiled.

For some reason that answer seemed to make him happy.

"Good."

They sat quietly for a moment.

The comfortable kind of silence they had somehow developed.

Not awkward.

Not forced.

Just easy.

Then Jane looked at him.

"What?"

Adam asked.

"Nothing."


"Jane."

She smiled.

"What?"

"You're doing that thing."

"What thing?"

"The thing where you're thinking something and pretending you're not."

Jane laughed.

"You really are annoying."

"So I've been told."

She shook her head.

"I was just thinking..."

His expression softened.

"About?"

Jane hesitated.

A little embarrassed suddenly.

"About how much I like hanging out with you."

The words came out before she could stop them.

Immediately she looked away.

"Oh my God."

"What?"

"I didn't mean..."

Adam was staring at her.

Not shocked.

Not uncomfortable.

Just looking at her.

Jane groaned.

"Please ignore that."

"No."

"Adam."

"No."

She covered her face.

"I hate everything."

Adam laughed.

"I don't."

Jane peeked at him through her fingers.

Unfortunately for her, he looked genuinely happy.

"Jane, it's okay."

"No, it isn't."

"It really is."

"It was weird."

“It wasn't weird."

"It was absolutely weird."

Adam shook his head.

"For the record..."

Jane immediately regretted everything.

"Oh no."

"For the record, I like hanging out with you too."

And just like that, she forgot what she was about to say.


The statement landed quietly between them.

Simple.

Honest.

Unexpectedly meaningful.

Neither tried to analyze it.

Neither seemed interested in ruining the moment.

Instead Jane glanced at her watch.

Then blinked.

"Oh no."

Adam looked confused.

"What?"

"We've been here almost three hours."

Adam checked his own watch.

Three hours.

Again.

He laughed.

"That seems to happen a lot."

"It probably shouldn't."

"It probably shouldn't."

Neither looked particularly concerned.

The realization made Jane smile.

Then an idea occurred to her.

"You said you like music."

Adam nodded.

"I do."

"Have you ever been to Thrive City?"

Adam looked genuinely puzzled.

"No."

"Really?"

"Really."

Jane stared at him.

"You've lived here for two years."

"I've been busy."

"That is such a doctor answer."

"It's true."

Jane immediately became more animated.

More excited.

"They have live music during lunch sometimes."

Adam listened.

"You can sit outside."

He nodded.

"People bring food."

Another nod.

"There are coffee shops nearby."

That got his attention.

"There it is."

"What?"

"The most important detail."

Jane laughed.

"Obviously."

For a moment she hesitated.

Then decided not to overthink it.

"If you're free tomorrow..."

Adam looked up immediately.

"...we could check it out."

There it was.

Out in the open.

The invitation.

No therapy.

No group session.

No hospital.

Just lunch.

Adam felt an immediate surge of excitement that surprised him.

He tried not to show it.

Failed.

"I'd like that."

Jane smiled.

"Yeah?"

"Yeah."

The answer came far too quickly for either of them to pretend otherwise.

Jane laughed.


"Good."

"When?"

"Tomorrow around noon?"

"I'm off."

Jane blinked.

"You actually have a day off?"

"I know."

Adam shook his head.

"Rare event."

"Should we meet there?"

Adam thought about offering to pick her up.

Then thought better of it.

"Meet there."

"Okay."

"Okay."

And suddenly both of them were smiling like they'd just planned something much more significant than lunch.

Neither understood why.

When they finally stood to leave, Jane felt lighter than she had in a long time.

Not healed.

Not fixed.

Just lighter.

Adam watched her walk toward the parking garage before heading back toward the hospital.

He had no idea why he was looking forward to tomorrow so much.

Neither of them knew that by this time tomorrow, everything would be different.
