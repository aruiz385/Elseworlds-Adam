# Chapter 73: Stuck With Us


Andrew was sitting alone in the physicians' lounge when Adam found him.


Coffee in hand.


Chart open.


Not reading a single word.


Just staring at the screen.


Thinking.


Which was usually dangerous.


Adam walked in, carrying two coffees.


Without a word, he set one in front of Andrew and sat down.


For a while neither spoke.


They'd known each other too long for silence to feel uncomfortable.


Finally Andrew looked up.


"Jane told you."


It wasn't a question.


Adam nodded.


"She told me."


Andrew sighed.


Of course she had.


For a few moments neither said anything.


Then Adam spoke.


"How are you doing?"


Andrew laughed softly.


The kind of laugh people made when they didn't actually know the answer.


"I don't know."


Adam nodded.


"Fair."


Another silence settled between them.


Finally Adam asked:


"Can I ask you something?"


Andrew looked suspicious immediately.


"That depends."


Adam smiled faintly.


"How does pushing Jane away help you deal with Kelly leaving?"


Andrew immediately groaned.


"That's not what I'm doing."


"Okay."


Adam nodded.


Genuinely.


Then he took a sip of coffee.


"Then help me understand."


Andrew looked down.


Already uncomfortable.


"It's not about Jane."


"Then why is Jane the one you're setting boundaries with?"


Andrew rubbed a hand over his face.


Because that was exactly the problem.


Wasn't it?


Adam stayed quiet.


Waiting.


Eventually Andrew sighed.


"It's complicated."


Adam laughed softly.


"That's a terrible answer and you know it."


A reluctant smile tugged at Andrew's mouth.


Then disappeared.


For a long moment he stared into his coffee.


When he finally spoke, his voice was quieter.


"I thought I was okay."


Adam didn't interrupt.


"I thought I'd dealt with everything."


Another.


"Kirby."


Another.


"Jane."


Another.


"All of it."


Another.


"I thought I moved on."


He laughed bitterly.


"Then Kelly left."


Silence.


Andrew looked away.


"I keep replaying everything."


Another.


"Every decision."


Another.


"Every mistake."


Another.


"The accident."


Another.


"That day at UCLA."


Another.


"All of it."


Adam nodded slowly.


Listening.


Not judging.


Just listening.


Finally he said:


"I don't think this is really about Kelly."


Andrew's eyes immediately dropped to the table.


And that told Adam everything he needed to know.


"Andrew."


His voice softened.


"You're my friend."


Another.


"I'm not asking because I'm judging you."


Another.


"I'm asking because I care."


Another.


"And because I'm trying to understand."


The room fell quiet.


Finally Andrew exhaled.


"I don't know what the hell I'm doing."


Adam nodded.


"Yeah."


Another.


"I figured."


For a moment neither spoke.


Then Adam leaned forward.


"Can I tell you what worries me?"


Andrew shrugged.


"Sure."


Adam thought carefully.


Choosing every word.


"Last night Jane asked me if she made the wrong choice."


Andrew froze.


Immediately.


"What?"


Adam nodded.


"She asked me if helping Kirby was the wrong choice."


Another.


"She asked me if she should've said no."


Andrew stared.


Completely stunned.


"Adam..."


"No."


Adam shook his head gently.


"You need to hear this."


Another.


"Andrew, you and Kirby made her question if she made the right choice these last five weeks."


The words landed hard.


Adam continued.


"She left her daughter."


Another.


"She left home."


Another.


"She barely slept."


Another.


"She lived inside that hospital."


Another.


"Because she loved all of you."


Another.


"Because there was never a version of this story where Jane wasn't going to help."


Andrew looked down.


Unable to meet his eyes.


Adam continued.


"She would've done it again."


Another.


"Tomorrow."


Another.


"Without hesitation."


Because that was who Jane was.


Adam sighed.


"Then yesterday she came home feeling guilty."


Another.


"Guilty for missing time with Lizzy."


Another.


"Guilty for leaving me behind."


Another.


"Guilty for putting her family second."


Andrew closed his eyes.


Immediately.


"Jesus."


Adam nodded.


"Yeah."


Another.


"Because after everything she sacrificed..."


He paused.


"...it felt to her like none of it mattered."


Andrew looked up again.


Hurt now.


Not defensive.


Hurt.


"That wasn't what I meant."


"I know."


Adam's answer was immediate.


"I know that's not what you meant."


Another.


"But intent and impact aren't always the same thing."


Silence.


Adam let that sit.


Then added quietly:


"She came home feeling tossed aside."


Another.


"Like she'd spent five weeks loving and supporting everyone."


Another.


"And then it became very easy for both brothers to tell her she could disappear from their lives."


Andrew flinched.


Physically.


"That's not fair."


"No."


Adam agreed.


Another.


"It isn't."


Another.


"Because I don't think that's what either of you meant."


Another.


"But that's how it felt to her."


Neither spoke for several moments.


Finally Andrew laughed bitterly.


"She really took it that badly?"


Adam looked at him.


"Andrew."


Another.


"She ran into the house crying."


Another.


"She was still crying when she went to bed."


The guilt on Andrew's face was immediate.


Adam softened.


Because this wasn't what Andrew wanted either.


"Look."


Another.


"I actually understand needing space."


Andrew looked surprised.


Adam smiled sadly.


"More than you think."


Another.


"I know what it's like to lose Jane."


Another.


"I know what it's like to think she's gone."


Another.


"I know what it's like to fight to get her back."


The room became quiet.


Because Andrew knew exactly what Adam meant.


For a while neither spoke.


Then Adam said:


"I don't think she needs to know you're not leaving."


He paused.


Then shook his head.


"No."


Another.


"That's not right."


Andrew frowned.


Adam looked directly at him.


"I think she just wants to know you're not pushing her out of your life."


Silence.


Deep silence.


Because suddenly Andrew understood.


Not the words.


Jane.


Adam continued.


"She doesn't understand why Kelly breaking up with you means she has to lose access to her best friend."


Another.


"She genuinely doesn't understand the connection."


Another.


"And honestly?"


Another.


"I don't think you've explained it."


Andrew laughed quietly.


"Probably because I can't."


Adam nodded.


"That's fair."


Another.


"But she loves you."


The words were simple.


Matter-of-fact.


True.


"As her best friend."


Another.


"As family."


Another.


"As Lizzy's godfather."


Another.


"As someone who's been there almost her entire life."


He smiled.


"And Jane loves fiercely."


That finally earned a smile from Andrew.


Because there was no arguing with that.


Adam continued.


"She isn't trying to fix you."


Another.


"She's trying to love you."


Another.


"There's a difference."


Andrew stared at his coffee.


Thinking.


Really thinking.


Then Adam smiled.


"And for the record?"


Andrew groaned.


"What now?"


"If she decides you're getting checked on in Hill Dale..."


Adam shrugged.


"I'm driving."


Andrew laughed.


Actually laughed.


"You would."


"Absolutely."


Another.


"She'll ask."


Another.


"I'll say yes."


Another.


"Then somehow Lizzy will end up coming too."


Andrew shook his head.


Smiling despite himself.


Adam pointed at him.


"My point is you're stuck with us."


Another.


"Whether you like it or not."


For a long moment neither said anything.


Then Adam's expression softened again.


"You don't have to do this alone."


Another.


"I'm not saying let her move into your apartment for the weekend."


Andrew snorted.


"Thank God."


"But maybe let people care about you."


Another.


"Maybe let people show up."


Another.


"That's all she was trying to do."


The room grew quiet again.


Finally Andrew leaned back in his chair.


Exhausted.


Thoughtful.


A little less lost than before.


"She's not going to let this go, is she?"


Adam laughed immediately.


"No."


Another.


"Not a chance."


Another.


"You've known her for thirty years."


Another.


"You should know better."


For the first time in days, Andrew smiled.


And for the first time since Kelly left...


The thought of Jane showing up anyway didn't feel like something to avoid.


It felt reassuring.
