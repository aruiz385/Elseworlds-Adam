# Chapter 15: Complicated (Part 2)

"Anyway, are you hungry?" Jane asked, setting the silver frame carefully back on the side table.

She turned toward the kitchen island, deliberately moving the conversation away from old memories.

"I have my list on the island and pizza is waiting. I have stuff in the fridge too if you don't want pizza."

She shot him a look over her shoulder.

"I wouldn't want to force you to eat something you don't like."

Adam followed her into the kitchen.

Even as she smiled, he could see the sadness lingering from the conversation about Andrew and the picture frame.

"Jane."

She looked up.

"You know I'm joking about hating pizza, right?"

"No."

The answer came so quickly he laughed.

"I really don't."

That earned a small smile from her.

He glanced around again.

"Your place is nice."

A pause.

"But it doesn't really look like anyone lives here."

The smile disappeared.

To his surprise, she didn't seem offended by the observation.

Instead she nodded.

"That's fair."

She grabbed paper plates from a cabinet.

"I'm barely home. I work a lot."

She placed the plates on the counter.

"Andrew helped clear out most of Kirby's things after he died."

Adam's chest tightened.

"And then it got even emptier a few years later when Andrew moved back upstairs."

She shrugged lightly.

"I honestly haven't changed much of anything."

That answer hurt more than he thought it would.

"Half the time I feel like a guest here."

She gestured toward the living room.

"There are two bedrooms I barely use except for storing clothes."

A pause.

"I spend most of my time on that couch."

Adam looked toward the couch again.

Suddenly it made sense.

The blankets.

The pillows.

The worn cushions.

That was the only place in the apartment that looked lived in.

"What can I get you to drink?" she asked.

She opened the refrigerator.

"I have water and enough diet soda to concern several healthcare professionals."

Adam folded his arms.

Jane immediately pointed at him.

"Don't."

"What?"

"Don't give me the doctor lecture."

"I wasn't going to."

"You absolutely were."

"I wasn't."

She narrowed her eyes.

Adam smiled.

"I'll take water."

Jane looked suspicious but handed him a bottle anyway.

He took a sip before asking:

"Why do you sleep on the couch?"

Jane immediately looked away.

"Because I like the couch."

"No one likes sleeping on the couch."

"I do."

"No."

She sighed.

Adam waited.

Eventually she gave up.

"The master bedroom was Kirby's."

The words came quietly.

"The guest room was Andrew's."

Adam said nothing.

"I still have a hard time going into either room."

She looked down.

"I'm working on it."

The matter-of-factness hurt.

Like she'd repeated the explanation enough times that she'd removed all emotion from it.

"Why do you stay?" he asked softly.

Jane blinked.

"What?"

"Why stay here?"

She looked around her apartment.

Confused by the question.

"Because this is my place."

A shrug.

"I own it."

Another.

"I like the building."

A faint smile.

"It's walking distance from work."

Adam wasn't sure if she was convincing him or herself.

Before he could respond, Jane snapped her fingers.

"Movies."

She pointed dramatically at the list on the island.

"We are supposed to be working on movies."

Adam laughed.

There she was.

Trying to redirect the conversation before it got too heavy.

She picked up the list.

"I would force Dirty Dancing on you."

"You absolutely would."

"But we both agree that I've Had The Time of My Life has become public enemy number one."

Adam nodded solemnly.

"A truly terrible song."

She gasped.

"It's not terrible."

A beat.

"It's just trying to emotionally destroy me."

"Fair."

Jane smiled proudly.

"Every Sunday."

Adam frowned.

"What?"

"I listen to it every Sunday."

He blinked.

On purpose?

"I think I'm almost there."

The hope in her voice stunned him.

She was proud of that progress.

And she deserved to be.

The realization made his chest ache.

Here she was.

Actually doing the work.

Showing up.

Trying.

And somehow mostly doing it alone.


"Besides Karen," he asked quietly, "do you have other friends?"

Jane looked up from the list.

Then back down.

"Not really."

The answer came too quickly.

"When Andrew and I were together, we had a group."

She turned the pages slowly.

"When we broke up, we kind of had to split everyone."

Adam frowned.

"They were mostly couples."

A sad smile appeared.

"So Andrew got the majority of them."

Something about that bothered him immediately.

"I still see Karen."

She smiled.

"Although currently she's seventy-five percent wedding planner, twenty percent chaos, and five percent human."

Adam laughed.

That sounded exactly right.

"I help her where I can."

Then she looked back at her notes.

"Okay."

She pointed.

"Some Like It Hot."

Flip.

"It Happened One Night."

Flip.

"50 First Dates—"

She stopped.

Adam hadn't responded.

He was staring at nothing.

Lost in thought.

"Hey."

Her hand reached across the island.

Touching his.

"You okay?"

Adam blinked.

"Yeah."

"No."

She squeezed his hand gently.

"You have that look."

"What look?"

"The 'I feel sorry for Jane' look."

He opened his mouth.

She held up a finger.

"Don't."

Adam laughed.

"I'm okay. Really."

Which was ironic considering she was comforting him.

Again.

"How about you stop worrying about me for five minutes?"

She tilted her head.

"Do you actually have friends?"

Adam smiled.

"I do."

"Name three."

"What am I, twelve?"

"Name. Three."

He sighed dramatically.

"Adam has friends."

Jane immediately pointed.

"Cheating."

"I'm not cheating."

"You are absolutely cheating."

"Fine."

He laughed.

"His dad."

"That feels mandatory."

"Scott."

"Also mandatory."

"Dr. Ryan."

Jane groaned.

"You are terrible at this."

Adam leaned back in his stool.

"Okay, coworkers that become friends sometimes."

"Better."

A pause.

"And you."

The statement landed harder than either expected.

Jane immediately looked away.

Adam continued before she could react.

"Scott keeps telling me I need more friends."

That got her attention.

"I'm with Scott."

"He also suggested I talk to Andrew."

Jane nearly dropped her soda.

"What?"

Adam laughed.

There it was.

Exactly the reaction he'd expected.

"Why would my uncle suggest you become friends with Andrew?"

A pause.

"That seems incredibly chaotic."

"Scott seems convinced we'd get along."

Jane looked horrified.

Which only made him smile.

Then his smile faded.

"I need to tell you something."

Immediately Jane sat up straighter.

"Oh no."

"It's not bad."

"That's what people always say before bad news."

Adam sighed.

Fair.

A very fair point.

"I told Andrew."

Jane froze.

"...told him what?"

"About Adam."

She stared.

"DID."

A pause.

"The patient."

Another.

"Everything."

Jane blinked.

And blinked again.

"He took it surprisingly well."

She wasn't surprised.

"Andrew is good like that."

The answer came immediately.

Without hesitation.

Without bitterness.

Just certainty.

Adam noticed.

Of course he noticed.

"He asked about you."

Jane looked up.

"He did?"

"Not you-you."

A smile.

"He asked if my girlfriend was okay."

Jane nearly choked.

Adam looked entirely too amused.

"I told him she had a panic attack."

Jane rubbed her face.

"Oh my God."

"He told me his ex dealt with those."

The smile disappeared.

"He offered to talk if I ever needed to."

Jane stared quietly at the countertop.

"He dealt with a lot."

The words came slowly.

"Probably more than he should have."

Adam listened.

"He handled most of it well."

A sad smile appeared.

"No one should have to deal with someone who can't deal with themselves."

"Jane—"

She shook her head.

"I'm serious."

She stood and carried her half-finished pizza toward the trash.

A sadness settled over her expression.

One Adam recognized immediately.

"I am happy he's free of all that."

The words sounded rehearsed.

Something she'd told herself hundreds of times.

"He gets to be happy."

A pause.

"With someone who doesn't give him nearly as much trouble."

Adam watched her carefully.

"Someone who can give him what he needs."

She threw away the pizza.

Then stood there staring into the sink.

Quiet.

Almost convincing.

Almost.

But not quite.

Because Adam could see it.

The grief.

The guilt.

And the heartbreaking certainty that somehow Andrew was better off without her.

And for the first time that evening, he felt something dangerously close to anger.

Not at Jane.

At the fact that she truly believed it.
