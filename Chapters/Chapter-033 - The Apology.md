# Chapter 33: The Apology

After work, Adam changed out of his scrubs.


Jeans.


A gray hoodie.


Running shoes.


Comfortable.


Familiar.


The version of himself he'd always been outside the hospital.


He grabbed his keys.


Then stopped.


Because he suddenly realized something.


He had absolutely no idea where Jane lived.


Which felt ridiculous.


He knew she lived in the same building as Andrew.


She'd told him that years ago.


Back when they used to get coffee after group therapy.


Back when their biggest conversations involved work, grief, and whether she was actually going to stick with therapy.


But while he'd known she lived one floor below Andrew...


He had never actually known where the building was.


Adam was heading toward the parking garage when he spotted Andrew.


"Hey."


Andrew looked up.


"Hey."


Adam hesitated.


Then:


"Jane agreed to talk to me."


Andrew immediately smiled.


A genuine smile.


"I'm glad."


The sincerity surprised him.


"You were hoping for that?"


"Yeah."


Adam laughed.


"Fair."


A pause.


"Actually..."


He rubbed the back of his neck.


"I don't know where she lives."


Andrew blinked.


Then immediately started laughing.


"Oh."


Right.


That.


Adam shook his head.


"It gets weirder every day."


Andrew grabbed a sticky note and wrote down an address.


"Same building as me."


A pause.


"One floor down."


Adam nodded.


"Thanks."


Andrew studied him for a moment.


Then smiled.


"Good luck."


Adam wasn't entirely sure whether that was encouraging or threatening.


An hour later he stood outside Jane's apartment door.


Staring at it.


Trying not to overthink.


Failing spectacularly.


He knocked.


Nothing.


Then again.


Still nothing.


Adam frowned.


Then heard music.


Loud music.


Coming from somewhere inside.


He sighed.


Of course.


Then he remembered the key.


Earlier that week he'd noticed one specific key attached to his keyring.


Labeled:


Jane's


He pulled it free.


Studied it.


Then looked at the door.


"Let's find out."


The key slid into the lock.


Turned.


And the door opened.


Adam blinked.


Well.


That answered that question.


He stepped inside carefully.


And immediately looked around.


The apartment felt warm.


Lived in.


Loved.


An open-concept living room flowed into the kitchen.


Photographs covered almost every available surface.


Karen.


James.


Andrew.


Chris.


Maria.


Kelly.


And everywhere...


Jane.


And his other self.


Karaoke nights.


Birthday parties.


Random dinners.


Karen and James's wedding.


Three years of memories.


Three years of a life.


Three years they had built together.


Adam stared at one picture longer than he meant to.


Jane laughing.


His other self laughing.


The look between them obvious.


Love.


His chest tightened.


Then he looked away.


Following the music instead.


Nothing's Gonna Stop Us Now.


One of his other self's favorite songs.


The sound led him down the hallway.


To the nursery.


Adam stopped.


The room was beautiful.


Yellow.


Purple.


White.


Every detail carefully chosen.


Then he saw it.


The Winnie the Pooh decal.


And beneath it...


The quote.


If ever there is tomorrow when we're not together, there is something you must always remember. You are braver than you believe, stronger than you seem and smarter than you think.


Adam read it twice.


Then smiled sadly.


Yeah.


His other self definitely chose that.


Movement caught his attention.


Jane sat cross-legged on the floor.


Wearing maternity overalls.


Surrounded by crib parts.


And looking thoroughly defeated.


Adam coughed.


Jane nearly launched herself off the floor.


"Oh my God!"


She grabbed her chest.


"How did you get in?"


Adam held up the key.


"I tried knocking."


A pause.


"You didn't answer."


Jane looked toward the speaker.


Then groaned.


"The music."


"The music."


She sighed dramatically.


"Sorry."


Adam gestured toward the disaster covering the floor.


"What are you doing?"


Jane pointed at the giant pile of wood and hardware.


"Attempting to tackle item number one on my to-do list."


"The crib?"


"The crib."


A pause.


"And losing."


Badly.


Adam sat beside her.


Picked up the instructions.


Studied them.


Then looked at the parts.


Then back at the instructions.


"This is basically high-stakes Legos."


Without hesitation Jane shoved the instruction booklet into his lap.


"Be my guest."


The defeat in her voice was impressive.


As Adam started sorting pieces, Jane found herself studying him.


Hoodie.


Jeans.


Running shoes.


Familiar.


Painfully familiar.


For a moment she was back almost four years.


Coffee.


Hospital hallways.


Three-hour conversations.


The Adam she'd met first.


The Adam who became her friend.


The Adam who always dressed exactly like this.


The Adam who listened.


The Adam who showed up with coffee and endless patience.


Her Adam had been different.


Not completely.


But different.


Better jackets.


Henleys.


Button-down shirts.


She'd spent years teasing him that he'd finally learned how to dress like a functioning adult.


This Adam looked exactly like the one she'd met in the beginning.


Before everything changed.


Before she fell in love.


She looked away.


"At least that's something you both have in common."


Adam glanced up.


"What?"


"Building things."


She gestured around the nursery.


"He built this room."


The statement surprised him.


Not because he didn't know.


Because of how certain she sounded.


Like every object carried a memory.


"Every shelf."


Another.


"Every decal."


Another.


"Every piece of furniture."


Another.


"He wanted it perfect."


Adam looked around again.


This time seeing not furniture.


But love.


The kind that started long before a child arrived.


Jane interrupted his thoughts.


"Are you hungry?"


"What?"


"I was going to make dinner."


A pause.


"Then the crib happened."


She pointed accusingly at the parts.


"So now I'm ordering food."


Adam grinned.


"Pizza?"


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"Pizza."


A pause.


"You know."


"Food."


Jane stared.


"You like pizza?"


Adam looked genuinely confused.


"Yeah."


Another.


"Who doesn't like pizza?"


Jane kept staring.


"Honestly."


Adam continued.


"I survived med school on pizza, caffeine and Diet Coke."


Another.


"I know that's not a very doctor thing to admit."


Another.


"But it's true."


Jane shook her head.


"And if I were to say pizza should be its own food group?"


"I would agree one hundred percent."


Adam answered immediately.


Still completely confused.


Jane laughed.


Actually laughed.


"I guess we never got lunch together."


"What?"


"All we ever did was drink coffee."


Adam smiled.


"That's true."


A pause.


"Lots of coffee."


Jane stood.


"Are you going to be okay in here while I figure out the pizza situation?"


Adam held up the instructions.


"I have entered the zone."


Jane rolled her eyes.


Then disappeared.


Forty-five minutes later she returned carrying pizza boxes.


And froze.


The crib was standing.


Perfectly assembled.


"Oh my God."


Adam looked up proudly.


"I know."


"You did it."


"I did."


Jane circled it suspiciously.


"Are you sure it won't collapse?"


"I'm certain."


A pause.


"Lizzy should get several quality years out of it."


The relief hit her instantly.


Before she could stop herself.


Before she could think.


Jane hugged him.


Hard.


"Thank you."


Another.


"Thank you."


Another.


"You have no idea how much this was stressing me out."


Adam froze.


Completely.


His eyes widened.


Jane immediately felt him tense.


And let go.


The kitchen.


The memory came flooding back.


"Oh."


Her face fell.


"I'm sorry."


Another.


"I didn't mean to invade your personal space."


The hurt on her face nearly killed him.


"Jane."


A pause.


"It's okay."


"No."


She shook her head.


"It isn't."


Adam took a breath.


"No."


Another.


"Listen."


Another.


"That's actually why I came."


Jane looked confused.


Adam rubbed the back of his neck.


Then finally said it.


"I wanted to apologize."


Silence.


"The kitchen."


Another.


"The way I treated you."


Another.


"He asked me to be kind."


His voice softened.


"And I wasn't."


Jane remained still.


"I woke up after three years."


Another.


"My brain completely short-circuited."


Another.


"But that's not an excuse."


Another.


"I hurt you."


Another.


"And I'm sorry."


Jane looked away.


"He told me not to stay."


A pause.


"I should have listened."


Another.


"I only stayed because I wanted to make sure you were okay."


Adam nodded.


"I know."


Another.


"Which somehow makes me feel worse."


Silence.


Finally Jane smiled faintly.


"Okay."


Adam blinked.


"Okay?"


"Okay."


Another.


"I forgive you."


Relief washed through him.


"So we're good?"


Jane thought about it.


Then nodded.


"We're good."


A few minutes later they migrated into the kitchen.


Pizza boxes open.


Paper plates.


The sort of dinner neither of them would recommend to patients.


"This is really good pizza."

Adam said.


Jane looked up.


"You picked it."


"Still."


Another bite.


"Excellent choice."


That earned the smallest smile.


"Least I can do."


A pause.


"You built Lizzy's crib."


Adam nodded.


"I'm glad I could help."


Another.


"If you need help with anything else..."


Another.


"Just let me know."


Jane looked at him.


"I mean it."


Another.


"I know Andrew promised to help."


Another.


"But I know things have been crazy for him."


Another.


"If you need something, I'd like to help."


Jane was quiet for a moment.


Then:


"Okay."


Adam smiled.


"Okay?"


"Okay."


Another.


"I'll let you know."


Adam nodded.


Then remembered something.


"Oh."


Jane looked up.


"What?"


Adam reached into his hoodie pocket.


And placed a small velvet box on the island.


Jane immediately froze.


The ring.


Adam pushed it toward her.


"I believe this belongs to you."


Jane stared at it.


Then pushed it right back.


"It doesn't."


Adam frowned.


"I found it in..."


"Top drawer."


Jane interrupted.


"Right side."


"Brown box."


"Under the sweaters."


Adam blinked.


Jane smiled faintly.


"He was terrible at hiding things."


Another.


"I found it three months before he actually tried to give it to me."


Against his will, Adam laughed.


That sounded exactly right.


"But it's meant for you."


Jane shook her head.


"No."


Another.


"I told him the day you came back it would go right back into that box."


Another.


"You can do whatever you want with it."


Adam looked uncomfortable.


"I don't think that's possible."


Jane frowned.


"What do you mean?"


"Heirloom."


Adam said simply.


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"Oh."


Adam looked surprised.


"He never told you?"


Jane shook her head.


Adam sighed.


"Of course he didn't."


Another.


"One of the first heart-shaped diamonds ever cut."


Another.


"My great-grandmother's."


Another.


"Then my grandmother's."


Another.


"My mom refused to wear it."


Another.


"So when my grandmother died she left it to me."


Jane stared at the ring.


Then immediately pushed it farther toward him.


"Oh no."


Adam laughed.


"Exactly."


"There is absolutely no universe where I keep a family heirloom."


Another.


"Take it."


Adam smiled.


"Even if I said I wanted you to keep it for Lizzy?"


Jane narrowed her eyes.


"That's not fair."


"Why not?"


"Because you're weaponizing sentimentality."


Adam laughed.


"Fair."


Jane pushed the box toward him again.


More firmly this time.


"Adam."


A pause.


"Now that I know what that ring is?"


Another.


"There is absolutely no universe where I keep it."


Another.


"I appreciate what you're trying to do."


Another.


"And I appreciate that you want Lizzy to have something from your family."


Another.


"But this is yours."


She nodded toward the box.


"Maybe one day you'll find someone to give it to."


Adam looked down at the ring.


Then back at Jane.


A strange sadness settled over him.


Not because she was wrong.


Because she wasn't.


Finally he nodded.


"Okay."


He picked up the box.


Then slipped it back into his pocket.


"I'll hold onto it."


Jane smiled.


Not noticing he never specified for whom.


The conversation drifted.


Work.


The nursery.


The baby shower.


Until Adam finally spoke again.


"I talked to Scott today."


Jane immediately looked suspicious.


"Should I be worried?"


"Probably."


That earned a reluctant laugh.


Then he grew serious.


"He told me about Oakland."


The room went quiet.


Jane looked down.


"Oh."


"Yeah."


A pause.


"He said you knew."


Jane nodded.


"I did."


Silence.


"The plan was for me to take the job."


Jane nodded again.


"It seemed like a good opportunity."


"It is."


A pause.


"It really is."


Jane waited.


Then Adam looked directly at her.


"I'm not taking it."


Everything stopped.


Jane blinked.


"What?"


"I'm turning it down."


Another.


"I'm staying."


Silence.


"Why?"


Adam thought about it.


Really thought about it.


Then finally:


"Because for the last two weeks everybody keeps telling me what my future is supposed to look like."


Another.


"Oakland."


Another.


"A fresh start."


Another.


"A new life."


He shook his head.


"But this is still my life."


Jane stared at him.


Unable to respond.


Because for the first time since he'd returned...


Adam wasn't following a plan someone else had made.


He was making a choice.


For himself.


"I haven't figured anything out yet."


He admitted.


"Not even close."


Another.


"But I know I'm not ready to leave."


Jane looked away.


Her emotions suddenly much harder to sort through.


"Okay."


Adam smiled.


"Okay?"


Jane nodded.


"Okay."


A few minutes later Adam stood to leave.


At the door he paused.


"Jane."


"Yeah?"


A small grin appeared.


"I made it this time."


Jane stared at him.


Then realization hit.


"Thrive City?"


Adam nodded.


"Thrive City."


For the first time that evening she laughed.


"You're only about three years late."


Adam smiled.


"Traffic was terrible."


Jane rolled her eyes.


"Don't get used to the praise."


Adam laughed.


Then left.


Jane stood alone in the apartment.


The completed crib sat quietly in the nursery.


One more thing checked off a very long list.


Her gaze drifted toward the front door.


Toward the place where Adam had been standing only moments before.


Because for the first time since that terrible morning in his apartment...


She wasn't entirely sure what came next.


And somehow...


That felt both terrifying and hopeful.
