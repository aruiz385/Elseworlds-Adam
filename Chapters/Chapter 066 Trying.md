# Chapter 66: Trying


Dr. Aaron Cohen's office was quiet.


Comfortably quiet.


The kind of quiet that somehow made people say things they weren't planning on saying.


Which was probably why he was good at his job.


Jane sat curled into the corner of the couch.


Lizzy slept peacefully against her chest.


One tiny hand wrapped around Jane's sweater.


Completely unaware that the adults around her were trying to navigate one of the strangest situations imaginable.


Adam sat beside them.


Close enough to touch them both.


Close enough to remind Jane that this was real.


That she was real.


That her life was real.


Dr. Cohen smiled when he noticed Lizzy.


"I have to say."


Another.


"This is the youngest patient I've ever had in couples therapy."


Adam looked down at his daughter.


Then back at him.


"She insisted on coming."


Dr. Cohen nodded seriously.


"Understandable."


Another.


"Very committed to her emotional support duties."


That earned the smallest smile from Jane.


Dr. Cohen noticed.


Because these days every smile looked earned.


"How are you two doing?"


Neither answered immediately.


Eventually Jane sighed.


"I don't know."


Another.


"That's the most honest answer I have."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"Fair."


Jane looked down at Lizzy.


Then back up.


"I spend all day helping Kirby."


Another.


"Trying to help him understand this world he woke up in."


Another.


"Trying not to overwhelm him."


Another.


"Trying not to hurt him."


Her voice cracked.


"And every day I have to hide the two people I love most."


The room fell silent.


Jane stared at the floor.


"He doesn't know about Adam."


Another.


"He doesn't know about Lizzy."


Another.


"And he asks questions."


Another.


"So many questions."


Another.


"And every answer feels like a lie."


Adam reached for her hand immediately.


Holding it tightly.


Jane squeezed back.


Without even thinking.


Dr. Cohen watched the exchange.


Then spoke gently.


"What are you most afraid of?"


Jane laughed once.


Without humor.


"Everything."


Another.


"I'm afraid he learns the truth."


Another.


"I'm afraid he doesn't."


Another.


"I'm afraid Scott's plan doesn't work."


Another.


"I'm afraid it does."


She rubbed her eyes.


Exhausted.


"I feel like no matter what happens..."


Another.


"Someone gets hurt."


Dr. Cohen nodded thoughtfully.


"And somehow you've decided that's your responsibility."


Jane didn't answer.


Because he wasn't wrong.


Dr. Cohen continued.


"Jane."


Another.


"Kirby's recovery isn't your responsibility."


Another.


"You can help him."


Another.


"You can support him."


Another.


"But you cannot carry him."


Jane looked away.


Because carrying people was what she did.


What she'd always done.


Dr. Cohen shifted his attention toward Adam.


"And how are you managing all of this?"


Adam sighed.


Immediately.


"The honest answer?"


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"Always."


Adam glanced toward Jane.


Then toward Lizzy.


Then back to Dr. Cohen.


"I understand why Scott is doing this."


Another.


"Professionally."


Another.


"I think easing Kirby into reality is probably the right decision."


Another.


"If I woke up tomorrow and discovered I missed eight years of my life, I wouldn't want everything dumped on me in one conversation either."


He paused.


"That doesn't make it easier."


Another.


"I see what this is doing to Jane."


Another.


"And every instinct I have tells me to protect her."


The confession sat heavily between them.


Adam rubbed the back of his neck.


"I'm trying to support Jane."


Another.


"I'm trying to support Kirby."


Another.


"And sometimes it feels like those two goals are fighting each other."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"Because they are."


Another.


"And that's okay."


Adam looked surprised.


Dr. Cohen smiled slightly.


"You're acting like you're supposed to know the right answer."


Another.


"There isn't one."


Another.


"There are only imperfect choices."


The room became quiet.


Dr. Cohen looked between them.


Then smiled.


"You know something?"


Jane and Adam both looked up.


"I've known the two of you a long time."


Another.


"Far longer than you've officially been a couple."


That earned matching eye rolls.


Dr. Cohen ignored them.


Professionally.


Mostly.


"I've always thought something."


Another.


"You never had a relationship problem."


Jane blinked.


"What?"


Adam laughed.


Because he'd heard versions of this before.


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"You had trauma."


Another.


"You had timing."


Another.


"You had fear."


Another.


"You had circumstances."


Another.


"But from the day I met you, I never thought you had a problem loving each other."


The room fell quiet.


Because neither of them could argue.


Dr. Cohen leaned forward.


"Now you've finally acknowledged you're a family."


Another.


"You're a couple."


Another.


"You're raising a child together."


Another.


"And my job isn't helping you figure out if you belong together."


Another.


"That question has already been answered."


Jane looked down immediately.


Emotional.


Dr. Cohen continued.


"My job is helping you navigate this."


Another.


"Together."


Adam squeezed her hand.


Jane squeezed back.


Dr. Cohen waited a moment.


Then looked at Adam.


"How is integration therapy going?"


Adam exhaled slowly.


"Complicated."


Dr. Cohen nodded.


"That's normal."


Adam glanced toward Jane.


"I've recovered more memories."


Another.


"Some are good."


Another.


"Some are difficult."


Another.


"And some raise more questions than answers."


Dr. Cohen turned toward Jane.


"And how are you handling that?"


Jane thought carefully.


"Better than I expected."


Adam looked genuinely surprised.


Jane smiled faintly.


"The memories don't scare me anymore."


Another.


"Not like they used to."


She looked at Adam.


"He does the work."


Another.


"He goes to therapy."


Another.


"He shows up."


Another.


"He keeps trying."


Her voice softened.


"And every day he's still him."


Adam's expression warmed immediately.


Dr. Cohen smiled.


"And Adam?"


Another.


"How are you handling the integration process while all of this is happening?"


Adam laughed softly.


"Probably not as elegantly as I'd like."


That earned a chuckle from Dr. Cohen.


Adam shrugged.


"The memories are hard."


Another.


"But honestly?"


Another.


"The bigger challenge right now is helping Jane through this."


Jane immediately rolled her eyes.


"There it is."


Another.


"There's the deflection."


Adam looked innocent.


Dr. Cohen laughed.


Actually laughed.


"She's right."


Adam groaned.


Traitor.


Dr. Cohen smiled.


Then became serious again.


"Let me give you both something practical."


Jane and Adam listened.


"You're trying to solve the entire situation."


Another.


"You can't."


Another.


"Not today."


Another.


"Maybe not for months."


He looked at Jane.


"Your job today is not figuring out how Kirby reacts to the truth."


Then Adam.


"Your job today is not protecting everyone from every possible outcome."


Another.


"Ask yourselves one question instead."


Jane frowned.


"What question?"


Dr. Cohen smiled.


"What does today need from me?"


The room grew quiet.


"Today."


Another.


"Not tomorrow."


Another.


"Not six months from now."


Another.


"Just today."


He looked at Lizzy.


Still sleeping peacefully.


"Today that answer might be as simple as being present."


Another.


"For each other."


Another.


"For your daughter."


Jane looked down at Lizzy.


At the little girl sleeping safely in her arms.


Then at Adam.


The man holding her hand.


The man who kept showing up.


The man she loved.


For the first time in days...


The future felt a little less overwhelming.


Because maybe Dr. Cohen was right.


Maybe they didn't have to solve everything.


Maybe they just had to survive today.


And today...


They were together.


For now, that was enough.
