# Chapter 21: Weird (Part 3)

Jane lasted almost twenty-four hours.

That was longer than Adam expected.

Shorter than she expected.


By Thursday afternoon she was standing in Karen's office doorway.


Karen looked up from her laptop.


Took one look at Jane.


And sighed.


"Oh no."


Jane pointed immediately.


"See?"


"What?"


"Why does everyone keep saying that?"


Karen leaned back in her chair.


"Because you have a face."


"I hate that all of you know my faces."


Karen laughed.


Then closed her laptop.


"What happened?"


Jane walked into the office and dropped into the chair across from her.


"Andrew had an idea."


Karen groaned.


"Oh God."


"That's exactly what I said."


"What kind of idea?"


Jane stared at her.


Karen froze.


Then slowly:


"...how bad is it?"


"Kelly wants the four of us to go on a double date."


Silence.


Karen blinked.


Once.


Twice.


Then buried her face in her hands.


"Oh."


"See?"


"Oh."


"Right?"


Karen laughed.


"That's...actually kind of weird."


"THANK YOU."


Jane felt vindicated.

For approximately three seconds.


Then Karen sighed.


And Jane's heart sank.


"No."


"What?"


"No."


Karen pointed.


"Don't do that."


"Do what?"


"That thing where you're right."


Karen laughed.


"I haven't even said anything."


"You were about to."


Unfortunately Karen smiled.


Which was answer enough.


"Oh my God."


"Jane."


"No."


"Jane."


"No."


Karen folded her arms.


"You realize you're going to spend the entire wedding weekend together anyway."


Jane stared at the ceiling.


"Why is everybody saying that?"


"Because it's true."


The worst part?

It was.


Karen's expression softened.


"Do you want my honest opinion?"


"No."


"Good."


Karen ignored her.


"I think you're scared of Kelly."


"No."


A pause.


"Maybe."


Another.


"A little."


Karen nodded.


That felt closer to the truth.


Not Kelly herself.

The memories.

The history.

The version of Jane that existed back then.


"I don't like who I was during that period."


The admission slipped out before she could stop it.


Karen's expression softened immediately.


"Oh, Jane."


"I was angry all the time."


A pause.


"Hurt."


Another.


"Suspicious."


She looked down.


"I hated feeling like that."


Karen reached across the desk and squeezed her hand.


"You were grieving."


Jane laughed.


"Story of my life."


"Maybe."


Karen smiled.


"But that's not who you are now."


The words stayed with her long after she left.


That evening Jane found herself standing in Adam's apartment watching the sun disappear beyond the bay.


Adam was reading.


Or pretending to.


The same page had been open for ten minutes.


He knew something was coming.


Eventually he lowered the book.


"You talked to Karen."


Jane narrowed her eyes.


"How do you know that?"


"You have the Karen face."


"Oh, for the love of God."


Adam laughed.


Then patted the spot beside him on the couch.


Jane immediately sat down.


"Well?"


"Well what?"


"What did Karen say?"


Jane groaned.


"Exactly what you and Andrew said."


Adam looked entirely too pleased with himself.


"I like Karen."


"Traitor."


"You keep saying that."


"Because it remains true."


A smile tugged at his mouth.


The room fell quiet.


Outside, the lights across the water slowly came alive.


Inside, Adam slipped an arm around her shoulders.


The motion so familiar now that neither of them thought about it.


For a few minutes neither spoke.


Then:


"Can I tell you something?"


Adam looked down.


"Always."


Jane stared out the window.


"It's weird."


"What is?"


"This."


She gestured vaguely.


"Being happy."


Adam's expression softened immediately.


Jane laughed quietly.


"That's a terrible thing to admit out loud."


"No, it isn't."


She leaned against him.


"It feels like every time something good happens I keep waiting for the catch."


A pause.


"The other shoe."


Another.


"Something."


Adam nodded.


Because he understood.


More than she realized.


"You know what I think?"


"What?"


"I think you've spent so long surviving that you forgot what normal feels like."


Jane considered that.


Unfortunately, he might have been right.


Again.


She hated when he did that.


"That's annoying."


Adam laughed.


"I know."


The conversation faded after that.


Not because it was over.


Because neither of them needed to say anymore.


Adam turned back to his book.


Jane curled against his side.


The city lights reflected across the bay.


Her overnight bag sat beside the door.

Right where it always did.


The wedding was a month away.

The double date was still unresolved.

The future remained terrifyingly uncertain.


But for this moment?


She was here.

Adam was here.

And somehow, despite all of the complications and weirdness and history between all of them, that felt like enough.

For now.
