# Chapter 11: Lunch with the Other Adam (Part 1)

The next morning, Jane was halfway out the door when she heard Andrew's voice.

She froze instantly.

It was a reflex at this point.

A year of avoidance had turned hiding from Andrew Call into something between an art form and a survival skill.

Without thinking, she stepped behind one of the large columns in the hallway of her building.

A second voice drifted toward her.

Kelly.

Of course.

Jane closed her eyes briefly.

Of course she was with him.

She hated the sharp twist of pain that realization still carried.

Then Andrew spoke again, and everything else disappeared.


"Kel, it was horrible."

His voice sounded tired.

Different.

Jane risked peeking around the column.

Andrew and Kelly were walking slowly toward the elevators.

"I tried to save him," Andrew continued quietly. "The kid was maybe five or six."

Jane's stomach dropped.


"Then Adam Carter, the kid's oncologist, walks in as the kid coded and he just froze."

Andrew looked down as he spoke.

"It was heartbreaking. I didn't know what to do."

Kelly reached for his hand.

"The boy died and Adam collapsed."

Jane closed her eyes.


"I feel for the guy. I do."

Andrew shook his head.

"I had to call it."

The words sounded heavy.

Like he hated them.

Like he was still hearing them.


"Afterward, I tried helping Adam, but it was like he wasn't there."

Jane listened silently.

Motionless.


"Scott came down and helped me get him into one of the offices."

A pause.

"About an hour later I went back to check on them and Adam was there."

Andrew frowned.

"But he looked weird."

Kelly glanced up at him.

"Weird?"


"I don't know how to explain it."

He ran a hand through his hair.

"He looked different."

The words caused a chill to run down Jane's spine.


"And even earlier today when I asked him to switch shifts with me, he still looked like a different person."

Andrew shook his head again.

"It was really strange."


For a moment neither of them spoke.

Then Andrew sighed.

"It has been one of the hardest shifts so far."

Kelly squeezed his hand.

Andrew squeezed back.


"I'm going to check on him again."

His voice softened.

"He is a nice guy."

A pause.

"I don't know him very well, but I like him."


The elevator doors opened.

Andrew and Kelly stepped inside.

And disappeared.


Jane remained frozen behind the column.

Everything finally made sense.

The code.

The collapse.

The Other Adam.

The grief.

The panic.


Adam hadn't forgotten about lunch.

He hadn't changed his mind.

He hadn't decided she was too much.

He'd been standing in an emergency room watching one of his young patients die.

A little boy.

A little boy who reminded him of everything he'd spent years trying to survive.


Jane's heart broke.


"Oh Adam..."

The words escaped quietly.

She rubbed at her eyes.

"God, I am so sorry."

A fresh wave of guilt washed over her.


"I wish I could have helped you."


A few minutes later she headed toward the restaurant.


The place overlooked the bay.

It was exactly the kind of restaurant she'd chosen because the original Adam would have liked it.

Outdoor seating.

Water views.

Enough distance from the hospital.

Enough distance from Andrew.

At least that had been the plan.


Jane stopped at the entrance.

Part of her wasn't convinced he would show up.

The Other Adam had promised.

But promises felt different now.

Everything felt different now.


Then she spotted him.


He was already there.

Standing near the entrance.

Waiting.


And yet somehow he looked nothing like Adam.


Not really.


The differences were subtle.

But once she noticed them, she couldn't stop seeing them.


Adam dressed comfortably.

Sweaters.

Soft colors.

Clothes that looked like he'd grabbed whatever was clean and hoped for the best.


This Adam looked like he belonged in a magazine.


Button-down shirt.

Dark pants.

Perfect posture.

Like he was always five minutes away from having to run back to the hospital.


The light stubble along his jaw didn't help.

Neither did the glasses.


On Adam, the glasses made him look intelligent.

Studious.

Approachable.


On this Adam they somehow made him look like he belonged on the cover of a magazine.


Jane hated that thought immediately.


If Kelly Healy could be on the cover of Vogue, this Adam could probably join her.


And that was deeply irritating.


A part of Jane still wished she would arrive and somehow discover that her Adam had returned.

That he'd been waiting for her.

That yesterday had all been some terrible misunderstanding.


But he hadn't.

And he wasn't.


"Hi Jane."


His smile appeared immediately.


Jane hesitated.


"Hey..."


The pause after the greeting was obvious.

And they both knew it.


His smile widened.


"Come on."


Jane looked up.


"You can say my name."


He folded his arms.

Amused.


"Say it."


Jane sighed.


"Hi Adam."


"There you go."

His smile softened.


"Was it that hard?"


Jane gave him a look.


"You know it was."


He seemed entirely too pleased with that answer.


"So here we are on our date."


Jane immediately narrowed her eyes.


"This is not a date."


"No?"


"No."


She pointed at him.


"This is more of a fact-finding mission."


Adam laughed.


"Is that so?"


"I thought you said you were going to give me a chance."


"I never said that."


His grin widened.


"Not in that way anyway."


"And what way would that be exactly?"


Jane groaned loudly.


"Do you plan on being serious for even one second during this lunch?"


To her surprise, he immediately relented.


"Okay."


His expression softened.


"How are you doing, Jane?"


The change was so abrupt it caught her off guard.


She stared at him.

Then sighed.


"Well..."


Her eyes drifted toward the water.


"You could have told me that Adam had a panic attack when he saw that boy die."


His expression immediately changed.


Protective.


"Did Scott tell you?"


Jane shook her head.

A tear slipped down her cheek.


"No."


She swallowed.


"I overheard Andrew talking to his girlfriend this morning."


A bitter laugh escaped her.


"While I was hiding behind a column trying to avoid having my own panic attack."


The words hung between them.


Jane stared down at the ground.


"I am sure he was thinking about his brother."


Her voice broke.


"When he saw that little boy die."


Before she realized what was happening, Adam stepped forward and wrapped his arms around her.


Instinctively.


Protectively.


"Jane."


His voice was calm.

Steady.


"It's okay."


She shook her head.


"No, it's not."


"Adam is fine."


His grip tightened slightly.


"I promise you."


Jane closed her eyes.


"He is going to be okay."


A pause.


"He just needs time."


The same answer he'd given yesterday.

Yet somehow hearing it again helped.


"It's okay."

His voice dropped lower.


"You're okay."


Jane took a shaky breath.

Then another.

Then another.


Eventually she looked up at him and desperately tried changing the subject.


"I actually need your doctor's opinion."


That got his attention immediately.


"Okay."


"D-Dr. Ryan prescribed Escitalopram."


He nodded.


"I took it."


A pause.


"And I feel a little loopy."


She looked nervous suddenly.


"Is that normal?"


"Yeah."

He answered immediately.


"That's normal."


Her shoulders relaxed marginally.


"But pay attention."


The doctor in him had fully taken over now.


"If you start feeling emotionally flattened..."


She blinked.


"What?"


"Like you're not feeling much of anything."


His expression was serious.


"Tell Dr. Ryan immediately."


Jane nodded.


"Okay."


"Do you live with anyone?"


His concern was genuine.


"Someone who can keep an eye on you?"


Jane laughed softly.


"No."


Then added:


"I live alone."


A pause.


"But I have my friend on speed dial."


Only after saying it did she realize he was still holding her.


Jane looked pointedly down at his arms.

Then back up at him.


He didn't move.


Instead he said:


"Well, I worry, Jane."


His voice softened.


"Just call me if you start feeling odd. Okay?"


Jane simply stared at him.


Then raised an eyebrow.


"Are you planning on letting me go?"


His smile returned immediately.


"Do you want me to?"


"Adam."


Her tone carried a warning.


"Come on."


Laughing, he finally released her.


"Fine."


Then he gestured toward the restaurant.


"You said lunch."


A grin tugged at the corner of his mouth.


"Let's get lunch."
