# Chapter 65: The First Year


Jane checked the time.


Then looked toward the hallway.


Her shoulders relaxed slightly.


It was time.


For the last week this had become routine.


Every day her parents brought Lizzy to the hospital.


Every day Adam found a way to carve out time between patients.


Every day Jane disappeared for a little while.


Just long enough to hold her daughter.


Just long enough to see Adam.


Just long enough to remember who she was outside of Room 401.


Adam refused to let her lose herself completely in Kirby's recovery.


And if Adam Carter decided something was important...


It generally happened.


Jane stood.


"I'll be back in a little while."


Kirby looked up.


"Meeting?"


Jane smiled faintly.


"Something like that."


Another.


"Try not to give Hayle a hard time."


That earned a small smile.


"No promises."


Jane laughed.


Then slipped out of the room.


The second she disappeared around the corner, the room felt different.


Quieter.


Kirby watched the doorway for a moment.


Then looked at Andrew.


"Can I ask you something?"


Andrew immediately knew where this was going.


And he already hated it.


"Sure."


Kirby hesitated.


Then finally asked:


"Why won't anybody tell me what happened to Jane?"


The question lingered between them.


Heavy.


Unavoidable.


Andrew set his tablet aside.


There it was.


The question everyone had been avoiding.


The one Scott kept trying to delay.


The one Jane dreaded.


Kirby leaned back.


"Everyone tells me what happened to them."


Another.


"I know Karen got married."


Another.


"I know James got married."


Another.


"I know you're a doctor."


Another.


"I know Dad retired."


Another.


"But nobody talks about Jane."


Andrew looked down.


Thinking.


Because Scott was right.


Kirby wasn't ready for everything.


But he deserved something.


Eventually Andrew nodded.


"Okay."


Another.


"You deserve part of the story."


Kirby's attention sharpened.


Andrew took a deep breath.


"After you died..."


The words still felt wrong.


Even now.


"Jane fell apart."


Kirby's face immediately changed.


Andrew continued.


"Completely."


Another.


"There isn't a prettier way to say it."


Another.


"She stopped eating."


Another.


"She barely slept."


Another.


"She cried constantly."


Another.


"Some days she wouldn't leave her bed."


The guilt appeared immediately on Kirby's face.


Andrew ignored it.


Because this wasn't about blame.


It was about truth.


"I was in medical school."


Another.


"Working."


Another.


"Trying to survive."


Another.


"And every chance I got I was running to check on her."


Kirby frowned.


"What do you mean?"


Andrew laughed once.


Without humor.


"We had something Karen called the Jane Watch."


Kirby's eyebrows lifted.


Andrew nodded.


"Karen."


Another.


"James."


Another.


"Me."


Another.


"Sometimes Dad."


Another.


"We took shifts."


The room went silent.


"To make sure she ate."


Another.


"To make sure she showered."


Another.


"To make sure she got out of bed."


Another.


"To make sure she wasn't alone."


Kirby looked away.


Unable to hide the pain.


Andrew's voice softened.


"There were days I wasn't sure we were going to keep her."


Neither man spoke for several moments.


Then Kirby finally asked:


"What happened after that?"


Andrew sighed.


Here came the hard part.


"You know I've always loved Jane."


Kirby's eyes widened slightly.


Andrew smiled sadly.


"You always knew."


Kirby nodded.


Because he had.


Even when they were teenagers.


Even when Jane married him.


Andrew rubbed his hands together.


"I'm not sure I ever stopped."


Another.


"Even now sometimes..."


Another.


"I think about what might have been."


The confession felt strange.


But honest.


Kirby didn't judge him.


Instead he asked:


"What happened?"


Andrew looked toward the window.


Lost for a moment.


"Jane and I tried."


The room became very quiet.


"For almost two years."


Kirby absorbed that.


Eventually:


"Why didn't it work?"


Andrew stared at the floor.


"Because we were broken."


Another.


"Both of us."


His voice grew softer.


"We lost a baby."


Kirby's breath caught.


Andrew nodded.


"It was early."


Another.


"But it destroyed us."


Another.


"Or maybe it just exposed how broken we already were."


He swallowed.


"Between losing our son..."


Another.


"And her guilt over you..."


Another.


"It just didn't work."


The room was silent.


"I wanted a future."


Another.


"A family."


Another.


"A life."


Another.


"And Jane couldn't see one."


Kirby stared at him.


Then frowned.


"I don't understand."


Another.


"Jane loved you."


Another.


"So much."


Another.


"Why would she just walk away?"


Andrew's expression immediately softened.


Because there had been a time when he'd asked himself that same question.


Every day.


For months.


"Because losing you was one of the hardest things she's ever had to do."


Another.


"The guilt consumed her."


Another.


"It consumed me too."


Another.


"But Jane constantly felt like she was betraying you with me."


Kirby looked away.


Pain flashing across his face.


Andrew continued.


"She felt like she couldn't give me what I needed."


Another.


"She felt like she wasn't enough."


Another.


"She was wrong."


Another.


"So very wrong."


His voice cracked slightly.


"But you know Jane."


Another.


"You know her better than most people."


Another.


"When she feels small..."


Another.


"When she thinks she's hurting someone..."


Another.


"She retreats."


Another.


"And then she convinces herself she has to give people up for the greater good."


Kirby stared at him.


Then suddenly laughed.


Just once.


Andrew looked confused.


"What?"


Kirby shook his head.


"Well."


Another.


"You and Jane have a lot in common."


Andrew groaned immediately.


"Oh God."


Another.


"Here we go."


That only made Kirby smile wider.


"I'm serious."


Another.


"If I could count the times you gave her up for the greater good..."


Andrew covered his face.


Kirby laughed.


For the first time in days.


"No offense."


Another.


"But you and Jane have always had a history of overcomplicating life and love."


Andrew shook his head.


Laughing despite himself.


"I was one of the people who thought you two were endgame."


Andrew's smile faded a little.


Kirby shrugged.


"Even after Scott told me I lost eight years."


Another.


"There was part of me that expected to hear that you finally got it together."


Another.


"Built a life."


Another.


"Had kids."


Another.


"The whole thing."


Andrew snorted.


"The whole thing?"


"The whole thing."


Another.


"I almost looked at Lizzy and thought Adam was covering for you."


Andrew blinked.


"Covering for me?"


Kirby nodded.


"I was half expecting you to tell me you and Jane were together."


Another.


"And Lizzy was your daughter."


Andrew stared at him.


For one horrifying second.


Then quickly recovered.


"You thought Lizzy was mine?"


Kirby shrugged.


"It's uncanny."


Another.


"She reminds me of Jane."


Another.


"Especially when she smiles."


Andrew smiled and shook his head.


"I don't think Kelly would forgive me."


Another.


"Though these days..."


Another.


"I'm not entirely sure."


His smile faded.


"She's been really shaken up since you came back."


Kirby looked away.


"Yeah."


Another.


"Kelly."


There was sadness there.


A sadness Andrew didn't fully understand.


But before he could ask about it, Kirby changed the subject.


"I like Adam."


Andrew smiled immediately.


"Yeah."


Another.


"So do I."


Kirby laughed.


"No."


Another.


"I mean it."


Another.


"He gave me a lot to think about."


Another.


"His story is unbelievable."


Andrew laughed.


"You have no idea."


Another.


"I've worked with him."


Another.


"And I've worked with the other him."


Kirby shook his head.


"I don't think I've ever met someone like him."


Another.


"But what impresses me isn't the DID."


Another.


"It's the life he built."


Andrew became quiet.


Kirby continued.


"His daughter is gorgeous."


Another.


"And the way he looks at her..."


Another.


"You can tell she's his world."


Andrew smiled.


Because that part was undeniably true.


Then Kirby's expression softened.


"I saw how Jane looked at Lizzy."


Andrew's heart skipped.


Another.


"Hard to believe she ever told you she couldn't see herself as a wife or a mother again."


Another.


"When she was holding that little girl..."


Another.


"That's all I saw."


Another.


"Love."


The room grew quiet.


"I'd hate to think she never gave that to someone."


Another.


"I'd hate to think she spent eight years just surviving."


Another.


"Because while I love her..."


Another.


"And I do..."


Another.


"I want more than that for her."


Kirby looked down at his hands.


For a long moment he didn't speak.


Then:


"God."


Another.


"I had no idea."


Andrew remained silent.


Kirby swallowed hard.


"I knew she'd be sad."


Another.


"I knew losing me would hurt."


Another.


"But hearing all of this..."


He shook his head.


"I never imagined it was that bad."


The guilt was obvious.


Written all over his face.


Kirby stared out the window.


"She was twenty-six."


Another.


"All she should have been worried about was building a life."


Another.


"Instead she was trying to survive one."


Andrew felt his chest tighten.


Because he remembered that version of Jane all too well.


Kirby looked back at him.


"Did she ever get happy again?"


The question came so fast Andrew almost missed it.


As if it was the only thing that truly mattered.


Andrew smiled.


"Yeah."


Another.


"Eventually."


Another.


"It took a long time."


Another.


"But yeah."


Relief crossed Kirby's face.


Immediate.


Genuine.


"Good."


Another.


"She deserves that."


The sincerity in his voice made Andrew's throat tighten.


Because there wasn't any jealousy there.


No possessiveness.


Only concern.


The same concern Kirby had always carried for Jane.


Even before they married.


"I look at her now..."


Kirby continued.


"And something feels off."


Andrew raised an eyebrow.


"Off?"


Kirby nodded.


"She smiles."


Another.


"She jokes."


Another.


"She takes care of everyone."


Another.


"But sometimes..."


Another.


"It feels like she's carrying something."


Another.


"Like there's a weight she never puts down."


Andrew looked away.


Because that observation was painfully accurate.


Kirby's voice softened.


"And when she thinks nobody is looking..."


Another.


"She looks tired."


Another.


"Not physically."


Another.


"The kind of tired that comes from carrying too much for too long."


The room fell quiet.


Then Kirby laughed weakly.


"You know what's crazy?"


Andrew shook his head.


"What?"


"I spent the last week thinking everybody was protecting me."


Another.


"But the more I learn..."


Another.


"The more I think you've all been protecting Jane."


The statement landed.


Hard.


Because it wasn't entirely wrong.


Andrew slowly nodded.


"Maybe."


Kirby stared toward the doorway where she'd left.


Thinking about the woman he'd loved.


The woman who still sat beside him every day.


The woman who somehow still held his hand whenever he was scared.


And for the first time since waking up...


His sadness wasn't for himself.


It was for her.


Because while everyone kept talking about the eight years he had lost...


Kirby was beginning to understand that Jane had lived every single one of them.


And some of those years had cost her far more than they had ever cost him.
