# Chapter 26: Wedding Magic (Part 1)

The inn had finally gone quiet.

Most of the wedding guests had drifted inside hours ago.

The lake stretched endlessly beneath the moonlight, calm and still.

From somewhere inside the building came the occasional burst of laughter, followed by Karen's unmistakable voice trying to organize something that probably didn't need organizing.


Adam stood at the edge of the dock staring out over the water.

A beer rested loosely in his hand.

For the first time all day, it was quiet enough to think.


"Figured I'd find you here."


Adam turned.

Andrew walked toward him carrying two beers.


"Am I that predictable?"


"Painfully."


Adam accepted the bottle.


"Thanks."


The two men settled against the railing.

For a while neither spoke.

The lake didn't require conversation.


Finally Adam sighed.


"Can I ask you something?"


Andrew immediately looked over.

Something in Adam's voice made him straighten.


"Yeah."


Adam stared out at the water.


"I'm not sure I'm going to be here."


Andrew's expression softened.


"Adam."


"No."

Adam shook his head.


"I'm serious."


A pause.


"I don't know when."


Another.


"I don't know if it's next month."


Another.


"Six months."


Another.


"A year."


His grip tightened slightly around the bottle.


"But I don't think I can keep pretending I know how this ends."


Andrew remained quiet.

Listening.


"I want to be here."

Adam said softly.


A pause.


"I want to see my baby."


Another.


"I want to be there when they're born."


His voice cracked.


"I really want that."


Andrew swallowed.


"You will."


Adam smiled sadly.


"You know better."


For a moment Andrew didn't answer.

Because he did know.

Not certainty.

Not doom.

Just uncertainty.

The thing all of them had been living with.


Finally Andrew nodded.


"Okay."


A pause.


"Then tell me what you need."


Adam let out a slow breath.

Relief.

Because Andrew understood.


"If I'm gone..."


Another pause.


"Take care of her."


Andrew nodded immediately.


"Always."


"And the baby."


"Always."


Adam laughed softly.


"I knew you'd say that."


Andrew nudged his shoulder.


"I mean it."


"I know."


The silence settled comfortably between them.


Then Adam spoke again.


"Jane and I talked."


"About Original Adam."


"Yeah."


Andrew wasn't surprised.


Adam smiled.


"She's stubborn."


Andrew barked out a laugh.


"See? We agree on everything."


"That wasn't a compliment."


"It wasn't supposed to be."


The two men laughed.


"She's absolutely determined to make sure he gets his life back."

Adam continued.


"That sounds like Jane."

Andrew said.


"It does."


Adam took another sip of his beer.


"She doesn't want him carrying responsibility for the baby."


Another.


"Or for her."


Andrew nodded.


"Because she cares about him."


"Exactly."


Adam looked out at the lake.


"And I need something from you."


"If I can."


"If he comes back..."


A pause.


"Tell him this wasn't his fault."


Andrew frowned.


"Adam."


"I'm serious."


A pause.


"He'll watch the videos."


Another.


"He'll see everything."


Another.


"And after he gets past being angry..."


Another.


"He's going to blame himself."


Andrew didn't argue.

Because he was probably right.


Adam shook his head.


"He'll think he ruined her life."


Another.


"He'll think everything happened because he disappeared."


Another.


"He'll think Jane suffered because of him."


The dock fell silent.


"And that's not true."

Adam said.


"No."

Andrew answered immediately.


"It isn't."


Adam finally looked at him.


"Promise me you'll tell him."


A pause.


"Promise me you'll make him understand she's okay."


Another.


"That she's happier."


Another.


"That everything is better."


Andrew nodded.


"I promise."


A pause.


"Because it's true."


Adam smiled.

A small one.

But real.


Adam closed his eyes briefly.


Relieved.


"Thank you."


For a while neither spoke.


Then Andrew noticed something.


The look on Adam's face.


The nervous one.


The one that meant there was more.


"What?"


Adam laughed.


Andrew pointed at him.


"I know that face."


Adam groaned.


"No you don't."


"I absolutely do."


A pause.


"What are you planning?"


Adam stared at the lake.


Then sighed.


"I want to ask Jane to marry me."


Andrew immediately smiled.


"There it is."


Adam laughed nervously.


"Yeah."


The smile faded slightly.


"But you know what she's going to say."


Andrew nodded.


Because he did.


"'Adam deserves a choice.'"


"'I can't put that on your other self.'"


"'It's not fair to him.'"


They said it almost simultaneously.


Then both laughed.


Because it was exactly what Jane would say.


Adam shook his head.


"She's right."


"She is."


A pause.


"So ask her anyway."


Adam looked over.


"What?"


Andrew smiled.


"You've spent two and a half years doing things Jane initially said no to."


"That sounds bad when you say it like that."


"It worked, didn't it?"


Adam laughed.


Andrew continued.


"The lunch."


A pause.


"The girlfriend title."


Another.


"The fact-finding mission."


Adam groaned.


"It was a date."


Andrew grinned.


"My point is..."


A pause.


"If she tells you no..."


Another.


"Do what you always do."


Adam narrowed his eyes.


"What's that?"


Andrew smiled.


"Ask anyway."


Adam stared at him.


Then laughed.


Hard.


"You are a terrible influence."


"I learned from the best."


For a moment they simply stood there.


Two men who loved the same woman in entirely different ways.


Both hoping for a future they couldn't predict.


Finally Andrew stepped forward and wrapped Adam in a quick hug.


The kind men rarely gave each other.


The kind that meant something.


When he stepped back, Andrew looked him directly in the eye.


"You're going to be okay."


Adam smiled.


"I hope so."


Andrew nodded.


"No."


A pause.


"You will."


For a moment Adam wanted desperately to believe him.


For a while neither spoke.


Then Adam laughed softly.


"What?"

Andrew asked.


Adam shook his head.


"Nothing."


A pause.


"I'm glad we became friends."


That surprised Andrew.


"Yeah?"


Adam nodded.


"Yeah."


Another.


"I didn't expect that."


Andrew laughed.


"Neither did I."


Adam smiled.


"I honestly thought you hated me."


"I did."

Andrew replied immediately.


Adam laughed.


"Fair."


"For like ten minutes."


"Ten whole minutes?"


"Maybe."


Then Andrew pointed toward the inn.


"But honestly?"


"What?"


"We desperately needed a fourth person."


Adam frowned.


"What?"


"To balance Chris out."


The two men immediately burst out laughing.


"Nobody balances Chris."


"Probably true."

Andrew admitted.


The laughter faded.


Then Adam glanced toward the inn.


"I'm glad she has you again."


Andrew's smile softened.


A pause.


"You know she missed you, right?"


Andrew looked down.


He did know.

But hearing it still mattered.


"All those years."

Adam continued.


"She missed you."


The lake sat quiet around them.


Finally Andrew nodded.


"I missed her too."


Adam smiled.


"I know."


A pause.


"And honestly?"


Another.


"I think you two would've found your way back eventually."


Andrew looked over.


"You do?"


Adam nodded.


"Twenty-five years is a long friendship."


Another pause.


"You don't just lose that."


Andrew smiled.


"Maybe."


Then looked at him.


"But you sped the process up."


Adam laughed.


"What does that mean?"


"It means you walked into a situation, decided everybody was being stupid, and fixed it."


The smile spread across Andrew's face.


"Very annoying quality, by the way."


"I've heard that."


"I'm grateful for it."


The sincerity caught Adam off guard.


Andrew shrugged.


"You helped bring my friend back."


A pause.


"You helped her heal."


Another.


"You helped her forgive me."


Adam looked away, uncomfortable with the praise.


"She did that herself."


"Maybe."

Andrew replied.


"But you helped."


Before Adam could answer, another voice called out.


"There you idiots are."


Both men groaned immediately.


Chris.


Of course.


James followed behind him carrying a beer.


Chris stopped several feet away and narrowed his eyes.


"Oh no."


"What?"

Andrew asked.


"You were having feelings."


"We were not."


"You absolutely were."


"We absolutely were not."


Chris pointed accusingly.


"I can smell emotional growth."


James laughed.


"That's not a thing."


"It is now."


The four men gathered near the railing.

The lake stretching out before them.


After a moment James raised his bottle.


"To surviving tomorrow."


The group laughed.


"To Karen not killing you before tomorrow."

Andrew added.


"A realistic goal."

Adam said.


James shook his head.

Still smiling.


Then lifted his bottle again.


"Seriously."


A pause.


"Thank you guys."


The laughter softened.


"To friendship."


Another pause.


"And to finally marrying the woman I love."


"It only took you fifteen years!"

Chris announced.


The dock erupted.


James immediately pointed at him.


"I hate you."


"No you don't."


"A little."


Chris grinned.


"I've seen glaciers move faster."


Andrew nearly spit out his drink.


Adam doubled over laughing.


James shook his head.


"You are unbelievable."


"And yet somehow I'm still invited."


"That remains under review."


The laughter continued for another moment.


Then James raised his bottle again.


"Despite him."


A glare toward Chris.


"To friendship."


"And to finally marrying the woman I love."


The four bottles clinked together.


Then Chris cleared his throat dramatically.


"Oh, I'm not done."


The entire group groaned.


"No."


"Oh yes."


Chris pointed first at Andrew.

Then at Adam.


"And to these two."


Adam sighed.


"There it is."


Chris ignored him.


"Getting it together with their women and one day doing the same."


Andrew laughed.


"I'm not touching that."


"Smart."

James replied.


Then Chris pointed directly at Adam.


"But you..."


A pause.


"You skipped a step or two."


The group laughed.


"A few."

Andrew agreed.


"A few."

James echoed.


Chris nodded.


"Straight past reasonable planning."


Another point.


"Right past normal progression."


Another.


"Completely ignored the instructions."


Adam was already laughing.


"And somehow ended up with a baby."


The dock erupted again.


Chris lifted his bottle.


"But seriously."


His tone softened.


"Cheers to you becoming a dad."


A smile spread across his face.


"Truly happy for you, brother."


For a moment Adam looked genuinely caught off guard.


Then he smiled.

A real one.


"Thanks."


Chris nodded.


"You're going to be good at it."


Adam glanced out toward the water.

Thinking of Jane.

The baby.

The nursery.

The future.


Then looked back at his friends.


"I hope so."


James raised his bottle one final time.


"You will."


The four bottles met again.


And for one perfect moment, beneath the lights of the inn and beside the quiet lake, none of them worried about tomorrow.


They were simply four friends celebrating a wedding, a baby, and whatever came next.
