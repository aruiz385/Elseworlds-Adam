# Chapter 19: Trying (Part 2)

The day after her conversation with Andrew, Jane received a text from Adam just before lunch.

Adam: I have a proposal.

Jane: That sounds dangerous.

Adam: Movie therapy.

Jane: Dr. Ryan would approve.

Adam: Exactly.

Jane: What's the catch?

Adam: Come over tonight.

Jane: To your place?

Adam: Look at you noticing details.

Jane: Adam.

Adam: Yes. My place.

Jane: This is suspicious.

Adam: I'm deeply trustworthy.

Jane: That's not how Scott describes you.

A few seconds later:

Adam: Scott's opinion has been noted and discarded.

Jane laughed.


That evening, she found herself standing outside Adam's apartment.

For the first time.

Which felt ridiculous considering how much time they'd spent together.

Most of that was Adam staying at her place.

Part of it was convenience.

Part of it was habit.

Part of it was that neither of them had really thought about it.

Now she was here.

And somehow she was nervous.


Adam opened the door before she could knock.


"Hey."


"Hey."


His smile softened immediately.


"Come in."


Jane stepped inside.

Then stopped.


Adam noticed instantly.


"What?"


She looked around slowly.


"This is not what I expected."


The apartment overlooked the bay.

One bedroom.

Comfortable.

Warm.

Lived in.


That was what surprised her.


It felt like someone actually existed here.


Books lined the shelves.

Medical journals sat in uneven stacks on the coffee table.

A sweatshirt was draped over a chair.

A blanket sat folded near the couch.

Coffee mugs occupied the sink.


Life.


The apartment felt full of it.


Adam shut the door behind her.


"So?"


"So what?"


"What exactly did you think my apartment looked like?"


Jane considered it.


"A hospital supply closet."


Adam stared.


"A what?"


"A really nice hospital supply closet."


"Jane."


"I'm serious."


He looked genuinely offended.


"You thought I lived in a doctor's office."


"I thought there was a strong possibility."


Adam sighed dramatically.


"You wound me."


She laughed.


Then her attention shifted toward the bookshelf.


Photographs.

Lots of photographs.


That surprised her too.


She moved closer.


The first photograph showed a woman with warm eyes and an easy smile.


His mother.


Jane picked it up carefully.


"Your mom?"


Adam looked over.

Immediately softening.


"Yeah."


A small smile appeared.


"I miss her."


Jane studied the picture.

Then looked at him.


"I can see her."


That surprised him.


"You can?"


"She looks kind."


Adam smiled.


"She was. She would have liked you. Her name was Elizabeth. My dad called her Lizzy. He really loved her"


Next to it sat another photograph.


Two little boys.

Maybe five years old.

Identical.

Covered in mud.

Grinning.


Jane's smile softened.


"Your brother?"


Adam nodded.


"Yeah."


She stared at the photo.


"You really were identical. What was his name?"


Adam laughed softly.


"Apparently. His name was Aidan"


A pause.


"I don't remember much from before the accident."


Jane's heart hurt immediately.


The accident.

The day he lost his mother.

The day he lost his twin brother.

The day everything changed.


"How old were you?"


"Five."


His answer was calm.

Not detached.

Not numb.

Just practiced.


"I remember pieces."


A pause.


"Mostly stories."


Another.


"My dad kept the photos."


Jane looked at the picture again.

The boys looked happy.

Safe.


"He would've liked you."


Jane looked up.


"What?"


Adam smiled at the photograph.


"My brother."


A pause.


"He would've thought you were funny."


Jane laughed softly.


"Funny isn't usually the first thing people say about me."


"Then he definitely would've liked you."


That made her smile.


Then her eyes landed on another photograph.


A teenage Adam.

A girl.

A beach.

Both laughing.


Jane froze.


Adam noticed instantly.


"Oh no."


"What?"


He pointed.


"That look."


"What look?"


"That look."


Jane narrowed her eyes.


Adam immediately started laughing.


"Jane."


"No."


"Jane."


She pointed toward the frame.


"Who is she?"


"There it is."


"Answer the question."


"Laney."


Jane blinked.


"Laney?"


Adam nodded.


"Adam's childhood friend and high school girlfriend."


Something about that immediately made her think of Andrew.

Apparently Adam saw the realization happen.


"There."


"What?"


"The Andrew comparison."


Jane groaned.


"Oh my God."


Adam laughed.


"You immediately thought about Andrew."


"Maybe."


"Exactly."


He smiled.


"They grew up together."


A pause.


"Knew each other forever."


Another.


"Thought they'd always end up together."


Jane looked back at the photograph.


"Life can be cruel."


Adam nodded.


"Sometimes."


Then he noticed she was still looking at it.

A little too long.


"Jane."


"What?"


"You have absolutely nothing to worry about."


She looked up.


"Really?"


"Really."


A pause.


"Not from me."


The answer immediately relaxed something inside her.


Then Adam smiled.


"Now the other Adam?"


Jane groaned.


"Adam."


"What?"


He laughed.


"I'm just saying."


A grin appeared.


"He was the one who liked Laney."


Jane folded her arms.


"That doesn't make this conversation any less weird."


"Fair."


Adam nodded.


"Honestly, I never felt any urge to go peek into Adam and Laney's relationship."


Jane frowned.


"What?"


Adam shrugged.


"Never crossed my mind."


A pause.


"Didn't care."


Another.


"Not even a little."


Then his smile shifted into something softer.


"But when Adam met you..."


Jane looked up.


"...that was different."


The words caught her off guard.


Adam smiled.


"I suddenly became very interested in what was going on over there."


Jane felt heat rise into her face.


"Adam."


"What?"


His grin widened.


"I'm just saying."


A pause.


"There was apparently something about you."


The way he said it made her blush even harder.


"Stop."


"I can't."


"Why?"


"Because it's funny."


Jane covered her face.


"You are impossible."


"And yet..."


His smile softened.


"You're standing in my apartment."


She rolled her eyes.

Trying very hard not to smile.

Failing miserably.


Adam laughed and slipped an arm around her waist.


"Come on."


"What?"


"Dinner."


The smell hit her as soon as they entered the kitchen.


Roasted chicken.

Vegetables.

A small serving of rice.


Healthy.

Ridiculously healthy.


Jane looked at the plate.

Then looked at Adam.

Then looked back at the plate.


"You planned this."


"I absolutely planned this."


"Where's the pizza?"


Adam pointed toward the counter.


"There is no pizza."


Jane stared.


"Adam."


"I am avoiding pizza at all costs."


She laughed.


"Pizza is a lifeline."


"It is not."


"It absolutely is."


Adam shook his head.


"You have clearly never had good food."


Jane gasped dramatically.


"We're fighting now."


"Fair."


"Pizza is one of humanity's greatest achievements."


Adam looked unimpressed.


"The wheel exists."


"Pizza."


"The internet exists."


"Pizza."


He started laughing.


"You are impossible."


"People keep telling me that."

And somehow, standing in his kitchen, surrounded by photographs of his past and teasing each other about pizza, Jane felt something she hadn't expected.

She wasn't just getting to know Adam.

She was getting to know the life that made him who he was.
