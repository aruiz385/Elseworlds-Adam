# Chapter 47: The Truth


Andrew held the door open.


"Come on."


Another.


"I'll make tea."


Jane followed him upstairs.


The familiar route felt strangely surreal.


Years ago she had practically lived up there.


Not quite.


But close.


Back when Andrew still left his door unlocked.


Back when half their friends treated the apartment as a second living room.


Back when Kirby was alive.


The thought hit harder than she expected.


Because this apartment had been Kirby's idea.


Of course it had.


Kirby had spent months trying to convince Andrew to buy the penthouse above Jane's apartment.


Some days the argument was family.


Some days it was convenience.


Some days it was an investment.


Knowing Kirby...


It was probably all three.


Jane could still remember helping him move in.


Boxes everywhere.


Karen directing traffic despite carrying exactly one lamp.


James somehow losing something important.


Kirby sitting in the middle of the floor laughing at all of them.


Somehow that had been years ago.


And somehow she hadn't been up here in a very long time.


The apartment looked almost exactly the same.


Which felt impossible.


Andrew disappeared into the kitchen.


A minute later he returned with a towel.


Without a word he draped it over her shoulders.


"Thanks."


He nodded.


Then started making tea.


Jane sat at the kitchen island.


Looking around.


Some of the furniture had changed.


A few pictures were different.


But it still felt like Andrew.


Practical.


Organized.


Comfortable.


Safe.


Which was probably why she suddenly felt like crying again.


A few minutes later a steaming mug appeared in front of her.


Andrew sat across from her.


Neither spoke immediately.


The rain tapped softly against the windows.


The apartment was warm.


Quiet.


Eventually Andrew broke the silence.


"So."


Jane laughed.


A tired laugh.


There it was.


The therapist voice.


"I hate when you start with 'so.'"


Andrew smiled.


"You say that every time."


"Because it's never followed by anything good."


"Fair."


The silence returned.


Jane wrapped both hands around the mug.


Letting the warmth seep into her fingers.


Eventually she sighed.


"I don't know."


Another.


"I just feel spent."


Andrew nodded.


"Okay."


Another.


"Why?"


Jane stared into the tea.


For a long moment she didn't answer.


Then:


"I finally told Adam."


Andrew looked up immediately.


"What do you mean?"


Jane laughed.


Without humor.


"I told him."


Another.


"Actually told him."


Another.


"No therapy language."


Another.


"No caveats."


Another.


"No excuses."


She swallowed.


"I told him I was in love with him."


The room fell silent.


Andrew blinked.


For a moment he forgot everything.


Forgot the hospital.


Forgot the last three days.


Forgot Kirby.


Because after all these months...


She had finally said it.


"What happened?"


The question came out quietly.


Jane's eyes filled immediately.


"Nothing."


Another.


"And that's the problem."


A tear rolled down her cheek.


"He didn't say anything."


Another.


"He just looked at me."


Another.


"He looked like..."


She stopped.


Andrew frowned.


"Like what?"


Jane stared into the tea again.


"Like he was done."


Silence.


Andrew almost laughed.


Not because it was funny.


Because it was impossible.


Adam?


Done?


With Jane?


Not a chance.


But he couldn't explain that.


Not yet.


"I think he finally gave up."


Andrew stared.


"What?"


Jane wiped at her eyes.


"How many times can you throw a ring back at somebody before they stop asking?"


Andrew winced.


That one hurt.


Because she wasn't entirely wrong.


But she was also completely wrong.


"Jane..."


She shook her head.


"No."


Another.


"Honestly?"


Another.


"I don't even blame him."


Andrew looked away.


Because he knew exactly what Adam had looked like all week.


And it wasn't someone who had given up.


It was someone carrying a secret.


Jane took another sip of tea.


Trying to steady herself.


"Anyway."


Another.


"I feel like we haven't talked in forever."


Another.


"What have you been up to?"


Andrew froze.


Immediately.


Jane noticed.


Of course she did.


"What?"


"Nothing."


She pointed at him.


"There."


Another.


"You just did it."


"Did what?"


"The thing."


Another.


"Adam's doing it."


Another.


"Scott's doing it."


Another.


"Now you're doing it."


Andrew said nothing.


Which was a mistake.


Because now she sat up straighter.


The knot in her stomach tightening.


"What is going on?"


Nothing.


Just silence.


And suddenly she was very cold.


Because Andrew never avoided her questions.


Not when something mattered.


Not when she was scared.


Not ever.


"Andrew."


Nothing.


"Andrew."


This time fear slipped into her voice.


Real fear.


"What happened?"


Andrew closed his eyes.


For a moment he was ten years old again.


Scared.


Helpless.


Watching his family fall apart.


Then he opened them.


And looked at her.


Really looked at her.


"You need to listen to me."


Every instinct in Jane immediately went on alert.


"What happened?"


"Just listen."


The words were barely above a whisper.


Jane's heart started pounding.


"Andrew."


"Two days ago."


Another.


"The hospital accepted several patients from a facility that was shutting down."


Jane frowned.


Not understanding.


"There was a John Doe."


Another.


"A long-term coma patient."


Another.


"No known family."


Another.


"No identification."


"Andrew."


Another.


"What does this have to do with me?"


His eyes filled instantly.


And now she was scared.


Truly scared.


"Nurse Hayle recognized him."


The room felt quieter somehow.


Smaller.


Jane's pulse pounded louder in her ears.


"Recognized who?"


Andrew held her gaze.


Then finally:


"Kirby."


Everything stopped.


The room.


The air.


The world.


Everything.


"What?"


The word barely existed.


Andrew swallowed hard.


"The patient."


Another.


"Jane..."


His voice broke.


"The patient looked exactly like Kirby."


She shook her head immediately.


"No."


Another.


"No."


Another.


"That's not funny."


She stood so fast the chair nearly toppled over.


"That's not funny, Andrew."


"I know."


Tears were running down his face now.


Because God...


He wished it was.


"We thought we were wrong."


Another.


"We hoped we were wrong."


Another.


"Nobody told you because we needed to know."


Jane stared at him.


Unable to process the words.


Unable to breathe.


Unable to think.


Then one realization cut through everything else.


"Adam knew?"


The question escaped before she could stop it.


Andrew closed his eyes.


"Yes."


The answer hit harder than everything else combined.


Adam knew.


For days.


And never told her.


Her knees nearly gave out.


Andrew was across the room before she hit the floor.


"Jane."


Another.


"Jane, look at me."


She couldn't.


Couldn't move.


Couldn't think.


Couldn't understand.


Then Andrew whispered:


"The DNA came back this afternoon."


Silence.


Complete silence.


"Jane."


Another.


"It's him."


And for the second time in her life...


Jane felt the world disappear beneath her feet.
