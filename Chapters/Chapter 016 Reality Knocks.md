# Chapter 16: Reality Knocks

The kitchen dance spiraled completely out of control.

What started as Adam proving that dancing in the kitchen was, in fact, a real thing quickly became something else entirely.

There was laughter.

Far too much laughter.

There were dramatic spins that nearly sent Jane crashing into the kitchen island.

There was terrible singing.

Mostly by Adam.

There were arguments about whether he was actually dancing or simply making things up as he went.

And there were kisses.

A lot of kisses.

By the time the song ended, neither of them was remotely interested in movies anymore.

Or pizza.

Or therapy lists.

Or anything resembling their original plan.

Somehow they had migrated from the kitchen to the couch.

Adam's arm was around her.

Jane was half curled against him.

And for the first time in what felt like forever, life felt uncomplicated.

Happy.

Safe.


Of course, life had a habit of ruining moments exactly like that.


Knock.


They both froze.


Knock.

Knock.


Adam pulled back slightly.

Still hovering over her.

Still holding her.


"Uh..."

He blinked.


"Are you expecting someone?"


Jane looked just as confused.


"Do you know me?"


Adam laughed.

Fair point.


Jane couldn’t remember the last time anyone knocked on her door.


A third knock echoed through the apartment.


Adam looked at her.

Then pointed.


"Hair."


Jane immediately reached for her ponytail.


"Oh God."


She tried to fix it with limited success. Adam laughed.


Another knock.


The expression on her face suggested she was about to disarm a bomb rather than answer a door.


Adam stayed on the couch.

At least initially.


Jane walked over and opened the door only a few inches.


Then instantly froze.


Andrew.


Standing in the hallway.

Looking equally uncomfortable.


For a brief moment panic flared.

Old habits.

Old wounds.

Old fears.


But surprisingly, she stayed grounded.


"Hey Jane."


Andrew shifted awkwardly.

Still not quite meeting her eyes.


"Hey Andrew."


Her voice remained steady.


"What are you doing here?"


Andrew rubbed the back of his neck.


"Well..."


A sigh.


"I was upstairs trying to study."


Jane waited.


"So was Kelly."


That sentence hurt more than she wanted it to.


"We heard extremely loud music coming from down here."


He gestured vaguely toward the apartment.


"Naturally Kelly got worried and sent me to check on you."


Jane stared.


"To make sure you were okay."


She nodded slowly.


"I'm fine."


A pause.


"Tell Kelly I appreciate the concern."


Another pause.


"Sorry about the noise."


She immediately started closing the door.


Andrew stopped it.


His hand landed against the frame.


"Jane."


Her eyes narrowed.


"Stop."


Something about his tone made her defensive immediately.


"We just wanted to make sure you're okay."


Finally he looked directly at her.


That was a mistake.


Because suddenly all the bottled-up frustration she'd been carrying for over a year surfaced.


"Oh really?"


Andrew looked taken aback.


"Because you haven't cared to check whether I was okay for over a year."


Silence.


Andrew looked away.


The guilt landed immediately.


"Jane."


A sigh.


"You know why we can't be around each other."


That answer only made her angrier.


"You know what, Andrew?"


Her voice sharpened.


"I do know."


A pause.


"And honestly, I've made peace with that”

Another.

She pointed behind her.

“And by the way the song was only two minutes and thirty-six seconds long."


"Again, I’m sorry about the noise. We’ll try to keep it down”


Andrew blinked.


"What?"


"You checked."


Jane folded her arms.


"I'm fine."


Then pointed toward the elevator.


"Tell Kelly thank you."


She tried closing the door again.


Andrew stopped it again.


Now his eyebrows were raised.


Something was bothering him.


She recognized the expression immediately.


Detective mode.


"Are you trying to get rid of me?"


Jane sighed.


"No."


His eyes narrowed.


"Are you alone?"


There it was.


The question.


Jane stared at him.

Then answered honestly.


"No."


Andrew straightened.


"So you're not alone."


"No."


A pause.


"So please leave."


Andrew suddenly looked uneasy.


More uneasy than she'd seen him in a long time.


"Jane."


His voice softened.


"What is happening?"


When she didn't answer, he continued.


"Are Karen and James here?"


Now it was her turn to stare.


"Seriously?"


"What?"


"You think the only people capable of visiting me are Karen and James?"


Andrew's silence wasn't helping.


"Andrew, please leave."


His concern visibly increased.


The problem was that concern was exactly what she didn't want right now.


"I know all your friends, Jane."


That immediately irritated her.


"Oh my God."


"If it isn't Karen or James or Maria, who is it?"


Jane threw her hands up.


"Why are you interrogating me like I'm a suspect?"


Andrew sighed.


"Because I care about you."


The words landed awkwardly.

Painfully.


"Because I'm worried about you."


Jane shook her head.


"No."


Andrew froze.


"You don't get to do that anymore."


Silence.


"And I don’t owe you an explanation..."


She stood a little taller.


"I'm fine."


A pause.


"Actually."


Another.


"I've never been better."


That was the statement that hurt Andrew.

Because for the first time in over a year, she sounded like she meant it.


Still, he didn't move.


And Jane knew if he kept standing there staring at her, eventually she would break.


From the couch, Adam had heard enough.


Every instinct told him to stay out of it.


Every instinct lost.


He stood.

Crossed the room.

And appeared behind Jane.


Andrew's expression immediately shifted from concern to pure confusion.


"Adam?"


Adam nodded.


"Hey Andrew."


For a moment nobody spoke.


Andrew looked at Jane.

Then Adam.

Then back to Jane.


"What is happening?"


Finally, Adam answered.


"You remember my girlfriend?"


Andrew frowned.


"The one who had the panic attack?"


Adam nodded.

Then pointed gently toward Jane.


"I do."


Andrew stared.


The realization took several seconds.


Then several more.


Jane closed her eyes.


This was officially the worst-case scenario.


"Yeah. This is her," Adam repeated calmly.


"She was having a panic attack because we were on a date and her ex-boyfriend walked into the restaurant with his current girlfriend."


Jane immediately pointed.


"It wasn't a date."


Adam looked at her.


"It absolutely was."


Then back to Andrew.


"I told her that from day one."


Andrew blinked.

Hard.


"Hello?"


He pointed at both of them.


"I'm still standing here."


Jane groaned.


"Can you both stop talking about me like I'm not in the room?"


That shut everyone up.


She looked at Andrew.


"You checked."


A pause.


"You saw."


Another.


"Is there anything else?"


Andrew hesitated.


"Jane, we need to talk."


She immediately shook her head.


"We really don't."


"Jane—"


"This doesn't concern you."


Her voice softened.


"I know what I'm doing."


A pause.


"I'm happy."


That one hurt him.

She could see it.


Then quietly:


"Please do what you've done for the last year."


Another pause.


"Stay away from me."


For several seconds Andrew simply stood there.


Then slowly nodded.


"Fine."


A sigh.


"Whatever you need."


He turned toward Adam.


"Big rotation day tomorrow."


Adam nodded.


"I know."


"Hope you're ready."


A meaningful look.


"I'm sure we'll catch up."


Adam smiled.


"I've the rotation covered. Yeah, let’s chat tomorrow"


A pause.


"Yeah."


"Tomorrow."


Andrew nodded.

Then walked away.


The second the apartment door closed behind him, Jane practically collapsed against Adam.


"Oh my God."


She buried her face in his chest.


"What was that?"


Adam laughed softly.


Then immediately became serious.


"Are you okay?"


He pulled back enough to look at her.


Really look at her.


And something surprised him.


The panic wasn't there.


The fear wasn't there.


The shaking wasn't there.


Jane blinked.

Thought about it.


Then smiled.


"Yeah."


A pause.


"I'm actually okay."


Adam's expression softened.


Jane wrapped her arms around his neck.


He held her tightly.


"I'm proud of you."


"What?"


"You handled that."


She laughed.


"You had to rescue me."


Adam shook his head.


"Nope."


He brushed her hair behind her ear.


"You were handling it."


His voice was full of pride.


"You looked him in the eye."


Jane realized he was right.


For the first time in over a year.

She had.


"This is what Dr. Ryan's been trying to get you to do."


A pause.


"Face him."


Jane looked down.


"I don't think I could've done it without you."


Adam smiled.


"I think you could."


Then kissed her forehead.


"But I've got your back anyway."


She laughed.


"Always?"


"Always."


A moment later she pointed at him.


"Okay."


"What?"


"You have a brutal shift tomorrow."


Adam groaned dramatically.


"And?"


"Why aren't you studying?"


Adam immediately brightened.


"Because."


He grinned.


"My brain needs mindless entertainment."


Jane narrowed her eyes.


"Dangerous statement."


"I have an important question."


"What?"


"Can we ignore your therapy list tonight?"


Jane gasped dramatically.


"The list? Well, I think we’ve been doing a pretty good job at ignoring the list thus far"


"So can we ignore The list."


"And watch one of my movies?"


A pause.


"Which movie?"


Adam's grin widened.


"Have you ever seen the eighties masterpiece known as *Mannequin*?"


Jane stared.


"A rom-com?"


"It is not a rom-com."


"It is exactly a rom-com."


"It's fantasy."


Jane burst out laughing.


"It has a great soundtrack."


"You like a rom-com."


"I refuse this slander."


Still laughing, she surrendered.


"Fine."


Adam immediately looked victorious.


Fifteen minutes later they were buried beneath blankets on the couch.


The movie played.

The apartment felt warmer.

Safer.

More alive than it had in years.


Adam held her close.

And for the first time in his life, he felt hopeful.


Not because everything was solved.

Not because things weren't complicated.


They were.

Terribly complicated.


But because the woman he loved loved him back.


And right now, that was enough.


By the end of the movie he was quoting scenes she didn't understand.

Singing songs she pretended to hate.

And making her blush far more than she wanted to admit.


Eventually the movie finished.


Neither moved.


The apartment grew quiet.


And sometime after midnight, still wrapped in blankets and tangled together on the couch, they both fell asleep.


For one night, at least, the world let them be happy.
